## THE MAKER'S MARKET RESEARCH

### Scene 1 (65 words)
The friends gathered at their secret fort with exciting news spread across their faces and genuine enthusiasm in their voices. "There is going to be a community Maker's Market next month in the park!" announced Layla enthusiastically, holding up a colorful flyer. "We can make things using our new woodworking, pottery, and design skills to sell there!" Everyone loved this idea immediately and completely. They could finally use everything they'd learned from the Master Thomas, Potter, and Inventor together to create something meaningful for their community!

### Scene 2 (65 words)
Benny jumped up excitedly with his characteristic energy and enthusiasm. "I will make more improved scarf clips for everyone!" he declared confidently. Riley wanted to make waterproof covers for sketchbooks and notebooks. Ellis wanted to make sturdy carrying trays with proper support beams. Layla wanted to make retractable pencil holders with optimal string length. They had so many creative ideas to share with each other! Their newly acquired skills could create so many potentially useful products for the community to purchase.

### Scene 3 (65 words)
"Wait just a moment," said Ellis thoughtfully, raising his hand for attention. He looked carefully at their meticulously drawn blueprints spread on the table. "How do we know what people actually want to buy at the market?" That was a genuinely good question that made everyone pause. What if they made things nobody needed or wanted to purchase? That would waste valuable materials and precious time completely. They could end up with unsold inventory that nobody wanted.

### Scene 4 (65 words)
Layla thought carefully about this important problem, remembering something her mother had mentioned. "We should ask people first before we start making anything at all," she suggested wisely and practically. "We can ask what they actually need in their daily lives. Then we can make those specific things!" Ellis nodded approvingly at this logical approach. "That is very smart thinking. We should do proper market research first before building anything at all. Businesses do research before creating products."

### Scene 5 (65 words)
"What exactly is market research?" asked Benny curiously, wanting to understand the concept fully. He had not heard that term used this way before in conversation. "Market research means finding out information systematically about what customers want and need," explained Layla clearly and professionally. "We ask questions to many people. We learn what people genuinely want and need. Then we know exactly what to make for them. Research prevents costly mistakes and wasted effort by providing data."

### Scene 6 (65 words)
Riley got paper and pencils from their fort supplies organized on the shelf. "We can make a proper customer survey," she said knowledgeably, remembering surveys from school. "A survey is a list of questions you ask many people to gather data." The friends worked together cooperatively and thoughtfully. They wrote thoughtful questions about what people needed at home, at work, or at school. Well-designed surveys help you gather useful information from many people efficiently and systematically.

### Scene 7 (65 words)
They wrote their first question carefully and precisely. "What do you need help organizing at home or work?" was one important question that could reveal needs. "What daily problems do you have that a product could solve?" was another useful one. Ellis wrote down seven well-thought-out questions on their survey form. Their survey looked quite professional and organized with clear formatting! Good questions get helpful, detailed answers that provide actionable data. Question design matters enormously.

### Scene 8 (65 words)
The next question was particularly important for business success and profitability. "How much would you be willing to pay for this item?" asked Riley practically and strategically. "We need to know the price range people are willing to pay." Layla agreed completely with this approach. "If we make it too expensive, nobody will buy it from us at all. Price matters a lot in selling things successfully to customers. Pricing affects demand significantly."

### Scene 9 (65 words)
The friends made twenty copies of their survey at the library copy machine. They each got five copies to complete systematically. "Let's go ask our neighbors systematically door-to-door," said Layla with determination and purpose. The friends walked through their neighborhood together confidently with clipboards. This was their first real market research project! They were gathering primary data about customer needs directly. Primary research means collecting your own data rather than using someone else's research.

### Scene 10 (65 words)
At the first house, Layla knocked politely on the door and waited patiently. Mrs. Chen answered with a friendly, welcoming smile. "We are doing market research for a community Maker's Market happening next month," explained Layla professionally and clearly. "Can we ask you some questions about your household needs?" Mrs. Chen smiled warmly and opened the door wider. "Of course I can help with your research!" she said kindly and supportively, inviting them inside.

### Scene 11 (65 words)
Mrs. Chen looked at the survey questions thoughtfully, taking her time. "I really need better decorative hooks for my keys," she said honestly and specifically. She also needed a convenient holder for her reading glasses that she kept losing constantly. Layla wrote down both answers carefully and clearly in neat handwriting. This information was proving very helpful for planning their production! Real customer feedback was valuable and actionable. Understanding specific needs helps you design better solutions.

### Scene 12 (65 words)
At the next house, Mr. Rodriguez had completely different needs from Mrs. Chen's requirements. "I desperately need something to organize and hold my mail efficiently," he explained with obvious frustration. "It gets very messy on my entry table every single day." Ellis wrote this down carefully in his detailed notes. People had so many different needs in their homes depending on lifestyle! Every household was unique with different problems. Diversity in needs requires diverse products.

### Scene 13 (65 words)
They surveyed twenty families systematically throughout the afternoon. Visiting homes in three different streets. Each family had different needs and preferences based on their circumstances. Some wanted organizers for various items cluttering their spaces. Some wanted plant holders for their gardens and windowsills. Some wanted hooks for bags, coats, and accessories. The friends wrote everything down meticulously in organized notes. They were systematically learning what people really wanted and would actually buy from their market booth!

### Scene 14 (65 words)
Back at the fort, they carefully analyzed all the survey answers together at their table. "Look at this clear pattern!" said Ellis excitedly, pointing at the data they'd compiled. "Eight people specifically want key hooks. That is the most popular need by far in our data." Patterns were clearly appearing in the data they collected today through systematic surveying. Patterns help you understand what customers want most urgently. Data reveals demand that might not be obvious otherwise.

### Scene 15 (65 words)
Riley counted and categorized all the answers methodically using tally marks. "Five people want plant holders for indoor or outdoor use," she reported accurately from her count. "Three people want mail organizers specifically for entryways." They made a comprehensive ranked list. The list showed what was needed most frequently in their community. This data helped them decide wisely what to make first with limited time and materials. Data drives smart business decisions and reduces risk significantly.

### Scene 16 (65 words)
"This research is so incredibly helpful and valuable!" said Benny with genuine appreciation and relief. "Before we asked people, I was planning to make scarf clips like before. But nobody even asked for those at all in our survey!" Layla nodded knowingly and wisely. "We could have wasted our time and materials completely on unwanted products. Research helps us make smart business choices and avoid costly mistakes entirely. This saves money and resources."

### Scene 17 (65 words)
Ellis explained the economics more clearly and thoroughly to the group. "If we make what people want, we will sell more successfully and earn more," he reasoned logically and analytically. "If we make what people don't want, we waste materials and time badly with no sales. Research saves us money and time significantly! It also reduces waste dramatically because we make only what people will actually buy. This is efficient resource allocation based on demand data."

### Scene 18 (65 words)
The friends decided together democratically based on their research findings. They would make key hooks first. Because eight people specifically wanted them. That represented strong demand in their market. Then plant holders because five people requested those items—moderate demand. They would use their woodworking and design skills effectively to meet real community needs efficiently. Supply should match demand carefully to avoid waste and maximize success. Understanding demand helps you allocate resources wisely.

### Scene 19 (65 words)
"We asked before we made anything," said Riley proudly and wisely, feeling accomplished. "That is smart, professional business practice used by successful companies." Layla agreed enthusiastically and completely. "We learned exactly what our community needs most urgently. Now we can help them effectively with useful products. That feels really good! This is how successful markets work properly and efficiently. Businesses research customer needs thoroughly before producing products to ensure demand exists."

### Scene 20 (65 words)
The friends started drawing detailed blueprints for key hooks and plant holders immediately and enthusiastically. They knew exactly what to make and why to make it. Market research had saved them from making the wrong things completely and wasting their limited resources. They felt prepared and confident now about the Maker's Market. "Let's create exactly what people actually need!" said Layla with determination and purpose. Research made them smart, efficient makers serving real needs.

## FL VOCABULARY

- **market research**. Systematically finding out what people want. Or need before making something to sell. Using surveys and data collection
- **survey**. A list of questions you ask. Many people to gather information and learn about their needs systematically
- **customer needs**. The specific things that people want or need. To buy to solve problems or improve their lives
- **demand**. How much people want to buy something. Strong demand means many people want it and will pay for it
- **supply**. The amount of something that is made or available to sell to customers in a market
- **primary research**. Collecting your own data directly from people. Rather than using existing information from others
