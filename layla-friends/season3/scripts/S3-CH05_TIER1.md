## THE POTTER'S VALLEY

### Scene 1 (32 words)
The friends walked to a beautiful green valley. Morning mist floated over the hills. They saw pottery wheels in a circle. A big brick kiln made soft smoke. This was where they would learn pottery.

### Scene 2 (33 words)
Layla held the clay pot invitation. Riley said, "It smells like earth." Ellis saw someone waving. A person in brown robes stood by the kiln. They walked down the grassy hill together to meet the Potter.

### Scene 3 (32 words)
Celeste, the Potter, was a kind woman with clay on her hands. "Welcome, young makers," she said. "I am Celeste, the Potter. Are you ready to learn?" The friends said yes. They were very excited to begin.

### Scene 4 (33 words)
The Potter showed them the pottery wheels. "Pottery is different from wood," she said. "Clay is soft. You must be gentle and patient. If you rush, the clay will fall down. Patience is very important here."

### Scene 5 (32 words)
Ellis asked, "How long does it take?" The Potter smiled. "Making takes an hour. Drying takes more time. Firing in the kiln takes many hours. Then cooling takes a whole day. You cannot rush pottery."

### Scene 6 (33 words)
Layla saw cracked pots on a shelf. "What happened to those?" she asked. The Potter said, "Those were rushed. They broke. Mistakes teach us lessons. Good pottery needs time and patience. Quality cannot be hurried at all."

### Scene 7 (32 words)
The Potter gave each friend clay. "First, wedge the clay," she said. She pushed and folded it. "This removes air bubbles. Air bubbles make clay explode in the kiln. So wedging is very important for safety."

### Scene 8 (33 words)
The friends wedged their clay. It was hard work! Riley's arms got tired. "Keep going," said the Potter. "The clay will get smooth." Ellis felt lumps in his clay. He kept working. Layla liked the rhythm. Push, fold, turn.

### Scene 9 (32 words)
After twenty minutes, the Potter checked each person's clay. "Good," she told Layla. "Almost ready," she told Ellis. "Keep trying," she told Benny. The clay needed to be perfect before they could make pots.

### Scene 10 (33 words)
Now everyone's clay was ready. The Potter sat at a wheel. "Your back should be straight. Your hands should be wet." She showed them how to sit. The friends each sat at a wheel. They were ready to start.

### Scene 11 (32 words)
"First, center the clay," the Potter said. She put clay on the wheel. She made it spin. Her hands pressed on the spinning clay. The wobbly clay became perfectly centered. "Stay calm," she said to them quietly.

### Scene 12 (33 words)
Layla tried. She put her clay on the wheel. She made it spin. The clay wobbled a lot! She pressed her hands on it. But it went more crooked. "Breathe slowly," the Potter said. "Be calm and gentle."

### Scene 13 (32 words)
Riley squeezed too hard. Her clay fell down. "Not too hard," said the Potter. "Not too soft. Just right. This is called 'touch.' Good potters learn the right touch. You are learning it now too."

### Scene 14 (33 words)
Ellis tried to think hard about it. But his hands were stiff. "Don't think so much," the Potter said. "Feel instead." Ellis closed his eyes. He felt the clay. Suddenly, it started to center! He smiled big.

### Scene 15 (32 words)
Benny's clay flew off the wheel! It went everywhere! He laughed. "I'm covered in clay!" he said. The Potter smiled. "Making mistakes is okay. Try again. Patience and practice make you better. Keep trying with good attitude."

### Scene 16 (33 words)
After one hour, Layla centered her clay. The Potter clapped. "Now make a bowl," she said. She pressed her finger into the clay. A small bowl started to form. It looked like magic. Everyone watched carefully and closely.

### Scene 17 (32 words)
Each friend tried to make a bowl. It was very hard! Too much pressure made holes. Too little did nothing. The clay had to be just right. Everything had to work together in perfect balance.

### Scene 18 (33 words)
By noon, they made wonky bowls. The bowls were crooked and lumpy. But they made them! "These will dry," the Potter said. "Next week we'll fire them in the kiln. You did well today. Good start, young makers."

### Scene 19 (32 words)
The friends walked up the hill. Riley looked at her clay hands. "I can't wait to see them finished," she said. Benny was already thinking about next week. They felt proud of their work today.

### Scene 20 (33 words)
Pottery was different from woodworking. Wood taught them about strength. Pottery taught them about patience. Each craft had different lessons. They were learning to make things in new ways. They felt like real makers now. They couldn't wait for more.
