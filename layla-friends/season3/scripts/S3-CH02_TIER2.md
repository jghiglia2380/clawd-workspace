## JOURNEY TO THE WOODWORKER'S GROVE

### Scene 1 (52 words)
The friends woke up before sunrise on Saturday morning. The sky was still dark outside their windows. Layla met Riley, Ellis, and Benny at the edge of the neighborhood. Everyone wore comfortable walking clothes and carried small backpacks. Their breath made little clouds in the cool morning air. The adventure was finally beginning.

### Scene 2 (53 words)
Ellis pulled out his map and the glowing invitation. The tree seal's green light still pointed east toward Willow Grove. "We should reach it in about an hour," he said. The friends started walking down the path into the woods. Morning birds were just beginning to sing their first songs of the day.

### Scene 3 (52 words)
The path through the woods was narrow but clear. Tall trees rose on both sides like natural walls. Sunlight was starting to peek through the branches above. Riley noticed beautiful things everywhere she looked. Mushrooms grew on old logs. Moss covered the rocks. "This forest feels magical," she whispered to her friends.

### Scene 4 (53 words)
Benny led the way with energy and excitement. His green scarf bounced as he walked. "I wonder what Master Thomas will be like," he said. Layla thought about it. "Probably patient and wise," she guessed. "You have to be patient to work with wood." Ellis nodded in agreement with that observation.

### Scene 5 (52 words)
After thirty minutes of walking, the path began to change. The regular trees gave way to much older and bigger ones. These ancient trees had thick trunks wider than cars. Their branches spread out high overhead, creating a natural ceiling. The light filtering through was greenish and golden. Everything felt peaceful and very old.

### Scene 6 (53 words)
"We must be getting close," said Ellis, checking his map again. The glowing tree symbol was brighter than ever now. The friends walked more slowly, looking around with wonder. Through the trees ahead, they could see an opening. A clearing was coming into view. This had to be Willow Grove at last.

### Scene 7 (52 words)
The friends stepped into the most beautiful clearing they had ever seen. Massive willow trees stood in a circle around an open space. Their long branches hung down like green curtains. In the center was a rustic workshop built from natural wood. Tools hung from tree branches. Sawdust covered the ground like golden snow.

### Scene 8 (53 words)
An elderly man worked at a large wooden table in the workshop. He had white hair and wore a leather apron covered with sawdust. His hands moved carefully over a piece of wood. He didn't look up right away. The friends waited quietly, not wanting to interrupt his focused work. Patience seemed important here.

### Scene 9 (52 words)
Finally, the man set down his tools and looked up at them. His eyes were kind and bright. "Ah, the invited ones have arrived," he said with a warm smile. "Right on time at sunrise. That shows respect for the craft." He brushed sawdust from his apron. "I am Master Thomas, the woodworker."

### Scene 10 (53 words)
Master Thomas walked toward them slowly. He moved with care, like every step mattered. "You received my invitation," he said. "Each of you carries something special inside—the desire to create with your hands." He gestured to the workshop around them. "This grove has taught woodworking for three hundred years. Today, you join that tradition."

### Scene 11 (52 words)
The friends introduced themselves one by one. Master Thomas listened carefully to each name and repeated it back. "Names are important," he explained. "In woodworking, everything has a name. Each tool. Each type of wood. Each technique. Learning the right names shows respect." He pointed to different tools hanging from branches overhead.

### Scene 12 (53 words)
"Before we begin working with wood," Master Thomas said, "you must understand where it comes from." He walked to one of the ancient willow trees and placed his hand on the bark. "These trees give us their wood after they fall naturally. We never cut living trees here. We wait patiently for their gift."

### Scene 13 (52 words)
Master Thomas showed them fallen branches and logs stacked neatly beside the workshop. Each piece was labeled with the tree it came from and the year it fell. "Wood has memory," he explained. "It remembers the seasons, the rain, the sunshine. When you work with wood, you work with something alive with history."

### Scene 14 (53 words)
He picked up a smooth plank and handed it to Layla. "Feel the grain," he instructed. Layla ran her fingers along the wood's surface. She could feel lines and patterns. "The grain shows you which direction the tree grew," Master Thomas explained. "If you work with the grain, the wood cooperates. Against it resists."

### Scene 15 (52 words)
Next, Master Thomas showed them the basic tools. Saws hung from one branch. Chisels from another. Sandpaper from a third. Each tool had its own special place. "Organization matters in any craft," he said. "When tools have homes, you spend less time searching and more time creating." Ellis appreciated this organized system.

### Scene 16 (53 words)
"Woodworking teaches patience," Master Thomas continued. "You cannot rush wood. If you hurry, you make mistakes. If you slow down and pay attention, the wood tells you what it wants to become." Riley thought about her art. Rushing never made her drawings better either. Patience helped in many kinds of creating.

### Scene 17 (52 words)
Master Thomas picked up a simple wooden box from his table. "This took three days to make," he said. The friends were surprised. It didn't look that complicated. "But look closely," he added. They examined it carefully. Every corner fit perfectly. The surface was smooth as glass. Quality took time to achieve.

### Scene 18 (53 words)
"Next week, you will return and begin learning to make boxes yourselves," Master Thomas announced. "Simple boxes. Nothing complicated yet. But you will learn the basic skills—measuring, cutting, sanding, joining." The friends felt excited. "For now, I want you to choose one piece of wood to take home. Study its grain tonight."

### Scene 19 (52 words)
The friends walked among the stacked wood, looking for pieces that called to them. Layla chose a smooth piece of maple. Riley picked oak with interesting grain patterns. Ellis selected straight-grained pine. Benny found walnut that felt warm in his hands. Each friend held their chosen wood carefully, feeling its weight and texture.

### Scene 20 (53 words)
Master Thomas walked them back to the edge of the grove. "You did well today," he said. "You listened. You showed respect. You asked good questions." The sun was fully up now, shining through the willow branches. "Study your wood this week. Learn to see its possibilities." The friends left feeling changed somehow.

- **tradition** — Knowledge passed from teacher to student over time
