## BUILDING BOXES

### Scene 1 (52 words)
The friends returned to Willow Grove for their third week of woodworking. They brought their imperfect boxes from last week to compare with today's work. Master Thomas examined each box carefully. "You will make new boxes today," he said. "The same design. But your hands remember more now. You will see the difference yourself."

### Scene 2 (53 words)
Master Thomas had them start fresh with new wood pieces. "Notice how measuring feels easier this time," he said as they worked. Layla realized he was right. Her hands knew where to place the measuring tape now. The pencil marks came out straighter. Repetition was teaching her body without her even thinking about it.

### Scene 3 (52 words)
Ellis measured his pieces twice like last time. But this week, both measurements matched perfectly on the first try. Last week, he had to measure three or four times to get it right. "Muscle memory," Master Thomas observed, watching him work. "Your hands are learning to be accurate automatically now through practice."

### Scene 4 (53 words)
When cutting time came, everyone noticed the improvement immediately. Riley's saw stayed on the line the whole way through. Benny didn't rush like last time. He moved slowly and the cut came out straight. "See how practice changes you?" Master Thomas said with approval. "Same people, same tools, different skills because of repetition."

### Scene 5 (52 words)
The sanding went faster this week too. Not because anyone rushed, but because they understood the motion better now. Layla's arms didn't get as tired because she was using better technique. "Efficiency comes from understanding, not from hurrying," Master Thomas explained. "Good technique makes the work easier and produces better results simultaneously."

### Scene 6 (53 words)
Master Thomas introduced something new this week: decorative details. "Now that your basic skills are developing," he said, "you can add small carved designs if you choose." He showed them simple patterns they could carve into their box lids. Stars, waves, simple flowers. "Details show that you care about your work beyond just function."

### Scene 7 (52 words)
Riley decided to carve a small flower pattern on her box lid. Master Thomas gave her a special carving tool and showed her how to hold it safely. "Carve away from your body, never toward yourself," he instructed firmly. Riley worked slowly and carefully. The flower shape emerged gradually from the smooth wood surface.

### Scene 8 (53 words)
Ellis chose to carve a geometric star pattern that appealed to his organized mind. Each point needed to be the same size and evenly spaced. He measured carefully with a ruler before carving any lines. "Planning before carving prevents mistakes," Master Thomas approved. "Measure, mark, then carve. Never rush into permanent changes without thinking first."

### Scene 9 (52 words)
Benny wanted to try carving too, but he chose a simpler wave pattern that wouldn't require as much precision. "Smart choice," Master Thomas said. "Know your current skill level and choose appropriate challenges." Benny's waves came out a bit wobbly but recognizable. He felt proud that he tried something new and succeeded reasonably well.

### Scene 10 (53 words)
Layla decided not to carve her box this week. "I want to focus on making the basic box better first," she explained. Master Thomas nodded with respect. "Wisdom," he said simply. "Knowing when to add complexity and when to master fundamentals first shows good judgment." Sometimes choosing not to do something was the right decision.

### Scene 11 (52 words)
When gluing time came, everyone applied just the right amount this week. No excess squeezing out like last time. No gaps from too little glue either. "Your hands remember the lesson," Master Thomas observed. The corners fit together more precisely too. Each friend had improved their alignment technique through last week's mistakes and corrections.

### Scene 12 (53 words)
While the glue dried, Master Thomas had them compare their new boxes with last week's boxes side by side. The difference was obvious to everyone. This week's boxes had straighter cuts, smoother surfaces, and tighter corners. "Same students, same teacher, one week of practice," he said. "Progress is always possible through dedicated effort."

### Scene 13 (52 words)
Riley held up both of her boxes to examine them closely. Last week's box had visible gaps and rough spots. This week's box felt smooth all over and the corners met perfectly. The carved flower added beauty too. "I made both of these," she said with wonder. "But this week's is so much better!"

### Scene 14 (53 words)
"The improvement you see comes from three things," Master Thomas explained to the group. "First, your hands learned through repetition. Second, your minds understood the mistakes from last time. Third, you cared enough to try again instead of giving up. All three together create growth." The friends listened carefully to this wisdom.

### Scene 15 (52 words)
Master Thomas showed them how to apply a simple oil finish to protect the wood. He rubbed oil onto a sample piece with a soft cloth. The wood's grain became more visible and beautiful under the oil. "This final step shows respect for your work and the wood," he explained while demonstrating the technique.

### Scene 16 (53 words)
Each friend applied oil to their new box carefully. As they rubbed the oil in, the wood seemed to come alive. Hidden grain patterns appeared. The color deepened and became richer. "The wood thanks you for finishing it properly," Master Thomas said. The boxes transformed from plain to beautiful under the protective oil coating.

### Scene 17 (52 words)
After the oil dried, the friends arranged their boxes on the workshop table. Four boxes in different stages of completion sat there together. One rough from week one. One improved from today. Some with carvings, some without. All showed progress in different ways. "This is what growth looks like," said Master Thomas.

### Scene 18 (53 words)
"Next week, you begin learning pottery from The Potter," Master Thomas announced. The friends had almost forgotten about the other invitations. "But you will return here eventually," he assured them. "Woodworking will always welcome you back. Skills don't disappear when you learn new ones. They wait patiently for you to return and practice again."

### Scene 19 (52 words)
The friends thanked Master Thomas deeply for teaching them. "You taught us more than woodworking," Layla said. "You taught us about patience, practice, and pride in our work." Master Thomas smiled warmly. "Those lessons apply to everything in life," he said. "I gave you tools that work in any craft you choose."

### Scene 20 (53 words)
Walking home through the familiar woods, the friends carried their finished boxes carefully. These boxes represented three weeks of learning, mistakes, improvements, and growth. "I can't wait to see what pottery is like," Riley said excitedly. "But I'll miss coming here." They all agreed. This grove had taught them valuable lessons they would carry forever.
