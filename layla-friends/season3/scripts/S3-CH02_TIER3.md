# Season 3, Chapter 2: Journey to the Woodworker's Grove
## Tier 3 Script (Target: 1,300 words, MSL 10-12)

**Scene 1** (68 words)
The friends woke up before sunrise on Saturday morning. The sky was still dark outside their windows. Layla met Riley, Ellis, and Benny at the edge of the neighborhood. Everyone wore comfortable walking clothes and carried small backpacks full of supplies. Their breath made little clouds in the cool morning air. The adventure was finally beginning after days of excited waiting and careful planning.

**Scene 2** (66 words)
Ellis pulled out his map and the glowing invitation. The tree seal's green light still pointed east toward Willow Grove. "We should reach it in about an hour," he said confidently. The friends started walking down the path into the woods together. Morning birds were just beginning to sing their first songs of the new day.

**Scene 3** (70 words)
The path through the woods was narrow but clear and easy to follow. Tall trees rose on both sides like natural walls protecting the way. Sunlight was starting to peek through the branches above them. Riley noticed beautiful things everywhere she looked with her artist's eyes. Mushrooms grew on old logs. Moss covered the rocks. "This forest feels magical," she whispered to her friends.

**Scene 4** (67 words)
Benny led the way with energy and excitement bouncing in every step. His green scarf bounced as he walked quickly ahead. "I wonder what Master Thomas will be like," he said. Layla thought about it carefully before answering. "Probably patient and wise," she guessed. "You have to be patient to work with wood." Ellis nodded in agreement.

**Scene 5** (69 words)
After thirty minutes of steady walking, the path began to change around them. The regular trees gave way to much older and bigger ones. These ancient trees had thick trunks wider than cars and trucks. Their branches spread out high overhead, creating a natural ceiling above. The light filtering through was greenish and golden. Everything felt peaceful and very old and special.

**Scene 6** (68 words)
"We must be getting close now," said Ellis, checking his map again carefully. The glowing tree symbol was brighter than ever before on the paper. The friends walked more slowly, looking around with wonder and curiosity. Through the trees ahead, they could see an opening in the forest. A clearing was coming into view. This had to be Willow Grove at last!

**Scene 7** (70 words)
The friends stepped into the most beautiful clearing they had ever seen. Massive willow trees stood in a circle around an open space in the middle. Their long branches hung down like green curtains swaying in the breeze. In the center was a rustic workshop built from natural wood. Tools hung from tree branches. Sawdust covered the ground like golden snow. Everything looked magical and peaceful.

**Scene 8** (67 words)
An elderly man worked at a large wooden table in the workshop. He had white hair and wore a leather apron covered with sawdust. His hands moved carefully over a piece of wood with skill. He didn't look up right away from his important work. The friends waited quietly, not wanting to interrupt his focused concentration. Patience seemed important here in this place.

**Scene 9** (69 words)
Finally, the man set down his tools carefully and looked up at them. His eyes were kind and bright with intelligence. "Ah, the invited ones have arrived," he said with a warm smile. "Right on time at sunrise. That shows respect for the craft." He brushed sawdust from his apron with steady hands. "I am Master Thomas, the woodworker."

**Scene 10** (68 words)
Master Thomas walked toward them slowly and carefully. He moved with intention, like every step mattered and had purpose. "You received my invitation," he said. "Each of you carries something special inside. The desire to create with your hands." He gestured to the workshop around them. "This grove has taught woodworking for three hundred years. Today, you join that tradition."

**Scene 11** (70 words)
The friends introduced themselves one by one politely. Master Thomas listened carefully to each name and repeated it back to them. "Names are important," he explained to them. "In woodworking, everything has a name. Each tool. Each type of wood. Each technique we use. Learning the right names shows respect." He pointed to different tools hanging from branches overhead in the trees.

**Scene 12** (67 words)
"Before we begin working with wood," Master Thomas said, "you must understand where it comes from." He walked to one of the ancient willow trees nearby. He placed his hand on the rough bark. "These trees give us their wood after they fall naturally. We never cut living trees here. We wait patiently for their gift."

**Scene 13** (69 words)
Master Thomas showed them fallen branches and logs stacked beside the workshop. Each piece was labeled with the tree it came from carefully. "Wood has memory," he explained to them. "It remembers the seasons, the rain, the sunshine over many years. When you work with wood, you work with something alive with history." The friends listened with growing respect for the material.

**Scene 14** (68 words)
He picked up a smooth plank and handed it to Layla carefully. "Feel the grain," he instructed her. Layla ran her fingers along the wood's surface slowly. She could feel lines and patterns in the wood. "The grain shows you which direction the tree grew," Master Thomas explained. "If you work with the grain, the wood cooperates. Against it resists."

**Scene 15** (70 words)
Next, Master Thomas showed them the basic tools for woodworking. Saws hung from one branch. Chisels from another branch. Sandpaper from a third branch. Each tool had its own special place in the workshop. "Organization matters in any craft," he said. "When tools have homes, you spend less time searching. You spend more time creating." Ellis appreciated this organized system very much.

**Scene 16** (67 words)
"Woodworking teaches patience," Master Thomas continued seriously. "You cannot rush wood at all. If you hurry, you make mistakes that waste the wood. If you slow down and pay attention, the wood tells you what it wants to become." Riley thought about her art. Rushing never made her drawings better either. Patience helped in many kinds of creating.

**Scene 17** (69 words)
Master Thomas picked up a simple wooden box from his table. "This took three days to make," he said. The friends were surprised at the time required. It didn't look that complicated to make. "But look closely," he added. They examined it carefully with new eyes. Every corner fit perfectly together. The surface was smooth as glass. Quality took time to achieve properly.

**Scene 18** (68 words)
"Next week, you will return and begin learning to make boxes," Master Thomas announced. "Simple boxes. Nothing complicated yet for beginners. But you will learn the basic skills we use. Measuring, cutting, sanding, joining wood together." The friends felt excited about the challenge ahead. "For now, I want you to choose one piece of wood. Take it home and study its grain tonight."

**Scene 19** (70 words)
The friends walked among the stacked wood carefully, looking for pieces. Layla chose a smooth piece of maple wood. Riley picked oak with interesting grain patterns that caught her eye. Ellis selected straight-grained pine wood. Benny found walnut that felt warm in his hands. Each friend held their chosen wood carefully, feeling its weight and texture. The wood felt special and full of possibility in their hands.

**Scene 20** (67 words)
Master Thomas walked them back to the edge of the grove. "You did well today," he said kindly. "You listened. You showed respect for the craft. You asked good questions." The sun was fully up now, shining through the willow branches. "Study your wood this week. Learn to see its possibilities." The friends left feeling changed somehow by the experience.
