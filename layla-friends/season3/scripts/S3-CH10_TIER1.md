## THE MAKER'S MARKET RESEARCH

### Scene 1 (32 words)
The friends met at their fort with exciting news. "There is going to be a Maker's Market next month!" said Layla. "We can make things to sell!" Everyone loved this idea.

### Scene 2 (33 words)
Benny jumped up. "I will make more scarf clips!" he said. Riley wanted to make covers. Ellis wanted to make trays. Layla wanted to make pencil holders. They had so many ideas to share!

### Scene 3 (32 words)
"Wait," said Ellis. He looked at their blueprints. "How do we know what people want?" That was a good question. What if they made things nobody needed? That would waste materials.

### Scene 4 (33 words)
Layla thought about this problem. "We should ask people first," she said. "We can ask what they need. Then we can make those things!" Ellis nodded. "That is smart. We should do research first."

### Scene 5 (32 words)
"What is research?" asked Benny. He had not heard that word before. "Research means finding out information," explained Layla. "We ask questions. We learn what people want. Then we know what to make."

### Scene 6 (33 words)
Riley got paper and pencils. "We can make a survey," she said. "A survey is a list of questions." The friends worked together. They wrote questions about what people needed at home or at school.

### Scene 7 (32 words)
They wrote their first question. "What do you need help organizing?" was one question. "What problems do you have at home?" was another. Ellis wrote down five good questions. Their survey looked professional!

### Scene 8 (33 words)
The next question was important. "How much would you pay?" asked Riley. "We need to know the price people want." Layla agreed. "If we make it too expensive, nobody will buy it. Price matters a lot."

### Scene 9 (32 words)
The friends made copies of their survey. They each got ten copies. "Let's go ask our neighbors," said Layla. The friends walked through their neighborhood together. This was their market research project!

### Scene 10 (33 words)
At the first house, Layla knocked on the door. Mrs. Chen answered. "We are doing research for a Maker's Market," explained Layla. "Can we ask you questions?" Mrs. Chen smiled. "Of course!" she said kindly.

### Scene 11 (32 words)
Mrs. Chen looked at the survey. "I need better hooks for my keys," she said. She also needed a holder for her reading glasses. Layla wrote down both answers carefully. This information was very helpful!

### Scene 12 (33 words)
At the next house, Mr. Rodriguez had different needs. "I need something to hold my mail," he said. "It gets messy on my table." Ellis wrote this down. People had so many different needs in their homes!

### Scene 13 (32 words)
They asked ten families. Each family had different needs. Some wanted organizers. Some wanted plant holders. Some wanted hooks for bags. The friends wrote everything down. They were learning what people really wanted!

### Scene 14 (33 words)
Back at the fort, they looked at all the answers. "Look at this!" said Ellis. "Five people want key hooks. That is the most popular need." Patterns were appearing in the data they collected today.

### Scene 15 (32 words)
Riley counted the answers. "Three people want plant holders," she said. "Two people want mail organizers." They made a list. The list showed what was needed most. This helped them decide what to make first.

### Scene 16 (33 words)
"This is so helpful!" said Benny. "Before we asked, I was going to make scarf clips. But nobody asked for those!" Layla nodded. "We could have wasted our time. Research helps us make smart choices."

### Scene 17 (32 words)
Ellis explained more. "If we make what people want, we will sell more," he said. "If we make what people don't want, we waste materials. Research saves us money and time!"

### Scene 18 (33 words)
The friends decided together. They would make key hooks first because five people wanted them. Then plant holders because three people wanted those. They would use their skills to meet real community needs effectively.

### Scene 19 (32 words)
"We asked before we made," said Riley proudly. "That is smart business." Layla agreed. "We learned what the community needs. Now we can help them. That feels good! This is how markets work."

### Scene 20 (33 words)
The friends started drawing blueprints for key hooks and plant holders. They knew exactly what to make. Market research had saved them from making the wrong things. They felt prepared. "Let's create what people actually need!" said Layla.

## FL VOCABULARY

- **market research** — Finding out what people want or need before making something to sell
- **survey** — A list of questions you ask people to learn information
- **customer needs** — The things that people want or need to buy
- **demand** — How much people want to buy something
- **community** — The people who live near you or in your neighborhood
