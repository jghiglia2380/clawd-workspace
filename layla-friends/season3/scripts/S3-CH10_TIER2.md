## THE MAKER'S MARKET RESEARCH

### Scene 1 (52 words)
The friends gathered at their secret fort with exciting news spread across their faces. "There is going to be a community Maker's Market next month!" announced Layla enthusiastically. "We can make things using our new skills to sell there!" Everyone loved this idea immediately. They could use their woodworking, pottery, and design skills together!

### Scene 2 (53 words)
Benny jumped up excitedly with energy. "I will make more scarf clips for everyone!" he declared. Riley wanted to make waterproof covers for sketchbooks. Ellis wanted to make sturdy carrying trays. Layla wanted to make retractable pencil holders. They had so many creative ideas to share! Their skills could create so many useful products for the community.

### Scene 3 (52 words)
"Wait just a moment," said Ellis thoughtfully. He looked at their carefully drawn blueprints on the table. "How do we know what people actually want to buy?" That was a genuinely good question. What if they made things nobody needed or wanted? That would waste valuable materials and precious time completely.

### Scene 4 (53 words)
Layla thought carefully about this important problem. "We should ask people first before we start making anything," she suggested wisely. "We can ask what they actually need in their lives. Then we can make those specific things!" Ellis nodded approvingly. "That is very smart thinking. We should do proper research first before building anything at all."

### Scene 5 (52 words)
"What exactly is research?" asked Benny curiously. He had not heard that word used this way before. "Research means finding out information systematically," explained Layla clearly. "We ask questions to people. We learn what people genuinely want and need. Then we know exactly what to make for them. Research prevents mistakes."

### Scene 6 (53 words)
Riley got paper and pencils from their fort supplies. "We can make a proper survey," she said knowledgeably. "A survey is a list of questions you ask many people." The friends worked together cooperatively. They wrote thoughtful questions about what people needed at home or at school. Surveys help you gather information from many people efficiently.

### Scene 7 (52 words)
They wrote their first question carefully. "What do you need help organizing at home?" was one important question. "What daily problems do you have?" was another useful one. Ellis wrote down five well-thought-out questions on their survey form. Their survey looked quite professional and organized! Good questions get helpful answers.

### Scene 8 (53 words)
The next question was particularly important for business. "How much would you pay for this item?" asked Riley practically. "We need to know the price people are willing to pay." Layla agreed completely. "If we make it too expensive, nobody will buy it from us. Price matters a lot in selling things successfully to customers."

### Scene 9 (52 words)
The friends made twenty copies of their survey at the library. They each got five copies to complete. "Let's go ask our neighbors systematically," said Layla with determination. The friends walked through their neighborhood together confidently. This was their first real market research project! They were gathering data about customer needs.

### Scene 10 (53 words)
At the first house, Layla knocked politely on the door. Mrs. Chen answered with a friendly smile. "We are doing market research for a community Maker's Market," explained Layla professionally. "Can we ask you some questions about your needs?" Mrs. Chen smiled warmly. "Of course I can help!" she said kindly and supportively.

### Scene 11 (52 words)
Mrs. Chen looked at the survey questions thoughtfully. "I really need better hooks for my keys," she said honestly. She also needed a convenient holder for her reading glasses that she kept losing. Layla wrote down both answers carefully and clearly. This information was proving very helpful for planning! Real customer feedback was valuable.

### Scene 12 (53 words)
At the next house, Mr. Rodriguez had completely different needs from Mrs. Chen. "I desperately need something to organize and hold my mail," he explained. "It gets very messy on my entry table." Ellis wrote this down carefully in his notes. People had so many different needs in their homes! Every household was unique.

### Scene 13 (52 words)
They surveyed twenty families throughout the afternoon. Each family had different needs and preferences. Some wanted organizers for various items. Some wanted plant holders for their gardens. Some wanted hooks for bags and coats. The friends wrote everything down meticulously. They were systematically learning what people really wanted and would actually buy!

### Scene 14 (53 words)
Back at the fort, they carefully analyzed all the survey answers together. "Look at this pattern!" said Ellis excitedly, pointing at the data. "Eight people want key hooks. That is the most popular need by far." Patterns were clearly appearing in the data they collected today. Patterns help you understand what customers want most.

### Scene 15 (52 words)
Riley counted and categorized all the answers methodically. "Five people want plant holders," she reported accurately. "Three people want mail organizers specifically." They made a comprehensive list. The list showed what was needed most in their community. This data helped them decide wisely what to make first. Data drives smart decisions.

### Scene 16 (53 words)
"This research is so incredibly helpful!" said Benny with genuine appreciation. "Before we asked people, I was planning to make scarf clips. But nobody even asked for those at all!" Layla nodded knowingly. "We could have wasted our time and materials completely. Research helps us make smart business choices and avoid mistakes entirely."

### Scene 17 (52 words)
Ellis explained the economics more clearly. "If we make what people want, we will sell more successfully," he reasoned logically. "If we make what people don't want, we waste materials and time badly. Research saves us money and time significantly! It also reduces waste because we make only what people will buy."

### Scene 18 (53 words)
The friends decided together democratically. They would make key hooks first because eight people specifically wanted them—that was strong demand. Then plant holders because five people requested those items. They would use their woodworking and design skills effectively to meet real community needs. Supply should match demand to avoid waste and maximize success.

### Scene 19 (52 words)
"We asked before we made anything," said Riley proudly and wisely. "That is smart business practice." Layla agreed enthusiastically. "We learned exactly what our community needs. Now we can help them effectively. That feels really good! This is how successful markets work properly. Businesses research customer needs before producing products."

### Scene 20 (53 words)
The friends started drawing detailed blueprints for key hooks and plant holders immediately. They knew exactly what to make and why. Market research had saved them from making the wrong things completely. They felt prepared and confident now. "Let's create exactly what people actually need!" said Layla with determination. Research made them smart, efficient makers.

## FL VOCABULARY

- **market research** — Finding out what people want or need before making something to sell, using surveys and questions
- **survey** — A list of questions you ask many people to gather information and learn about their needs
- **customer needs** — The things that people want or need to buy to solve problems or improve their lives
- **demand** — How much people want to buy something; strong demand means many people want it
- **supply** — The amount of something that is made or available to sell to customers
- **community** — The people who live near you or in your neighborhood who might buy from you
