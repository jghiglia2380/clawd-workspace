# CHAPTER 12: THE MAKERS' FAIRE - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Full entrepreneurial cycle from creation to sale
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where friends showcase and sell products at a Makers' Faire, celebrating their journey from learning skills to running real businesses
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Our Makers' Faire: Season Showcase & Sale"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-4:** Friends set up their booth with products, displays, and pricing → Activity includes booth setup with student-made items and price tags
- **Scenes 5-8:** First customers arrive, ask questions, and make purchases → Activity includes real "shopping" with play money and customer interactions
- **Scenes 9-10:** Families buy products, friends explain their learning process → Activity includes presenting the story behind each product
- **Scenes 11-13:** Mentors visit and celebrate the friends' growth → Activity includes reflection on skills learned throughout the season
- **Scenes 14-17:** Items sell out, friends count revenue and calculate profit → Activity includes transaction tracking and profit calculation
- **Scenes 18-20:** Reflection on everything learned and excitement for future making → Activity concludes with season reflection and celebration

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand complete business cycle from production to sale to profit calculation
2. **Entrepreneurship:** Experience running a real booth with products, pricing, and customer service
3. **Mathematics:** Track sales, count money, calculate revenue and profit
4. **Communication:** Present products persuasively and explain creation process to customers
5. **Reflection:** Synthesize all Season 3 learning (materials, production, design, market research)
6. **Celebration:** Take pride in work accomplished and skills developed over the season

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Mini Makers' Circle

**Materials Needed:**
- Student-made items from earlier in the season (or simple items made just for this activity)
- Paper for price tags
- Scrap paper "play money"
- Pencils or crayons

**Steps:**
1. **Setup Display (2 min):** Each student arranges 1-2 items on their desk with a price tag (numbers provided by teacher or student-chosen)
2. **Shopping Round 1 (3 min):** Half the class "shops" with play money while half stays at their booth. Practice: "I'll buy this for [price]."
3. **Switch Roles (3 min):** Shoppers become sellers, sellers become shoppers. Continue buying and selling.
4. **Count Money (1 min):** Count how much "money" you collected from sales.
5. **Reflection (1 min):** Circle share: "I sold ___ items. I learned how to ___."

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Did you sell your items? How did it feel?"
- "What did you learn about selling?"
- "What was your favorite thing you made this season?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Classroom Makers' Faire with Sales Tracking

**Materials Needed:**
- Student-made items from the season (pottery, inventions, blueprints, decorated boxes, etc.)
- Index cards for price tags and "About This Product" cards
- Play money (bills and coins)
- Simple sales tracking sheet (tally marks or numbers)
- Display materials (fabric scraps, small boxes for height variation)

**Steps:**
1. **Booth Setup (5 min):** Students arrange items on desk with price tags and product description cards ("I made this [item]. It is made from [materials]. It took [time].")
2. **Gallery Preview Walk (2 min):** Everyone walks around viewing all booths before shopping opens. Mental note of favorites.
3. **Shopping Round 1 (5 min):** Half the class shops with $50 in play money. Make purchasing decisions, practice counting change, track sales.
4. **Switch & Round 2 (5 min):** Shoppers become sellers, sellers become shoppers. Continue faire.
5. **Count Revenue & Calculate Profit (2 min):** Count total money collected. If using costs: subtract material costs (from earlier activities) to find profit.
6. **Reflection Circle (1 min):** Share: "I earned $___. The best part of the Makers' Faire was ___."

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What sold the fastest? Why do you think?"
- "How did you decide what to buy when you were shopping?"
- "Which product took the most work to make? Did it cost more?"
- "What was the hardest part of running your booth?"
- "What did you learn from this whole season?"

**Extension:** Create a class "Makers' Faire Report" with photos of booths, total items sold, and favorite moments.

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Full Makers' Faire with Business Reporting and Season Celebration

**Materials Needed:**
- Complete collection of student-made items from S3 chapters (pottery, woodworking projects, inventions, blueprints, decorated items, prototypes)
- Professional-looking price tags (printed or nicely handwritten)
- "Product Story" cards (name, materials, time to make, inspiration, skills learned)
- Play money (variety of bills and coins)
- Sales tracking logs (detailed transaction sheets)
- Display materials (fabric, risers, booth signs, string lights optional)
- Certificates for season awards
- "Business Report" worksheet (revenue, costs, profit calculation)

**Steps:**
1. **Complete Booth Setup (8 min):**
   - Arrange multiple items with attractive display (height variation, visual appeal)
   - Create booth sign with student/team name: "[Name]'s Makers' Booth"
   - Set up price tags based on actual material costs + time/skill value
   - Display "Product Story" cards explaining the creation process and skills used
   - Arrange sales log and "cashbox" area
2. **Opening Ceremony (2 min):**
   - Teacher "cuts ribbon" to open the faire
   - Brief reminder of shopping etiquette and booth responsibilities
3. **Shopping Period 1 (8 min):** Half class shops with budget ($100-150 in play money), half manages booths. Record every sale on log.
4. **Switch & Shopping Period 2 (8 min):** Roles reverse. Continue shopping and selling.
5. **Business Closing (5 min):**
   - Count all revenue (cash in box)
   - Review sales logs (how many items sold?)
   - Calculate profit using worksheet: Revenue - Material Costs = Profit
   - Record results on Business Report
6. **Season Awards Ceremony (3 min):**
   - Present certificates: Best Product Design, Best Display, Most Sales, Most Creative Item, Best Customer Service, Most Improved Maker
7. **Season Reflection (1 min):** Circle share: "The most important thing I learned as a maker is ___."

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What strategies did you use to attract customers to your booth?"
- "Which products sold out? What made them popular?"
- "How did your pricing compare to others? Was yours higher or lower? Why?"
- "Looking at your profit, was your business successful?"
- "If you did this faire again, what would you do differently?"
- "Which skill you learned this season (woodworking, pottery, invention, design) was most valuable?"
- "How did the market research from CH10 help you know what to make?"
- "What's the difference between revenue and profit? Why does that matter?"
- "Would you want to be a real maker when you grow up? Why or why not?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Invite another class or families to attend the faire as customers
- Create "Makers' Portfolio" with photos of all products from the season
- Write reflective essays: "My Journey as a Maker"
- Research real makers' faires (Maker Faire Bay Area, local craft markets) and compare
- Plan a Season 4 business based on the products that sold best
- Create video "commercials" for products before the faire

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Simple setup: 1-2 items per student, clear price tags with numbers
- Shopping with teacher guidance: "Give them this money. They give you the toy."
- Focus on the experience of buying and selling, not complex math
- Draw pictures of "best sale" instead of writing reflections
- Basic vocabulary: sell, buy, customer, money, faire
- Celebrate participation over profit

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- Multiple items with simple price tags
- Practice counting money and making change with small amounts ($1, $5, $10)
- Simple sales tracking: tally marks for each item sold
- Product cards with sentence frames: "I made a ___. It is made from ___."
- Vocabulary expansion: revenue, booth, display, customer service, faire
- Count total money collected, discuss what "a lot" or "a little" means

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- Full booth setup with varied pricing
- Calculate change for purchases up to $20-50
- Detailed sales logs: item name, price, customer (first name)
- Product story cards explaining creation process
- Vocabulary: profit, cost, materials, market, entrepreneur
- Calculate simple profit: money collected minus estimated costs

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- Professional booth with strategic pricing based on material costs and time investment
- Complex transactions with bills and coins, accurate change-making
- Comprehensive business reporting: revenue, itemized costs, profit calculation, profit margin
- Written reflections on business success and lessons learned
- Advanced vocabulary: revenue vs. profit, overhead costs, market positioning, value proposition, return on investment
- Compare booth performance: who sold most? Who had highest profit margin? Why?
- Strategic analysis: which products should they make more of for future faires?

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual price tags with numbers prominently displayed
- Bilingual product cards: bowl/tazón, toy/juguete, price/precio
- Demonstrate transactions before faire begins
- Partner with fluent speaker during shopping rounds
- Use gestures and pointing: "This costs five dollars" (hold up five fingers)
- Sentence frames: "I want ___. It costs ___. Here is the money."

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Simplified booth: 1-3 items only to reduce overwhelm
- Pre-set prices in easy amounts ($1, $5, $10 - no change needed)
- Teacher or aide support during transactions
- Visual "sales script" poster (1. Greet customer, 2. Show item, 3. Say price, 4. Take money, 5. Say thank you)
- Quiet shopping time option for sensory-sensitive students
- Option to work in pairs at shared booth for support

**For Advanced Students:**
- Lead booth setup demonstration for younger students
- Create comprehensive business report with graphs and analysis
- Research real Makers' Faire economics (booth costs, typical sales, maker stories)
- Develop marketing materials: posters, flyers, product catalogs
- Calculate profit margin percentage, not just dollar amount
- Present "business case study" of their faire experience to class
- Mentor younger students through their first sales
- Design improvements for next year's faire based on data

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 12 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **Complete Business Cycle**
   - Core Lesson: Business involves production → pricing → marketing → sales → revenue → profit calculation → reflection
   - Activity Connection: Students experience EVERY step of the entrepreneurial cycle in one comprehensive activity
   - Age-Appropriate: K-1 experience it simply; 3-4 track and calculate each step with numbers

2. **Revenue vs. Profit**
   - Core Lesson: Money collected (revenue) is different from money you get to keep (profit). Costs must be subtracted.
   - Activity Connection: Calculate total sales, then subtract material costs from earlier chapters to find true profit
   - Age-Appropriate: Younger students understand "we spent some, we earned some, this is what's left"; older students calculate precise percentages

3. **Value of Skilled Labor**
   - Core Lesson: Handmade items cost more than materials alone because maker's time, skill, and creativity have value
   - Activity Connection: Products made with learned skills (pottery, woodworking, invention) command higher prices than raw materials cost
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete experience that skilled work = higher value, reinforced by customer willingness to pay

4. **Customer Service Matters**
   - Core Lesson: How you treat customers affects whether they buy and whether they come back
   - Activity Connection: Practice respectful, helpful, enthusiastic customer interactions; reflect on which booths attracted more customers
   - Age-Appropriate: Role-play and observe that friendly sellers do better business

5. **Market Dynamics in Action**
   - Core Lesson: Popular items sell out fast; less popular items remain; this data tells you what to make more of
   - Activity Connection: Track which items sell first; discuss why some products were more popular than others
   - Age-Appropriate: Observable cause-and-effect that leads to future business decisions

6. **Season Synthesis: Production Has Economic Value**
   - Core Lesson: Everything learned this season (materials knowledge, production skills, design, market research, creation) translates into real products with real market value
   - Activity Connection: The faire proves that their learning has economic worth - people pay money for what they made
   - Age-Appropriate: Tangible proof that skills = value, motivating continued learning

**Non-Moralistic Approach:**
- Activity celebrates making and selling without judgment on buying vs. making
- No shame language: "Buying is bad" ❌ Instead: "Making things yourself is one way to get what you want" ✓
- Acknowledges different people have different skills and interests (not everyone wants to be a maker)
- Focuses on pride in craftsmanship and business skills, not character judgments
- Celebrates all students regardless of how much they sold (participation and learning matter most)

**Connection to Season Arc:**
- Culmination of Season 3's journey from "What can I make?" to "I am a maker and entrepreneur"
- Brings together all chapter concepts: skills (CH1), materials (CH2), precision (CH3), packaging (CH4), crafting (CH5-7), invention (CH8-9), planning/research (CH10-11)
- Demonstrates tangible outcome of learning: products people actually want to buy
- Sets up Season 4's focus on business operations by showing students they CAN run real businesses

**Bridge to Season 4:**
- The success of the faire raises questions: "What if we did this regularly?" → business operations
- Profit earned could fund continued production → reinvestment concept
- Best-selling products suggest business focus → market-driven decision making
- Teamwork in running booths → partnership structures explored in S4

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**

**Weeks Before:**
- Collect student-made products from all S3 chapters (CH4 boxes, CH5 clay items, CH6 decorated pieces, CH8 inventions, CH11 production items)
- Send home note about Makers' Faire (explain concept, invite family attendance if doing extended version)
- Prepare play money supply (print or purchase enough for class)
- Create or print product card templates for students to fill in
- Plan booth layout (how many students per booth? Individual or pairs/groups?)
- Decide on pricing structure: teacher-set, student-choice, or collaborative decision?

**Days Before:**
- Have students create product description cards for each item they'll sell
- Help students set prices based on material costs + skill/time value (or provide pricing guidance)
- Practice customer service scenarios: greeting, explaining, thanking, handling objections
- Set up faire space (rearrange desks/tables, create aisles for browsing)
- Prepare certificates for awards ceremony
- Organize play money by denomination for easy distribution

**Day Of:**
- Allow extra setup time - booth arrangement always takes longer than expected
- Have backup items in case students forgot their products
- Designate a "faire manager" role (teacher or advanced student) to handle problems
- Set clear time signals for shopping rounds
- Keep camera ready for documentation photos

**Classroom Management:**
- Establish "Faire Rules" before starting: respectful browsing, no grabbing items, both parties must agree on price, sellers stay at booths, shoppers move politely
- Use a bell or music to signal shopping round starts/stops
- Designate "shopping aisles" to manage traffic flow
- Have a "help desk" (teacher station) for students who need transaction assistance
- Prepare for: students whose items don't sell (emphasize learning over profit, suggest they're "ahead of market trends"), students who run out of items quickly (can help others, become "faire assistants"), money disputes (teacher is banker/judge), students overwhelmed by crowds (quiet booth in corner, support person assigned)

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "Nobody's buying my stuff!" | Emphasize learning/participation over profit; coach on customer engagement; teacher "buys" item; discuss that artists often keep inventory |
| "I sold out in 2 minutes!" | Celebrate success; assign them helper roles (faire ambassador, assistant to teacher, help younger students); discuss scarcity and demand |
| Items priced too high/low | Before faire, review pricing together; discuss material costs + time; compare to similar items; adjust if needed |
| Confusion with change-making | Pre-assign "banker" buddies to help; use simple pricing ($1, $5, $10 - no change needed for younger grades); teacher circulates to assist |
| Arguments over transactions | Teacher is final authority; pause faire to review rules; model respectful disagreement resolution; fairness above all |
| Some booths crowded, others empty | Coach less-visited students on attracting customers (stand up, smile, invite people over, explain products); teacher directs traffic |
| Students forget products at home | Have backup items from classroom projects; let them "sell" services (draw custom picture for customer, teach a skill); focus on process not products |
| Running out of time | Extend if possible; allow students to finish next day; or pause shopping and jump to counting/reflection with "faire continues later" promise |

**Time Management:**
- Setup ALWAYS takes longer than planned - build in 2x expected time
- For 20-min version, have booths pre-setup or use desks as-is
- Use timers visible to class for shopping rounds
- Have extension activities ready for early finishers (draw favorite sale, interview classmates, help others)
- If running long, pause at natural break point and continue next class
- Keep awards ceremony brief but meaningful (pre-select recipients if time is tight)

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pair less confident students together at shared booth for mutual support
- Allow anxious students to practice customer interactions with teacher before faire opens
- Provide visual scripts for students who need structured language
- Designate "faire ambassador" role for students who'd rather not sell (they help shoppers find what they want)
- Advanced students can run "market analysis booth" tracking all sales across the faire for data presentation
- Students without products can offer services (custom drawings, poems, origami made on-spot)

**Celebration Focus:**
- This is the SEASON FINALE - make it feel special even on minimal budget
- Every student gets recognition for something (participation, creativity, effort, kindness, perseverance)
- Emphasize growth over the season, not just faire sales
- Take lots of photos for documentation and memory
- Consider inviting school admin or other classes to visit if time allows
- End with genuine appreciation for students' hard work all season

## SAFETY NOTES

**Physical Safety:**
- **Booth Setup:** Ensure displays are stable and won't tip over when browsing
- **Aisle Space:** Maintain clear aisles for movement without crowding or tripping hazards
- **Item Safety:** Verify all student-made products are safe (no sharp edges, small parts for choking, toxic materials)
- **Supervision:** Active teacher presence during all shopping rounds to manage crowd flow and prevent accidents

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Sales Pressure:** Emphasize that not selling out is perfectly fine; learning and participation matter most
- **Comparison:** Avoid direct competition ("who sold the most"); focus on individual growth and effort
- **Inclusion:** Ensure every student has items to display, even if they missed earlier activities (provide backup projects)
- **Gentle Feedback:** Frame unsold items positively ("you get to keep your beautiful work!" or "collectors often hold onto pieces")
- **Support for Disappointment:** Be ready to comfort students upset about low sales; emphasize lessons learned and pride in creation

**Financial Safety (Teaching Appropriate Lessons):**
- **Realistic Expectations:** This is simulation with play money; real business has more complexity and risk
- **Equity:** All students start shopping rounds with equal budgets to ensure fairness
- **No Real Money:** Keep activity entirely in play currency to avoid economic disparities among families
- **Profit Understanding:** Clarify that profit is reward for work/skill, not moral judgment on worth

**General:**
- Active supervision during all transaction activity
- Clear rules about touching items (ask permission, handle gently)
- Plan for what happens if item breaks during faire (seller keeps broken item if customer caused damage; customer keeps if already purchased)
- Have backup plan for students who experience faire anxiety (quiet observation role, buddy support, opt-in participation)
- Ensure all students get at least one "customer" visit (teacher visits under-trafficked booths)

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Items not selling | Products don't appeal to buyers; poor display; shy student doesn't engage | Coach on presentation; teacher "customers" visit; emphasize learning over sales; discuss that all art/products don't sell immediately |
| Everything sells in first 2 minutes | Highly desirable products; good pricing; lucky booth placement | Celebrate success; assign helper roles for remainder; discuss scarcity/demand; plan to make more next time |
| Student prices items at $1,000 | Doesn't understand realistic pricing; wants to "win" by earning most | Pre-faire pricing discussion; teacher reviews prices; connect to actual material costs; set price caps if needed |
| Arguing over change-making | Math errors; misunderstanding amounts; both think they're right | Teacher acts as banker/arbitrator; model correct counting; use simpler denominations next time; calculator for complex transactions |
| "That's not fair, they sold more!" | Comparison and competition | Emphasize different products appeal to different people; celebrate individual accomplishments; awards for varied categories (not just "most sales") |
| Booth overcrowding causes chaos | Popular booth or poor traffic management | Assign "line manager" to organize waiting customers; set browsing time limits; teacher directs traffic flow |
| Student forgot all products at home | Disorganization; items lost; lack of home support | Keep backup classroom projects; allow them to "sell" blank product with description; focus on participation; make new quick item during setup time |
| Sadness/tears over disappointing sales | High expectations; comparison to peers; emotional investment | Validate feelings; private conversation about effort vs. outcome; highlight what they DID accomplish; ensure they get award for something; discuss famous artists whose work didn't sell initially |
| Customer service conflicts (rude behavior) | Stress; competition; immature social skills | Pause faire to review respectful behavior; model kind interactions; timeout for persistent rudeness; practice empathy ("How would you feel if...?") |
| Running out of play money mid-faire | Didn't prepare enough; students hoarding cash | Have "bank" with reserves; students can "deposit" money and get receipt to free up bills; simplify to paper tallies if needed |
