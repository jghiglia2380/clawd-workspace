# CHAPTER 8: THE INVENTOR'S TOWER - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Invention and problem-solving
**Story Connection:** Based on scenes where friends visit an inventor's tower and learn that the best products solve real problems—invention starts with noticing a need, then creating a solution
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Invention Challenge: Solve a Real Problem"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-5:** Friends arrive at inventor's tower filled with creative solutions to everyday problems → Activity begins with problem identification
- **Scenes 6-10:** Learning that good inventions solve real problems people actually have → Activity includes needs assessment
- **Scenes 11-15:** Designing solutions through brainstorming and prototyping → Activity provides hands-on invention process
- **Scenes 16-20:** Pitching inventions and getting feedback → Activity concludes with presentations

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand that products exist to solve problems—market demand starts with customer needs
2. **Design Thinking:** Practice the invention process: identify problem → brainstorm → design → prototype → test
3. **Problem-Solving:** Develop creative solutions to real challenges
4. **Entrepreneurial Mindset:** Learn to see problems as business opportunities
5. **Communication Skills:** Pitch ideas effectively
6. **Critical Thinking:** Evaluate which problems are worth solving and which solutions are viable

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Problem Hunt & Quick Invention Sketch

**Materials Needed:**
- Paper
- Pencils, crayons
- Chart paper for class problems list

**Steps:**
1. **Problem Brainstorm (3 min):** Class identifies everyday annoyances: "What's something that bothers you? Something hard to do? Something that breaks or doesn't work well?" List 10 problems on board.
2. **Choose & Sketch (5 min):** Each student picks ONE problem. Draw an invention that would solve it. Name it.
3. **Quick Share (2 min):** 3-4 students show invention and explain: "This solves ___ because ___."

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What problem does your invention solve?"
- "Who would want to buy this?"
- "Does solving problems = business ideas?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Design Thinking Process with Prototype

**Materials Needed:**
- Problem identification worksheet
- Drawing paper
- Basic crafting materials (cardboard, tape, straws, cups, etc.)
- Invention planning template

**Steps:**
1. **Problem Interview (5 min):** Students pair up. Interview partner: "What's hard for you? What do you wish was easier?" Each identifies ONE real problem their partner has.
2. **Invention Design (8 min):** Design solution to partner's problem. Complete template: Problem / Who has it / My solution / How it works / Materials needed. Sketch design with labels.
3. **Quick Prototype (5 min):** Build very simple prototype or model using craft materials. Doesn't have to work—just show the idea.
4. **Partner Pitch (2 min):** Present invention to partner who had the problem. Get feedback: "Would this help? Would you use it?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "How did interviewing help you understand the problem?"
- "Is your solution something people would actually use?"
- "What makes a good invention? (Solves real problem, actually works, people want it)"
- "How is inventing like starting a business?"

**Extension:** Vote on "most likely to sell" invention. Discuss why that one has market potential.

---

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Full Invention Process with Shark Tank Pitch

**Materials Needed:**
- Design thinking worksheet (detailed)
- Invention planning template with market analysis
- Prototyping materials (cardboard, craft supplies, recyclables, tape, scissors, markers)
- Pitch template and timer
- Optional: simple "Shark Tank" setup with judges' table

**Steps:**
1. **Problem Identification & Research (8 min):**
   - Students interview 3 classmates: "What's frustrating? What's difficult? What breaks?"
   - Identify patterns—problems multiple people have = bigger market opportunity
   - Choose ONE problem to solve
   - Define: Who has this problem? How often? How bad is it?
2. **Solution Design (10 min):**
   - Brainstorm 3 possible solutions
   - Choose best solution based on: feasibility, cost, how well it solves problem
   - Sketch detailed design with labels
   - List materials needed and estimate cost to make
   - Name the invention
3. **Prototype Building (10 min):**
   - Create physical model or prototype
   - Doesn't need to fully function, but should demonstrate concept
   - Test with partner if possible
4. **Shark Tank Pitch (7 min):**
   - Prepare 1-minute pitch: "I'm solving [problem]. [X number] of people have this problem. My invention is [name]. It works by [explanation]. It costs $[amount] to make and I'd sell it for $[price]. Who wants to invest?"
   - 4-6 students pitch to class ("sharks")
   - Sharks vote: Would you invest? Why or why not?
   - Feedback: What's strong? What needs work?

**Discussion Questions:**
- "How did you decide which problem to solve?"
- "Why did you choose this solution over your other ideas?"
- "Who is your customer? How many potential customers exist?"
- "What would it cost to make? What would you charge?"
- "What feedback did you get? Would you change your invention based on it?"
- "Which invention presented today has the best chance of success? Why?"
- "What's the difference between a cool idea and a marketable invention?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Research real inventors—how did they identify problems to solve?
- Calculate break-even point: how many units to sell to cover development costs?
- Create marketing materials for invention
- Improve prototype based on feedback
- Research: has anyone already invented this? How is yours different?

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Collect prototyping materials ahead (cardboard, recyclables, craft supplies)
- Prepare example invention to demonstrate process
- Review design thinking process yourself to guide students
- Have backup problems in mind if students struggle to identify any
- Set up "Shark Tank" presentation area if doing pitches

**Classroom Management:**
- Emphasize: problems = opportunities, not complaints
- Set positive tone: "We're inventors today—we solve things!"
- Time-box each phase or students will get stuck in one stage
- Circulate during problem identification to ensure problems are real/solvable
- Manage prototyping chaos—set clear material use and cleanup expectations

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "I can't think of any problems!" | Prompt: "What's hard about school? Eating? Playing? Cleaning your room? Organizing things?" Everyone has frustrations. |
| Problem is too big (world hunger, climate change) | "That's important but too big for one invention. Can you find a smaller version? Like 'my lunch gets warm' instead of 'world hunger'?" Scale down. |
| Solution already exists | "Great minds think alike! How could you make it BETTER? Cheaper? Different?" Iterate on existing. OR "That's okay—pick another problem." |
| Prototype doesn't work | "That's inventing! Thomas Edison failed 1000 times. What did you learn? What would you try next?" Embrace iteration. |
| Student stuck on fantasy invention (jetpack, teleporter) | "Fun idea! For today, let's focus on something you could actually build with real materials." Redirect to feasible. |
| Pitch is too vague ("It helps people") | "WHO specifically? HOW exactly? Be specific: 'It helps kids aged 7-10 who lose their pencils by...'" Coach specificity. |
| Comparing inventions competitively | "All inventions solve different problems. There's no 'best'—just different needs." Validate diversity. |

**Time Management:**
- Problem identification can spiral—set timer
- Prototyping always takes longer than planned—build buffer
- Pitches run long—set strict 1-minute limit with timer
- Have extension for early finishers (improve prototype, design packaging, create marketing)
- Can split across two days: Day 1 = design, Day 2 = prototype and pitch

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pair struggling students with creative buddies for brainstorming
- Provide problem cards for students who can't think of any
- Let advanced students research competitive products
- Offer low-complexity prototyping materials for fine motor challenges
- Allow written pitch for students anxious about speaking

## SAFETY NOTES

**Physical Safety:**
- **Sharp Tools:** Scissors/cutters require supervision; age-appropriate tools only
- **Small Parts:** For K-1, avoid choking hazards in prototypes
- **Hot Glue:** If using, low-temp guns with strict supervision
- **Construction:** Ensure stable prototypes that won't collapse and injure

**Material Safety:**
- **Non-toxic:** All materials safe for children
- **Clean Materials:** Ensure recyclables are washed
- **Allergies:** Check for latex, etc. in craft materials

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Failure is Normal:** Many inventions don't work first try—this is expected
- **No "Dumb Ideas":** All ideas welcome in brainstorming
- **Constructive Feedback:** Model giving helpful, kind feedback on pitches
- **Respect Ideas:** No stealing/mocking others' inventions

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Can't think of problems | Too abstract; not used to noticing frustrations | Prompt categories: "Problems at school? At home? With toys? With food?" Give examples from your life. |
| Problem too big/abstract | Idealistic; doesn't understand scope | "That's important but huge. Can you find one small piece? 'Save environment' → 'reduce our classroom waste'" Scope down. |
| Solution is fantasy (magic, impossible) | Imagination unconstrained by reality | "For today, let's invent things you could actually build. What's a realistic version?" Ground in feasibility. |
| Copies another invention exactly | Lack of confidence; easier than creating | "Great inspiration! Now what can you add or change to make it YOUR version?" Encourage variation. |
| Prototype falls apart immediately | Poor construction; rushed | "Inventors build many prototypes. Let's strengthen this—where can we add more tape/support?" Iterate. |
| Pitch is all tech details, no business case | Focuses on "cool" over "useful" | "Sharks want to know: who buys this? How many? For how much? Add business info to your pitch." Coach business thinking. |
| Perfectionism—won't present "because it's not good enough" | High standards; fear of judgment | "Professional inventors pitch rough ideas all the time. This is version 1—you'll improve it later. Let's share!" Remove pressure. |
