# CHAPTER 4: BUILDING BOXES - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Packaging, branding, and perceived value
**Story Connection:** Based on scenes where friends build boxes to hold and display their products, learning that packaging affects how customers perceive value
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Package Design & Branding Workshop"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-5:** Friends realize their handmade products need attractive packaging → Activity includes box design and decoration
- **Scenes 6-10:** They learn that the same item looks more valuable in nice packaging → Activity demonstrates packaging's impact on perceived value
- **Scenes 11-15:** Creating brand identity (name, logo, colors) for their boxes → Activity includes branding elements
- **Scenes 16-20:** Testing reactions—customers drawn to well-packaged items → Activity concludes with comparison and analysis

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand that packaging adds perceived value and affects what customers will pay
2. **Design Skills:** Create functional and attractive packaging
3. **Branding Basics:** Learn elements of brand identity (name, logo, colors, message)
4. **Marketing Awareness:** Understand that presentation influences purchasing decisions
5. **Cost Analysis:** Recognize that packaging is a real business cost
6. **Creative Problem-Solving:** Design packaging that protects, displays, and brands product

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Plain vs. Decorated Bag Test

**Materials Needed:**
- Brown paper bags (2 per demo, or 2 per student)
- Crayons, markers
- Small item to "package" (cookie, toy, pencil)

**Steps:**
1. **Set-Up (2 min):** Show two identical items (cookies). Put one in plain brown bag, one in decorated bag with colorful design and label.
2. **Vote (2 min):** "If these cost the same, which would you buy?" Students vote. Decorated usually wins.
3. **Decorate Your Own (5 min):** Each student gets plain bag and item. Decorate bag to make item look special.
4. **Gallery Walk (1 min):** Display bags. Discuss: "How did decorating change how the item looks?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Which bag looked more special?"
- "Would you pay more for the decorated one?"
- "Why does packaging matter?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Box Building & Brand Design

**Materials Needed:**
- Simple box templates (pre-scored or flat boxes to assemble)
- Markers, colored pencils, stickers
- Small item for each student to package
- "Brand Planning" worksheet (name, logo sketch, color choice)

**Steps:**
1. **Brand Planning (5 min):** Students complete worksheet: What's your brand name? (can be your own name + "Creations"). Sketch simple logo. Choose 2-3 brand colors.
2. **Box Assembly (3 min):** Fold pre-scored boxes or assemble simple boxes from template.
3. **Design & Decorate (10 min):** Transfer brand name and logo to box. Use brand colors. Add decorative elements. Make it look "store quality."
4. **Comparison Test (2 min):** Teacher shows same item in plain box vs decorated box. Class votes which they'd buy. Discuss why packaging matters.

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What makes packaging look professional?"
- "If two products are the same but one has better packaging, which costs more?"
- "How much would you pay for your product in plain packaging? In your branded packaging?"
- "What does your packaging say about your product?"

**Extension:** "Shopping" activity where students browse each other's packaged products and note which ones attract them most.

---

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Packaging Design Challenge with Cost Analysis

**Materials Needed:**
- Box-building materials (cardboard, templates, or flat boxes)
- Full art supply set (markers, colored pencils, stickers, ribbons, stamps)
- Brand design template (detailed)
- Product for packaging (can be something students made earlier, or provided item)
- Cost analysis worksheet

**Steps:**
1. **Brand Development (8 min):**
   - Create complete brand identity: business name, tagline, logo, color palette
   - Design packaging that reflects brand
   - Consider target customer: "Who will buy this? What do they like?"
2. **Box Construction & Design (15 min):**
   - Build or assemble box
   - Apply brand elements (logo, name, colors) to all sides
   - Add finishing touches (ribbon, seal, thank-you note inside)
   - Quality check: does it look professional?
3. **Cost Analysis (7 min):**
   - Calculate packaging costs: plain box = $0.50, decoration supplies = $0.25-1.00 depending on use
   - Compare: product alone vs. in plain box vs. in branded box
   - Estimate: "What could I charge for each version?"
   - Discuss: packaging cost vs. increased perceived value—worth it?
4. **Presentation & Investor Pitch (5 min):**
   - 4-5 students present their packaged product to class (1 min each)
   - Explain brand choices and target customer
   - Class feedback: would you buy it? Does packaging match product?

**Discussion Questions:**
- "How did you choose your brand colors and design?"
- "Does your packaging make the product look more valuable? How?"
- "You spent $0.75 on packaging. Can you charge $2 more because of it? Why or why not?"
- "What's the difference between cheap-looking and professional packaging?"
- "How do real companies use packaging to sell products?"
- "Can packaging be TOO fancy for a simple product? When does it not match?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Create multiple packaging versions and price test with another class
- Research real product packaging and analyze design choices
- Design eco-friendly packaging (minimal materials but still attractive)
- Create packaging "brand family" for multiple products
- Photography session: product with and without packaging—compare appeal

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Collect sample packaging ahead of time (cereal boxes, gift bags, product boxes) for inspiration
- Test box template yourself to ensure it's age-appropriate
- Pre-score boxes for younger students to make folding easier
- Have extra boxes—some will tear or be "practice rounds"
- Set up examples of plain vs. decorated packaging for comparison

**Classroom Management:**
- Establish "brand planning before decorating" rule—prevents hasty, unplanned designs
- Protect work surfaces (packaging can get messy with glue, markers)
- Set clear time limits for each phase (planning, assembly, decoration)
- Have cleanup supplies ready (packaging projects generate scrap)
- Create display area for finished packaging—celebrates student work

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "My box fell apart!" | Use sturdier boxes or more glue/tape. Teach: structural integrity matters in packaging too. Have backup boxes ready. |
| Random decoration with no coherent brand | Pause class: "A brand has a plan. Pick 2-3 colors max and repeat them. Pick one logo and use it everywhere. Consistency = professional." |
| "I can't draw a good logo!" | "Logos can be simple! A circle with your initial. Your name in a cool font. A simple shape. Professional doesn't mean complicated." Provide templates. |
| Rushing through design to "finish fast" | Reframe: "In business, packaging is marketing. Rushing makes it look cheap. Take your time—this is what customers see first." |
| "Why does packaging matter? The product inside is the same!" | Perfect! Use this as teaching moment: "Exactly—but would YOU pay more for the nicely wrapped version? Most people would. That's marketing." |
| Perfectionism paralysis | Set time limit and emphasize "done is better than perfect." Show that professional packaging has some imperfections too. |

**Time Management:**
- Box assembly takes longer than expected—have pre-assembled for younger students
- Brand planning can expand endlessly—use timer and templates to keep focused
- Save detailed cost analysis for older students; younger ones focus on design
- Have early finishers help others or create second package

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Provide design templates for students who struggle with creativity
- Let artistic students do complex designs; others can do simple, elegant minimalism
- Pair students: designer + assembler partnerships work well
- Advanced students design packaging for classmates' products

## SAFETY NOTES

**Physical Safety:**
- **Cutting:** If cutting boxes, use safety scissors or have adult cut
- **Glue/Adhesive:** Use non-toxic, washable glues
- **Small Parts:** For K-1, avoid small decorative elements (choking hazard)
- **Sharp Edges:** Check box edges, especially on cardboard

**Material Safety:**
- **Allergies:** Check for latex in glue, tape
- **Ventilation:** If using markers heavily, ensure good air flow
- **Cleanup:** Wash hands after—glue, marker ink

**Environmental:**
- Discuss recycling and reusing packaging materials
- Talk about waste: excessive packaging vs. necessary protection
- Eco-friendly options

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Box assembly is too hard | Fine motor challenges; complex template | Simplify: use bags instead, or provide pre-assembled boxes for decoration only |
| Incoherent design (too many colors, random elements) | Lack of planning; excitement overcomes strategy | Require brand planning FIRST. Limit color choices: "Pick your 3 brand colors NOW." |
| "My logo looks bad" | Perfectionism; unrealistic standards | Show real logos—many are simple! "Your initial in a cool shape IS a logo. McDonald's is just an M!" Normalize simplicity. |
| Student copies peer's design | Lack of confidence; easier than creating original | "I love that you like their style! Can you use similar COLORS but make your own logo?" Guide toward inspiration, not copying. |
| Wants elaborate packaging school can't afford | Big imagination; seen professional packaging | "Let's design the DREAM version, then create the version we can actually make. Real businesses do this too!" |
| Frustration with packaging not looking "professional" | High standards; lack of experience | Reframe: "First draft NEVER looks perfect. Professional designers do 10 versions. You're learning!" Show improvement over time. |
