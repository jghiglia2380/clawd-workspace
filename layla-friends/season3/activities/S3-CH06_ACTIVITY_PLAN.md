# CHAPTER 6: THROWING AND TURNING - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Value-added production through decoration and finishing
**Story Connection:** Based on scenes where friends learn pottery decoration techniques—glazing, painting, carving designs—discovering that decoration transforms a basic pot into something people will pay much more for
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Design Upgrade: Adding Value Through Decoration"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-5:** Friends see plain pottery vs. decorated pottery—huge price difference → Activity compares plain vs. decorated items
- **Scenes 6-10:** Learning decoration techniques (painting, carving, glazing) → Activity includes hands-on decorating
- **Scenes 11-15:** Discovering that decoration time adds to product cost but increases value more → Activity tracks time and value added
- **Scenes 16-20:** Selling decorated pottery for significantly more than plain → Activity concludes with pricing analysis

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand that decoration adds value beyond materials and basic production
2. **Design Skills:** Learn to enhance products through thoughtful decoration
3. **Value-Added Production:** Grasp the concept of raw → finished → decorated value chain
4. **Customer Appeal:** Recognize that aesthetics affect purchasing decisions
5. **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Calculate whether decoration cost justifies price increase
6. **Customization Value:** Learn that personalized/unique items command premium prices

---

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Plain vs. Decorated Item Comparison

**Materials Needed:**
- Plain items (wooden spoons, paper cups, simple boxes, or clay items from CH5)
- Markers, stickers, paint pens
- Two identical items for demonstration

**Steps:**
1. **Price Comparison (2 min):** Show two identical cups/spoons. One plain, one decorated. "If these were in a store, plain = $2, decorated = $8. Why?" Discuss.
2. **Quick Decoration (6 min):** Each student decorates their plain item. Encourage: make it special, unique, beautiful.
3. **Value Vote (2 min):** Display all items. "Which would you pay more for? Why?" Count votes for most valuable-looking.

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Does decoration make something worth more?"
- "Would you pay more for the decorated one?"
- "How much more time did decorating take?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Before & After Decoration with Price Tags

**Materials Needed:**
- Plain items to decorate (wood, paper, clay from previous session)
- Full art supplies (paint, markers, stickers, stamps, ribbons)
- "Before" price cards and "After" price cards
- Timer

**Steps:**
1. **Value Assessment (3 min):** Students receive plain item. "What's this worth in a store? $2? $5?" Write "before" price.
2. **Decoration Planning (2 min):** Sketch quick design plan. What colors? What patterns? What will make it special?
3. **Decoration Execution (12 min):** Decorate item with quality and care. Track time spent.
4. **New Value Assessment (3 min):** "Now what's it worth? Would someone pay $10? $15? Why?" Write "after" price. Calculate value added.

**Discussion Questions:**
- "How much did decoration increase the value?"
- "Was the decoration time worth it?"
- "If decoration took 15 minutes, and you charge $10 per hour for your time, decoration costs $2.50. If you can now charge $8 more, is that a good deal?"
- "What makes some decoration more valuable than others?"

**Extension:** Create price tags explaining why item is worth the new price ("Hand-painted by [name], one-of-a-kind design, hours of careful work").

---

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Value-Added Production Analysis

**Materials Needed:**
- Plain items (ideally pottery from CH5, or wood items, boxes, etc.)
- Professional art supplies (acrylic paint, fine brushes, markers, stamps, gold leaf/metallic accents if available)
- Before/after photography setup
- Value chain worksheet
- Time tracking sheets
- Market research survey cards

**Steps:**
1. **Value Chain Introduction (5 min):**
   - Show progression: Raw materials → Basic product → Decorated product
   - Real example: Clay $1 → Plain bowl $5 → Decorated bowl $25
   - Each step adds value through labor, skill, design
2. **Design & Planning (8 min):**
   - Research: look at professional decorated pottery/items (examples or photos)
   - Design detailed decoration plan
   - Choose techniques that add value (hand-painted > stickers in professional market)
   - Estimate time needed
3. **Professional Decoration (15 min):**
   - Execute design with quality focus
   - Track actual time spent
   - Take "before" and "after" photos
   - Quality check: Does this look professional enough to sell?
4. **Value Analysis & Pricing (7 min):**
   - Calculate costs: materials + time (at $10/hour labor) + skill premium
   - Survey classmates: "Would you buy this? For how much?"
   - Compare estimated value to actual market prices
   - Determine optimal price point
   - Calculate profit margin: (price - cost) / price = margin %

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Your plain item was worth $5. Decoration cost you $3 in time and materials. You can now sell for $15. What's your profit?"
- "Why do people pay more for hand-decorated items?"
- "Is decoration always worth it? When might it not be?"
- "How does uniqueness affect value? (One-of-a-kind vs. mass-produced)"
- "What decoration techniques add the most value? Why?"
- "If you decorated 100 items, would you get faster? Would that change your pricing?"
- "Compare to factory decoration—why does hand-painted cost more?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- A/B test: create two versions (simple decoration vs. elaborate), survey which people prefer
- Cost different decoration levels: minimal (+$2), moderate (+$5), elaborate (+$10)
- Research professional decorators—what do they charge? Calculate hourly rates
- Create "artist statement" explaining decoration choices
- Photography for marketing—does good photo increase perceived value?

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Protect surfaces—paint and markers can stain
- Test decoration materials on sample item beforehand
- Prepare examples: plain vs. beautifully decorated comparison
- Have extras of plain items (decoration mistakes happen)
- Set up drying area for painted items
- Take "before" photos to show transformation

**Classroom Management:**
- Emphasize quality over speed—rushing shows in decoration
- Set clear cleanup expectations before starting
- Circulate with encouragement—decoration can be intimidating
- Have extension activities for fast decorators
- Display all finished items to celebrate diverse styles

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "I can't paint/draw well!" | "Decoration doesn't have to be perfect! Patterns, stripes, dots are beautiful and easy. Or try sponge painting, stamping, stickers." Provide accessible techniques. |
| Sloppy, rushed decoration | "This will be sold for $20. Would a customer pay that for rushed work? Take your time—decoration is where the value comes from." Connect to pricing. |
| Excessive decoration (too much, clashes) | "Sometimes less is more. Professional designers often use 2-3 colors max. Can you simplify?" Teach restraint. |
| Student gives up after mistake | "Paint over it! Professionals fix mistakes constantly. This is how you learn." Show that mistakes can be fixed. |
| "My decoration is ugly" | "Beauty is subjective! Some people love simple, some love elaborate. Your style is YOUR style." Validate diverse aesthetics. |
| Decoration falling off/not adhering | Check material compatibility. Some paints don't stick to certain surfaces. Provide appropriate materials for item type. |
| Time pressure (decoration takes longer than planned) | Expected! Decoration IS time-intensive. That's why it costs more. Build in buffer time or extend to two sessions. |

**Time Management:**
- Decoration always takes longer than estimated
- Young students need more time per technique
- Drying time required for paint (air dry or heat gun)
- Cleanup is significant (paint on hands, tables, etc.)
- Have fast decorators start on second item or help others

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Provide decoration templates (stencils) for students who need structure
- Let artistic students freestyle
- Offer variety of techniques—everyone can succeed at something
- Pair students for design feedback and encouragement

## SAFETY NOTES

**Physical Safety:**
- **Paints:** Use non-toxic, washable paints suitable for children
- **Brushes:** Check for loose bristles (choking hazard for very young)
- **Spray Sealant:** If using, teacher applies in ventilated area, not students
- **Hot Glue:** If embellishing, use low-temp glue guns with supervision
- **Sharp Tools:** If carving, strict supervision and age-appropriate tools only

**Material Safety:**
- **Allergies:** Check for latex in paints, adhesives
- **Ventilation:** Ensure good airflow if using strong-smelling materials
- **Skin Sensitivity:** Some students react to paints—test first or provide gloves
- **Ingestion:** Enforce "paint stays on item, not in mouth"

**Cleanup:**
- Wash hands thoroughly—paint can irritate if left on skin
- Clean spills immediately to prevent slips
- Dispose of paint water properly (not down sink if special paints)

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Decoration doesn't match item | Lack of planning; style clash | "Before you decorate, plan: what style fits this item? Rustic? Modern? Cute? Match your decoration to your product." Teach cohesion. |
| Too much decoration (overwhelming) | Excitement; "more is better" thinking | "Professional designers use restraint. Can you make it beautiful with LESS?" Show minimalist examples. Teach editing. |
| Perfectionism paralysis | Fear of "ruining" item with bad decoration | "You can paint over mistakes! Artists revise constantly. Start decorating—you can always change it." Remove pressure. |
| Very simple decoration (not much value added) | Lack of confidence or rushed | "This decoration added $1 of value. Can you add more? Try another layer of detail." Encourage depth. |
| Paint bleeding, smudging | Wrong paint for surface; impatience | "Let layers dry fully. Use appropriate paint for your material." Teach patience and material science. |
| "Mine looks worse than the plain version!" | Design didn't work out; skill mismatch | "Not every decoration succeeds—professionals have failures too. What did you learn? Try again, or keep plain." Validate learning through failure. |
