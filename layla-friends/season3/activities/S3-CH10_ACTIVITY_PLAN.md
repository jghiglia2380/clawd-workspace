# CHAPTER 10: THE MAKER'S MARKET RESEARCH - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Market research and customer needs
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where friends learn to survey potential customers before making products, discovering that research prevents waste and helps match supply to demand
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Would You Buy This? Survey & Market Research"**

---

- **Scenes 3-4:** Ellis asks how they know what people want; Layla suggests asking first → Activity demonstrates why research prevents wasted effort
- **Scenes 5-7:** Friends learn about surveys and write questions → Activity includes creating real survey questions

- **Scenes 18-20:** Making smart decisions based on customer feedback → Activity concludes with product adjustments based on real feedback

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand that market research reduces business risk
2. **Data Analysis:** Practice collecting, organizing, and interpreting survey data
3. **Customer Focus:** Learn that successful products solve real customer needs
4. **Decision-Making:** Use evidence (data) instead of assumptions to make choices
5. **Communication Skills:** Practice creating clear questions and interviewing people
6. **Economics Concepts:** Grasp that supply should match demand to avoid waste

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Quick Product Survey

**Materials Needed:**
- Simple survey template (1 question pre-printed)
- Pencils
- Paper for tallying

**Steps:**
1. **Product Brainstorm (2 min):** Each student thinks of one product idea (toy, craft, snack, school supply). Share with partner.
2. **Create Question (2 min):** Class creates ONE survey question together: "Would you buy [product] for [price]? Yes or No."
3. **Survey Classmates (3 min):** Students ask 5 classmates their question. Tally yes/no answers.
4. **Count Results (2 min):** Count tallies. "How many said yes? How many said no?"
5. **Reflection (1 min):** "Should you make this product? Why or why not?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Did everyone say yes? What does that tell you?"
- "What if most people said no? Should you still make it?"
- "How does asking first help you make smart choices?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Market Research Project

**Materials Needed:**
- Survey templates with 3 questions
- Clipboards or hard surface for writing
- Pencils
- Tally/data collection sheets
- Chart paper or whiteboard for graphing

**Steps:**
1. **Product Selection (3 min):** Each student chooses a product they want to "sell" (real or imaginary). Write it down with estimated price.
2. **Survey Design (4 min):** Create 3-question survey: "Would you buy this? How much would you pay? What features do you want?"
3. **Data Collection (6 min):** Survey 8-10 classmates (or buddy class if available). Record answers carefully.
4. **Tally Results (4 min):** Count answers. Create simple bar graph showing results (how many yes/no, price preferences).
5. **Analysis & Decision (2 min):** "Based on your data, should you make this product? What changes should you make?"
6. **Share Findings (1 min):** Partner share: "What did your research tell you?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Did anyone's data surprise them?"
- "What's the most popular price range?"
- "Did anyone decide NOT to make their product after the survey? Why?"
- "How is research different from guessing?"
- "What if you skipped research and made 100 products nobody wanted?"

**Extension:** Create a class "Market Research Report" poster showing all findings.

---

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Market Research & Product Adjustment

**Materials Needed:**
- Multi-page survey templates (5-7 questions)
- Clipboards
- Data collection worksheets
- Graph paper or printed graph templates
- Colored pencils/markers for graphs
- "Market Research Report" template
- Optional: access to another class for broader survey base

**Steps:**
1. **Product Development (5 min):**
   - Each student designs a product (sketch + name + description + initial price)
   - Write "hypothesis": "I think [number] out of 10 people will want to buy this"
2. **Survey Creation (6 min):**
   - Design 5-7 questions: Would you buy? Price range? Preferred features/colors? What problems does it solve? What would make it better?
   - Test questions on partner for clarity
3. **Data Collection (10 min):**
   - Survey 10-15 people (classmates, another class, or school staff)
   - Record all answers on data sheets
   - Note unexpected feedback or suggestions
4. **Data Analysis (8 min):**
   - Tally all responses
   - Create bar graphs showing: interest level, price preferences, feature preferences
   - Calculate percentages (e.g., "60% said yes, 40% said no")
   - Compare hypothesis to actual results
5. **Product Adjustment (4 min):**
   - Based on data, adjust product: change price, add/remove features, change target customer
   - Create "before and after" comparison showing changes made
6. **Market Research Report (2 min):**
   - Complete one-page report: Product idea, survey results (graphs), key findings, product changes made based on feedback

**Discussion Questions:**
- "How many people's hypothesis was wrong? What does that teach us?"
- "What's the risk of skipping market research?"
- "If 8 out of 10 people want blue but you only make red, what happens?"
- "How does research save money and materials?"
- "What did you learn that you couldn't have guessed?"
- "How do real companies use market research?"
- "What's the difference between making what YOU like vs. what CUSTOMERS want?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Survey a second group and compare results (do different groups want different things?)
- Calculate potential profit: "If 60% of 100 people buy at $5, and it costs $2 to make, what's your profit?"
- Research real market research examples (taste tests, focus groups, beta testing)
- Create survey for school cafeteria or library to improve services
- Design a "bad survey" with confusing questions, then redesign it properly

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Single yes/no question surveys
- Focus on counting yes vs. no
- Simple tally marks (IIII = 5)
- Teacher-led graphing on board
- Oral reporting ("5 said yes, 3 said no")
- Basic vocabulary: survey, question, yes, no, count

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- 2-3 simple questions
- Practice writing answers neatly
- Create simple bar graphs with teacher support
- Compare numbers ("More people said yes than no")
- Vocabulary expansion: survey, data, tally, graph, most, least, pattern

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- 3-5 questions with variety (yes/no, multiple choice, price range)
- Independent graphing with templates
- Calculate simple percentages (5 out of 10 = half)
- Written analysis: "Most people want blue"
- Vocabulary: market research, data analysis, results, pattern, preference

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- 5-7 detailed questions including open-ended
- Create multiple types of graphs (bar, pie chart basics)
- Calculate percentages accurately (8/15 = 53%)
- Written market research report with findings and recommendations
- Compare hypothesis to results
- Advanced vocabulary: hypothesis, demographic, correlation, statistically significant, bias

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual survey templates with pictures
- Bilingual vocabulary: survey/encuesta, data/datos, research/investigación
- Partner with fluent speaker for interviewing
- Use gestures and visuals during surveys
- Sentence frames: "Would you buy ___? How much would you pay for ___?"

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Simplified surveys (1-2 questions max)
- Visual supports (picture choices instead of written)
- Adult partner during survey collection
- Focus on one type of graph (bar graph only)
- Verbal reporting instead of written
- Celebrate participation in data collection

**For Advanced Students:**
- Design complex multi-question surveys
- Survey multiple groups and compare results
- Create sophisticated data analysis (cross-tabulation: "Girls prefer A, boys prefer B")
- Research real market research methods
- Present findings with multimedia (poster, digital slides)
- Calculate profit projections based on survey data

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 10 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **Market Research Reduces Risk**
   - Core Lesson: Asking customers before making products prevents wasted time, money, and materials
   - Activity Connection: Students experience firsthand how surveys reveal what people actually want vs. what you assume
   - Age-Appropriate: K-1 see that asking saves mistakes; 3-4 calculate cost of making unwanted products

2. **Supply Should Match Demand**
   - Core Lesson: Make what people want (demand), not just what you like
   - Activity Connection: Data shows which products have high/low demand, guiding smart production decisions
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete experience of "lots of people want this" vs. "nobody wants this"

   - Core Lesson: Good decisions are based on information (data), not feelings or guesses
   - Activity Connection: Students make product changes based on survey results, not personal preference
   - Age-Appropriate: Scaffolded from simple counting to percentage analysis and written reports

   - Core Lesson: Successful businesses solve real customer problems
   - Activity Connection: Survey questions reveal customer needs, problems, and price sensitivity
   - Age-Appropriate: From "Do you want this?" (K-1) to "What problem does this solve?" (3-4)

**Non-Moralistic Approach:**

- Acknowledges that even with research, some products still fail (that's okay—learn and adjust)
- Focuses on tools and skills, not character judgments
- Celebrates both confirming hypotheses AND discovering you were wrong (both are learning)

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Decide WHO students will survey (classmates, buddy class, other teachers, families)
- If surveying another class, coordinate ahead with that teacher
- Pre-print survey templates if using them (or create board template to copy)
- Prepare sample survey questions to show as examples
- Have backup plan if surveying off-site isn't possible (survey each other, survey staff)

**Classroom Management:**
- Set clear rules for interviewing: polite, respectful, accept "no" or "no time"
- Demonstrate good interviewing: "May I ask you some questions? It will take 2 minutes."
- Establish quiet survey zones if surveying classmates simultaneously
- Use timer for data collection phase to keep activity moving
- Have extension activities ready for fast finishers (help others, start graphing)

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "Everyone says yes because they're being nice" | Teach that honest feedback is most helpful; emphasize anonymous surveys; ask "Would you pay $X?" to get real data |
| "Not enough people to survey" | Survey younger class, other teachers, families (send home), or reduce required number |
| Confusing/biased survey questions | Model good vs. bad questions; "Do you want this amazing product?" vs. "Would you buy this product?" |
| Students discouraged by negative data | Reframe: "Data showing 'no' saves you from wasting time! Now you can adjust!" |
| Graphing takes too long | Use pre-made graph templates; do class graph together first; or simplify to tally marks only |
| Data collection too noisy | Stagger survey times; assign partners; create "quiet survey zones" |
| Students ignore data and keep original plan | Discuss: "What's the point of research if we don't use it?" Make data-based change required |

**Time Management:**
- Survey creation always takes longer than expected—build in buffer
- If running long, split over two days: Day 1 = create survey and collect data; Day 2 = analyze and graph
- Set specific time limits for surveying (use timer)
- Pre-make graphs for younger students to just fill in
- For quick version, use one class question and survey in circle

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pair struggling students with strong readers during survey creation
- Allow verbal surveys for non-readers (partner reads questions)
- Provide picture choices for ELL students
- Advanced students can survey multiple groups and compare
- Struggling graphers can use stickers or stamps instead of drawing bars

## SAFETY NOTES

**Interviewing Safety:**
- **Supervision:** Students should only survey with teacher supervision or pre-approved people (buddy class, staff)
- **Boundaries:** Teach "If someone says no or looks busy, thank them and move on"
- **Respect:** Practice polite approaches and gracious responses to both yes and no
- **Privacy:** Survey answers should be anonymous in reporting (don't say "Billy said no")

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Handle Negative Data:** Prepare students that not everyone will like their idea—this is helpful, not hurtful!
- **No Forced Participation:** Students can observe rather than participate if overwhelmed
- **Respectful Peer Feedback:** When surveying classmates, enforce kind honesty (no mocking ideas)
- **Teacher Support:** Be ready to help students who are upset by disappointing data

**General:**
- Clear rules about respectful surveying
- Practice interview skills before live data collection
- Debrief after surveys to process what they learned
- Celebrate both positive and negative data as useful information

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| "Everyone says yes to be nice" | Social pressure; want to be supportive | Add price question ("Would you pay $10?"); emphasize anonymous honesty; explain that "no" helps more than fake "yes" |
| Survey questions are confusing | Lack of experience writing clear questions | Model examples; practice with partner first; teacher reviews before surveying |
| Not enough time to survey enough people | Underestimating time needed | Reduce required number; split over two days; survey buddy class; send surveys home |
| Students want to ignore negative data | Emotional attachment to original idea | Celebrate data as helpful; reframe "no" as money-saving; make data-based change required for grade |
| Graphing is too difficult/time-consuming | Complex graphs for grade level | Use simpler graph type; provide templates; do class graph together first |
| Can't find people to survey | Limited access to diverse respondents | Survey each other in multiple rounds; survey other classes; send home to families; survey school staff |
| Results are split 50/50 | Genuine indifference or poor question | Discuss: "What does mixed data tell you? Maybe try different version for each group?" |
| Students copy each other's survey questions | Easier than creating original | Require unique product ideas first; review questions before copying; allow similar structure with different content |

1. Survey a second group and compare results
2. Create a more sophisticated graph type (pie chart, double bar graph)
3. Help classmates analyze their data
4. Research how real companies do market research
5. Design a survey about school/class improvement ideas

**For Advanced Learners:**
1. Survey multiple demographics and compare (boys vs. girls, different ages, different locations)
2. Calculate advanced statistics (median, mode, range of price answers)
3. Create cross-tabulation analysis ("Of people who said yes, 80% preferred blue")
4. Write formal Market Research Report with executive summary
5. Research and present on real market research failures (New Coke, Crystal Pepsi)
6. Design survey for real school or community need
7. Create infographic of findings using digital tools

**For Additional Days/Sessions:**
- **Day 2:** Re-survey after making product changes to see if approval increased
- **Week 2:** Test actual prototypes with customers and gather feedback
- **Extension:** Conduct ongoing research for Makers' Faire products
- **Cross-Class:** Partner with another grade to survey each other

**Seasonal Variations:**
- **Fall:** Survey about fall festival booth ideas
- **Winter:** Holiday gift preferences survey
- **Spring:** Garden club planting preferences
- **Summer:** Summer program activity survey
