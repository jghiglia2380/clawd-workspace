# Season 4, Chapter 8: THE PIVOT
## Layla | FL Integration: Strong

### Story Summary
Month two begins. Revenue plateaus around $90-95 per week despite more effort. Layla reviews the data: pottery = 60% of revenue (growing), repairs = 25% (stable), birdhouses = 10% (declining), journals = 5% (dead). Painful decisions: birdhouses become order-only (stop making inventory). Layla kills her own product line — journals — that SHE loves but the market doesn't. Two weeks post-pivot: revenue $115 (up), costs $26 (down), profit $89 (best ever). The map reveals customer demand clusters.

### FL Concepts
- Pivoting (strategic product changes)
- Reading market signals
- Data-driven decisions
- Opportunity cost (time spent on journals could go to pottery)
- Dropping unprofitable products
- Revenue by product category
- Seasonal product planning
- Focus vs. diversification

### Character Notes
- **Layla (spotlight)**: The hardest business decision she's made. Journals were HER thing — from Ch1, they represented her creativity. Market doesn't care. She has to kill what she loves to serve what works. Growth through painful pruning. Connects to Riley's Ch4 lesson about customer needs vs. personal preference.
- **Riley**: Empathizes deeply. She knows what it's like to love a product the market doesn't want (rejected platter in Ch4). Artist vs customer: "I get it. Sometimes what we love and what they need aren't the same thing."
- **Ellis**: Pragmatic. Birdhouses aren't worth the time. Sees the data clearly. Supports Layla's decision even though it's hard.
- **Benny**: Surprised. "But journals are beautiful!" Layla: "Beautiful doesn't mean profitable."
- **Frances**: Garden metaphor for pruning. "Sometimes you cut away good branches to make room for great ones."
- **Neville callback**: Brief scene. Benny mentions Neville quit his fix-it shop. Overextended, poor quality, couldn't sustain it. Contrast: Maplewood Makers succeeded by making hard data-driven decisions.

### Map Progression
Some lines fade and disappear (birdhouse customers who don't reorder, journal customers who never were). It looks like loss at first. But the remaining lines glow brighter and new pottery lines appear — stronger, more numerous. The map reveals clustering patterns: pottery customers tend to be near the café, repair customers cluster on three specific streets. Data becomes visible geography. The pruning created clarity and focus.

### Scene-by-Scene Outline (20 scenes)

1. **Month two begins**: Calendar flips. They've been in business for 5 weeks. Momentum feels... stuck.
2. **Revenue plateau concern**: Three weeks in a row: $91, $94, $92. Not growing despite more effort.
3. **Layla's investigation**: She reviews all order records. Where is revenue actually coming from?
4. **The data reveal**: Pottery 60%, repairs 25%, birdhouses 10%, journals 5%. (Scaffold: Scorecard enhanced with categories)
5. **Trend analysis**: Pottery growing (from 40% to 60% over 8 weeks). Journals declining (from 12% to 5%).
6. **Fort meeting**: Layla presents findings. Team sees the pattern. Pottery is the winner.
7. **The birdhouse decision**: Stop making inventory. Make them only when ordered. Team agrees — easy call.
8. **The journal problem**: Silence. Journals are Layla's product. Her pride. Her creativity.
9. **Riley's empathy**: "I know how this feels." References Ch4's rejected platter. "Sometimes what we love isn't what they need."
10. **Layla's struggle**: "But they're beautiful. I worked so hard on them." Ellis gently: "The market doesn't care about effort. Just about value."
11. **Frances's garden visit**: Layla goes alone. Upset. Frances offers pruning metaphor: cutting good branches for great ones.
12. **The decision**: Layla agrees to kill journals. Voice cracks. "No more journals."
13. **Pivot implementation**: Monday morning. Order board reflects new focus. (Scaffold: Board now pottery + repairs only)
14. **Freed capacity**: Time previously spent on journals (2-3 hours per week) now goes to pottery.
15. **Riley's increased output**: With Layla helping with pottery prep, Riley can make more pieces per week.
16. **Week one post-pivot**: Revenue climbs to $107. Costs drop to $28 (no journal supplies). Profit $79.
17. **Week two post-pivot**: Revenue hits $115. Costs $26. Profit $89 — record. (Scaffold: Money jars at highest point yet)
18. **The validation**: Data proves the pivot worked. Hard decision, right decision.
19. **Neville callback**: Benny mentions Neville quit his business. Couldn't sustain it. Layla: "We're still here because we make hard choices."
20. **Map moment**: The map shows the faded journal lines gone, pottery lines glowing brighter and multiplying. Customer clusters visible around café area. Map reveals demand geography. Layla: "The map shows what works. We just have to be brave enough to follow it."
