# Season 4, Chapter 6: HIDDEN COSTS
## Layla | FL Integration: Strong

### Story Summary
Three things hit at once: Ellis's saw blade snaps ($12 replacement), Riley's glaze runs out ($18 refill), and Layla realizes they've been using Mr. Mason's workshop electricity for free. They discover "overhead" — the costs that don't show up in material lists. They recalculate past profits, adjusting for tool depreciation, electricity usage, supply restocking, and bulk purchasing opportunities. True profit over four weeks: $155 (not $217 as they thought). An eye-opening but necessary correction.

### FL Concepts
- Overhead costs
- True cost accounting
- Tool depreciation
- Supply restocking
- Bulk purchasing benefits
- Hidden expenses
- Adjusted profit margins
- Cost of doing business

### Character Notes
- **Layla (spotlight)**: Her strength — tracking and analysis — reveals uncomfortable truth. Initial pride in their $217 profit turns to concern when she adjusts for overhead. Not defeat, but maturation. "We're still profitable. But we need to know the REAL numbers."
- **Ellis**: Frustrated when his saw blade breaks. "I've been careful!" Mr. Mason explains: "Tools wear out. That's not failure — it's use."
- **Riley**: Her glaze running out seems obvious (she used it all), but bulk purchasing insight surprises her. Buying larger containers saves 30%.
- **Benny**: Doesn't understand overhead at first. "But we didn't spend money on electricity — we used Mr. Mason's." Layla explains: "We didn't pay, but someone did. If we grow, we'll need our own space."
- **Mr. Mason**: Key teaching moment. Shows his own overhead sheet. Rent, electricity, tool replacement, insurance. "These are the costs of being in business, separate from making any specific thing."

### Map Progression
The bright golden customer lines are still there, strong and glowing. But beneath them, dim gray shadows appear — visualization of hidden costs that were always there but invisible until now. The shadows don't replace the golden lines; they reveal the full picture. The map shows both value created (bright lines) and costs incurred (shadows) — a more mature, complete view of the business.

**Layla's Order Board**:

### Tier 1 Concept Anchors
- **Overhead** → "Hidden Helpers": The things you use but forget to count — like water for washing hands or the room you play in
- **Tool depreciation** → "Crayons Get Short": Every time you use a crayon, it gets a little shorter. Eventually you need a new one.
- **True cost** → "The Whole Story": Counting everything, not just the obvious things
- **Bulk purchasing** → "Big Box Savings": Buying a big box of crackers costs less per cracker than buying tiny bags
- **Electricity usage** → "The Light Bill": Keeping the lights on costs money even if you don't think about it

### Scene-by-Scene Outline (20 scenes)

1. **The saw blade snaps**: Ellis mid-cut on a shelf. Blade breaks. Work stops.
2. **Replacement needed**: Hardware store trip. $12 for new blade. Ellis frustrated — wasn't budgeted.
3. **Riley's glaze problem**: Opens kiln. Perfect mugs. Opens glaze jar — nearly empty. Needs refill before next order.
4. **Glaze shopping**: $18 for a small jar (what she's been buying) or $45 for large container (lasts 3x longer). She usually buys small.
5. **Fort meeting**: Unexpected expenses piling up. Where does this come from in the budget?
6. **Layla's investigation**: Reviews the pricing sheet. These costs weren't included. "We priced materials for each product. But what about tools? Space? Supplies?"
7. **Mr. Mason's workshop visit**: They ask about tool costs. He shows his overhead tracking sheet. Eye-opening.
8. **Electricity realization**: Layla asks Mr. Mason what electricity costs. He estimates: "Maybe $30-40 a month for the space you use." They've been using it free.
9. **The overhead conversation**: Mr. Mason explains: "Overhead is what it costs to exist as a business, separate from making specific products."
10. **Bulk purchasing lesson**: Mr. Mason shows how he buys sandpaper in bulk. "Unit price drops 40% when I buy the big pack."
11. **Back to the fort**: Layla proposes recalculating their true profit. Team nervous but agrees.
12. **The reconciliation scene**: Layla lists adjustments: Past saw blade wear ($6 estimate), Riley's glaze (should have bought bulk, $12 lost savings), Mr. Mason's electricity ($22 estimate for 4 weeks). (Scaffold: Money jars adjusted)
13. **The new number**: $217 reported profit becomes $155 adjusted profit. Silence. Is that bad?
14. **Perspective check**: Layla: "We're still profitable. But now we know the REAL number. That's better than thinking we made money we didn't."
15. **Going-forward changes**: They decide to factor 15% overhead into future pricing. Pricing sheet updated.
16. **Bulk purchase planning**: Riley orders the large glaze container. Higher upfront cost but better long-term value.
17. **Tool reserve fund**: They allocate $10 to RESERVE jar specifically for tool replacement. (Scaffold: Reserve jar now has purpose beyond emergency)
18. **The overhead sheet creation**: Layla makes the tracking page. (Scaffold: Overhead Sheet introduced)
19. **Ellis's acceptance**: "I was mad about the saw blade. But Mr. Mason's right — tools wear out. We just have to plan for it."
20. **Map moment**: The map shows the shadow-costs beneath the bright lines. It's not discouraging — it's honest. Riley: "It's like seeing the roots under a flower. You didn't know they were there, but they were always feeding it."
