# Season 4, Chapter 1: GOING SOLO
## Ensemble | FL Integration: Medium

### Story Summary
Three weeks after the Makers' Faire, demand hasn't stopped — 14 requests sit in Ellis's notebook. Each friend tries going solo to handle the workload: Riley takes pottery commissions, Ellis does repairs, Layla tries selling journals, Benny becomes an errand runner. By week's end, each has hit a different wall. They compare notes at the fort: combined, they made $58 for dozens of hours. Layla sees the pattern: "What if those aren't four problems? They're one problem — we're each trying to do everything alone." They decide to merge into one business for a trial week.

### FL Concepts
- Entrepreneurship (identifying marketable skills)
- Supply meeting demand
- The limits of solo operation
- Opportunity cost (time vs. money earned)
- Division of labor benefits

### Character Notes
- **Riley**: Discovers that taking commissions means saying yes to everything — even pieces she doesn't want to make. Foreshadows Ch4's artist vs customer tension.
- **Ellis**: Keeps detailed notes on repair requests but realizes he's spending more time coordinating than fixing.
- **Layla**: Her journals are beautiful but slow to make. Market doesn't value the time she puts in. Foreshadows Ch8's painful pivot decision.
- **Benny**: Loves running errands but has no system for tracking who owes what. Foreshadows his sales role.
- **Mr. Mason**: Appears briefly when Ellis needs a tool. Asks questions: "How many hours did you work? What did you earn per hour?"
- **Frances**: Garden visit. Offers metaphor: "One tomato plant gives you some tomatoes. A whole garden feeds the neighborhood."

### Map Progression
The magical map shows four disconnected, dim dots — one for each kid working solo. No connections between them. Each dot pulses independently, showing isolated activity but no network. Visual metaphor for fragmentation and inefficiency. By chapter's end, the dots begin moving closer together as the kids discuss merging.

### Visual Scaffolds
**Ellis's Notebook** (appears throughout chapter):
- Simple list of 14 repair/order requests with names and brief descriptions
- Tier 1: "Ellis's notebook had fourteen names. That was a lot of work."
- Tier 4: "Ellis reviewed the request log: fourteen potential customers, each requiring material estimates, time allocation, and delivery coordination."

**The $58 Scene** (appears in final fort meeting):
- Physical money spread out: Riley's bills, Ellis's coins, Layla's few dollars, Benny's change
- Visual impact of seeing the combined total vs. the hours worked
- Tier 1: "They put all their money together. Fifty-eight dollars. That seemed like a lot until Layla said how many hours they worked."
- Tier 4: "When they aggregated their individual revenues — $22, $19, $12, and $5 respectively — the total of $58 represented less than $2 per hour across an estimated 35 combined work hours."

### Tier 1 Concept Anchors
- **Entrepreneurship** → "Helping Hands": Doing something helpful and getting thanked for it (with a little money as thanks)
- **Supply meeting demand** → "Sharing Toys": If lots of kids want to borrow your toy, you can decide who gets it when
- **Solo operation limits** → "Carrying Too Much": When you try to carry all the groceries yourself, you drop things. Sometimes you need help.
- **Opportunity cost** → "Missing Playtime": If you spend all afternoon helping one person, you can't help anyone else or play

### Scene-by-Scene Outline (20 scenes)

1. **Three weeks later**: Fort meeting. Ellis shows notebook with 14 requests. Excited but overwhelmed.
2. **Decision to split up**: Each kid claims different types of orders. Confident they can handle it solo.
3. **Riley's first commission**: Woman wants matching mugs. Riley agrees without asking what "matching" means.
4. **Ellis's first repair**: Broken gate. Discovers he doesn't have the right saw blade.
5. **Layla's journal attempt**: Sets up at the park with handmade journals. Two hours, zero sales.
6. **Benny's errands begin**: Runs three errands for neighbors. Forgets to write down who paid what.
7. **Mid-week check-in**: Quick fort meeting. Everyone says it's "going fine" but looks tired.
8. **Riley's problem**: Customer wants six mugs but keeps changing the design. Riley remakes them twice.
9. **Ellis's problem**: Spends half a day traveling between jobs. Realizes he's inefficient.
10. **Layla's problem**: Finally makes a sale ($8 for a journal that took 4 hours to make). Math doesn't work.
11. **Benny's problem**: Accidentally gives Mrs. Chen's payment to Mr. Patel. Has to fix it with his own money.
12. **Frances's garden visit**: Layla visits garden to think. Frances offers tomato metaphor.
13. **Thursday exhaustion**: Ellis turns down two new requests. He's at capacity alone.
14. **Riley's breaking point**: Customer rejects the "final" version. Riley wants to quit commissions.
15. **End of week approaches**: Each kid counts their money privately. Disappointed by totals.
16. **The fort meeting**: Saturday morning. They compare notes and money. (Scaffolds: $58 scene)
17. **Mr. Mason's question**: Ellis mentions the $58. Mr. Mason asks about hours worked. Reality hits.
18. **Layla's pattern recognition**: "What if these aren't four problems? We're each doing everything alone."
19. **The proposal**: Layla suggests merging into one business. Trial week. Vote is unanimous.
20. **Map moment**: They look at the neighborhood. The map shows their four dim, disconnected dots beginning to move together. End with hope and determination.

---

**Notes for Script Generation:**
- Focus on the exhaustion and small frustrations that accumulate when working solo
- Each kid's "wall" should feel different: Riley's is creative compromise, Ellis's is logistics, Layla's is economics, Benny's is organization
- The $58 scene is the emotional climax — make the contrast between effort and reward clear
- End with optimism and team spirit, not defeat
