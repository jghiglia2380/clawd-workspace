# Season 4, Chapter 4: THE UNHAPPY CUSTOMER
## Riley | FL Integration: Medium

### Story Summary
Riley pours her heart into a decorative platter — blue flowers, her best work yet. The customer says: "It's beautiful work. But it's not what I had in mind." They have to redo it — doubling materials cost and time. The team creates a new rule: for custom orders over $10, show a sketch before starting. Riley turns the rejected platter into a new "artist's choice" product line. A separate quality issue (slightly leaning birdhouse) gets fixed for free, earning word-of-mouth praise.

### FL Concepts
- Customer satisfaction
- Quality control
- Refunds vs. redos (cost implications)
- Reputation management
- Managing customer expectations
- Turning mistakes into opportunities
- The cost of rework
- Customer communication

### Character Notes
- **Riley (spotlight)**: The inciting incident for her seasonal arc. She learns the painful lesson that "beautiful" isn't always "right." Initial defensiveness ("It IS beautiful!") gives way to professionalism. Creates the artist's choice line as a way to preserve her creative freedom while also serving customers.
- **Artist vs Customer tension**: Riley used to make whatever she wanted. Now she has to make what customers want. This chapter crystallizes the tension she'll navigate all season.
- **Layla**: Helps Riley understand that it's not about the work being bad — it's about matching the customer's vision. "We're not making art for us. We're making what they need."
- **Ellis**: The birdhouse quality issue. Takes responsibility, fixes it free. "Our reputation is worth more than fifteen dollars."
- **Benny**: Delivers the rejected platter, hears the feedback, feels terrible telling Riley. Learns that delivering bad news is part of customer service.
- **Mr. Mason**: Offers perspective: "Every woodworker has a piece rejected. It teaches you to ask more questions upfront."

### Map Progression
Most customer lines glow steady and bright. But one line — to the platter customer — flickers and dims (visual tension when the piece is rejected). When Riley redoes it correctly, that line glows steady again. When Ellis fixes the birdhouse for free, its line grows brighter (stronger relationship from recovery). The map shows that problems can be repaired, and recovery builds trust.

### Visual Scaffolds
**The Money Jar System** (impact of rework visible):
- Initial platter cost: $8 materials, $18 projected revenue, $10 profit expected
- After redo: $16 materials total, $18 revenue, $2 profit (80% profit loss)
- Physical scene: Riley has to take money from PROFIT jar and put it back in COSTS jar for second round of materials
- Tier 1: "Riley needed more clay and more glaze. The profit from the platter was almost gone."
- Tier 4: "The rework requirement doubled material costs to $16 while revenue remained fixed at $18, compressing the margin from 56% to 11% and demonstrating the financial penalty of misaligned expectations."

**Layla's Order Board**:
- Platter order card moves from YELLOW back to GREEN (restart), then back to YELLOW
- Visual representation of lost time and capacity

**The Sketch Rule Card** (new scaffold, created this chapter):
- Small card added to order board: "Custom orders $10+ → Sketch First"
- Referenced whenever they take custom work going forward
- Tier 1: "They made a new rule. Before making something special, show the customer a drawing first."
- Tier 4: "The sketch approval protocol for custom commissions exceeding $10 minimized downstream rework risk by establishing client-approved design specifications prior to material commitment."

### Tier 1 Concept Anchors
- **Customer satisfaction** → "Making Someone Smile": Did they like what you made? If not, how do you fix it?
- **Managing expectations** → "Describing the Game": Before you play, everyone agrees on the rules so nobody gets upset
- **Rework cost** → "Making It Twice": If you have to do something over, it takes twice as long and uses twice the crayons
- **Quality control** → "Checking Your Work": Looking to make sure it's right before you give it to someone
- **Reputation** → "Being Known For": What do people say about you when you're not there?

### Scene-by-Scene Outline (20 scenes)

1. **New custom order**: Woman at the café asks about a decorative platter. Riley excited — this is her specialty.
2. **Taking the order**: Woman describes what she wants. "Something with flowers. You choose!" Riley hears freedom.
3. **Riley's creative process**: She designs blue morning glories. Spends hours perfecting it. Her best work yet.
4. **Delivery day**: Benny delivers the platter. Riley waits at fort, proud and confident.
5. **Benny returns**: Somber. "She said it's beautiful. But it's not what she wanted." Riley's heart sinks.
6. **The rejection conversation**: Customer wanted roses, not morning glories. Warm colors, not blues. "I thought you'd know."
7. **Riley's reaction**: Defensive. "It IS beautiful! I worked so hard!" Layla gently intervenes.
8. **The cost reality**: Layla shows the math. $8 materials gone. If they redo it: another $8. $18 revenue - $16 costs = $2 profit. Almost nothing.
9. **Team discussion**: Do they refund, redo, or compromise? Ellis says reputation matters more than one job's profit.
10. **Riley's decision**: She'll redo it. But she's hurt and frustrated.
11. **Mr. Mason's wisdom**: Riley visits workshop. Mr. Mason shares his own rejection story. "Now I always ask questions first."
12. **The sketch rule born**: Layla proposes new system: approve designs before starting. Team agrees. (Scaffold: Sketch Rule Card)
13. **Riley makes the second platter**: Red roses, warm tones. Technically correct but feels mechanical. Not her heart.
14. **Second delivery success**: Customer loves it. Pays happily. But Riley feels hollow.
15. **The leftover platter problem**: Riley has the rejected morning glory platter. Can't sell it custom. Can't return the materials.
16. **Artist's choice idea**: Frances visits. Suggests: "Make what you love. Call it 'artist's choice.' Someone will want it because YOU chose it."
17. **The birdhouse quality issue**: Separate subplot. Customer mentions birdhouse leans slightly. Ellis investigates.
18. **Free fix**: Ellis repairs birdhouse, doesn't charge. Customer impressed: "You stand behind your work!"
19. **Word spreads**: Customer tells neighbors about the free fix. Generates two new orders. Goodwill has value.
20. **Map moment**: Riley looks at the platter — morning glory and roses both on display. The map shows steady lines. She's learning to balance art and customer needs. The artist's choice platter sits waiting for the right buyer.

---

**Notes for Script Generation:**
- This chapter introduces Riley's key seasonal tension: artist vs customer
- Make Riley's emotions real — the rejection hurts because she cared
- The sketch rule should feel like a natural solution, not a punishment
- Ellis's free fix is a subplot showing a different aspect of customer service
- End with growth, not defeat: Riley is learning, not giving up her artistry
- The artist's choice concept will pay off in later chapters (Ch10, Ch12)
- Tier 1 needs emotional resonance: "Riley felt sad. She made something beautiful, but it wasn't what the customer wanted."
