# Season 4, Chapter 7: THE BIG MISTAKE
## Benny | FL Integration: Medium

### Story Summary
Benny promises seven new orders in one week — on top of four existing orders. The team's realistic capacity is about six orders total per week. They're at nearly double capacity. Everything suffers: quality issues, missed deadlines, rushed work, exhausted kids. They create Benny's Capacity Card system: a green/yellow/red card that Layla updates daily showing current workload. Benny carries it when talking to customers. Revenue hits $132 (highest ever), but profit per hour went DOWN because of waste and overtime.

### FL Concepts
- Capacity limits (you can only do so much)
- Overcommitting consequences
- Delivery timelines
- The cost of rushing (quality suffers, waste increases)
- Demand management
- Waitlist psychology (making customers wait can build value)
- Profit per hour vs. total profit
- Sustainable growth vs. burnout

### Character Notes
- **Benny (spotlight)**: His enthusiasm becomes a liability. Loves saying "yes" to customers. Doesn't want to disappoint anyone. The week of chaos teaches him that "yes" without capacity means "no" to quality. Learns that managing expectations is part of sales.
- **Riley**: Suffers most from the overcommitment. Custom work requires creative energy — rushing destroys it. Her platter for Mrs. Davis is mediocre because she had only 3 hours instead of the 8 she needed. Artist vs customer tension flares again.
- **Ellis**: Tries to work faster. Makes mistakes: wrong measurements on a shelf (has to redo), drops and breaks a birdhouse (waste).
- **Layla**: The early warning system. By Wednesday, she sees disaster coming. "We can't do all this." But they're committed.
- **Mr. Mason**: Observes the chaos. Gentle intervention: "Benny, if you promise more than you can deliver, you've made two mistakes — the promise AND the delivery."

### Map Progression
The web overloads visually. Too many lines pulsing at once — some flickering yellow or orange (stressed connections). One line goes red and breaks (the late delivery). The map shows the network straining under excessive load. Unlike Ch5's competition or Ch6's hidden costs, this is self-inflicted stress. By chapter's end, after implementing the capacity card, the pulsing calms back to steady glowing lines.

### Visual Scaffolds
**Benny's Capacity Card** (introduced this chapter, appears through rest of season):
- Simple index card Benny carries: one side GREEN, flip reveals YELLOW, flip again reveals RED
- Layla updates it daily based on current workload:
  - GREEN = Can take 2-3 more orders this week
  - YELLOW = At capacity, can take 1 more small order
  - RED = Fully booked, only emergency/waitlist
- Tier 1: "Benny carried a card. When it was green, he could promise new work. When it was red, he had to say 'not this week.'"
- Tier 4: "The capacity signaling protocol provided real-time workload status, enabling dynamic commitment management and preventing overbooking scenarios through visual traffic-light indicators Benny consulted pre-commitment."

**Layla's Order Board** (overwhelmed this chapter):
- Opens chapter: 4 cards in YELLOW (normal)
- Mid-chapter: 11 cards in YELLOW (crisis)
- Cards overlapping, some falling off the board — visual chaos
- End of chapter: 6 cards, properly spaced — sustainable

**The Money Jar System**:
- Week ends with $132 revenue (highest yet!) and $43 costs
- But waste from rushed work adds $12 to costs (materials ruined)
- Profit per hour calculation: $89 profit ÷ 52 hours worked (13 hours each × 4 kids) = $1.71/hour
- Previous weeks averaged $2.50-3.00/hour
- Tier 4: "While gross revenue reached an unprecedented $132, total labor investment of 52 hours yielded per-hour compensation of $1.71, representing a 43% efficiency decline compared to historical $2.50-3.00/hour baselines due to rush-induced waste and rework."

### Tier 1 Concept Anchors
- **Capacity limits** → "Full Hands": You can only carry so many things at once — trying to carry more makes you drop them all
- **Overcommitting** → "Too Many Friends": Saying yes to three playdates on the same day means you can't really play with anyone
- **Rushing** → "Coloring Too Fast": When you rush, the picture doesn't look good and you mess up
- **Waitlist** → "Taking Turns": Sometimes people have to wait for their turn, and that's okay
- **Profit per hour** → "Getting Paid for Your Time": How much money you make for every hour you work

### Scene-by-Scene Outline (20 scenes)

1. **Monday morning optimism**: Board shows 4 orders in progress. Good pace. Normal week expected.
2. **Benny's sales spree begins**: Monday afternoon. Runs into Mrs. Garcia. She needs two shelves. Benny promises them for Friday.
3. **Tuesday morning**: Benny lands three more orders during school drop-off conversations. Doesn't check with team.
4. **Wednesday ambush**: Fort meeting. Benny proudly reports seven new orders. Team's faces drop.
5. **Layla's math**: Current 4 + new 7 = 11 total. Capacity is maybe 6. "Benny, we can't do this."
6. **Benny's guilt**: "But I already promised! I can't tell them no now."
7. **Commitment trap**: They're stuck. Can't cancel — reputation matters. Have to deliver somehow.
8. **The chaos week begins**: Thursday-Friday-Saturday become a blur of rushed work.
9. **Riley's platter problem**: Mrs. Davis's custom platter. Riley has only 3 hours (needs 8). She rushes. It's... fine. Not great. Artist vs customer tension. (Connection to Ch4)
10. **Ellis's measurement mistake**: Distracted, measures wrong. Cuts a shelf board too short. Has to scrap it and start over. Waste.
11. **Friday crisis**: Two orders due. One is late. Customer calls. Benny has to deliver bad news: "Tomorrow morning instead?"
12. **Saturday marathon**: All four kids working from 7am to 5pm. Exhausted. They deliver everything, but barely.
13. **The late delivery**: Sunday morning delivery (should have been Saturday). Customer annoyed but accepts it.
14. **Revenue celebration, exhaustion realization**: They count money. $132! Record high. But they're wiped out.
15. **Layla's analysis**: Calculates hours worked. 52 total. Profit per hour: $1.71. Lower than before despite higher revenue.
16. **Mr. Mason's observation**: Visits fort Sunday afternoon. Sees exhausted kids. Gentle lesson about promising vs. capacity.
17. **The capacity card idea**: Layla proposes giving Benny a real-time signal. (Scaffold: Capacity Card created)
18. **System testing**: They try it. Layla sets card to YELLOW (6 orders in progress). Benny practices saying "We're at capacity this week, but I can put you on the list for next week."
19. **Benny's acceptance**: Hard for him to say no. But he understands. "I wanted everyone to be happy. I made everyone stressed instead."
20. **Map moment**: The overloaded web calms down. Lines return to steady glow. The capacity card sits on the table — a tool to prevent future chaos. Benny picks it up: "Green means go. Red means grow the business first."

**Notes for Script Generation:**
- This chapter should FEEL chaotic — rushed dialogue, quick scene cuts, mounting stress
- Benny's spotlight: his strength (enthusiasm, people skills) becomes a weakness without systems
- Make the exhaustion physical: kids yawning, rubbing eyes, making mistakes from fatigue
- The $132 celebration should feel hollow — they're too tired to enjoy it
- Riley's artist vs customer tension resurfaces: "I made her a platter. But I didn't make her MY best platter."
- End with the capacity card as a system win, not a personality fix: "We love that Benny wants to help everyone. The card helps him do it smartly."
- Tier 1 needs causal clarity: "They said yes to too much work. That made them tired and they made mistakes."
