# Season 4, Chapter 3: OPEN FOR BUSINESS
## Benny | FL Integration: Strong

### Story Summary
First week of real operations. They install Mrs. Torres's shelf ($15, $10.50 profit), fix a gate on Elm Street ($20 + $5 tip), deliver a custom 6-mug set ($48), and take on several smaller jobs. Benny discovers that selling isn't convincing — it's listening to what people need. End of week: $93 revenue, $22 materials, $71 profit. But Layla spots a concern: 60% came from two big orders. "What if next week we don't get a big order?"

### FL Concepts
- Revenue (money coming in)
- First sales / customer acquisition
- Tracking income and expenses
- Tips as income
- Revenue concentration risk
- Profit calculation (revenue minus costs)
- Customer relationships

### Character Notes
- **Benny (spotlight)**: Discovers his gift for sales. Initially tries to "convince" people, then learns to listen. His personality — friendly, enthusiastic, genuine — becomes the business's face.
- **Layla**: Tracks every dollar in a notebook. Celebrates the $71 profit but immediately sees the concentration risk pattern.
- **Ellis**: Pride in the shelf installation. Quality work leads to word-of-mouth.
- **Riley**: The 6-mug set is her best work yet. Customer delight validates her craft.
- **Mr. Mason**: Brief appearance when they buy materials. Reminds them to keep receipts.
- **Mrs. Torres**: First major customer. Thrilled with the shelf. Tells her sister about Maplewood Makers.

### Map Progression
First customer connections light up! Lines extend from the fort to Mrs. Torres's house (warm gold), to Elm Street (steady glow), to the mug customer's location (bright and strong). Each completed job adds a new glowing thread. The network is small but real. The map shows the fort as the center with six or seven golden lines radiating outward.

### Visual Scaffolds
**Layla's Order Board** (introduced this chapter, appears through rest of season):
- Physical board in the fort with cards in three columns: GREEN (ready to take), YELLOW (in progress), RED (at capacity)
- Week starts with 4 orders in YELLOW
- Tier 1: "The board showed four cards in the 'working on it' section. Benny wanted to add more orders."
- Tier 4: "The capacity tracker indicated four active commitments in yellow, suggesting moderate utilization with bandwidth for 2-3 additional projects before approaching the red threshold."

**The Money Jar System** (active this chapter):
- Scene where they move coins from COSTS jar to PROFIT jar after Mrs. Torres pays
- $15 revenue: $4.50 to COSTS jar, $10.50 to PROFIT jar
- First time the PROFIT jar has money — celebratory moment
- End of week: PROFIT jar has $71, RESERVE jar still has $10

**The Weekly Scorecard** (introduced this chapter):
- Simple chart Layla creates: Revenue | Costs | Profit
- Week 1 totals: $93 | $22 | $71
- Tier 1: "Layla wrote down all the money they made and all the money they spent. The difference was seventy-one dollars."
- Tier 4: "The P&L summary indicated gross revenue of $93, direct costs of $22, yielding net profit of $71 — a healthy 76% margin suggesting efficient operations and appropriate pricing."

### Tier 1 Concept Anchors
- **Revenue** → "The Thank-You Money": When you help someone, they thank you with dollars
- **Profit** → "What's Left Over": After you pay for supplies, what's left is yours to keep
- **Revenue concentration** → "The Friendship Mix": Putting all your trust in one friend — what if they move away or get busy?
- **Tracking** → "The Memory Helper": Writing things down so you don't forget who gave you what
- **Tips** → "The Extra Thank-You": Sometimes people are so happy they give you more than you asked for

### Scene-by-Scene Outline (20 scenes)

1. **Monday morning launch**: Fort meeting. Four orders on the board. They're officially open for business. (Scaffold: Order Board introduced)
2. **Mrs. Torres's shelf job**: Ellis measures the wall. Riley helps carry tools. Professional and careful.
3. **Installation success**: Shelf is perfect. Mrs. Torres is delighted. Pays $15 cash. First revenue!
4. **The money jar moment**: Back at fort, they do the math and move money between jars. $10.50 profit from one job. (Scaffold: Money Jars active)
5. **Benny's first solo sale attempt**: Tries to convince Mr. Patel to buy a birdhouse. Mr. Patel says no.
6. **Benny's reflection**: Talks to Layla. "I was trying to make him want it. I should have asked what he needed."
7. **Elm Street gate repair**: Broken latch. Ellis diagnoses the problem. Simple fix but important.
8. **The tip surprise**: Customer pays $20 (agreed price) plus $5 tip. "You saved me a service call. That's worth extra."
9. **Benny's learning moment**: Second sales attempt. Asks questions first. Listens. Lands a small order.
10. **Mid-week check**: Three jobs complete, two in progress. Money jars filling up. Excitement builds.
11. **The custom mug order**: Woman wants six matching mugs for her sister's birthday. Riley excited but nervous.
12. **Riley's careful work**: She focuses on consistency. Same glaze, same shape. Quality control matters.
13. **Mug delivery**: Customer opens the box and gasps. "These are perfect!" $48 payment — biggest single job.
14. **Thursday tally**: They count revenue so far. Over $80. They're succeeding.
15. **Small jobs add up**: Quick repairs, a delivered shelf, a journal sale (Layla surprised). Momentum.
16. **Saturday wrap-up**: Week is over. Time to tally final numbers. (Scaffold: Weekly Scorecard introduced)
17. **The $93 celebration**: Revenue total is announced. They high-five. More than they expected.
18. **Layla's math**: Subtracts $22 in costs. $71 profit. They stare at the number. It's real.
19. **Layla's concern**: "But sixty percent came from the gate and the mugs. What if we don't get big orders next week?"
20. **Map moment**: They look at the neighborhood. The map shows golden lines connecting them to customers. The network is growing. But they notice some areas are dark — opportunities not yet reached.
