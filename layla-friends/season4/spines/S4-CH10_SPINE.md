# Season 4, Chapter 10: THE SEASONAL RUSH
## Riley | FL Integration: Strong

### Story Summary
Holiday season arrives. Demand spikes dramatically. Strategy: pre-orders with 50% deposit upfront to manage cash flow and commitment. Riley discovers batch production — making 14 ornaments in batches of 7 takes 8 hours instead of 14 hours (efficiency gains). The café reorders pottery twice. Holiday month: $396 total revenue, $243 profit. But Layla insists on setting aside 30% ($73) as reserve for slow season ahead. Four kids, four different approaches to their windfall earnings.

### FL Concepts
- Seasonal demand (holiday rush)
- Inventory planning
- Pre-orders and customer deposits
- Upfront deposits (reducing risk)
- Batch production efficiency
- Revenue spikes (temporary vs. sustained)
- Building reserves for slow periods
- Cash flow management

### Character Notes
- **Riley (spotlight)**: Batch production revelation transforms her relationship with pottery. She designs the ornament ONCE (creative satisfaction), then produces multiples (efficiency). Artist vs customer tension resolves: "I can be creative in the design and efficient in the making." This is her seasonal arc payoff (from Ch4's rejection through Ch7's rushing to Ch10's solution).
- **Layla**: Plays Cassandra. Everyone wants to celebrate and spend. She insists on reserve fund. "January will be slow. We need to plan for it." Not popular but necessary.
- **Ellis**: Builds display racks for ornaments. Enjoys the production challenge.
- **Benny**: Overwhelmed by demand but uses capacity card to manage it. Learns to use waitlist as sales tool: "Pre-order now for guaranteed delivery."
- **Ellen**: Reorders twice. The café partnership proves its value during peak demand.

### Map Progression
The map is dense and golden during holiday season — maximum connections. Lines are bright, numerous, pulsing with activity. The café line is particularly thick, branching to many customers. The visual is lush, abundant, almost overwhelming. But there's a subtle warning: some lines are orange-tinted (overheated) rather than pure gold. Foreshadows the exhaustion and coming slowdown.

**Riley's Batch Production Chart** (new scaffold):

### Tier 1 Concept Anchors
- **Seasonal demand** → "Ice Cream Weather": Some things people want more at certain times (ice cream in summer, hot chocolate in winter, gifts in December)
- **Pre-orders** → "Saving Your Spot": Telling someone you want it before it's made, so they save one for you
- **Deposits** → "The Promise Money": Paying part now shows you're serious and helps them buy supplies
- **Batch production** → "Making Them Together": Baking 12 cookies at once is faster than baking them one at a time
- **Reserves** → "The Seed Jar": Saving some seeds so you can plant again when spring comes
- **Revenue spike** → "The Busy Day": Sometimes lots of people want help all at once, but it won't always be that way
