# Season 4, Chapter 2: THE BUSINESS PLAN
## Ellis | FL Integration: Strong

### Story Summary
They name the business "Maplewood Makers." Layla insists on a plan before selling anything. They visit Mr. Mason, who teaches them real costing: "What does it cost to make? What will you charge? What happens when things go wrong?" He introduces PRICE - COST = PROFIT. Frances asks "Who are your customers?" They build a pricing sheet, calculate startup costs ($60), design roles (Layla manages, Ellis builds, Riley creates, Benny sells and delivers), create an order system, and set aside an emergency fund.

### FL Concepts
- Business planning
- Startup costs
- Pricing strategy (PRICE - COST = PROFIT)
- Profit margins
- Cost of goods sold
- Target market identification
- Role specialization
- Emergency reserves

### Character Notes
- **Ellis (spotlight)**: Takes the lead on calculating costs. His detail-oriented nature shines. Realizes that planning prevents the chaos he experienced in Ch1.
- **Layla**: Insists on planning first. "We can't just start selling. What if we run out of money?" Her journal business failure taught her this.
- **Riley**: Wants to skip planning and just create. Layla convinces her: "Planning now saves fixing later."
- **Benny**: Excited about his official role as "sales and delivery." Finally has a system to replace his Ch1 chaos.
- **Mr. Mason**: Key mentor role. Teaches through questions. Shows them his own workshop cost breakdown. Light skin, curly gray hair, wire-rimmed glasses. Patient and methodical.
- **Frances**: Brief appearance. Asks about target customers. "Who needs what you make? A garden grows what the soil can support."

### Map Progression
The four dots have merged into one brighter dot centered at the fort. Faint lines begin extending toward Mr. Mason's workshop (glowing warmly) and Frances's garden (soft green glow). The connections are forming but still thin — the network is just beginning. No customer connections yet.

### Visual Scaffolds
**The Money Jar System** (introduced this chapter, appears through rest of season):
- Three clear jars labeled with index cards: COSTS, PROFIT, RESERVE
- Scene where they physically divide their $60 startup money: $50 to COSTS jar, $10 to RESERVE jar
- Tier 1: "They put fifty dollars in the jar that said COSTS. Ten dollars went in the jar that said RESERVE. The PROFIT jar was empty. Not yet."
- Tier 4: "Initial capital allocation: $50 to materials inventory, $10 to emergency reserves. The profit jar remained empty pending revenue generation, a visual reminder that earnings must precede distribution."

**The Pricing Sheet** (created this chapter, referenced throughout season):
- Hand-drawn chart on large paper: Product | Materials Cost | Time | Price | Profit
- First entries: Shelf ($6 materials, 2 hours, $15 price, $9 profit)
- Tier 1: "Ellis wrote down each thing they could make and how much it would cost."
- Tier 4: "The pricing matrix incorporated direct material costs, estimated labor allocation, and target profit margins of 40-60% across product categories."

**Roles Chart** (created this chapter):
- Simple paper with four columns, one per kid, listing responsibilities
- Layla: Manager, money tracker, planning
- Ellis: Building, repairs, quality control
- Riley: Pottery, design, custom work
- Benny: Sales, delivery, customer contact
- Visual reference for "who does what"

### Tier 1 Concept Anchors
- **Business planning** → "The Playdate Plan": Before friends come over, you decide what games to play and what snacks to have
- **Startup costs** → "Getting Ready": You need supplies before you can play the game (crayons before you can color)
- **PRICE - COST = PROFIT** → "The Cookie Math": If you pay 50 cents for ingredients and sell a cookie for one dollar, you keep 50 cents
- **Roles** → "Team Jobs": Everyone has a special job so nobody does everything and nobody does nothing
- **Emergency fund** → "The Backup Snack": You save one granola bar in your backpack in case you get hungry later

### Scene-by-Scene Outline (20 scenes)

1. **Naming the business**: Brainstorm session at the fort. "Maplewood Makers" wins by vote.
2. **Layla's insistence**: "We need a plan first." Ellis and Benny want to start immediately. Riley sides with Layla.
3. **Visit to Mr. Mason**: They explain their idea. Mr. Mason asks: "What will it cost you to make these things?"
4. **The cost lesson**: Mr. Mason shows his workshop ledger. Every piece of wood has a cost. Every hour has value.
5. **PRICE - COST = PROFIT**: Mr. Mason introduces the formula. They practice with examples.
6. **What happens when things go wrong**: Mr. Mason's story of a broken tool that cost him two jobs. Hence: emergency fund.
7. **Back to the fort**: They begin listing products and materials needed.
8. **Pricing the shelf**: Ellis calculates: wood ($4), screws ($1), sandpaper ($0.50), stain ($0.50) = $6 cost. How much should they charge?
9. **The pricing debate**: Benny says $8. Layla says $12. Ellis says $15. They discuss what feels fair vs. what covers time.
10. **Frances's question**: They visit the garden. "Who are your customers? Who needs what you make?"
11. **Target market discussion**: Kids? Adults? People who need repairs or people who want art? They decide: "anyone who needs something made or fixed."
12. **Role assignment**: Natural fit — Layla's organized, Ellis builds, Riley creates, Benny loves talking to people.
13. **Creating the roles chart**: They write it down so nobody forgets. (Scaffold: Roles Chart)
14. **Startup cost calculation**: They list everything needed to start. Total: $60.
15. **Funding the startup**: They pool money from allowances and Ch1 earnings. Just enough.
16. **The money jar scene**: They create the three-jar system. (Scaffold: Money Jars introduced)
17. **Order system creation**: Layla designs a simple form: Customer name, item requested, price agreed, deadline.
18. **Pricing sheet completed**: All their main products priced. (Scaffold: Pricing Sheet created)
19. **Mr. Mason's final advice**: "You've got a plan. Now execute it. And track everything."
20. **Map moment**: The four dots are now one. Lines reach to Mr. Mason and Frances. They're ready. The map seems to pulse with anticipation.
