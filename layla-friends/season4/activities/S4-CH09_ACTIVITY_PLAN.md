# CHAPTER 9: THE PARTNERSHIP - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Formalizing partnerships with written agreements
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where Ellen proposes a consignment partnership, the friends negotiate terms, write everything down, and successfully share both work and profit
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Partnership Agreement: Who Does What?"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-3:** Ellen proposes selling pottery at her cafe → Activity begins with understanding what partnerships are
- **Scenes 4-6:** Mr. Mason explains consignment and profit sharing → Activity includes learning partnership structures
- **Scenes 7-9:** Benny negotiates better terms (30¢ instead of 40¢) → Activity practices negotiation skills
- **Scenes 10-12:** Partners agree to write everything down so nobody forgets → Activity creates actual written partnership agreements
- **Scenes 13-17:** The partnership works — mugs sell and both sides benefit → Activity demonstrates win-win outcomes
- **Scenes 18-20:** Careful record-keeping tracks partnership income separately → Activity includes tracking systems for partnership finances

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand partnership structure and profit sharing
2. **Negotiation Skills:** Practice discussing terms until both sides feel the agreement is fair
3. **Communication:** Learn to articulate roles, responsibilities, and expectations clearly
4. **Legal Thinking:** Grasp that written agreements prevent misunderstandings and protect everyone
5. **Conflict Prevention:** Understand how planning ahead (what if someone doesn't do their part?) reduces future problems
6. **Math Skills:** Calculate profit splits, percentages, and shared revenues

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Simple Partnership Template Fill-In

**Materials Needed:**
- Simple partnership template worksheet (printed or written on board)
- Pencils
- Partners assigned or self-selected

**Steps:**
1. **Pair Up (1 min):** Students find a partner. Explain: "You're starting a pretend business together."
2. **Fill Template (5 min):** Complete simple template together:
   - Our Business Name: _____________
   - Partner 1 Name & Job: _____________
   - Partner 2 Name & Job: _____________
   - How We Split Money: _____________
3. **Sign It (1 min):** Both partners sign their names at the bottom.
4. **Share One (2 min):** 3-4 pairs share their business name and how they split the money.
5. **Reflection (1 min):** "Why is it good to write down who does what?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What business did you and your partner create?"
- "What job will each person do?"
- "Why did you decide to split the money that way?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Negotiated Partnership Agreement with "What If" Clauses

**Materials Needed:**
- Partnership agreement template with sections for roles, profit split, and problems
- Pencils
- Scrap paper for negotiation notes
- Optional: sample agreements to review

**Steps:**
1. **Introduction & Pairing (2 min):** Students pair up. Review what a partnership is (two people working together, sharing work AND money).
2. **Negotiation Round (6 min):** Partners discuss and decide:
   - What will we sell/make?
   - Who does what job? (one person makes, one person sells? both do both?)
   - How do we split the money? (50/50? 60/40? why?)
3. **Write Agreement (8 min):** Fill in partnership agreement template:
   - Business name
   - Partner names and specific jobs
   - Profit split with reason
   - What happens if someone doesn't do their part?
4. **Sign & Witness (2 min):** Both partners sign. Teacher or another student pair witnesses.
5. **Share & Compare (2 min):** 3-4 pairs share their agreements. Notice: did everyone split money the same way? Why or why not?

**Discussion Questions:**
- "How did you decide who does which job?"
- "Was it easy or hard to agree on how to split the money?"
- "What happens if one partner does all the work but the other partner wants half the money?"
- "Why is it important to have a written agreement?"
- "What did Benny do in the story when he thought 40¢ was too much?"

**Extension:** Role-play a problem scenario (partner doesn't show up, partner forgets to do their job) and use the agreement to solve it.

---

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Partnership Contract with Negotiation & Legal Review

**Materials Needed:**
- Comprehensive partnership agreement template
- Negotiation planning worksheet
- Colored pencils or pens for signing
- "Lawyer Review" checklist for peer feedback
- Sample real-world partnership agreements (simplified for kids)
- Optional: notary stamps (teacher-made or real)

**Steps:**
1. **Partnership Types & Examples (4 min):**
   - Teacher presents: What is a partnership? (shared work, shared profit, shared risk)
   - Show real examples: business partners, creative partnerships (author + illustrator)
   - Review key terms: profit split, equity, roles, responsibilities, dispute resolution
2. **Planning Phase (6 min):**
   - Partners use planning worksheet to brainstorm BEFORE writing agreement:
     * What business will we run?
     * What skills does each partner bring?
     * What jobs need doing? (production, sales, marketing, money management)
     * Who is better at which job?
     * Should we split profit equally or based on work done?
3. **Negotiation Phase (8 min):**
   - Partners negotiate the tough questions:
     * Profit split: 50/50, 60/40, 70/30, or another split? Why?
     * Decision-making: What if we disagree? (vote? take turns? ask a third person?)
     * Work commitment: How many hours per week? What if someone gets sick?
     * Exit plan: What if one partner wants to leave? Can they? What happens to the business?
4. **Write Complete Partnership Agreement (10 min):**
   - Business name and purpose
   - Partner names with roles and responsibilities
   - Profit sharing agreement with justification
   - Decision-making process when partners disagree
   - Dispute resolution clause
   - Exit clause (what happens if someone wants to leave)
   - Duration (how long does this partnership last?)
   - Signatures and date
5. **Peer "Lawyer Review" (4 min):**
   - Trade agreements with another partnership
   - Review using checklist: Is it clear? Is it fair to both sides? What could go wrong?
   - Give feedback, make revisions if needed
6. **Official Signing & Filing (3 min):**
   - Partners sign final agreement
   - Teacher "notarizes" (stamps or signs as witness)
   - File in class "business records"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What negotiation strategies worked best for reaching agreement?"
- "Did any partnership split money unevenly? Why was that fair?"
- "What's the difference between a fair split and an equal split?"
- "Why do real businesses write contracts instead of just making promises?"
- "What clause in your agreement do you think will be most important?"
- "In the story, what would have happened if Ellen and the friends DIDN'T write everything down?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Compare to real-world business partnerships (research famous business partnerships)
- Create a class "Partnership Law Firm" that reviews all agreements for fairness
- Role-play dispute scenarios and use the agreements to resolve them
- Calculate actual profit splits with sample revenue numbers
- Research consignment vs. other partnership types (joint venture, LLC, etc.)

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Very simple partnership template with picture prompts
- Focus on: "You do this job, I do that job, we both get money"
- Teacher guides negotiation with questions: "Who wants to draw? Who wants to sell?"
- Basic vocabulary: partner, share, job, fair, together
- Emphasis on cooperation and taking turns

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- Simple written agreement with sentence frames: "I will ____. My partner will ____. We will split money ____."
- Understanding that both partners must agree before signing
- Introduction to basic profit splits (half and half, or one person gets more if they do more work)
- Vocabulary expansion: agreement, profit, split, decide together, fair share

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- More detailed agreement including "what if" scenarios
- Negotiation practice with give-and-take
- Understanding percentages in simple form (50/50, 60/40)
- Vocabulary: negotiate, compromise, dispute, responsibilities, terms

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- Complete partnership contract with multiple clauses
- Complex negotiation including decision-making processes and exit strategies
- Percentage calculations and profit split scenarios
- Advanced vocabulary: equity, consignment, revenue split, dispute resolution, exit clause, legal agreement, contract terms

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual partnership diagrams showing roles and money flow
- Bilingual vocabulary cards: partner/socio, agreement/acuerdo, share/compartir, profit/ganancia
- Sentence frames for negotiation: "I think we should ____. What do you think?"
- Partner with fluent speaker for negotiation practice
- Use gestures and drawings during planning

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Pre-filled partnership templates with blanks to complete (scaffold)
- Visual flowcharts: "Partner 1 does X → Partner 2 does Y → Both get money"
- Teacher support during negotiation ("What job do you want to do?")
- Option to dictate agreement to scribe instead of writing
- Simplified choices (3 profit split options instead of open-ended)

**For Advanced Students:**
- Research real business partnership structures (LLC, LLP, joint ventures)
- Create multi-party partnerships (3-4 students) with complex role assignments
- Calculate actual profit splits with sample revenue scenarios
- Write "partnership operating agreements" with quarterly review clauses
- Present partnership structure to class with visual aids
- Create a guide: "How to Choose a Good Business Partner"

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 9 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **Partnership Structure**
   - Core Lesson: Partners share both work and profit; neither can succeed without the other
   - Activity Connection: Students create real partnership agreements defining roles and profit splits
   - Age-Appropriate: K-1 experience simple sharing; 3-4 calculate complex percentage splits

2. **Consignment Explained**
   - Core Lesson: Consignment means the shop doesn't buy items upfront — they only pay when items sell
   - Activity Connection: Understand that Ellen takes some profit because she provides space, customers, and sales effort
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete example of how retail partnerships actually work

3. **Written Agreements Prevent Problems**
   - Core Lesson: "If it's not written down, people forget" — contracts protect everyone
   - Activity Connection: Students write and sign actual agreements, experiencing the security of documentation
   - Age-Appropriate: Even K-1 can understand that writing things down helps us remember

4. **Negotiation as a Business Skill**
   - Core Lesson: Partners negotiate until both feel the agreement is fair (Benny's 30¢ instead of 40¢)
   - Activity Connection: Practice proposing terms, listening to concerns, and adjusting until both agree
   - Age-Appropriate: Scaffolded from simple "Is this fair?" to complex multi-clause negotiation

5. **Profit Sharing Can Be Unequal and Still Fair**
   - Core Lesson: 50/50 isn't always the right split — fairness depends on contribution, not just equality
   - Activity Connection: Students justify unequal splits based on effort, skill, time, or resources contributed
   - Age-Appropriate: Older students calculate and defend 60/40 or 70/30 splits

**Non-Moralistic Approach:**
- Activity presents partnerships as one option, not the only way to run a business
- No judgment: "Going solo is bad" ❌ Instead: "Partnerships can help both people succeed" ✓
- Acknowledges that some people prefer working alone and that's valid too
- Focuses on skills (negotiation, documentation) applicable beyond partnerships
- Celebrates successful agreements regardless of profit split structure

- Builds on CH2's business planning by adding formal structure

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Create partnership template in advance (or use provided template)
- Decide on pairing method: teacher-assigned or student-choice?
- Prepare sample agreements to show (modify real business partnership summaries)
- Consider having "sample scenarios" ready for students who struggle to think of a business idea
- Have extra templates available for students who want to revise

**Classroom Management:**
- Establish "negotiation rules" before starting: respectful language, both people must agree before moving forward, no bullying or pressure
- Circulate during negotiation phase to ensure both partners are participating
- Intervene if you hear unfair negotiations ("I get 90% because I said so" — help them discuss why)
- Set clear time limits for each phase to prevent overthinking
- Prepare for students who can't agree — have mediation process ready

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "We can't agree on how to split the money!" | Ask: "What does each person contribute? If one person does more work, should they get more money?" Guide them to justification. |
| "My partner wants to do all the fun jobs!" | Discuss: "How can you split jobs so both people do some fun and some hard work?" |
| "50/50 is the only fair way!" | Challenge with scenario: "What if Partner A works 10 hours and Partner B works 2 hours? Is 50/50 still fair?" |
| One partner dominates negotiation | Intervene: "Partner B, what do YOU think about this plan?" Ensure both voices are heard. |
| Students finish too quickly without thinking | Add "lawyer review" requirement — another pair must approve their agreement as fair and clear. |
| "What if we can't think of a business?" | Provide idea cards: lemonade stand, lawn mowing, dog walking, craft sales, tutoring, car washing. |
| Students make joke agreements | Guide back to reality: "Real partnerships depend on these agreements. What would really happen if these were the rules?" |

**Time Management:**
- Negotiation always takes longer than expected — build in buffer time
- For 20-min version, consider pre-assigning business types to speed up planning
- Set timer for each phase and give 2-minute warning
- Have extension activity ready for pairs who finish early (design business logo, create sample sales scenario)
- If running long, pause after negotiation and complete writing the next day

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pair students strategically: confident negotiators with quieter students (but ensure quiet student isn't steamrolled)
- Provide sentence frames for struggling writers: "I will ____ because ____."
- Allow advanced students to create 3-way partnerships with more complex agreements
- Let students who need movement negotiate while standing or walking
- Offer visual templates with icons for students who struggle with text-heavy documents

## SAFETY NOTES

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Fair Partnerships:** Monitor negotiations to ensure no bullying or pressure. Both partners should genuinely agree, not just give in.
- **Conflict Support:** Be ready to mediate if partners genuinely can't agree. Help them find compromise or, if necessary, allow them to dissolve the partnership and find new partners.
- **No Forced Partnerships:** If a student is very uncomfortable with their assigned partner, allow re-pairing when possible.
- **Respect Differences:** Emphasize that different profit splits can all be fair as long as both partners agree. Prevent judgment ("That's not fair!" when it actually is based on different contributions).

**Social Dynamics:**
- **Inclusion:** Ensure no student is left without a partner. Teacher can partner with odd-student-out if necessary.
- **Equity:** Watch for partnerships where one student does all the thinking/writing while the other just agrees. Encourage active participation from both.
- **Friendship Dynamics:** Be aware that best friends might have trouble negotiating objectively; sometimes strategic pairing works better than friendship pairing.

**General:**
- Clear expectations about respectful negotiation language
- Teacher approval before finalizing agreements (check for fairness and clarity)
- Option to revise agreements if students realize something is missing
- Privacy for partnership terms if students request it

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| "We both want to be the boss!" | Neither wants to share control | Discuss: partnerships mean SHARED decisions; include decision-making clause in agreement |
| Partners can't agree on profit split | Different ideas of fairness; one wants more | Ask: "What does each person contribute? Time? Skills? Money? Materials?" Help them justify with evidence |
| One student does all the work while partner watches | Unequal engagement; shyness or dominance | Intervene: "Partner B, what job do YOU want to do?" Ensure both contribute actively |
| "50/50 is the ONLY fair way" (inflexibility) | Oversimplified understanding of fairness | Present scenario: "If one person works 1 hour and the other works 10 hours, is 50/50 fair?" Expand thinking |
| Agreement is too vague ("we'll figure it out later") | Avoiding difficult decisions | Require specifics: "Exactly how much? Exactly who? Exactly when?" Push for clarity |
| Best friends make unrealistic agreement | Friendship clouds business judgment | Encourage: "What if you weren't best friends? Would this still be fair?" Reality-check the terms |
| Students want to change agreement after signing | Buyer's remorse; new information | Teachable moment: "Contracts can be changed if BOTH partners agree. Negotiate an amendment." |

## EXTENSIONS & VARIATIONS

**For Early Finishers:**
1. Design a business logo for their partnership
2. Create a sample sales scenario and calculate profit split with real numbers
3. Write a "Partnership Handbook" for younger students
4. Role-play a partnership dispute and resolve it using their agreement
5. Research one famous business partnership and present findings

**For Advanced Learners:**
1. Create three-way or four-way partnership with complex role and profit assignments
2. Research different business structures (LLC, LLP, sole proprietorship, corporation) and compare to partnerships
3. Calculate profit splits with multiple scenarios (variable revenue, expenses, time commitment changes)
4. Write partnership "operating agreement" with quarterly review clauses
5. Present to class: "What Makes Partnerships Succeed or Fail" with examples
6. Create partnership dispute mediation service for the class
7. Design a "Partnership Finder" matching system based on complementary skills

**For Additional Days/Sessions:**
- **Day 2:** Role-play partnership scenarios (disputes, successes, growth) using the written agreements
- **Week 2:** Revisit agreements after one week — do partners want to revise anything?
- **Extension:** Actually implement a class partnership business (for real school store, event, etc.)
- **Long-term:** Track partnership "businesses" over a month with revenue/profit logs

**Seasonal Variations:**
- **Fall:** Partnership for school fundraiser or fall festival booth
- **Winter:** Partnership for holiday market or gift-making business
- **Spring:** Partnership for school garden or outdoor cleanup service
- **Summer:** Partnership for summer business ideas (lemonade, yard work, pet sitting)

**Benny (Enthusiastic/Social):**

- Students experience Benny's pride when his negotiation succeeds
