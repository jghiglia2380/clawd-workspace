# CHAPTER 6: HIDDEN COSTS - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** True cost accounting and overhead expenses
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where friends discover costs beyond materials — tools wear out, electricity costs money, and buying in bulk saves over time. Real profit requires counting ALL costs.
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"True Cost Detective: Finding Hidden Expenses"**

- **Scenes 3-4:** Riley discovers empty glaze jar and learns bulk buying saves money → Activity demonstrates bulk purchasing economics

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand that true business costs include more than just materials
2. **Overhead Concepts:** Learn what overhead means (rent, utilities, equipment, wear and tear)
3. **Bulk Purchasing:** Discover that buying larger quantities often costs less per unit
4. **Depreciation:** Grasp that tools and equipment wear out and need replacement
5. **True Profit Calculation:** Calculate real profit after accounting for ALL costs
6. **Budget Planning:** Learn to set aside reserve funds for predictable future expenses
7. **Critical Thinking:** Develop detective skills to find costs others might miss

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Hidden Cost Hunt Challenge

**Materials Needed:**
- Picture cards or real items (cookies, lemonade cup, handmade bracelet)
- Whiteboard or large paper
- Markers

**Steps:**
1. **Show Product (2 min):** Hold up a cookie (or picture). "This cookie costs 50¢ for ingredients. What ELSE costs money to make it?"
2. **Brainstorm Hunt (4 min):** Students call out hidden costs. Teacher writes on board: oven/electricity, time, spoon to mix, cleanup, plate, broken cookies (waste).
3. **Count Hidden Costs (2 min):** Count how many costs we found. "We found 6 hidden costs beyond the 50¢ ingredients!"
4. **True Price Discussion (2 min):** "If ingredients cost 50¢ and we found 6 other costs, should we sell for 50¢? Or more?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What hidden costs did we find?"
- "Why do things cost more than just the materials?"
- "Which hidden cost surprised you most?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** True Cost Detective — Product Investigation

**Materials Needed:**
- 3-4 simple products (real or pictures): cookie, paper airplane, friendship bracelet, lemonade
- "Hidden Cost Detective" worksheet (printed or paper divided into sections)
- Pencils, calculators (optional)
- Cost cards (pre-made or written on board)

**Steps:**
1. **Detective Training (3 min):** Explain detective job: find ALL costs, not just obvious ones. Categories: Materials, Tools/Equipment, Energy/Utilities, Time, Waste, Space.
2. **Product Investigation (8 min):** In pairs, choose one product. Use worksheet to hunt for costs in each category. "For a cookie: Materials = flour, sugar. Tools = bowl, spoon, oven. Energy = electricity for oven. Time = 30 minutes. Waste = broken cookies. Space = kitchen."
3. **Cost Reveal (5 min):** Each pair shares their findings. Teacher records on board. Count total costs found per product.
4. **True Cost Calculation (3 min):** Using simple numbers, calculate: "Cookie materials = 50¢. Hidden costs = 50¢ more. True cost = $1. What price should we charge to make profit?"
5. **Reflection (1 min):** "Did you find more hidden costs than you expected?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What's the difference between material cost and true cost?"
- "Which category had the most hidden costs?"
- "Why do businesses need to count ALL costs, not just materials?"
- "What happens if you only count material costs when setting your price?"

**Extension:** Create a "True Cost Report Card" rating how many hidden costs each product has (1-5 stars).

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete True Cost Investigation with Bulk Buying Analysis

**Materials Needed:**
- Product cards (6-8 different items)
- "True Cost Detective Report" worksheets (detailed template)
- Calculators
- Bulk buying comparison cards (small pack vs. large pack with prices)
- Play money for demonstration
- PROFIT jar and COSTS jar (labeled containers)
- Chart paper for class overhead tracking

**Steps:**
1. **Case Introduction (5 min):**
   - Present the mystery: "A bakery sells cupcakes for $2. They lose money on every sale. Why?"
   - Students guess, then reveal: they only counted flour and sugar ($1), not oven electricity, baker's time, broken cupcakes, boxes, etc.
   - Total hidden costs = $1.50. True cost = $2.50. Selling at $2 = losing 50¢ per cupcake!

2. **Detective Investigation (12 min):**
   - Teams of 3-4 choose a product or business scenario
   - Use detailed worksheet with cost categories:
     * **Materials:** Direct product materials
     * **Tools & Equipment:** Items that wear out (saw blades, mixing bowls, craft scissors)
     * **Utilities:** Electricity, water, heat
     * **Space:** Rent or space usage
     * **Labor:** Time spent (even if not "paid" yet)
     * **Waste:** Mistakes, broken items, extra materials
     * **Packaging/Supplies:** Bags, boxes, labels, tape
   - Document EVERY cost found with estimated dollar amounts
   - Calculate material cost vs. true cost

3. **Bulk Buying Analysis (8 min):**
   - Present scenario cards: "Small glaze jar = $18 (makes 10 mugs). Large jar = $45 (makes 30 mugs)."
   - Students calculate cost per unit:
     * Small: $18 ÷ 10 = $1.80 per mug
     * Large: $45 ÷ 30 = $1.50 per mug
     * Savings: 30¢ per mug by buying bulk
   - Try 2-3 different bulk buying scenarios
   - Discuss: "When does buying bigger make sense? When doesn't it?"

4. **Profit Recalculation Demo (6 min):**
   - Set up PROFIT jar with play money ($217 like in the story)
   - List hidden costs found (saw blade $6, glaze savings lost $12, electricity $22, etc.)
   - Move money from PROFIT to COSTS jar as you list each hidden cost
   - Final profit = $155 (like in story)
   - Discuss: "Did we lose money? No! We just learned the TRUTH about our profit."

5. **Creating Overhead Tracking System (3 min):**
   - Class creates "Costs That Don't Go in Products" chart (like Layla's notebook)
   - Categories: Tools/Equipment, Space/Utilities, Bulk Savings Opportunities, Other Overhead
   - Each team adds their findings to class chart

6. **Pricing Adjustment Exercise (1 min):**
   - If material cost is $10 and overhead is 15%, new price calculation:
   - $10 × 1.15 = $11.50 (adding 15% overhead)
   - Practice with 2-3 examples

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What's the difference between overhead and material costs?"
- "Why do we need a RESERVE jar for future tool replacement?"
- "How does bulk buying change your true cost over time?"
- "What happens to businesses that don't count hidden costs?"
- "Is it 'bad' to discover hidden costs? Or is it helpful to know the truth?"
- "How much overhead should we add to our prices? 10%? 20%? How do we decide?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Track a week of classroom hidden costs (markers that dry out, paper waste, electricity for lights)
- Research real business overhead percentages by industry (restaurants 30-40%, retail 20-30%)
- Create a "Reserve Fund Calculator" for different business scenarios
- Visit a local business and interview owner about their overhead costs
- Calculate true cost of a school fundraiser (bake sale, car wash, etc.)

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Focus on concrete, visible costs only (spoon, bowl, oven)
- Simple counting: "How many things do we need to make this?"
- Visual learning: show real items that cost money
- Basic vocabulary: cost, price, need, money, tools
- One-step thinking: "What else do we need?"

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- Add less visible costs (electricity, time)
- Simple categorization: materials vs. tools vs. energy
- Basic addition: count total number of costs
- Introduce "hidden" concept: things you don't see right away
- Vocabulary: hidden costs, tools, electricity, bulk buying

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- Include abstract costs (wear and tear, wasted materials)
- Multi-category sorting: 4-5 cost categories
- Simple calculation: add up estimated costs, compare prices
- Understanding that costs exist even if you don't pay them directly (using someone else's workshop)
- Vocabulary: overhead, waste, depreciation, reserve, true cost

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- Full overhead analysis including proportional cost allocation
- Complex calculations: bulk buying unit cost comparisons, percentage additions for overhead
- Understanding opportunity cost and capital allocation (money tied up in bulk inventory)
- Grasping that "profit" needs redefinition when hidden costs are discovered
- Advanced vocabulary: depreciation, reserve fund, overhead percentage, per-unit cost, capital allocation, economies of scale

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual cost category cards with pictures (lightbulb for electricity, tools icon, etc.)
- Bilingual vocabulary: overhead/gastos generales, depreciation/depreciación, bulk buying/compra al por mayor
- Sentence frames: "This product needs ___. That costs ___."
- Partner with fluent speaker for investigation work
- Use real objects whenever possible instead of abstract discussion

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Simplified cost categories (3 instead of 7)
- Visual checklist with pictures for each cost type
- Pre-filled examples to start the investigation
- Calculator always available for any math
- Focus on finding costs (detective work) rather than calculating them
- Option to dictate findings instead of writing

**For Advanced Students:**
- Lead class investigation, teaching others about cost categories
- Research real business overhead percentages and compare to our findings
- Create "Overhead Calculator Tool" for different business types
- Investigate economies of scale: why do big companies pay less per unit?
- Write persuasive essay: "Why Hidden Costs Are Actually Good to Find"
- Calculate break-even point when overhead is included

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 6 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **Overhead Costs**
   - Core Lesson: Businesses have costs beyond materials — rent, utilities, equipment, supplies — that must be covered
   - Activity Connection: Students hunt for and categorize overhead costs across multiple cost categories
   - Age-Appropriate: K-1 identify that electricity and tools cost money; 3-4 can calculate overhead percentages and allocate them to pricing

2. **Depreciation**
   - Core Lesson: Tools and equipment wear out through normal use and eventually need replacement
   - Activity Connection: Saw blade breaking becomes example of depreciation requiring reserve funds
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete experience of "things wear out" (all ages) progressing to depreciation as a calculable business cost (3-4)

3. **Bulk Purchasing Economics**
   - Core Lesson: Buying larger quantities often reduces per-unit cost, saving money over time despite higher upfront cost
   - Activity Connection: Calculate and compare small vs. large package costs per unit across multiple scenarios
   - Age-Appropriate: Simple "big box has more" (K-1) to per-unit cost division and savings percentage calculations (3-4)

4. **True Cost vs. Material Cost**
   - Core Lesson: The full cost of producing something includes materials PLUS all overhead, not just the ingredients
   - Activity Connection: Detective investigation reveals 5-10 costs beyond materials for any product
   - Age-Appropriate: Experiential discovery that "there's more than we thought" (all ages) to precise true cost calculation (3-4)

5. **Reserve Funds**
   - Core Lesson: Setting money aside for predictable future expenses (tool replacement) prevents crises
   - Activity Connection: Creating RESERVE jar and calculating how much to set aside
   - Age-Appropriate: "Saving for later" concept (K-1) to calculating reserve percentages based on depreciation rates (3-4)

6. **Honest Profit Calculation**
   - Core Lesson: Real profit is revenue minus ALL costs, not just the ones you remembered to count
   - Activity Connection: Recalculating profit after discovering hidden costs (moving money from PROFIT to COSTS jar)
   - Age-Appropriate: "The number changed when we learned more" (K-1) to understanding why accurate accounting matters for business decisions (3-4)

**Non-Moralistic Approach:**
- Activity presents cost discovery as helpful detective work, not shame for "missing" something
- No judgment language: "You did it wrong" ❌ Instead: "Look what we discovered!" ✓
- Celebrates students who find MORE costs (good detectives) rather than shaming initial low counts
- Acknowledges that ALL businesses miss costs at first — it's part of learning
- Focuses on empowerment through knowledge: knowing true costs helps you make better decisions
- Frames recalculating profit as "now we know the truth" rather than "we were wrong"

**Connection to Season Arc:**
- Chapter 6 builds on CH1-5's business operations by adding financial accuracy
- Directly sets up CH7 (Too Much Work — will they have budget for help now that overhead is counted?)
- Connects to CH10 (Seasonal Rush) inventory planning with bulk buying knowledge
- Reinforces CH2's business planning: a good plan includes ALL costs
- Establishes financial discipline that carries through to CH12's Big Picture reflection

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Decide which product(s) students will investigate (keep it simple for younger grades)
- Prepare cost category examples ahead of time for scaffolding
- If using real products, gather samples or clear pictures
- Create visual aids for cost categories (icons help visual learners)
- Prepare bulk buying scenario cards with realistic price comparisons
- Have jars or containers ready for PROFIT/COSTS demonstration

**Classroom Management:**
- Start with concrete, visible costs before moving to abstract ones (electricity, depreciation)
- Use think-pair-share for cost brainstorming to engage all students
- Celebrate every cost found — make it a treasure hunt, not a test
- For younger students, work through one product together as a class before independent investigation
- Use timer for investigation period to maintain focus and energy
- Prepare for "But that's not fair!" reactions when profit jar shrinks — use as teaching moment about honesty vs. wishful thinking

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "We can't find any more hidden costs!" | Give category prompts: "What about energy? What about the room/space? What about mistakes?" |
| "This is too hard / too much math" | Simplify to counting costs rather than calculating dollar amounts; use calculator; pair struggling students with confident math students |
| "Why does the profit go down? That's sad." | Reframe: "Profit didn't actually go down — we just learned the TRUTH. Would you rather think you made $217 but be wrong, or know you really made $155?" Truth is power. |
| Confusion between materials and overhead | Use clear examples: "Materials go INTO the product and become part of it. Overhead helps MAKE the product but doesn't become part of it." |
| "Bulk buying costs MORE money!" (upfront cost confusion) | Visual demonstration: show small pack ($8 for 10 = 80¢ each) vs. large pack ($15 for 50 = 30¢ each). Focus on "cost for ONE thing" not total price. |
| Students list irrelevant "costs" | Gently redirect: "Does that help make the product? Does it get used up or wear out? If not, it might not be a business cost." |
| Overwhelm from too many cost categories | Start with 3 categories (Materials, Tools, Energy) and add more only if students are ready |
| Different teams find very different numbers of costs | Celebrate highest finding: "This team found 12 costs! Let's hear what they discovered that we might have missed." Encourage learning from each other. |

**Time Management:**
- Investigation period always takes longer than expected — build in buffer time
- For 20-min version, focus on brainstorming only, skip calculations
- If running long, pause and continue calculations the next day
- Set timer for investigation rounds to keep momentum
- Have extension ready for fast finishers (investigate a second product)

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Provide pre-filled examples for students who need scaffolding
- Pair strong readers with emerging readers for scenario card activities
- Allow drawing instead of writing for cost documentation (K-1)
- Provide calculators freely — this is about concepts, not computation practice
- Let advanced students research real business overhead percentages and compare to our findings
- For students who finish early: "Can you find 3 MORE hidden costs everyone else missed?"

**Physical Safety:**
- If using real products for investigation, ensure no allergens for students (food items)
- Calculators: check battery compartments are secure (choking hazard for K-1)
- Paper cuts: supervise younger students with worksheets and cost cards
- If demonstrating real tools (to show depreciation), keep students at safe distance, handle tools yourself

**Emotional Safety:**
- Frame cost discovery as POSITIVE detective work, not failure to think ahead
- When profit jar shrinks in demonstration, emphasize "we didn't lose money — we learned the truth"
- Validate that finding hidden costs is hard and that even adults/real businesses miss costs initially
- Celebrate finding MORE costs rather than shaming low initial counts
- Normalize recalculating when new information appears — sign of intelligence, not failure

**Conceptual Safety:**
- Avoid shaming families with limited resources: "Some families buy small packages because they can't afford the big one upfront, even if it costs more over time. That's a real constraint."
- Don't present business decisions as "right vs. wrong" but as "informed by complete information vs. incomplete information"
- Acknowledge that sometimes hidden costs CAN'T be avoided, even when you know about them

**General:**
- Supervise calculator use to ensure students are calculating, not playing
- Monitor group dynamics during team investigations — ensure all voices heard
- Be prepared for math anxiety during calculation portions — have tools and support ready
- Watch for frustration when students can't find "enough" costs — provide category prompts

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| "I can't think of any more costs!" | Limited schema for business operations; haven't thought about invisible costs | Provide category prompts (Tools? Energy? Space? Waste?); show examples from story (electricity, saw blade); give time to observe real product being made |
| Confusion between materials and overhead | Both are "costs" but serve different functions | Clear definition with examples: "Materials become PART of the product (flour in cookie). Overhead HELPS make product but doesn't become part of it (oven, bowl)." Visual sorting activity. |
| "Bulk buying costs more money!" (missing per-unit concept) | Focusing on total price ($45) instead of cost per item ($1.50) | Visual demonstration: physically separate large pack into units. "50 sheets cost $15 total. But ONE sheet costs 30¢. 10 sheets cost $8 total. But ONE sheet costs 80¢. Which ONE costs less?" |
| Sadness/frustration when profit jar shrinks | Emotional attachment to higher number; feels like losing money | Reframe as GAINING truth: "Would you rather THINK you have $217 but be wrong? Or KNOW you really have $155 and make good decisions?" Truth is power, not punishment. |
| Wildly inaccurate cost estimates | No experience with actual prices; developmental stage | Provide realistic price ranges or examples; focus on finding costs (detective work) rather than estimating exact amounts; use "low/medium/high" instead of dollars |
| "This is too much math / too hard!" | Calculation anxiety; skill gaps | Provide calculators freely; allow partner work; simplify to counting costs instead of calculating amounts; offer pre-calculated scenarios; focus on concepts over computation |
| Listing irrelevant costs | Overgeneralizing "everything costs money" | Gently redirect: "That's true, but does it help make THIS product? Is it used up or worn out by making THIS product?" Keep focus narrow. |
| Different teams find very different totals | Different thoroughness levels; different interpretations | Celebrate as learning opportunity: "Team A found 6 costs. Team B found 11! What did Team B find that we missed?" Share discoveries. Learning from peers. |
| "My family can't afford bulk buying" (defensive reaction) | Sensitivity about family financial constraints | Acknowledge constraints are real: "Bulk buying saves money OVER TIME, but requires more money UPFRONT. Not everyone can do that, and that's a real challenge." Validate without judgment. |

## EXTENSIONS & VARIATIONS

**For Early Finishers:**
1. Investigate a second, more complex product
2. Create visual poster: "All the Costs to Make a ___" with drawings and labels
3. Research: find real business overhead percentages online (with teacher guidance)
4. Design an overhead tracking system for our classroom
5. Calculate: if our class were a business, what would our overhead be? (electricity for lights, teacher time, materials)

**For Advanced Learners:**
1. Research industry-specific overhead percentages (restaurant vs. retail vs. manufacturing)
2. Create "True Cost Calculator" tool for different business types
3. Investigate economies of scale: why do large companies pay less per unit than small businesses?
4. Write persuasive essay: "Why Finding Hidden Costs Helps Businesses Succeed"
5. Interview local business owner about their overhead costs and create presentation
6. Calculate break-even point when overhead is included in pricing
7. Design reserve fund formula: how much should businesses save monthly for tool replacement?

**For Additional Days/Sessions:**
