# CHAPTER 11: THE SLOW SEASON - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Managing slow periods in business
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where the team faces January's slow season after December's rush and learns to use downtime strategically — improving workspace, building inventory, strengthening relationships, and preparing for future opportunities
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Slow Season Strategy: What Do Smart Businesses Do?"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-3:** Order board nearly empty, revenue drops to $18/week, team worries business is over → Activity explores recognizing and responding to slow periods
- **Scene 4:** Layla reveals the RESERVE jar with $73 saved from December's rush → Activity includes emergency fund planning and use
- **Scenes 5-6:** Frances shows them her garden — roots growing underground, preparing for spring → Activity connects to preparing during downtime
- **Scenes 7-9:** Team uses slow time productively — Ellis builds better workbench, Layla invests in packaging, Riley makes inventory → Activity brainstorms slow season strategies
- **Scenes 10-13:** Benny seeks customers, café relationship sustains them, slow progress continues → Activity includes relationship-building and steady customer importance
- **Scenes 14-17:** Ms. Rivera calls with $180 wedding order because Mrs. Torres recommended them → Activity demonstrates how good relationships during busy times pay off during slow times
- **Scenes 18-20:** Team uses slow time to plan wedding order carefully, reserve fund works, spring is coming → Activity concludes with reflection on strategic waiting and preparation

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand that businesses have cycles — busy seasons and slow seasons are both normal
2. **Strategic Thinking:** Learn to use slow periods productively rather than panicking or giving up
3. **Resource Management:** Practice budgeting reserves for slow times and investing in future capacity
4. **Relationship Building:** Recognize that customer relationships during good times sustain business during slow times
5. **Long-Term Planning:** Develop patience and strategic thinking about business cycles
6. **Problem-Solving:** Generate creative solutions for maintaining business during low-demand periods

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Slow Season Brainstorm Circle

**Materials Needed:**
- Large paper or whiteboard
- Markers
- Index cards or small paper (1 per student)

**Steps:**
1. **Problem Setup (2 min):** "Imagine you run a lemonade stand. In summer you sell 20 cups a day. In winter you sell 2 cups a day. What do you do during winter?"
2. **Individual Brainstorm (2 min):** Each student writes or draws ONE thing they could do during slow season (practice making lemonade, design new signs, learn new flavors, save money, find new customers).
3. **Share & Sort (4 min):** Students share ideas. Teacher writes them on board in categories: PREPARE (get ready for busy time), IMPROVE (make business better), LEARN (get better at skills), CONNECT (talk to customers).
4. **Best Idea Vote (1 min):** Class votes on which strategy they think would help most.
5. **Connection (1 min):** "In the story, what did the team do during slow January? Ellis built a workbench, Riley made inventory, Layla bought packaging. They PREPARED for spring!"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Is slow season bad or just different?"
- "What's one thing you can do when you have extra time?"
- "Should you give up when business is slow? Why not?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Slow Season Strategy Planner

**Materials Needed:**
- "Slow Season Strategy Planner" worksheets (template with 4 sections)
- Pencils, crayons, markers
- Optional: example pictures of businesses in slow vs. busy seasons

**Steps:**
1. **Scenario Setup (3 min):** Present 3 business scenarios: ice cream shop in winter, swim instructor in November, holiday decorator in February. "What happens to these businesses when their busy season ends?"
2. **Strategy Brainstorm (7 min):** In pairs or small groups, students complete "Slow Season Strategy Planner" with 4 sections:
   - **PREPARE:** What can you make or plan for busy season? (like Riley making spring pottery)
   - **IMPROVE:** What can you fix or make better? (like Ellis building workbench)
   - **LEARN:** What skills can you practice? (like trying new recipes)
   - **CONNECT:** Who can you talk to about future business? (like Benny putting up signs)
3. **Share Strategies (5 min):** Each group shares their best idea from each category. Class discusses which strategies would help most.
4. **Reserve Fund Discussion (3 min):** "In the story, Layla had a RESERVE jar with $73. Why did they save money during busy December? How did it help in slow January?" Introduce concept of emergency fund.
5. **Reflection (2 min):** "Slow time isn't wasted time if you use it to get ready. What will YOU do the next time you have 'slow' time?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Why do some businesses have slow seasons?"
- "Is it smart to give up during slow times? What should you do instead?"
- "How does saving money during good times help during slow times?"
- "What did Frances's garden teach the team about slow seasons?"
- "How did being nice to Mrs. Torres help the team in January?"

**Extension:** Create a class "Slow Season Strategies" poster with all the best ideas from each group.

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Slow Season Survival Simulation

**Materials Needed:**
- "Business Scenario" cards (6-8 different slow season scenarios)
- "Slow Season Action Plan" worksheets (detailed template)
- Play money for budgeting activity
- Research materials (printed examples of real businesses' slow season strategies)
- Chart paper and markers for presentations
- Timer

**Steps:**
1. **Real-World Research (5 min):**
   - Show examples of real businesses with seasonal cycles: ski resorts in summer, farmers in winter, tax accountants in August, school photographers in December
   - Discuss: "What do these businesses do during slow times?"
   - Quick research share: students contribute ideas they know about (maybe ice cream shops offer hot chocolate in winter, or landscapers plow snow)

2. **Scenario Assignment (2 min):**
   - Divide class into teams of 3-4
   - Each team receives a "Business Scenario" card:
     * Beach equipment rental shop in October-March (6 slow months)
     * Wedding photographer in January-February (2 slow months)
     * School supply store in June-July (2 slow months)
     * Pool cleaning service in November-February (4 slow months)
     * Pumpkin farm in January-August (8 slow months)
     * Snow removal service in May-October (6 slow months)
   - Each scenario includes: what they sell, busy season revenue, slow season revenue, money saved, upcoming expenses

3. **Strategy Development (12 min):**
   Teams complete comprehensive "Slow Season Action Plan":

   **PART 1: Understanding the Problem**
   - Current situation (how much money coming in now vs. busy season?)
   - How long will slow season last?
   - Do we have reserve funds? How much?

   **PART 2: Survival Strategies**
   - What can we PREPARE for next busy season? (build inventory, make products, plan marketing)
   - What can we IMPROVE right now? (fix equipment, reorganize, train skills)
   - What can we LEARN to get better? (take classes, practice, research)
   - What RELATIONSHIPS can we strengthen? (call past customers, thank people, ask for referrals)
   - Can we offer a DIFFERENT product for this season? (pivot: pool company offers hot tub service, pumpkin farm hosts winter events)

   **PART 3: Budget Planning**
   - Use reserve funds: Yes or No? How much?
   - Cut expenses: What can wait?
   - Investments worth making: What will help later?

4. **Presentation Round (10 min):**
   - Each team presents 2-minute "Slow Season Survival Plan" to class
   - Must include: their business, their main challenge, their top 3 strategies, and budget plan
   - Class asks one question per presentation

5. **Comparison to Story (3 min):**
   - "What strategies did the Maplewood Makers team use?"
   - Ellis: IMPROVED workspace (better workbench)
   - Layla: INVESTED in future (better packaging)
   - Riley: PREPARED inventory (spring pottery)
   - Benny: CONNECTED with customers (signs, calls, outreach)
   - Team: USED RESERVES ($73 saved from December)
   - Result: Ms. Rivera's $180 wedding order came because they MAINTAINED RELATIONSHIPS (Mrs. Torres recommended them)

6. **Real-World Connection & Reflection (3 min):**
   - Create class anchor chart: "Smart Slow Season Strategies"
   - Personal reflection: "When is YOUR slow season? (Summer break from school? Rainy days when you can't play outside?) What can you prepare or improve during that time?"
   - Financial literacy connection: "This is why businesses save money during good times — to survive slow times and invest in getting better"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What's the difference between a failing business and a business in slow season?"
- "Why is giving up during slow season a bad idea?"
- "How can slow times actually be GOOD for a business?"
- "What does 'the roots are growing underground' mean for business?" (Frances's garden metaphor)
- "Why did Ms. Rivera call? How did something the team did in November help them in January?" (relationship building)
- "What's an emergency fund and why do businesses need one?"
- "Can you think of businesses in our community that have slow seasons? What do they do?"
- "How is 'slow season' different from 'no one wants your product'?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Research actual businesses in your community and interview owners about their slow seasons
- Track a year-long calendar of business cycles (when are different businesses busy/slow)
- Create a "Slow Season Survival Guide" booklet for young entrepreneurs
- Calculate: if you make $200 in December and $20 in January, how much should you save from December to survive January?
- Compare to personal finances: "Sometimes your family has busy expensive months (back to school, holidays) and quiet months. Why might families save during good months?"

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Very simple scenarios: "Your lemonade stand is busy in summer, slow in winter. What do you do?"
- Draw pictures instead of writing plans
- Focus on one or two strategies: "Practice" and "Get ready"
- Use concrete examples they understand (playground busy at recess, empty during class)
- Teacher heavily scaffolds brainstorming
- Vocabulary: slow, busy, wait, ready, practice

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- Simple scenarios with clear busy/slow distinction
- Fill-in-the-blank strategy worksheets: "During slow time I can ___."
- 2-3 strategy categories: Prepare, Practice, Save Money
- Pair work with teacher check-ins
- Vocabulary: season, prepare, customer, save, emergency fund

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- More detailed scenarios with financial numbers
- Four strategy categories: Prepare, Improve, Learn, Connect
- Introduction to budget planning (should we spend reserve money?)
- Small group collaboration with roles (recorder, presenter, artist)
- Vocabulary: budget, reserve fund, relationship, investment, strategic

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- Complex scenarios requiring math and multi-step planning
- Full action plan with budget, timeline, and prioritization
- Understanding business cycles and how to predict them
- Research real businesses and how they handle slow seasons
- Cost-benefit analysis: "This improvement costs $50 but will earn $200 next season"
- Advanced vocabulary: business cycle, seasonal variation, capital investment, customer retention, cash flow, forecasting

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual scenario cards with pictures of businesses in different seasons
- Bilingual vocabulary: slow season/temporada lenta, busy season/temporada alta, prepare/preparar, save/ahorrar
- Sentence frames: "During slow time we can ___." "We should save money for ___."
- Partner with fluent speaker for brainstorming and presentation
- Use gestures and visuals to represent concepts (full hands = busy, empty hands = slow)

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Simplified choice boards: pick 3 strategies from a list of 6 with pictures
- Visual timelines showing busy and slow months
- Concrete manipulatives: use blocks to represent money saved/spent across months
- Reduced writing: use checkboxes, circles, or drawings instead of paragraphs
- Option to present verbally instead of written report
- Teacher support during group work to ensure participation

**For Advanced Students:**
- Research actual businesses in community and interview owners about slow season strategies
- Create detailed financial models: if busy season earns $X and slow season earns $Y, how much reserve needed?
- Design comprehensive year-long business plans accounting for seasonal variation
- Compare multiple industries: which has longest slow season? Shortest? Why?
- Write persuasive proposal: "Why our business should invest $500 during slow season"
- Create visual presentations or infographics about business cycles

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 11 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **Business Cycles Are Normal**
   - Core Lesson: All businesses have busy times and slow times — this is not failure, it's a natural pattern
   - Activity Connection: Students analyze different businesses and see seasonal patterns are predictable and normal
   - Age-Appropriate: K-1 understand "busy vs quiet times"; 3-4 can analyze cycles and predict patterns

2. **Emergency Funds/Reserves Sustain You Through Hard Times**
   - Core Lesson: Saving money during good times allows survival during slow times without panic
   - Activity Connection: Layla's RESERVE jar with $73 saved in December helps team survive $18 week in January
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete example of "save when you can, use when you need"

3. **Use Downtime Strategically**
   - Core Lesson: Slow periods are opportunities to improve, prepare, learn, and build — not wasted time
   - Activity Connection: Ellis improves workspace, Riley builds inventory, Benny seeks customers, team plans
   - Age-Appropriate: Directly applicable to students' lives (rainy day is chance to plan, not just boring)

4. **Relationships Are Business Assets**
   - Core Lesson: Being good to customers during busy times pays dividends during slow times through referrals
   - Activity Connection: Mrs. Torres (from earlier) recommends team to Ms. Rivera, generating $180 wedding order
   - Age-Appropriate: Clear cause-effect: be kind and helpful → people remember → they help you later

5. **Investment vs. Expense**
   - Core Lesson: Spending money during slow times can be smart if it prepares you for busy times
   - Activity Connection: Layla spends $12 on better packaging — not generating immediate revenue but will increase future value
   - Age-Appropriate: 3-4 can understand "spend money now to make more later"

**Non-Moralistic Approach:**
- Activity presents slow seasons as natural business reality, not punishment or failure
- No shame language: "You should have prepared better" ❌ Instead: "Slow times are normal. Let's plan smart strategies" ✓
- Acknowledges that sometimes external factors (holidays, weather, economy) affect business beyond control
- Focuses on problem-solving and strategic thinking, not character judgments about preparation
- Celebrates creativity and resilience, not "perfect planning"

**Connection to Season Arc:**
- Follows CH10 (The Seasonal Rush) directly — after busy December comes slow January
- Demonstrates complete business cycle: preparation → launch → challenges → growth → rush → slow → prepare again
- Sets up CH12 (The Big Picture) where team reflects on full year of business including all cycles
- Shows maturity of team: they don't panic, they strategize (growth from CH1 solo panic)

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Read Chapter 11 story ahead of time to understand all slow season strategies team uses
- Prepare scenario cards in advance with clear financial details
- Have real-world examples ready (local businesses, familiar examples kids will relate to)
- Consider inviting a local business owner to talk about their slow season (optional)
- Create visual examples of business cycles (graph showing revenue across 12 months)

**Classroom Management:**
- During brainstorming, encourage ALL ideas — no strategy is "wrong"
- Use timer to keep activity moving (brainstorm: 5 min, planning: 10 min, presentations: 2 min each)
- For team work, assign roles: Strategy Leader, Budget Manager, Presenter, Recorder
- Have extension activities ready for groups that finish early
- Prepare for: students who want to argue "just lower prices" (great teaching moment about race to bottom), students who think slow season means business failed (clarify difference)

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "Why don't they just lower prices to get customers?" | Excellent question! Discuss race to bottom, costs still exist, and that sometimes demand just isn't there. Connect to story: even with good prices, people spent money in December and are careful in January. |
| "Why don't they just close the business and start again later?" | Great thinking! Some businesses DO close seasonally (summer camps, ski resorts). Discuss costs of closing/reopening and value of steady relationships (café kept ordering). |
| Students think slow season = business failure | Clarify distinction: failing business = no one wants product ever; slow season = timing/external factors. Use Frances's garden: "Are the plants dead or resting?" |
| "The team should just work harder to find customers" | Benny tried that (Scene 11)! Sometimes you CAN'T make people buy when they're not ready. That's when you prepare for when they ARE ready. |
| Difficulty understanding reserve fund concept | Use concrete example: "You get $5 allowance per week. Summer (12 weeks) you save $1 each week = $12 saved. Winter you can't do extra chores. But you have $12 saved!" |
| Teams stuck on brainstorming | Provide category prompts: "What could you MAKE? What could you FIX? Who could you CALL? What could you LEARN?" |
| Presentations taking too long | Set strict 2-minute timer, have teams practice and prioritize top 3 strategies only |

**Time Management:**
- 20-min version: keep brainstorm focused on just 1-2 strategies per student
- 30-min version: use timer for each section, have worksheets prepared in advance
- 45-min version: build in buffer time, but if running long, reduce presentation time to 90 sec per team
- Have extension ready for fast finishers: "Research one real business with slow season and write how they handle it"

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pair struggling students with stronger partners for scenario analysis
- Provide modified worksheets with pictures and fill-in-the-blank for emerging readers
- Allow verbal brainstorming for students who struggle with writing
- Give advanced students complex scenarios requiring multi-step financial calculations
- Use visual supports: charts, graphs, pictures for all key concepts
- Provide sentence frames for presentations: "Our business is ___. The slow season is ___. We will ___, ___, and ___ to survive."

**Real-World Connections:**
- "What businesses in our town have slow seasons?" (Ice cream shops, pool stores, landscapers, tourist businesses)
- "When do families have 'slow seasons'?" (After holidays, summer when not much happening, rainy weeks)
- "How do YOU use 'slow times'?" (Rainy days to read, summer break to learn new skills, evenings to prepare for school)
- Show how this concept applies beyond business: athletes train in off-season, farmers prepare fields in winter, teachers plan during breaks

- "What happened to Maplewood Makers in January?"
- "What is a RESERVE jar? Why did Layla save money?"
- "What did Frances's garden teach about slow seasons?"
- "What's the difference between a slow season and a failing business?"
- "What should a business DO during slow times?"
- "How did being nice to Mrs. Torres help the team later?"

***Family Connection Activity:***
Discuss "slow seasons" in your family life:
- When are busy times? (back to school, holidays, sports seasons)
- When are quiet times? (mid-winter, summer break, between activities)
- How does your family prepare during busy times for slower times?
- How do you use slow times to get ready for what's coming next?
- Why might it be smart to save money during busy months?

***Financial Literacy at Home:***
- This is a perfect time to discuss savings accounts with your child
- Explain how families save during good months to prepare for unexpected expenses or slower months
- Share (age-appropriately) how your family's income or expenses might vary across the year
- Practice patience: sometimes the smart choice is to wait and prepare rather than panic and react

*Thank you for supporting your child's financial literacy journey!*

**Family Extension Ideas:**

1. **Seasonal Observation:** Walk around your neighborhood or town and identify businesses that might have slow seasons. Discuss when they're busy and when they're quiet.

2. **Family Budget Talk:** Discuss (age-appropriately) months when your family has more or less expenses. How do you plan for expensive months?

7. **Gratitude During Slow Times:** Practice appreciating quiet times — sometimes slow is restful, not bad. What are the benefits of NOT being busy all the time?

## SAFETY NOTES

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Normalize Struggle:** Emphasize that slow seasons/hard times are NORMAL for all businesses and all people, not signs of failure
- **No Shame:** Some students' families may be experiencing financial difficulty — be sensitive that "slow season" discussions don't cause embarrassment
- **Privacy:** Don't require students to share personal family financial information
- **Supportive Language:** Frame challenges as opportunities for growth and problem-solving, not character flaws
- **Celebrate All Strategies:** Every idea in brainstorming has value — no student should feel their contribution was "wrong"

**General:**
- Supervision during group work to ensure all students participate and are included
- Monitor presentations to ensure respectful listening and constructive feedback only
- Be prepared to support students who may find financial concepts triggering due to family circumstances
- Keep play money secure and ensure all students have equal amounts for budgeting activities
- Ensure all materials are accessible to students with different abilities

**Sensitive Topics:**
- Be aware that some families may be experiencing actual "slow seasons" (job loss, reduced hours, financial stress)
- Frame discussions positively: "challenges can be managed with planning and strategy"
- Avoid language that blames or judges people in difficult financial situations
- If students bring up real family financial stress, redirect to general business examples and speak to student privately if needed

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| "Just lower prices to get customers!" | Logical first thought; kids see price as main variable | Great teaching moment! Discuss: costs still exist, you can't sell below cost forever, sometimes demand just isn't there regardless of price. Connect to story: even with good products and prices, people spent money in December. |
| "The business should just close and reopen later" | Some seasonal businesses DO this | Excellent observation! Discuss when that works (summer camps) vs. when steady presence matters (relationships with café kept them afloat). What are costs of closing/reopening? |
| Students equate slow season with failure | Common misconception | Clearly distinguish: failing business = no one wants your product EVER; slow season = timing/external factors. Use Frances's garden: plants aren't dead, they're preparing. |
| "They should just work harder!" | Achievement-oriented thinking | Benny tried that! Sometimes effort doesn't change external factors (people spent money, winter demand is low). That's when you prepare smarter, not just work harder. |
| Difficulty grasping reserve fund concept | Abstract concept for young kids | Use concrete personal example: allowance/money they receive varies. Save during good weeks, use during lean weeks. Physical demonstration with play money in jars helps. |
| Teams struggle to brainstorm strategies | Need more scaffolding | Provide category prompts: "What could you MAKE ahead? What could you FIX or improve? Who could you TALK to? What could you LEARN?" Show examples from story. |
| One student dominates team planning | Group dynamics issue | Assign specific roles (Strategy Leader, Budget Manager, Recorder, Presenter) so all have responsibility. Rotate roles if doing multiple rounds. |
| "This is boring, nothing's happening" | Slow season feels inactive | Perfect! That's the challenge! The team felt that too. The GAME is: what can you do when "nothing's happening" to prepare for when things ARE happening? Shift from passive waiting to active preparation. |
| Students want immediate action/results | Patience is hard for kids | Frances's garden metaphor helps: roots growing underground, can't see progress but it's happening. Discuss delayed gratification: do hard work now, see results later. |
