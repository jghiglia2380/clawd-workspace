# CHAPTER 8: THE PIVOT - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Business adaptation through data-driven decisions
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where Maplewood Makers discovers their journals aren't selling, and they must pivot their business model to focus on pottery instead
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Pivot Challenge: Adapt Your Business"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-2:** Layla notices revenue is flat at ~$92/week for three weeks → Activity begins with stagnant business scenario
- **Scenes 3-6:** Layla creates a bar chart showing pottery makes the most, journals make almost nothing → Activity includes data analysis and visualization
- **Scenes 7-10:** Team must decide to stop making journals despite Layla's personal attachment → Activity requires making hard business choices
- **Scenes 11-12:** Frances teaches Layla that "cutting helps what's left grow stronger" → Activity emphasizes strategic focus
- **Scenes 13-17:** After pivoting to pottery-only, revenue jumps from $92 to $115 with higher profits → Activity demonstrates pivot success through results
- **Scenes 18-20:** Friends reflect that following the data saved their business when others failed → Activity concludes with data-driven decision validation

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand that businesses must adapt when something isn't working
2. **Data Analysis:** Learn to read bar charts and use data to make decisions
3. **Opportunity Cost:** Recognize that doing one thing means not doing another
4. **Strategic Focus:** Grasp that doing fewer things well beats doing many things poorly
5. **Emotional Intelligence:** Practice making hard choices that conflict with personal preferences
6. **Problem-Solving:** Develop pivot strategies when faced with business challenges

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Quick Pivot Decision Game

**Materials Needed:**
- 1 scenario card per student (printed or teacher reads aloud)
- Scrap paper
- Pencils or crayons

**Steps:**
1. **Introduce Scenario (2 min):** Teacher presents failing business scenario: "Your lemonade stand has no customers because it's raining. Your business is failing. What do you change?"
2. **Individual Think Time (3 min):** Each student draws or writes ONE thing they would change to save the business.
3. **Share Solutions (4 min):** Students share their pivots with a partner. "I would change ___ to ___."
4. **Class Discussion (1 min):** "Which pivots would work? Which wouldn't? Why?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What did you change about the business?"
- "Would that help? Why?"
- "Is it better to quit or to try something new?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Pivot Planning Workshop

**Materials Needed:**
- 3 scenario cards (different failing businesses)
- Chart paper or large paper for each group
- Markers, crayons, colored pencils
- "Before and After" template worksheets

**Steps:**
1. **Form Teams & Assign Scenarios (2 min):** Divide into groups of 3-4. Each group gets a different failing business scenario (ice cream shop in winter, toy store where nobody buys dolls, pizza place with too many menu items nobody orders).
2. **Analyze the Problem (3 min):** Groups identify WHY the business is failing. Write or draw the problem.
3. **Brainstorm Pivot Options (5 min):** What could they change? Product? Location? Target customers? Time of day? Groups list 3-5 possible pivots.
4. **Choose Best Pivot (3 min):** Select ONE pivot and create a "Before → After" chart showing what changes.
5. **Present to Class (5 min):** Each group shares their pivot. Class discusses: "Would this work? Why or why not?"
6. **Vote & Reflection (2 min):** Class votes on most creative pivot. Quick discussion on what makes a good pivot.

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What was wrong with the business before?"
- "What did you change to fix it?"
- "How is pivoting different from quitting?"
- "Can you think of a real business that changed what they sell?"

**Extension:** Create a "Pivot Success Story" bulletin board showing before/after for each scenario.

---

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Business Pivot Simulation

**Materials Needed:**
- Detailed scenario cards with sales data
- Bar chart templates or grid paper
- "Pivot Plan" worksheets (detailed)
- Play money or tokens
- Presentation materials (poster paper, markers)
- Real-world pivot example cards

**Steps:**
1. **Real-World Pivot Examples (5 min):**
   - Teacher shares real business pivots: Play-Doh was wallpaper cleaner → toy; Netflix mailed DVDs → streaming; restaurant that only sold breakfast (failing) → added lunch and dinner (success)
   - Discussion: "What did they change? Why did it work?"

2. **Scenario Assignment & Data Analysis (8 min):**
   - Teams receive detailed failing business scenarios with 4 weeks of sales data
   - Create bar charts showing which products sell well vs. poorly (like Layla's pottery vs. journals)
   - Identify the problem through data visualization
   - Calculate: revenue, costs, and profit before pivot

3. **Pivot Strategy Development (10 min):**
   - Teams complete "Pivot Plan" worksheet:
     * What we're STOPPING (like journals)
     * What we're FOCUSING ON (like pottery)
     * Why this will work (evidence from data)
     * What we'll lose (emotional cost, like Layla missing her journals)
     * What we'll gain (predicted new revenue and profit)
   - Create visual presentation: "Before Pivot" vs. "After Pivot" comparison

4. **Investor Pitch Presentations (8 min):**
   - Each team presents their pivot to "investors" (rest of class)
   - Pitch must include: the problem, the data, the pivot decision, predicted results
   - Investors ask questions: "Why not just lower prices?" "What if people want the old product?" "How do you know this will work?"

5. **Post-Simulation Analysis (4 min):**
   - Reveal "results" for each pivot (teacher pre-determines which pivots would succeed based on logic)
   - Calculate new profit numbers
   - Discuss: Which pivots worked best? What made them successful?
   - Connect to Layla's story: "She followed the data even though it was hard. That's what real business owners do."

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What makes a pivot successful vs. a pivot that fails?"
- "How did you use data to make your decision?"
- "Was it hard to stop doing something even if it wasn't working? Why?"
- "When should a business pivot vs. when should it keep trying?"
- "What's the difference between pivoting and quitting?"
- "How do feelings make business decisions harder?" (Layla loved journals but had to stop)

**Extension Ideas:**
- Track a real local business over a month to see if they change anything
- Interview a business owner about times they had to pivot
- Create "Pivot or Persist?" decision flowchart
- Research famous business pivots (Twitter, Instagram, YouTube)
- Calculate: "If Layla kept making journals losing $10/week, how much would she lose in a year?" (opportunity cost)

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Simple one-problem scenarios with teacher guidance
- Focus on "This isn't working. What do we change?"
- Draw pictures of before/after instead of written plans
- Practice identifying problems vs. solutions
- Basic vocabulary: change, fix, problem, try again, different

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- Two-option scenarios (stop this, start that)
- Simple data: "10 people bought X, but only 2 bought Y. Which should we sell?"
- Sentence frames: "The problem is ___. We should change ___ to ___."
- Understanding that change can make things better
- Vocabulary expansion: pivot, adapt, change, data, numbers, chart, business decision

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- Multi-step pivot planning with 2-3 options to consider
- Bar chart creation showing sales data across 4 products
- Detailed planning: what stops, what continues, what grows
- Understanding emotional difficulty of change (like Layla)
- Vocabulary: pivot, adapt, focus, opportunity cost, data-driven, trade-off

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- Complex scenarios with multiple products and detailed sales data
- Full financial analysis (revenue, costs, profit before and after)
- Written pivot plans with justification based on evidence
- Understanding strategic focus and competitive advantage
- Advanced vocabulary: strategic focus, differentiation, market analysis, revenue optimization, calculated risk, ROI (return on investment)

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual scenario cards with pictures of failing businesses
- Bilingual vocabulary cards: pivot/girar, adapt/adaptar, change/cambiar, data/datos
- Partner with fluent speaker for planning discussions
- Use gestures and drawings to show before/after
- Sentence frames: "Before: ___. Problem: ___. Pivot: ___. After: ___."

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Simplified scenarios (one clear problem, one clear solution)
- Teacher support during planning ("What's not working? What could work better?")
- Visual planning templates with pictures and checkboxes
- Option to draw solutions instead of writing them
- Work in smaller groups (pairs) rather than 4-person teams

**For Advanced Students:**
- Lead pivot presentations for their team
- Calculate detailed financial projections (revenue, costs, profit over 6 weeks)
- Research and present real-world business pivots
- Create decision matrices comparing multiple pivot options
- Calculate opportunity cost of NOT pivoting

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 8 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **Data-Driven Decision Making**
   - Core Lesson: Numbers and charts tell you what's working and what isn't—use data instead of guessing
   - Activity Connection: Students create and analyze bar charts showing product performance, then pivot based on evidence
   - Age-Appropriate: K-1 see simple "more vs. less" comparisons; 3-4 calculate actual revenue, costs, and profit changes

2. **Strategic Focus (Doing Less, Better)**
   - Core Lesson: Spreading resources too thin leads to mediocrity—focusing on what works leads to excellence
   - Activity Connection: Teams must eliminate underperforming products to concentrate on winners
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete experience that trying to do everything means doing nothing well

3. **Opportunity Cost**
   - Core Lesson: Every choice means NOT choosing something else—time spent on failures is time NOT spent on successes
   - Activity Connection: Calculate time and money saved by stopping journals, redirected to pottery production
   - Age-Appropriate: K-2 understand "if I do this, I can't do that"; 3-4 calculate actual dollar costs of bad choices

4. **Emotional vs. Rational Business Decisions**
   - Core Lesson: Good business decisions sometimes conflict with personal feelings—data should guide choices
   - Activity Connection: Students experience the tension Layla felt: loving journals but knowing they must stop
   - Age-Appropriate: Scaffolded from "this makes me sad but it's right" to complex analysis of emotional barriers to change

**Non-Moralistic Approach:**
- Activity presents pivoting as smart adaptation, not failure
- No shame language: "You failed at journals" ❌ Instead: "The market showed us what customers want" ✓
- Acknowledges emotional difficulty: "It's hard to stop doing something you love"
- Focuses on evidence-based decision-making, not character judgments
- Celebrates flexibility and resilience instead of stubbornness

**Connection to Season Arc:**
- Chapter 8 is the turning point of Season 4—friends learn to adapt or die
- Builds on CH1-7: identified problem (CH1), made plan (CH2), opened business (CH3), handled challenges (CH4-7)
- Sets up CH9-12: successful growth after pivot, managing seasonal changes, big picture reflection
- Reinforces that business requires constant adaptation, not just one-time setup

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Select 3-4 failing business scenarios appropriate for your students' age
- Prepare sample bar chart data for each scenario
- Have real-world pivot examples ready (Play-Doh, Netflix, local businesses)
- Consider inviting a local business owner who's pivoted to speak (if time allows)
- Prepare "results" for each possible pivot so you can reveal outcomes

**Classroom Management:**
- Emphasize that pivoting is SMART, not failure—reframe "giving up" vs. "adapting"
- Circulate during planning to ensure teams use data, not just guessing
- If groups struggle, ask guiding questions: "What does your chart show? Which bar is tallest?"
- Prepare for emotional reactions—some students will relate to Layla's attachment to journals
- Use timer for presentations to keep activity moving

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "We don't want to stop anything!" | Remind them: limited time and money. "If you do everything, nothing gets done well." Show Layla's example. |
| Teams pivot to something unrelated to the data | Redirect: "Look at your chart. What's actually selling?" Require evidence-based pivots. |
| Students suggest "just lower the price" for everything | Good discussion point! "Will that work? What if it costs more to make than the new price?" Calculate together. |
| Confusion about what "pivot" means | Use concrete analogy: "Like turning your bike when the path is blocked. You change direction but keep going." |
| Teams can't agree on pivot strategy | Teach voting or "let's test the top 2 ideas by calculating which would make more money" |
| Some students think pivoting means quitting | Clarify: "Quitting = give up entirely. Pivoting = change strategy to keep going." Huge difference! |
| Emotional resistance to "killing" favorite products | Honor the feelings: "It's okay to feel sad. Layla felt sad too. But data helps us make smart choices." |
| Advanced students finish pivot plan too quickly | Extension: Calculate 6-week projections, create multiple pivot scenarios, research real business pivots |

**Time Management:**
- Data analysis takes longer than expected—build in buffer time
- For 20-min version, teacher presents data rather than students creating charts
- If running long, do presentations as gallery walk instead of full presentations
- Have extension activities ready for fast finishers (research real pivots, create additional scenarios)

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pair less confident students with data-savvy partners
- Provide partially completed bar charts for students who struggle with graphing
- Allow students to draw pivot plans instead of writing them
- Advanced students can create detailed financial models with spreadsheets (if available)
- ELL students benefit from visual scenario cards and sentence frames

## SAFETY NOTES

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Validate Feelings:** Pivoting involves loss—it's okay to feel sad about stopping something you care about (like Layla with journals)
- **Reframe "Failure":** Emphasize that data showing something doesn't work is INFORMATION, not personal failure
- **Support Risk-Taking:** Create a classroom culture where trying new ideas and adapting is celebrated, not mocked
- **Gentle Feedback:** When teams present weak pivots, frame feedback as "What if you also considered..." rather than "That's wrong"

**Physical Safety:**
- No physical safety concerns for this primarily discussion/planning activity
- Standard classroom movement protocols for team groupings and presentations

**Psychological Safety:**
- **No Public Shaming:** If a student's pivot idea doesn't work in the simulation, frame it as "great try, here's what the data tells us"
- **Team Support:** If groups have internal disagreements about pivot strategy, teach respectful disagreement and voting
- **Personal Stories:** If students share stories of family businesses that failed, handle with care and sensitivity
- **Opt-Out Option:** Allow overwhelmed students to work individually on simpler scenarios rather than group work

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Students confuse "pivot" with "quit" | Vocabulary confusion | Use concrete metaphor: "Pivot = turn your bike to avoid a rock. Quit = get off your bike and walk home. Big difference!" |
| Teams resist stopping ANY product | Want to do everything | Show Layla's story: "She tried that. Stayed at $92/week. After pivot: $115/week. Data doesn't lie." Calculate opportunity cost. |
| "Just lower the price!" for every problem | Oversimplified thinking | Great teaching moment! "Let's calculate: costs $5 to make, sells for $4. How much do you lose per item? Can you stay in business?" |
| Students pick pivots based on what THEY like, not data | Personal preference over evidence | Redirect: "I hear that you love that product. But look at the chart—what does the data say? Business owners follow data, not just feelings." |
| Emotional reactions to "killing" favorite products | Genuine attachment (like Layla) | Honor the emotion: "It IS hard. Layla cried too. But what did Frances say? 'Cutting helps what's left grow stronger.' That's true for gardens AND businesses." |
| Bar chart confusion | Data literacy gaps | Start simpler: "Point to the tallest bar. That's what sells most. Point to shortest. That sells least. Which should the business focus on?" Build from there. |
| Teams can't agree on pivot direction | Genuine strategic disagreement | Teach business decision-making: "Calculate both options. Which makes more money? That's your answer. In business, data breaks ties." |
| "But what if customers change their mind later?" | Anxiety about permanence | Good question! "Smart businesses track data EVERY month. If journals start selling again, add them back. Pivot can pivot again!" Flexibility is key. |

## PREP TIME

**Tier 1 (Free/Minimal):** 15-20 minutes
- Select or create 1-2 simple failing business scenarios (5 min)

## REAL-WORLD PIVOT EXAMPLES (For Teaching)

**Age-Appropriate Business Pivots to Share:**

**Elementary-Friendly Examples:**
1. **Play-Doh:** Made wallpaper cleaner → kids played with it → became a toy (saved the company!)
2. **Nintendo:** Made playing cards for 100 years → saw video games were popular → now makes Mario and Pokemon
3. **Local Ice Cream Shop:** Only sold ice cream → nobody came in winter → added hot chocolate and cookies → now open year-round
4. **School Book Fair:** Only sold books → kids wanted other items → added bookmarks, posters, toys → sells more now

**Older Elementary Examples (3-4):**
1. **Netflix:** Mailed DVDs → internet got faster → streaming video → now makes TV shows
2. **YouTube:** Dating website → people uploaded random videos instead → became video sharing → now billions of users
3. **Instagram:** Check-in app nobody used → focused only on photo sharing → became hugely popular
4. **Slack:** Gaming company failed → used their internal chat tool → pivoted to business communication app

**Local Business Examples:**
- Restaurant that changed from fancy dinners to casual family meals
- Bookstore that added cafe because people weren't buying enough books
- Toy store that started selling online when foot traffic decreased
- Farmer's market vendor who stopped selling vegetables (low margin) and focused on jams/preserves (high margin)

**Teachable Lessons from Each:**
- Pivoting saved businesses that would have died
- All used data/observation: "this isn't working, but THIS is!"
- None gave up entirely—they adapted
- Willingness to change = survival
