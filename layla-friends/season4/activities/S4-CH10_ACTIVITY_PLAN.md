# CHAPTER 10: THE SEASONAL RUSH - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Managing seasonal demand and inventory
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where Maplewood Makers faces a December rush of holiday orders and must manage overwhelming demand, batch production, deposits, and saving for the slow season ahead
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Holiday Rush: Inventory & Demand Forecasting"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-2:** Holiday orders flood in, overwhelming the friends with demand → Activity begins with predicting holiday customer demand
- **Scenes 3-4:** Friends implement "half now, half later" deposit system → Activity includes deposit tracking and prepayment concepts
- **Scenes 5-7:** Riley discovers batch production saves time and effort → Activity demonstrates efficiency of batch production vs. one-at-a-time
- **Scenes 8-12:** Business hits peak with waitlists and record sales → Activity includes managing waitlists and capacity limits
- **Scenes 13-15:** Three weeks of high revenue followed by post-holiday slowdown → Activity tracks weekly demand fluctuations
- **Scenes 16-17:** Friends vote to save 30% for the slow January ahead → Activity concludes with planning for seasonal cash flow
- **Scenes 18-20:** Frances's wisdom: "You saved seeds. Winter is coming." → Activity reinforces saving during good times for lean times

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand seasonal business cycles (rush periods vs. slow periods)
2. **Forecasting Skills:** Predict demand based on patterns and plan inventory accordingly
3. **Cash Flow Management:** Learn to save profits during peak season for slow season expenses
4. **Inventory Management:** Balance having enough stock without overproducing (waste)
5. **Batch Production:** Discover efficiency gains from making items in groups
6. **Strategic Planning:** Make decisions under pressure with incomplete information
7. **Mathematical Thinking:** Calculate supply needs, track weekly revenue, compute savings percentages

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Simple Prediction & Supply Game

**Materials Needed:**
- 20 craft items (pom-poms, beads, or paper squares) representing "products"
- 3 cups labeled "Week 1," "Week 2," "Week 3"
- Paper for tracking

**Steps:**
1. **Setup (1 min):** Show students the 20 items. "These are ornaments your store can make. You have to decide how many to make each week for the holiday rush."
2. **Prediction Round (2 min):** "How many ornaments do you think customers will want Week 1? Week 2? Week 3?" Students guess. Distribute items into cups based on class consensus.
3. **Reveal Actual Demand (3 min):** Teacher reveals the "real" customer demand: Week 1 = 5, Week 2 = 10, Week 3 = 4. Count items in each cup. Did you make enough? Too many?
4. **Discussion (3 min):** "What happened when we made too many? (Waste.) What happened when we didn't make enough? (Lost sales.) How could we plan better?"
5. **Saving Connection (1 min):** "If Week 1 and 2 were busy and you made money, should you save some for Week 3 when it's slower?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Was it easy or hard to guess how many people would want?"
- "What happens if you make too many?"
- "Why should you save money from busy weeks?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Holiday Store Manager Simulation

**Materials Needed:**
- "Order form" cards (15-20 cards with customer orders)
- Play money ($200 in small bills)
- "Supply ordering" worksheet
- Tracking sheets for weekly sales
- Timer

**Steps:**
1. **Store Setup (3 min):** Students work in pairs as "store managers." Give each pair $50 starting budget. Explain: "You sell ornaments for $6 each. Each ornament costs you $2 to make. You have 4 weeks before the holidays."
2. **Week 1 Planning (4 min):** "Based on this being early December, how many ornaments should you make?" Pairs decide and "order supplies" (spend $2 per ornament on worksheet). Reveal Week 1 demand: 5 customers want 1-2 ornaments each. Calculate sales and profit.
3. **Week 2 Rush (5 min):** "It's mid-December now. Holiday shopping peaks!" Pairs predict demand and order supplies. Reveal Week 2 demand: 10 customers (big rush!). Did they make enough? Track sales and profits.
4. **Week 3 & 4 Slowdown (4 min):** Week 3: moderate demand (6 customers). Week 4: post-holiday (2 customers). Calculate final revenue and leftover unsold inventory (waste).
5. **Saving Discussion (4 min):** Count total profit. "The friends saved 30% for January when business would be slow. If you made $30 profit, how much should you save?" Calculate as a class. Discuss why saving matters.

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Which week was hardest to predict? Why?"
- "Did anyone run out of inventory? How did that feel?"
- "Did anyone make too many and have leftovers? What's the cost of that?"
- "Why do businesses save money during busy seasons?"
- "How could you use last year's data to predict this year better?"

**Extension:** Graph the 4-week demand on bar chart. Identify the peak week and slowest week visually.

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Retail Simulation with Forecasting, Batch Production & Cash Flow

**Materials Needed:**
- Detailed "last year's sales data" handout
- Supply order forms with pricing
- Weekly demand cards (sealed envelopes)
- Play money ($500 per team)
- Inventory tracking worksheets
- "Batch production" vs. "one-at-a-time" timing cards
- Calculator (or mental math)
- Savings jars or envelopes
- Revenue/profit tracking sheets

**Steps:**
1. **Historical Data Analysis (5 min):**
   - Give teams "Last Year's Holiday Sales Data": Week 1 (12 sales), Week 2 (28 sales), Week 3 (35 sales), Week 4 (8 sales)
   - Teams analyze: "When was the rush? When did it slow down? Use this data to predict THIS year."
2. **Forecasting & Supply Ordering (6 min):**
   - Teams predict demand for 4 weeks and complete supply order form
   - Product cost: $3 per ornament to make, sells for $8
   - Budget: $100 starting capital
   - Decision: Order all supplies upfront (cheaper bulk rate: $2.50 each) OR order weekly (more expensive: $3 each, but less risk)?
3. **Week 1 Simulation (5 min):**
   - Open Week 1 demand envelope: 15 orders
   - Teams fulfill orders if they have inventory, track sales
   - Introduce deposit option (like Scenes 3-4): "You can ask for 50% deposit on large orders"
   - Calculate Week 1 revenue and profit
4. **Week 2 RUSH with Batch Production (6 min):**
   - Open Week 2 envelope: 40 orders (huge rush!)
   - Demonstrate batch production: "Making 10 ornaments one-at-a-time takes 10 hours. Batch production (making them all together) takes 6 hours."
   - Teams decide: Did we make enough inventory? Do we take deposits and promise delivery next week?
   - Track revenue, profit, and any waitlist/backorders
5. **Weeks 3-4 Slowdown (5 min):**
   - Week 3 envelope: 25 orders (still busy but declining)
   - Week 4 envelope: 6 orders (post-holiday crash)
   - Teams fulfill remaining orders, calculate unsold inventory (waste = lost money)
6. **Cash Flow Analysis & Saving (8 min):**
   - Total all 4 weeks: revenue, costs, profit
   - Calculate 30% savings rule: "Like Layla in the story, save 30% of profit for January when sales are slow"
   - Teams physically move 30% of profit to "savings jar"
   - Remaining 70% is "split among partners" (divide by number of team members)
   - Create final profit & loss statement

**Discussion Questions:**
- "How accurate were your predictions compared to actual demand?"
- "What strategy worked best: ordering all supplies upfront, or weekly?"
- "When did batch production help the most?"
- "What was the cost of having leftover inventory? What was the cost of running out?"
- "Why does saving 30% during busy season make business sense?"
- "How could you use this year's data to plan for next year?"
- "What real businesses face seasonal rushes like this?" (Toy stores at Christmas, ice cream shops in summer, ski resorts in winter)

**Extension Ideas:**
- Graph revenue across all 4 weeks to visualize the seasonal curve
- Calculate "opportunity cost" of missed sales when inventory ran out
- Research real retail businesses and their seasonal patterns
- Create a "Year 2 Plan" incorporating lessons learned from Year 1
- Explore "just-in-time inventory" vs. "bulk ordering" trade-offs
- Calculate break-even point and profit margins

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Simple counting: "How many do we need?"
- Physical manipulatives for all inventory
- Teacher-led prediction discussions
- Focus on "more," "less," "just right" concepts
- Basic vocabulary: busy, slow, save, spend, enough, too many
- Visual tracking with pictures and tally marks

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- Simple addition and subtraction for inventory
- Guided prediction with choices: "5, 10, or 15?"
- Introduction to "last year's data" concept
- Sentence frames: "I predict ___ customers will come because ___."
- Vocabulary: predict, demand, inventory, save, profit, rush
- Simple bar graphs for weekly comparison

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- Multiplication for batch calculations
- Independent predictions with justification
- Compare predictions to outcomes with reflection
- Calculate percentages for savings (with support): "30% of $100"
- Vocabulary: forecast, seasonal, batch production, cash flow, waste, opportunity cost
- Create data charts and identify patterns

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- Complex calculations: profit margins, break-even analysis
- Multi-week strategic planning
- Advanced percentage calculations independently
- Graph seasonal curves and analyze trends
- Advanced vocabulary: forecasting, inventory management, bulk ordering, just-in-time, profit margin, scalability
- Write business recommendations based on simulation results

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual aids: pictures for "rush" (many customers), "slow" (few customers)
- Bilingual vocabulary: demand/demanda, inventory/inventario, save/ahorrar, profit/ganancia
- Hands-on counting reduces language barriers
- Partner with fluent speaker for planning discussions
- Sentence frames for predictions: "I think Week ___ will have ___ customers."
- Number-focused tasks reduce verbal requirements

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Simplified choices (predict from 3 options rather than open-ended)
- Visual timelines showing 4 weeks clearly
- Manipulative-based inventory (physical counting)
- Calculator or hundred chart support for math
- Pre-filled templates with blanks to complete
- Option to focus on one week at a time rather than all four
- Clear visual signals for "demand up" (green) and "demand down" (red)

**For Advanced Students:**
- Add variables: "What if materials cost increased? How would that change your strategy?"
- Multi-product inventory (ornaments AND mugs) with different profit margins
- Create Excel-style spreadsheet for all calculations
- Research real retail seasonal data and compare to simulation
- Design a "Year 2" plan incorporating improvements
- Calculate ROI (return on investment) for different ordering strategies
- Explore "dynamic pricing" (charging more during peak demand)

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 10 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **Seasonal Business Cycles**
   - Core Lesson: Many businesses have predictable busy and slow periods based on time of year
   - Activity Connection: Students experience the December rush followed by January slowdown firsthand
   - Age-Appropriate: K-1 notice "more customers" and "fewer customers"; 3-4 understand cyclical patterns and can plan for them

   - Core Lesson: Businesses must balance having enough inventory to meet demand without overproducing (waste)
   - Activity Connection: Students predict demand and face real consequences of being over/under-stocked
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete experience with "too many = waste" and "too few = missed sales"

3. **Cash Flow Management & Reserves**
   - Core Lesson: Saving money during profitable periods prepares you for lean times when revenue drops
   - Activity Connection: 30% savings rule (from story) applied to simulation profits
   - Age-Appropriate: Experiential learning that "saving seeds for winter" is smart business

4. **Batch Production Efficiency**
   - Core Lesson: Making items in groups is faster and more efficient than one-at-a-time production
   - Activity Connection: Calculate time savings from batch production (Riley's 8 hours vs. 14 hours)
   - Age-Appropriate: Tangible math showing efficiency gains

5. **Deposits & Prepayment**
   - Core Lesson: Taking partial payment upfront helps businesses manage cash flow and reduces risk
   - Activity Connection: "Half now, half later" system from Scenes 3-4 applied to large orders
   - Age-Appropriate: Simple fraction concept (1/2) with real business application

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Pre-fill "last year's sales data" if using advanced version (make it realistic: gradual increase to peak, sharp drop after holiday)
- Create demand cards in sealed envelopes so students can't peek ahead
- Have extra play money available in case of calculation errors
- Prepare visual aids showing seasonal demand curves (graphs with obvious peaks)
- Consider running a quick demo round yourself to test timing
- If using actual craft items, pre-count inventory sets so setup is quick

**Classroom Management:**
- Establish "business teams" of 2-4 students for collaborative planning
- Use a timer to keep weeks moving (5 min per week maximum)
- Signal transitions clearly: "Week 1 planning time ends in 30 seconds"
- Have a visible "weekly calendar" on board showing which week you're in
- Circulate during planning rounds to check math and answer questions
- Prepare for: students who over-order and waste money (teachable moment), students who under-order and miss sales (also teachable moment), arguments over team predictions (voting or consensus practice)

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "We ran out of inventory and lost sales!" | Perfect teachable moment: "What's the cost of missing sales? How could you have predicted better?" |
| "We made too many and have leftovers" | Discuss waste and unsold inventory costs; in real business, this affects profit |
| Math errors in revenue calculation | Provide calculators or hundred charts; pair students for checking each other's work |
| Students peek at demand cards ahead of time | Use sealed envelopes; emphasize that real businesses don't know the future |
| Disagreement within team on predictions | Practice consensus-building: "Explain your thinking. Can you find a compromise?" |
| Students want to change predictions mid-week | Real businesses can't undo orders already placed; decisions have consequences |
| Finishing early | Extension: Graph your results, write a report to "investors," plan for Year 2 |
| Activity running long | Pause after Week 2, continue next day; the simulation can span 2 days if needed |

**Time Management:**
- The 45-min version is data-heavy; for younger students, consider spreading over 2 days
- Pre-printed worksheets save significant time vs. hand-drawing tracking sheets
- Have "last year's data" ready to distribute immediately
- Use timers to keep each week's simulation moving
- Build in 2-minute buffer for questions and clarification
- If running short on time, skip Week 4 and just discuss the post-holiday slowdown conceptually

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pair strong math students with those who need support
- Offer prediction ranges to choose from (5, 10, or 15) rather than open-ended for younger students
- Advanced students can manage multi-product inventory (ornaments AND mugs)
- Visual learners benefit from graphing demand immediately after each week
- Students who struggle with uncertainty need reassurance: "Forecasting is educated guessing. Being wrong is part of learning."
- Kinesthetic learners can physically move inventory items as they're "sold"

## SAFETY NOTES

**Physical Safety:**
- **Small Items:** If using beads, pom-poms, or buttons as inventory, monitor younger students (choking hazard for K-1)
- **Play Money:** Ensure it's clearly marked as not real currency; supervise to prevent mix-ups with real money
- **Calculators:** If using devices, monitor for appropriate use

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Handling "Failure":** Students may feel frustrated if predictions are very wrong; frame as learning opportunity, not failure
- **Competition Stress:** Some students feel pressure during simulations; emphasize that mistakes are part of learning
- **Math Anxiety:** Provide calculators, partner support, and reassurance for students who struggle with calculations
- **Team Dynamics:** Monitor for dominant students steamrolling quieter team members in planning; ensure all voices heard

**Financial Safety (Conceptual):**
- **Realistic Expectations:** Clarify this is a simplified simulation; real businesses face many more variables
- **Savings Context:** 30% rule is specific to this seasonal scenario; not universal advice for all situations
- **Risk Discussion:** Real businesses face real consequences; acknowledge that forecasting is uncertain and involves risk

**General:**
- Supervision during all simulation rounds
- Clear rules about handling play money and materials
- Plan for what happens if students lose materials (have extras ready)
- Ensure all students participate in planning, not just one "leader" per team

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| "Our prediction was way off!" | Forecasting is hard; lack of experience | Celebrate learning: "Now you know! What data would help next time?"; emphasize that real businesses get it wrong too |
| Frustration with math calculations | Revenue/profit math can be complex for young students | Provide calculators, pre-made tracking sheets with formulas, partner support; simplify numbers for younger tiers |
| Team disagreements on predictions | Different students have different reasoning | Practice consensus: "Each person explains their thinking, then we vote or find middle ground"; this IS a business skill |
| Students want to change orders mid-week | Impulse to "fix" mistakes once demand is revealed | Explain that real businesses can't undo orders already placed; decisions have consequences; this is the learning |
| Running out of play money or materials | Calculation errors or over-ordering | Have backup supplies ready; use as teachable moment about resource limits |
| "This is too hard!" | Overwhelm from tracking multiple weeks | Break it down: "Let's just focus on Week 1 first"; reduce to 2-3 weeks for younger students |
| Finishing too quickly | Some teams work faster | Extensions ready: graph results, plan Year 2, calculate "what if" scenarios, help other teams |
| Students save too little/too much | Misunderstanding the 30% concept | Model the calculation clearly: "If profit is $30, 30% is $9. Show me $9."; use visual fraction models for K-2 |

## EXTENSIONS & VARIATIONS

**For Early Finishers:**
1. Graph your 4-week results on a bar chart and label the peak week
2. Calculate what happens if materials cost increased by $1 per item
3. Design a "Year 2 Plan" with improvements based on Year 1 results
4. Interview teacher about real-world seasonal business example
5. Create an advertisement for the slow season to boost sales
6. Calculate opportunity cost of missed sales when inventory ran out

**For Advanced Learners:**
1. Add a second product with different profit margin; manage both simultaneously
2. Introduce "bulk discount" concept: ordering 50+ items costs less per unit, but ties up capital
3. Research real retail business quarterly reports; identify seasonal patterns in data
4. Create Excel-style spreadsheet modeling entire simulation with formulas
5. Explore "dynamic pricing" strategy: raising prices during peak demand
6. Calculate break-even point and ROI for different inventory strategies
7. Write a business case study: "How [Student Name] Business Survived the Holiday Rush"

**For Additional Days/Sessions:**
- **Day 2:** Run simulation again with different demand pattern (summer rush instead of winter)
- **Week 2:** Compare two different industries' seasonal patterns (toy store vs. garden center)
- **Extension:** Track a real local business's seasonal pattern over several months
- **Cross-Grade:** Partner with older students who share their forecasting strategies

**Seasonal Variations:**
- **Fall:** Back-to-school supply rush (August peak)
- **Winter:** Holiday gift rush (December peak)
- **Spring:** Garden center or bike shop rush (April-May peak)
- **Summer:** Ice cream or pool supply rush (June-July peak)

**Real-World Application:**
- Visit a local seasonal business and interview owner about how they prepare
- Analyze your school supply list costs in August vs. February
- Track local grocery store seasonal product availability (strawberries in summer vs. winter)
