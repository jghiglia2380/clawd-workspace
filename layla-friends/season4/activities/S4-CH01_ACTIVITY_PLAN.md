# CHAPTER 1: GOING SOLO - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** The limits of working alone
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where each friend tries running their own business solo and discovers it's exhausting, inefficient, and isolating — leading them to realize partnership is essential
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Solo Challenge: Can You Do It All Alone?"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-2:** Friends decide to split up and work separately → Activity begins with solo task assignment
- **Scenes 3-11:** Each friend struggles alone with their work (Riley remakes mugs 3 times, Ellis walks across town repeatedly, Layla can't sell, Benny makes costly mistakes) → Activity demonstrates limits of solo work
- **Scenes 12-14:** Friends realize they're saying no to opportunities and feeling overwhelmed → Activity creates the same overwhelmed feeling
- **Scenes 15-18:** Counting money reveals low hourly earnings despite hard work → Activity includes productivity measurement
- **Scenes 19-20:** Friends realize partnership is the answer → Activity concludes with team comparison and reflection

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand that time is a limited resource and working alone limits earning potential
2. **Opportunity Cost:** Experience firsthand that doing one task means you can't do another simultaneously
3. **Division of Labor:** Discover that specialization and teamwork increase efficiency and output
4. **Business Operations:** Learn that running a business requires multiple simultaneous tasks
5. **Self-Awareness:** Recognize personal limits and the value of asking for help
6. **Entrepreneurship:** Understand why partnerships and teams exist in business

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Quick 3-Task Solo Sprint

**Materials Needed:**
- 3 task stations per student: drawing paper, folding paper (origami or paper airplanes), organizing supplies (blocks or crayons)
- Timer (teacher's phone or classroom clock)
- Scrap paper for tracking

**Steps:**
1. **Setup (1 min):** Each student has 3 tasks in front of them: (1) Draw a star, (2) Fold a paper airplane, (3) Organize 10 crayons by color
2. **Solo Challenge (5 min):** "You have 5 minutes to finish ALL THREE tasks. Ready? Go!" Students work alone trying to complete everything
3. **Count Results (1 min):** How many students finished everything? How many got 2 done? Only 1?
4. **Reflection (3 min):** "Was that hard? Why?" "What if you could ask a friend to help with one task?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Did anyone finish everything? How did you feel?"
- "If you could only pick ONE task, which would it be? What happens to the others?"
- "How is this like the story when Riley kept remaking mugs while Ellis was walking across town?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Solo vs. Team Challenge with Productivity Tracking

**Materials Needed:**
- 4 task stations: poster drawing, paper airplane folding, supply organizing, price list writing
- Timer
- Tracking sheets (simple tally marks)
- Optional: play money for "earnings"

**Steps:**
1. **Round 1 - Solo Challenge (6 min):** Each student must rotate through all 4 stations alone, spending 90 seconds at each. Track what they complete. Timer rings every 90 seconds - move to next station whether finished or not.
2. **Count Solo Output (2 min):** Tally on board: How many total tasks completed across whole class?
3. **Round 2 - Team Challenge (6 min):** Divide class into teams of 4. Each person takes ONE station and becomes the expert. Same 90-second rotations but now each person only does their specialty. Count completed tasks.
4. **Compare Results (3 min):** Graph both rounds on board. Team output is MUCH higher. Why?
5. **Reflection (3 min):** "In the story, the friends made $58 in 35 hours working alone. What do you think would happen if they worked together?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Which was less stressful - doing everything or specializing in one thing?"
- "How much more did we produce as a team?"
- "Why do you think Ellis said 'I walk more than I work'?"
- "When might working alone be better than working with others?"

**Extension:** Calculate "hourly earnings" - if each completed task = $1, divide total earnings by minutes worked. Compare solo vs. team "wages."

---

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Solo-to-Partnership Simulation with Financial Analysis

**Materials Needed:**
- 5 task stations (drawing, folding, organizing, writing, decorating)
- Detailed tracking sheets with time logs
- Play money ($1 per completed task)
- Calculator (or pencil for math)
- Large chart paper for class data visualization

**Steps:**
1. **Solo Challenge Setup (2 min):**
   - Explain: "You're running your own business. You must do ALL these jobs yourself."
   - Each student receives a tracking sheet to log time spent and tasks completed
2. **Round 1 - Solo Operations (10 min):**
   - Students rotate through 5 stations, working alone
   - Must track: time at each station, tasks completed, frustration level (1-5 scale)
   - Pay rate: $1 per completed task
3. **Solo Results Analysis (5 min):**
   - Count money earned individually
   - Calculate time worked vs. money earned (earnings per minute)
   - Record frustration ratings
   - Discuss: "How did that feel? Were you rushing? Did you feel overwhelmed?"
4. **Partnership Formation (2 min):**
   - Form teams of 5 (or adapt to class size)
   - Each person chooses ONE specialty station
5. **Round 2 - Partnership Operations (10 min):**
   - Same tasks, but now each team member specializes
   - Team tracks total output and time
   - Pay rate stays same: $1 per completed task, but now earnings are pooled
6. **Partnership Results & Comparison (6 min):**
   - Calculate team earnings, divide by 5 members
   - Compare individual solo earnings vs. team partnership share
   - Compare frustration levels (solo vs. partnership)
   - Graph class data: Total productivity solo vs. team

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Who made more money working solo or in partnership? Why?"
- "How did your stress level change between rounds?"
- "What are the ADVANTAGES of working alone? What are the DISADVANTAGES?"
- "In the story, the friends made less than $2/hour working alone. How could working together help?"
- "Why do real businesses have many employees instead of one person doing everything?"
- "What jobs REQUIRE teams? What jobs can be done alone?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Calculate class total: sum all solo earnings vs. all partnership earnings - visualize the difference
- Research real businesses: how do they divide labor? (Restaurant: chef, server, cashier, dishwasher)
- Write reflection: "When I tried to do everything alone, I felt... When I worked with a team, I felt..."
- Connect to Season 3: "At the Makers' Faire, did the friends work alone or together? How did that help?"

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Only 2-3 simple tasks (draw a shape, fold paper once, put toys in a box)
- Focus on feeling: "Is this hard? Do you want help?"
- Partner pairs for team round instead of larger groups
- Basic vocabulary: alone, together, help, team
- Concrete understanding: "One person can't do everything at once"

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- 4 tasks with simple tracking (check boxes for completed)
- Introduction to counting productivity
- Small teams (3-4 students)
- Vocabulary expansion: business, task, alone, partnership, complete
- Beginning to understand time as a resource

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- 4-5 tasks with time tracking (how long did each take?)
- Calculate simple earnings ($1 per task)
- Teams of 4-5 with assigned roles
- Vocabulary: efficiency, productivity, opportunity cost, earnings
- Understanding that working alone has limits

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- 5+ tasks with detailed time logs
- Full financial calculation (earnings per minute, comparison of solo vs. team hourly rates)
- Strategic team formation and role selection
- Advanced vocabulary: specialization, division of labor, productivity rate, time management, partnership structure
- Deep analysis of why partnerships make economic sense

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual task cards with pictures (draw a star - show image of star)
- Partner with fluent speaker during activity
- Simplified tracking sheets with checkboxes instead of written logs
- Bilingual vocabulary cards: alone/solo, together/juntos, team/equipo, work/trabajo
- Demonstrate each task before starting

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Reduce number of tasks (2-3 instead of 5)
- Provide visual timer they can see at their station
- Offer choice: which task do you want to be your specialty in round 2?
- Quiet workspace option if chaos of rotation is overwhelming
- Extended time or modified expectations for task completion
- Option to observe round 1 before participating in round 2

**For Advanced Students:**
- Lead a team in round 2 as "business manager"
- Create detailed financial reports comparing rounds
- Design additional tasks to add to the challenge
- Calculate class-wide productivity statistics and create graphs
- Research real-world examples: solo entrepreneurs vs. large companies
- Write analysis: "The Economic Benefits of Partnership"

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 1 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **Time as a Limited Resource**
   - Core Lesson: You can only do one thing at a time; time is your most constrained resource in business
   - Activity Connection: Students physically experience the impossibility of doing multiple tasks simultaneously
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete, hands-on demonstration makes abstract concept visceral

2. **Opportunity Cost**
   - Core Lesson: Choosing to do Task A means you cannot simultaneously do Task B
   - Activity Connection: When drawing, you cannot fold; when folding, you cannot organize
   - Age-Appropriate: Lived experience creates deeper understanding than lecture

3. **Division of Labor Increases Productivity**
   - Core Lesson: When people specialize, total output increases dramatically
   - Activity Connection: Round 2 team productivity is measurably higher than Round 1 solo productivity
   - Age-Appropriate: Data comparison (we made X alone, Y together) makes the principle clear

4. **Economic Rationale for Partnerships**
   - Core Lesson: Businesses form teams not just for social reasons, but for financial efficiency
   - Activity Connection: Partnership earnings (when calculated per person) often exceed solo earnings
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete money calculation grounds abstract business concept

**Non-Moralistic Approach:**
- Activity doesn't shame individual work or imply working alone is "bad"
- Acknowledges both solo work (autonomy, personal satisfaction) and teamwork (efficiency, support) have value
- Focuses on WHEN each approach makes sense, not moral superiority of one over the other
- No language like "selfish to work alone" ❌ Instead: "Different situations call for different approaches" ✓
- Celebrates students who excel at solo work AND those who thrive in teams

**Connection to Season Arc:**
- Foundation for entire Season 4: establishes WHY the friends form Maplewood Makers partnership
- Sets up CH2 (writing business plan requires team planning)
- Prepares for CH3-8 (navigating partnership challenges)
- Makes CH12 reflection meaningful (look how far partnership brought us)

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Set up task stations before activity (saves precious activity time)
- Test timing yourself: can tasks actually be done in allotted time?
- Prepare tracking sheets ahead (photocopied or drawn on board)
- Have backup tasks ready if some finish early
- Consider class size: adjust team sizes accordingly (teams of 3-5 work well)

**Classroom Management:**
- Clear signal for rotation (bell, hand clap, timer sound)
- Establish expectations: rotate when timer rings even if task isn't finished (this creates the "overwhelm" feeling that teaches the lesson)
- Circulate during Round 1 to observe frustration (this is the learning moment)
- Encourage (don't rescue) during solo round: "Is this hard? That's okay - keep trying!"
- Celebrate both solo effort AND team collaboration

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "I finished all my solo tasks easily!" | Great! Add a 6th task or increase complexity. Ask: "Could you do this for 8 hours straight? What if you had 20 tasks?" |
| Students get frustrated and give up in Round 1 | Perfect! That's the point. Validate feelings: "This IS hard. How do you think Riley felt making mugs over and over?" |
| Uneven participation in team round | Assign explicit roles: "You are the folder. This is YOUR station." Rotate if time allows. |
| Students want to help each other in Round 1 | Gently redirect: "In Round 1, you're working alone like the friends did in the story. Round 2 is for teamwork!" |
| Finishing too quickly | Add more tasks, increase quality standards ("airplane must fly 5 feet"), or increase quantity ("fold 3 airplanes") |
| Taking too long | Reduce number of tasks, increase time limit, or pause and reconvene next day for Round 2 |
| Arguments over team roles | Let teams negotiate, but have teacher assign if needed. Discuss: "How do real businesses decide who does what?" |

**Time Management:**
- Round 1 will feel chaotic - that's intentional
- Build in transition time between rounds (2-3 min)
- If running short on time, do Round 1 one day, discuss, then Round 2 next day
- Have extension activities ready for fast finishers (improve quality, help others, design new tasks)

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pair struggling students with supportive partners in Round 2
- Allow students with attention challenges to observe first round before participating
- Provide visual task checklists for students who need structure
- Let advanced students track additional data (time per task, quality ratings)
- Modify task complexity based on grade level

## SAFETY NOTES

**Physical Safety:**
- **Scissors:** If cutting is part of tasks, ensure age-appropriate scissors and supervision
- **Movement:** Students rotating between stations - ensure clear pathways, no running
- **Shared Materials:** Establish taking turns and handling shared supplies respectfully
- **Clutter:** Keep task stations organized to prevent tripping hazards

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Frustration is Expected:** Normalize that Round 1 SHOULD feel hard - that's the learning point
- **No Shame:** Avoid comparing students ("Why couldn't you finish when Sarah did?")
- **Validate Feelings:** "This is frustrating" is a learning moment, not a failure
- **Success Redefined:** Finishing isn't the goal - understanding WHY it's hard is the goal
- **Celebrate Effort:** Recognize both solo attempts and team contributions

**General:**
- Supervise all stations during activity
- Have a calm-down corner available for students who become overwhelmed
- Build in breaks if needed (pause between rounds)
- Check in with students who seem particularly frustrated

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| "I finished everything easily!" | Student is very fast, or tasks are too simple | Add complexity or quantity; discuss scalability ("Could you do 100 of these?") |
| Meltdown during solo round | Genuine overwhelm (which is the point, but still hard) | Validate feelings; allow break if needed; emphasize "this is supposed to be hard - you're learning!" |
| Refusal to try in Round 1 | Anticipating failure feels bad | Reframe: "The goal isn't to finish - it's to SEE what happens when you try everything alone" |
| Unequal team contribution in Round 2 | Personality differences, task difficulty variance | Rotate roles if time, or discuss: some jobs are easier/harder - that's real life too |
| Arguments about who gets which role | Everyone wants the "fun" task | Random assignment, draw straws, or rotate every 2 minutes |
| Faster teams finish and get bored | Task completion variance | Have extension: improve quality, help other teams, design a new task |
| Students help each other in Round 1 despite instructions | Natural inclination to help | Gently remind: "Save the teamwork for Round 2 - we need to see what solo work feels like first" |
