# CHAPTER 12: THE BIG PICTURE - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Business reflection and strategic planning
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where friends review six months of business, calculate their profit and hourly earnings, reflect on personal growth, and decide their future direction
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Business Expo & Season Reflection"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-5:** Friends review six months of sales, calculate revenue ($1,247), expenses ($389), and profit ($858) → Activity includes financial tracking and profit calculation
- **Scenes 6-8:** They calculate earnings per person ($214.50) and per hour ($16), analyzing which products were most profitable → Activity includes comparative analysis and data visualization
- **Scenes 9-13:** Each friend reflects on personal growth beyond money — leadership, artistry, teamwork, boundaries → Activity includes personal reflection on non-financial gains
- **Scenes 14-17:** Friends evaluate three strategic options (quit, grow big, stay sustainable) and choose Option B → Activity includes strategic planning and decision-making
- **Scenes 18-20:** They create a year-long plan and celebrate the beautiful web of connections they've built → Activity concludes with future planning and community recognition

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Calculate revenue, expenses, profit, and earnings per hour
2. **Business Analysis:** Evaluate product performance and identify most profitable offerings
3. **Reflection Skills:** Assess personal growth and non-financial benefits of entrepreneurship
4. **Strategic Thinking:** Evaluate business options and make informed decisions about future direction
5. **Data Visualization:** Present financial and operational data through charts and graphs
6. **Public Speaking:** Communicate business journey and lessons learned to an audience
7. **Growth Mindset:** Recognize progress over time and identify areas for continued improvement

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Season Reflection Circle

**Materials Needed:**
- Chart paper or whiteboard
- Markers
- One index card per student

**Steps:**
1. **Introduction (2 min):** "In the story, the friends looked back at everything they learned. Not just money — but how they grew as people. What did YOU learn this season?"
2. **Individual Reflection (3 min):** Each student draws or writes ONE business lesson on their card. "The most important thing I learned about business is..."
3. **Share Circle (4 min):** Students sit in circle. Each shares their lesson. Teacher writes themes on chart paper.
4. **Class Summary (1 min):** "Look at everything we learned together! Which lesson shows up the most?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What was the most fun activity we did this season?"
- "What was the hardest lesson to learn?"
- "Would you want to run a real business someday?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Mini Business Expo with Reflection Posters

**Materials Needed:**
- Large paper (1 per student or small group)
- Markers, colored pencils, crayons
- "Business Journey" template (optional)
- Reflection prompts printed or written on board

**Steps:**
1. **Create Business Journey Poster (12 min):** Students create visual display showing:
   - Business name and what they "sold" (from previous activities)
   - 3 things they learned about business
   - 1 challenge they faced
   - 1 thing they're proud of
   - Drawing of their favorite product or activity
2. **Gallery Walk Setup (2 min):** Display posters around room. Students stand by their poster.
3. **Gallery Walk (4 min):** Students rotate viewing each poster. They can ask questions or give compliments.
4. **Whole Group Reflection (2 min):** "What patterns did you notice? What did lots of people learn?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Which business lesson will you use in real life?"
- "What surprised you about running a business?"
- "If you could do one activity again, which would it be?"
- "How did working with partners help you?"

**Extension:** Create "Class Business Awards" — vote on categories like "Best Problem Solver," "Best Teammate," "Most Creative Product."

---

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Business Expo with Financial Analysis & Presentations

**Materials Needed:**
- Large poster board or tri-fold display boards (1 per student/team)
- Financial tracking worksheets
- Calculators
- Chart/graph templates
- Markers, colored pencils, decorating supplies
- Play money or "profit certificates"
- Award ribbons or certificates
- Optional: photos from previous activities

**Steps:**
1. **Financial Review & Calculation (8 min):**
   - If students ran simulated businesses during previous activities, compile their data
   - Calculate total "revenue" (sales from previous activities)
   - Calculate total "expenses" (materials costs)
   - Calculate profit (revenue - expenses)
   - Calculate earnings per partner (if team-based)
   - Create simple bar graph comparing revenue vs. expenses
2. **Business Expo Booth Creation (12 min):**
   - Design display showing:
     * Business name and logo
     * Products offered (photos or samples from previous activities)
     * Financial summary (revenue, expenses, profit chart)
     * "What We Learned" section with 3-5 key lessons
     * "Challenges We Overcame" with specific examples
     * "Our Future Plan" (keep going, try something new, what's next?)
   - Decorate professionally with colors, borders, organized layout
3. **Presentation Preparation (3 min):**
   - Practice 1-2 minute "investor pitch" covering:
     * What was our business?
     * What were our results?
     * What did we learn?
     * What's our plan for the future?
4. **Business Expo Presentations (10 min):**
   - Half class presents to "investors" (other half + teacher)
   - Switch roles
   - Investors ask 1-2 questions per business
5. **Awards Ceremony & Reflection (2 min):**
   - Distribute awards: Best Financial Analysis, Most Creative Business, Best Teamwork, Most Innovative Product, Best Presentation, Most Growth Shown
   - Final reflection: "What will you remember most about this season?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- **Financial Analysis:** "Which business made the most profit? Why do you think that happened?"
- **Product Performance:** "If you could only sell ONE product, which would you choose and why?"
- **Strategic Thinking:** "The friends in the story chose Option B — stay the same size. Would you make the same choice? Why or why not?"
- **Personal Growth:** "How did you grow as a person, not just as a business owner?"
- **Partnership Reflection:** "What made a good business partner? How did you handle disagreements?"
- **Future Planning:** "If you ran your business for another six months, what would you change?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Invite parents/families to attend expo as "investors"
- Create video presentations of business journeys
- Write reflective essays: "My Season as an Entrepreneur"
- Develop actual year-long plan similar to friends in story (Spring/Summer/Fall/Winter goals)
- Research real young entrepreneurs and their stories
- Create "Business Advice" book for next year's students

## DISCUSSION QUESTIONS

**Pre-Activity (Connect to Story):**
- "In the story, the friends reviewed six months of business. What do you think they were most proud of?"
- "They discovered each person earned $214.50 for 53 hours of work. Is that good? Why or why not?"
- "What did each friend learn beyond just making money?"
- "The friends had three choices: stop, grow big, or stay sustainable. Which would YOU choose?"
- "Why did they create a plan for the next year instead of just winging it?"

**During Activity (K-1):**
- "What did you like most about our business activities?"
- "What did you make or do that you're proud of?"
- "Did you work with friends? What was that like?"
- "Would you want to have a business when you grow up?"

**During Activity (2-4):**
- "Which activity taught you the most about business?"
- "What was your biggest challenge? How did you solve it?"
- "If you ran a real business, what would you sell?"
- "How did teamwork make business easier or harder?"
- "What would you do differently if you could start over?"

**Post-Activity (Reflection):**
- "What's the difference between revenue and profit? Why does it matter?"
- "Which business skill will help you in real life, outside of business?"
- "What surprised you most about running a business?"
- "If you could give advice to someone starting a business, what would you say?"

**Financial Literacy Deepening (3-4):**
- "Why do the friends calculate earnings per hour, not just total profit?"
- "Pottery made $748 while journals made $62. Should they stop making journals? Why or why not?"
- "What does 'sustainable growth' mean? Why did the friends choose that over getting bigger fast?"
- "The friends made $858 profit together but only $14.50 each when working alone. What does that teach us about partnerships?"
- "What are some costs the friends DIDN'T include in their calculations? (Their time building the fort, marketing effort, etc.)"
- "If you were their business advisor, what would you recommend for next year?"

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Simple reflection: draw favorite activity, circle happy/sad face
- One-word or one-sentence learning ("I learned: teamwork")
- Focus on feelings: "Did you like being in business?"
- Visual displays with lots of pictures, minimal words
- Teacher scribes if needed

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- Sentence frames for reflection: "I learned that businesses need ___. My favorite part was ___."
- Simple financial concepts: more money IN than OUT = profit
- Picture-based graphs with teacher support
- Vocabulary: business, customer, profit, teamwork, plan

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- Paragraph reflection on 2-3 key lessons
- Simple calculations: revenue - expenses = profit
- Create basic bar graphs comparing products/results
- Understanding that business teaches life skills
- Vocabulary: revenue, expenses, sustainable, partnership, strategy

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- Detailed written reflection covering financial AND personal growth
- Multi-step calculations: profit margin, earnings per hour, comparative analysis
- Create multiple graph types (bar, pie, line) to show data
- Strategic thinking: evaluate options, justify decisions
- Advanced vocabulary: profit margin, overhead, strategic planning, diversification, scalability

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual timeline of season activities with photos
- Bilingual vocabulary cards: business/negocio, profit/ganancia, team/equipo
- Sentence frames for presentation practice
- Partner with fluent speaker for presentation
- Allow drawing + labeling instead of full sentences

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Pre-made templates with fill-in-blanks
- Option to present to teacher only instead of whole class
- Visual reflection cards (happy/frustrated/proud faces) instead of writing
- Extended time for poster creation
- Simplified financial calculations or use calculator
- Option to create digital presentation if writing is challenging

**For Advanced Students:**
- Calculate profit margins, not just profit totals
- Create comprehensive business analysis report
- Compare class businesses to real-world companies
- Design detailed year-long strategic plan with quarterly goals
- Research and present on real young entrepreneurs
- Lead awards ceremony or serve as "investor judges"

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 12 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **Comprehensive Financial Analysis**
   - Core Lesson: Successful businesses track revenue, expenses, and profit over time
   - Activity Connection: Students calculate and visualize financial performance
   - Age-Appropriate: K-1 grasp "more money in than out"; 3-4 calculate actual profit margins and per-hour earnings

2. **Data-Driven Decision Making**
   - Core Lesson: Use real numbers to evaluate which products/strategies work best
   - Activity Connection: Analyze which "products" (activities) were most successful and why
   - Age-Appropriate: Compare simple results → create graphs → draw conclusions from data

3. **Non-Financial Value of Business**
   - Core Lesson: Business teaches skills beyond money (leadership, teamwork, resilience, boundaries)
   - Activity Connection: Reflection explicitly addresses "what did you learn besides money?"
   - Age-Appropriate: All ages can identify personal growth through guided questions

4. **Strategic Planning and Sustainability**
   - Core Lesson: Businesses must choose direction intentionally — grow fast, stay sustainable, or close
   - Activity Connection: Students evaluate options and make strategic choices based on values
   - Age-Appropriate: Simple choices (keep going or stop) → complex strategic analysis with tradeoffs

5. **Return on Investment (Time and Money)**
   - Core Lesson: Calculate what you earned per hour of work, not just total dollars
   - Activity Connection: Compare effort invested across season to outcomes achieved
   - Age-Appropriate: 3-4 can calculate actual per-hour earnings; K-2 grasp "was it worth the time?"

**Non-Moralistic Approach:**
- Activity celebrates ALL outcomes — profit AND learning, success AND struggle
- No shame for "low profit" businesses — focus on lessons learned
- Acknowledges that some students may choose NOT to pursue business, and that's valid
- Emphasizes multiple definitions of success beyond just making money
- Honors different values (Option A isn't wrong, just different from friends' choice)

**Connection to Season Arc:**
- Culminates Season 4's business operations journey
- Synthesizes all previous activities into coherent narrative
- Connects back to CH1 (working solo vs. together) showing full growth arc
- Prepares students to apply FL concepts beyond the classroom

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Collect data throughout the season if possible (sales logs, production counts, time tracking)
- Take photos during previous activities to include in final displays
- Create sample business expo display to show as model
- Prepare reflection prompts appropriate to grade level
- Decide on award categories ahead of time to ensure every student receives recognition

**Classroom Management:**
- Set clear expectations for expo presentations (time limits, respectful listening)
- Create presentation schedule to avoid chaos
- Use timer to keep presentations on track
- Designate workspace for poster creation to manage supplies
- Plan for varying work speeds — have extension activities for early finishers

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| "We didn't track data all season" | Class didn't complete previous activity simulations | Use estimates or focus on qualitative reflections rather than exact numbers |
| "I don't remember what we did" | Difficulty with long-term recall | Show photos, review activity titles, use class timeline as memory prompt |
| "My business wasn't successful" | Perfectionism or comparison to peers | Reframe: "Failure = learning. What did you discover? That's valuable data." |
| "I'm nervous about presenting" | Public speaking anxiety | Allow paired presentations, smaller audience, or alternative formats (video, written report) |
| Students comparing results competitively | Natural competitive instinct | Emphasize: everyone learned different lessons; success = growth, not just profit |
| Running out of time for all presentations | Too many students/too little time | Do gallery walk instead, or spread presentations over two days |
| Disagreement among partners about what to present | Different perspectives on journey | Mediate: each person labels their contribution. All partners sign. |
| Students want to skip reflection and just make posters | Preference for hands-on over thinking | Explain: the thinking IS the learning; poster just shows your thinking |

**Time Management:**
- Financial calculations always take longer than expected — build in buffer
- For 45-min version, consider doing calculations one day, displays another day
- Set timer for each activity section to stay on track
- Have a "minimum viable product" in mind if time runs short
- Early finishers can help others or add decorative elements

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pre-fill financial templates for struggling students
- Allow verbal presentations instead of written for students with writing challenges
- Provide graphic organizers for reflection prompts
- Let advanced students add complexity (more graphs, deeper analysis, strategic recommendations)
- Pair students strategically for peer support

## SAFETY NOTES

**Physical Safety:**
- **Display Boards:** Ensure tri-fold boards are stable and won't fall on students
- **Scissors/Art Supplies:** Standard classroom safety for cutting and crafting
- **Movement:** Manage expo foot traffic to prevent collisions during gallery walks
- **Allergens:** If using any food items in displays from previous activities, note allergens

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Comparison Anxiety:** Proactively address that everyone's business journey is unique; comparing = learning, not competing
- **Presentation Nerves:** Offer alternatives for extremely anxious students (written report, video, smaller audience)
- **"Failure" Framing:** Reframe struggles as valuable learning experiences
- **Privacy:** Students can choose what personal reflections to share publicly vs. keep private
- **Inclusivity:** Ensure awards categories allow EVERY student to be recognized for something

**General:**
- Supervision during all activities
- Clear expectations for respectful audience behavior during presentations
- Plan for what to do with displays afterward (take home, hallway display, portfolio)
- Have backup plan for absent students on expo day

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| "We didn't do any businesses this season" | Class didn't complete previous activity simulations | Frame as "imaginary business" or focus on what students learned from the STORY rather than doing |
| Student fixated on "my business failed" | Perfectionism or comparison to peers | Reframe strongly: "Failure = learning. What did you discover? That's valuable data." Share real entrepreneurs who failed first |
| Blank page syndrome during reflection | Difficulty with metacognition | Provide specific sentence starters: "I used to think... Now I think..." "The hardest part was... I solved it by..." |
| Unequal contribution in team displays | Different work speeds/investment levels | Address directly: "Each person labels their contribution. All partners sign." OR allow individual displays |
| Student wants to redo entire season differently | Regret over choices made | Validate: "That shows growth! What you'd do differently = what you learned. Write that down." |
| Too many students want to present at once | Enthusiasm (good problem!) | Create presentation schedule, gallery walk format, or spread over two days |
| Student's presentation is too short/long | Inexperience with time management | Practice with timer, use visual timer during presentations, adjust expectations by grade |
| Comparing profit amounts competitively | Natural competitive instinct | Redirect: "Profit isn't the goal — LEARNING is. What lessons did high-profit and low-profit businesses teach us?" |

## EXTENSIONS & VARIATIONS

**For Early Finishers:**
1. Create "Advice Book for Future Entrepreneurs" with tips learned
2. Design award categories and vote ballots for class
3. Interview classmates about their businesses (reporter role)
4. Calculate class-wide totals: total revenue, total profit, average per student
5. Create timeline showing season progression from CH1 to CH12

**For Advanced Learners:**
1. Complete profit margin analysis (profit ÷ revenue × 100 = profit margin %)
2. Research real young entrepreneurs and create comparison presentation
3. Develop detailed 12-month strategic plan with quarterly milestones
4. Write business advice column: "Dear Young Entrepreneur..." with Q&A
5. Create video documentary of season journey
6. Design survey to assess customer satisfaction and analyze results
7. Calculate return on investment (ROI) for time and money invested

**For Additional Days/Sessions:**
- **Day 1:** Financial calculations and analysis
- **Day 2:** Display creation
- **Day 3:** Presentation practice and peer feedback
- **Day 4:** Business Expo event with families invited
- **Extension Week:** Implement "next year's plan" — actually continue simplified businesses

**Cross-Grade Variations:**
- Partner with younger grades to present business lessons
- Invite older students as "business mentors" to give feedback
- Create school-wide expo with all grades participating
- Alumni business owners visit to share real experiences

**Seasonal Variations:**
- **End of Quarter:** Quarterly business reviews throughout year
- **End of Semester:** Half-year reflection and mid-course correction
- **End of Year:** Full year review synthesizing all four seasons
- **Career Day:** Incorporate into school career exploration events

## CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT ALIGNMENT

**Layla (Leader/Organizer):**
- Story shows Layla facilitating reflection and strategic planning → Activity mirrors structured review process
- Students embody Layla's data-driven decision-making approach
- Reinforces that good leaders use information, not just instinct

**Riley (Creative/Emotional):**
- Story shows Riley discovering she can be artist AND entrepreneur → Activity celebrates both creative and business success
- Students experience Riley's integration of passion and profit
- Validates that "doing what you love" can also make money

- Story shows Ellis keeping detailed logs and tracking hours → Activity includes organized documentation and analysis

- Reinforces that good records = good decisions

**Benny (Enthusiastic/Social):**
- Story shows Benny learning to say "not this week" → Activity includes reflection on boundaries and capacity
- Students feel Benny's growth from overcommitting to thoughtful limits
