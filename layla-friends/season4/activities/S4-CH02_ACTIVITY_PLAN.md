# CHAPTER 2: THE BUSINESS PLAN - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Planning before action
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where friends create a real business plan with Mr. Mason's help — learning that a plan on paper prevents chaos in practice
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Write Your Mini Business Plan"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-2:** Friends choose business name and Layla insists on planning first → Activity begins with naming and planning discussion
- **Scenes 3-6:** Mr. Mason teaches PRICE - COST = PROFIT formula and emergency funds → Activity includes pricing math and reserve planning
- **Scenes 7-9:** Friends list products, calculate materials costs, and set prices → Activity mirrors this product planning process
- **Scenes 10-13:** Frances asks about customers, friends define roles → Activity includes customer identification and role assignment
- **Scenes 14-18:** Money pooling, jar system, order forms, pricing sheets → Activity creates simplified versions of these business tools
- **Scenes 19-20:** Mr. Mason approves their plan, map glows with readiness → Activity concludes with plan presentation

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand the profit formula (Price - Cost = Profit)
2. **Business Planning:** Learn that planning prevents problems before they start
3. **Cost Calculation:** Practice adding up materials to find true cost
4. **Pricing Strategy:** Learn to set prices that cover costs AND pay for your time
5. **Role Assignment:** Discover that teams work better when everyone has a clear job
6. **Emergency Planning:** Understand why businesses need reserve funds for unexpected costs
7. **Customer Focus:** Identify who needs your product and why they'll buy it

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Fill-in-the-Blank Business Plan Template

**Materials Needed:**
- Simple one-page business plan template (photocopied or drawn on board)
- Pencils or crayons
- Optional: example business names on cards for inspiration

**Steps:**
1. **Business Name (2 min):** Students work with partner to create a fun business name. Share 2-3 examples with class.
2. **What We Sell (3 min):** Fill in: "Our business sells ___ because ___." Draw a picture of their product.
3. **Price Math (3 min):** Teacher models: "If it costs $2 to make and we charge $5, we make $3 profit." Students fill in one simple example.
4. **Roles (1 min):** Circle who does what: maker / seller / organizer / deliverer
5. **Share (1 min):** Turn to partner and read your business plan aloud.

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Why did Mr. Mason want the friends to make a plan?"
- "What's the difference between cost and price?"
- "Why does everyone need a job in the business?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** One-Page Business Plan with Pricing Calculations

**Materials Needed:**
- Business plan template with sections: Name, Products, Costs, Prices, Profit, Roles, Customers
- Pencils, colored pencils for illustrations
- Simple calculator or hundreds chart for math support
- Sample cost lists (optional: pretend prices for clay, wood, paper, etc.)

**Steps:**
1. **Name & Product (4 min):** Small groups (3-4 students) choose business name and list 2-3 products they could make. Draw sketches.
2. **Cost Calculation (5 min):** For ONE product, list materials needed and add up costs. Use sample price list or estimate. Teacher circulates to help with addition.
3. **Pricing Decision (4 min):** Discuss: "If it costs $4 to make, what should we charge?" Groups decide on price that makes profit. Calculate profit (Price - Cost = Profit).
4. **Roles Assignment (3 min):** Each group member chooses role based on strengths. Write names next to jobs.
5. **Customer Identification (2 min):** Fill in: "Our customers are ___ because they need ___."
6. **Sharing Circle (2 min):** One group member reads business plan. Class gives one compliment.

**Discussion Questions:**
- "How did you decide what to charge for your product?"
- "What happens if your price is LOWER than your cost?"
- "Why did Layla make a RESERVE jar? What's that for?"
- "How did you decide who does which job?"

**Extension:** Create a simple "Emergency Fund" section where students decide how much to save for problems (10% of profit? $5 per month?).

---

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Business Plan with Multi-Product Pricing and Investor Pitch

**Materials Needed:**
- Comprehensive business plan template with all sections from story
- Calculators
- Sample materials cost sheet (wood: $4, clay: $3, paint: $2, etc.)
- Chart paper or poster board for final presentation
- Markers, colored pencils
- Optional: play money for "investment" simulation

**Steps:**
1. **Business Formation & Naming (3 min):**
   - Teams of 4 form and vote on business name (like friends voted on "Maplewood Makers")
   - Create simple logo or brand mark
2. **Product Development (6 min):**
   - List 3-4 products your business could make
   - For each product, sketch design and list materials needed
   - Use sample cost sheet to price materials
3. **Full Cost Calculation (6 min):**
   - For each product, add up ALL material costs
   - Discuss: "How much is your TIME worth?" Add labor cost (e.g., $2 per hour)
   - Calculate total cost per item including materials AND time
4. **Strategic Pricing (5 min):**
   - Apply profit formula: Price - Cost = Profit
   - Set prices for each product ensuring positive profit
   - Create pricing sheet matching Ellis's format from story
5. **Role Assignment & Customer Analysis (5 min):**
   - Each team member chooses specialty (like friends did: manager, builder, artist, sales/delivery)
   - Identify target customers: Who needs what we make? Why will they buy from us?
   - Write customer profile
6. **Financial Planning (4 min):**
   - Pool imaginary starting money ($60 like in story, or different amount)
   - Allocate to three "jars": COSTS, PROFIT (empty), RESERVE (10%)
   - Create simple budget showing where money will go
7. **Investor Pitch Preparation (3 min):**
   - Prepare 1-minute presentation covering: business name, products, prices, profit potential, team roles
   - Practice pitch within team
8. **Pitch Presentations (3 min total):**
   - Teams present to "investors" (class and teacher)
   - Investors give feedback: Is the plan clear? Does the math work? Would you invest?

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What was the hardest part of making your business plan?"
- "How did calculating costs change your pricing decisions?"
- "In the story, Mr. Mason taught PRICE - COST = PROFIT. Why is this formula so important?"
- "Why do you think Layla wanted order forms and pricing sheets written down?"
- "What could go wrong if you DON'T make a plan first?" (Connect to Benny's mistake in CH1)
- "How does having clear roles prevent confusion and arguments?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Compare your business plan to a real kid business (lemonade stand, craft sale, dog walking)
- Research: What business plans do adult businesses need? (Permits, insurance, marketing plans)
- Create order form template like Layla made
- Calculate: How many products would you need to sell to make $100 profit?

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Very simple template with pictures and fill-in-blanks
- One product only
- Round number costs ($2, $5, $10) for easy mental math
- Teacher does most calculations on board, students copy
- Focus on concepts: "We sell this. It costs this. We charge this."
- Basic vocabulary: business, sell, cost, price, job

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- Template with clear sections and sentence frames
- 2-3 products
- Simple addition for costs (single digits)
- Introduction to profit formula with visual model
- Vocabulary expansion: profit, customer, materials, team, plan
- Beginning to understand that planning prevents problems

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- Multi-section template with calculation spaces
- 3 products with full cost analysis
- Two-digit addition for material costs
- Labor cost introduction (time has value)
- Vocabulary: revenue, expenses, reserve fund, allocation, roles
- Understanding that good planning includes backup plans

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- Comprehensive business plan with all elements from story
- 4+ products with detailed cost breakdown
- Multi-step calculations including labor, materials, overhead
- Profit margin analysis (what percentage profit?)
- Advanced vocabulary: overhead, pricing strategy, target market, capital, profit margin
- Deep understanding of business planning as risk reduction

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual business plan template with icons for each section
- Bilingual vocabulary cards: business/negocio, profit/ganancia, cost/costo, plan/plan
- Partner with fluent speaker for calculation discussions
- Use gestures and drawings to explain concepts
- Sentence frames: "Our business sells ___. It costs ___. We charge ___."

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Simplified template with one section at a time
- Calculator provided for all math
- Pre-populated cost list (no searching/estimating)
- Graphic organizer showing profit formula visually (pie chart or bar model)
- Option to draw instead of write for some sections
- Extended time or reduce number of products

**For Advanced Students:**
- Create detailed multi-page business plan
- Research actual materials costs online or in catalogs
- Calculate break-even point (how many sales to cover startup costs?)
- Add marketing plan, competitive analysis, growth projections
- Present to "investors" with Q&A session
- Calculate different pricing scenarios (what if we charge $10 vs $12?)

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 2 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **The Profit Formula: Price - Cost = Profit**
   - Core Lesson: To make money, what you charge must be MORE than what it costs you
   - Activity Connection: Students practice this formula with real calculations for their products
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete math with specific numbers makes abstract concept tangible

2. **Cost Accounting (All Costs Must Be Counted)**
   - Core Lesson: The true cost includes materials, time, tools, and overhead — not just obvious materials
   - Activity Connection: Students list ALL materials and discuss labor cost
   - Age-Appropriate: Scaffolded from simple material costs (K-1) to comprehensive cost analysis (3-4)

3. **Emergency Funds / Reserve Capital**
   - Core Lesson: Businesses save money for unexpected problems (broken tools, slow months, mistakes)
   - Activity Connection: Students allocate portion of budget to RESERVE jar like in story
   - Age-Appropriate: Concrete example (Mr. Mason's broken tool) makes abstract concept real

4. **Role Specialization in Business**
   - Core Lesson: Clear roles prevent confusion and use everyone's strengths effectively
   - Activity Connection: Students assign roles based on skills and interests
   - Age-Appropriate: Builds on CH1's division of labor lesson with practical application

5. **Customer-Focused Thinking**
   - Core Lesson: Businesses exist to serve customers; knowing who they are and what they need drives decisions
   - Activity Connection: Students identify target customers and explain why they'd buy
   - Age-Appropriate: Personal experience as customers helps students understand business perspective

**Non-Moralistic Approach:**
- Activity presents business planning as practical tool, not moral imperative
- No shame language: "Not planning is irresponsible" ❌ Instead: "Planning helps prevent problems" ✓
- Acknowledges that some learning happens through doing, not just planning
- Celebrates both careful planners AND flexible adapters
- Focuses on skills and critical thinking, not character judgments

**Connection to Season Arc:**
- Foundation for all subsequent chapters: business operates from THIS plan
- Sets up CH3 (implementing the plan = opening for business)
- Pricing decisions here affect CH5 (competition) and CH6 (hidden costs)
- Reserve fund here prevents crisis in CH11 (slow season)
- Makes CH12 reflection possible: look at plan vs. reality

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Review profit formula before teaching: Price - Cost = Profit (or Revenue - Expenses = Profit for older students)
- Prepare sample cost list ahead of time (clay $3, wood $4, paint $2, etc.) to reduce decision fatigue
- Test timing: calculation steps take longer than expected with young students
- Have extra calculators or hundreds charts available for math support
- Consider class size: teams of 3-4 work well for collaboration without chaos

**Classroom Management:**
- Establish clear expectations for team decision-making (everyone's voice counts, majority vote if disagreement)
- Circulate during calculation phase to catch math errors early
- Use visual timer for each section to keep activity moving
- Prepare extension activities for early finishers (design logo, create advertisement, write product descriptions)
- Have exemplar business plan ready to show (but not before they try, to avoid copying)

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "I don't know what to sell!" | Brainstorm class list of products kids can actually make: crafts, snacks, services, art. Post for reference. |
| Students set prices too low (at or below cost) | Redirect: "If it costs $5 to make and you charge $5, what's your profit? Can you pay yourself for your time?" |
| Math mistakes in cost calculation | Provide calculator, hundreds chart, or partner-checking system. Emphasize that mistakes are okay — this is practice! |
| Team can't agree on business name | Set timer (2 minutes to suggest, 1 minute to vote). Teacher breaks ties if needed. Name is less important than plan. |
| Students skip reserve fund / emergency money section | Connect to story: "Remember Mr. Mason's broken saw blade? That's why we save emergency money. What if YOUR tool breaks?" |
| Unrealistic product choices | Guide toward products kids could actually make with supervision: baked goods, crafts, art, services like dog-walking |
| Students finish planning in 5 minutes | Check for depth: Did you calculate costs? Set prices? Choose roles? Identify customers? Add more products if too simple. |

**Time Management:**
- Calculation phase always takes longer than planned — build in buffer time
- For 20-min version, have template pre-filled partially to save time
- If running long, pause after planning and do presentations next day
- Set timers for each section and give "2-minute warning" before transitions
- Have extension work ready for teams that finish early

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pair struggling math students with strong calculators (but ensure both participate)
- Provide pre-populated cost lists for students who struggle with decision-making
- Allow drawing instead of writing for product descriptions
- Let advanced students add sections (marketing plan, competition analysis, growth strategy)
- Offer one-on-one support during calculation phase for students who need it

## SAFETY NOTES

**Physical Safety:**
- If students use scissors or other tools for logo/product design, ensure age-appropriate tools and supervision
- Keep workspace organized to prevent tripping over materials
- Ensure adequate space between teams for focused work

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Normalize Math Mistakes:** Business planning involves lots of calculations — mistakes are learning opportunities
- **No Shame Around "Bad Ideas":** All business ideas are valid starting points; planning helps refine them
- **Team Dynamics:** Monitor for dominating voices or excluded members; ensure all students contribute
- **Competitive Spirit:** If multiple teams present, frame as "learning from each other" not "winning/losing"
- **Real-World Context:** Acknowledge that real businesses often fail in planning phase — that's why adults hire consultants and accountants

**General:**
- Supervise team work to ensure equitable participation
- Have conflict resolution process ready for teams who disagree
- Validate that planning is hard work — slower students aren't failing, they're thinking deeply
- Celebrate diverse business ideas without judgment

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Students want to skip planning and "just start" | Planning feels boring compared to doing | Connect to story: "Benny wanted to start too! But Layla said plan first. What happened when friends worked without a plan in CH1?" |
| Pricing at cost or below | Don't understand profit must cover time + materials | Visual model: "You spend $5 on materials + 2 hours of your time. If you charge $5, you worked for FREE. Is that fair?" |
| Can't think of realistic products | Lack of entrepreneurial experience | Brainstorm class list: cookies, art, dog walking, car wash, craft items, services. Post for reference. |
| Team conflict over business name | Everyone wants their idea to win | Democratic vote with 2-minute time limit. Emphasize: name matters less than plan quality. |
| Math frustration during cost calculation | Multi-digit addition is challenging | Provide calculators, hundreds charts, or partner systems. Emphasize effort over accuracy. |
| Forgetting reserve fund section | Seems unimportant compared to product planning | Storytelling: "What if your tool breaks like Mr. Mason's saw blade? Or clay runs out? Or winter comes and nobody buys?" |
| Unrealistic cost estimates | No real-world pricing knowledge | Provide sample cost sheet OR have students research online/in catalogs (with supervision) |
| Speed disparities (some finish in 5 min, some in 20 min) | Varied processing speeds and detail orientation | Extension activities for early finishers; break plan into phases so slower teams can finish core sections |

1. Create multi-page business plan with executive summary
2. Research actual materials costs from online catalogs
