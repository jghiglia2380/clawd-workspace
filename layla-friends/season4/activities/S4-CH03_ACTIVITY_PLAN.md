# CHAPTER 3: OPEN FOR BUSINESS - ACTIVITY PLAN

**Chapter Theme:** Running a classroom store and tracking revenue
**Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where Maplewood Makers officially opens, makes their first sales, tracks profits, and learns about customer relationships and revenue patterns
**Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10)
**Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency)

---

## ACTIVITY TITLE
**"Grand Opening: Run a Classroom Store"**

---

## STORY CONNECTION

**Explicit Scene Links:**
- **Scenes 1-3:** Opening for business and making first sale (shelf for Mrs. Torres) → Activity begins with store opening and first transactions
- **Scenes 4-7:** Using the money jar routine, calculating profit, handling tips → Activity includes revenue tracking and profit calculation
- **Scenes 8-9:** Riley's mugs delivered, customer happiness matters → Activity emphasizes customer satisfaction alongside sales

---

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand revenue (money coming in) vs. profit (after costs)
2. **Transaction Skills:** Practice making sales, counting money, and giving change
3. **Record-Keeping:** Learn to track every transaction systematically
4. **Customer Service:** Experience how happy customers drive business success
5. **Data Analysis:** Identify patterns in sales (which products sell? when? to whom?)
6. **Business Awareness:** Recognize that diverse customer base reduces risk

## THREE TIME OPTIONS

### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity)
**Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes

**Activity:** Practice Buy & Sell Transactions

**Materials Needed:**
- 5-10 simple items (classroom supplies, book fair books, or pretend items)
- Play money (paper bills in $1, $5, $10 denominations)
- Paper for simple transaction log
- Pencils

**Steps:**
1. **Setup (1 min):** Display 5 items with clear price tags ($1-$10). Teacher is the "shopkeeper."
2. **Practice Buying (4 min):** Students take turns buying one item. Practice: "I'd like to buy this." Count out money. Shopkeeper counts and says "Thank you."
3. **Role Reversal (3 min):** Student volunteers become shopkeepers. Others shop. Focus on polite transactions.
4. **Quick Count (1 min):** Count total money in "register." How much did the store make?
5. **Reflection (1 min):** "What did you buy? Was it easy or hard to count money?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "How did it feel to be the shopkeeper?"
- "Did you count the money correctly?"
- "What would happen if we didn't write anything down?"

---

### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity)
**Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling
**Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes

**Activity:** Rotating Store with Transaction Logs

**Materials Needed:**
- 15-20 items with price tags (mix of low $2-5 and high $10-15 prices)
- Play money for each "customer" ($20 starting budget)
- Transaction log sheets (columns: Item Sold, Price, Customer Name)
- "Store Register" box
- Pencils

**Steps:**
1. **Store Setup (3 min):** Arrange items in "store" area. Assign 2-3 students as first shopkeepers. Others get $20 in play money.
2. **Shopping Period 1 (6 min):** Customers shop. Shopkeepers make sales, count money, record each transaction on log. Practice making change.
3. **Rotation (1 min):** New shopkeepers take over. Previous shopkeepers become customers.
4. **Shopping Period 2 (6 min):** Continue sales with new shopkeepers. Track everything.
5. **Store Close & Count (3 min):** Total all transactions from logs. Count money in register. Do they match?
6. **Reflection (1 min):** Share: "What sold the most? What didn't sell? Why?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "Which items were easiest to sell? Why do you think that is?"
- "Did the total money match your transaction log?"
- "What was hardest about being the shopkeeper?"
- "How did you decide what to buy?"
- "What happened when you ran out of money?"

**Extension:** Graph which items sold the most using tally marks or bar graph.

---

### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity)
**Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration
**Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes

**Activity:** Complete Store Operation with Profit Analysis

**Materials Needed:**
- 25-30 items organized by "department" (books, supplies, crafts, toys)
- Price tags (range $1-20)
- Play money ($30 per customer)
- Detailed transaction logs (columns: Time, Item, Price, Cost, Profit, Customer)
- "Cost sheets" showing what each item "cost the store" to stock
- Store register box
- Calculator (optional)
- "Store Manager Reports" worksheet

**Steps:**
1. **Store Organization (5 min):**
   - Set up 4 departments with clear signage
   - Assign roles: 2 shopkeepers per shift, 1 "accountant" tracking logs, rest are customers
   - Each item has TWO tags: selling price (front) and cost to store (back)
2. **Opening Day Shift 1 (8 min):**
   - Customers shop across all departments
   - Shopkeepers make sales and give change
   - Accountant records every transaction including COST of item
   - Calculate profit per sale (Price - Cost = Profit)
3. **Shift Change (2 min):** New team takes over store. Count register, hand off logs.
4. **Shift 2 (8 min):** Continue operations with new staff
5. **Store Closing Procedures (7 min):**
   - Count total revenue (all money collected)
   - Calculate total costs (add up costs from transaction logs)
   - Calculate profit: Revenue - Costs = Profit
   - Compare to story: "Just like Layla calculated $93 revenue - $22 costs = $71 profit"
6. **Data Analysis (5 min):**
   - Which department made the most revenue?
   - Which items sold out? Which didn't sell at all?
   - Did we have enough customers? Too many?
   - What would we change for next week?

**Discussion Questions:**
- "What's the difference between revenue and profit?"
- "In the story, 2 big jobs (shelf and mugs) were $63 of their $93 total. Why did Layla worry about that?"
- "What if a customer is unhappy? How does that affect future business?"
- "Why is it important to track EVERY transaction, not just the big ones?"
- "If you were running a real store, what would you sell?"
- "How did tips work in the story? Should tips go to profit or split among workers?"

**Extension Ideas:**
- Create a "Store Report" poster showing revenue, costs, profit
- Graph sales by department or time of day
- Compare Week 1 (this activity) to Week 2 (run the store again next week)
- Research real store operations: inventory, employees, customer service
- Calculate: if this were real money, what could the profit buy?

## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES

**By Reading Level (Tier):**

**Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):**
- Simple transactions with teacher support: "This costs $5. Can you count 5 dollars?"
- Focus on identifying money amounts and polite exchanges
- Pre-counting items in register: "We started with 10 dollars, now we have 15"
- Basic vocabulary: buy, sell, cost, money, customer, store
- Visual supports: money chart showing bills

**Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):**
- Practice making change with teacher guidance
- Simple transaction logs: item name and price only
- Counting total sales at end: add up all prices
- Vocabulary expansion: price, change, total, register, receipt
- Understanding: money in = revenue

**Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):**
- Independent change-making with simple amounts
- Transaction logs include customer names and running total
- Introduction to costs vs. price: "This sold for $10 but cost us $3 to stock"
- Vocabulary: revenue, cost, profit, transaction, inventory
- Calculate simple profit: $10 - $3 = $7 profit

**Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):**
- Complex transactions with change from $20 or $50 bills
- Full transaction logs with time, item, customer, cost, price, profit
- Calculate total revenue, total costs, net profit for entire store
- Analyze patterns: peak shopping times, popular items, slow sellers
- Advanced vocabulary: revenue, expenses, net profit, margin, customer retention
- Strategic thinking: "If we only sell to 2 customers like in the story, what's the risk?"

**For English Language Learners:**
- Visual price tags with pictures and numbers
- Bilingual money vocabulary: dollar/dólar, change/cambio, buy/comprar, sell/vender
- Sentence frames: "This costs ___." "I would like to buy ___." "Here is your change: ___."
- Partner with fluent speaker for first transactions
- Calculator available to check addition

**For Students with Learning Differences:**
- Pre-counted money bags (exactly right amount for one purchase, no change needed)
- Transaction log with visual supports (draw the item, circle the price)
- "Store Helper" role: organize items, greet customers, don't handle money if that's challenging
- Quiet shopping time before busy period if sensory needs
- Visual steps poster: 1. Customer chooses, 2. Count money, 3. Say thank you, 4. Write it down

**For Advanced Students:**
- Run the "accounting department" — track all transactions and calculate final profit
- Create efficiency improvements: organize items by price, create "fast checkout" lane
- Analyze data: create graphs of sales by time or category
- Design promotional strategies: "Sale on books!" or "Buy 2, get discount"
- Compare to real stores: research how Target or local shops operate

## FL INTEGRATION

**Chapter 3 Financial Literacy Concepts:**

1. **Revenue vs. Profit**
   - Core Lesson: Revenue is money coming in; profit is what's left after costs
   - Activity Connection: Students track total sales (revenue) AND item costs to calculate profit
   - Age-Appropriate: K-1 track "money we made," 3-4 calculate Revenue - Costs = Profit explicitly
   - Story Link: Layla's calculation in Scene 13: "$93 revenue - $22 costs = $71 profit"

2. **Transaction Tracking & Record-Keeping**
   - Core Lesson: Writing down every sale creates accurate financial records
   - Activity Connection: Every student transaction is logged, then totaled and verified
   - Age-Appropriate: Simple logs for younger students, detailed multi-column logs for older
   - Story Link: Layla "tracked every dollar" and listed "each job and payment" (Scenes 12-13)

3. **Customer Satisfaction Drives Business**
   - Core Lesson: Happy customers return and tell others (word of mouth)
   - Activity Connection: Polite service and quality products make customers want to return
   - Age-Appropriate: Experiential — students feel the difference between good and poor service
   - Story Link: "Mrs. Torres told her sister about us" (Scene 5), "Making customers happy felt as good as earning money" (Scene 9)

4. **Revenue Patterns & Customer Dependency**
   - Core Lesson: Relying on a few big customers is risky; diverse customer base is safer
   - Activity Connection: Data analysis shows which products/customers generated most revenue
   - Age-Appropriate: Observational for K-2, explicit analysis for 3-4 with graphs
   - Story Link: Scenes 15-17 where Layla notices "$63 of $93 came from just two jobs" and realizes risk

**Non-Moralistic Approach:**
- Activity presents running a store as practice, not judgment on any business model
- No shame language: "Big stores are greedy" ❌ Instead: "Stores track sales to know what customers want" ✓
- Acknowledges all businesses (big and small) use these same principles
- Focuses on skills (counting, tracking, customer service) not character judgments
- Celebrates successful transactions regardless of amount

**Connection to Season Arc:**
- Foundation for Season 4's business operations theme
- Introduces record-keeping used throughout the season
- First real experience with revenue and profit calculations
- Sets up customer service concepts explored in CH4

## TEACHER TIPS

**Preparation:**
- Decide: real items students keep, or borrowed items returned after?
- Create or print play money 2-3 days ahead
- Set clear "store hours" so students know when they're customers vs. staff
- If using real items, ask families to donate gently used books/supplies
- Create simple transaction log template (sample on classroom document camera)
- Establish "store rules" before opening: polite language, careful with items, honest transactions

**Classroom Management:**
- Use a bell or signal for "store opening" and "store closing"
- Limit number of customers shopping at once (3-5) to prevent chaos
- Assign specific roles: shopkeeper, accountant, organizer, customers
- Rotate roles every 5-10 minutes so everyone experiences each position
- Circulate during shopping to support transactions and model counting
- Have a "manager" (teacher or advanced student) to handle problems

**Common Challenges & Solutions:**

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| "I can't make change!" | Provide change-making chart (price $7, paid $10, change $3). Practice with teacher support. Use calculator if needed. |
| Students spend all money immediately | Discuss budgeting: "Save some money for later!" Optional: 2 shopping periods so they practice pacing. |
| Transaction logs are messy/incomplete | Use pre-printed templates with clear boxes. Model first transaction together as class. Check logs periodically. |
| Money in register doesn't match logs | Perfect teaching moment! Find the error together. Discuss importance of accuracy. This is why real businesses reconcile daily. |
| Some items never sell | Great discussion point: "Why didn't anyone buy this? Price too high? Don't need it? Discuss supply/demand. |
| Disagreements over who's turn it is | Use timer (3 min per customer) and numbered rotation list posted on board. |
| Students hoard popular items | Limit purchases: 2 items per person in first round, then open if money remains. Discuss fairness. |
| Counting money takes too long | For younger students: pre-price items in round numbers ($1, $5, $10) to simplify. Have extra staff help count. |

**Time Management:**
- Setup takes longer than expected with young students — build in 5-minute buffer
- For 20-min version, have items pre-priced and store set up before story
- Use timer visible to students so they know when shopping periods end
- If running long, pause and continue next day (real stores operate multiple days!)
- Have extension ready for students who finish shopping early (count their purchases, draw favorite item)

**Differentiation in Practice:**
- Pair less confident students with "store buddy" for first transactions
- Allow students with math anxiety to use calculator for change-making
- Let students who struggle with crowds shop during quieter period
- Advanced students run accounting desk, tracking all logs and doing final calculations
- Visual supports: money value chart, transaction log example, step-by-step checkout poster

## SAFETY NOTES

**Money Handling:**
- **Supervision:** Monitor all money handling to ensure honesty and accuracy
- **Small Parts:** If using coin play money, ensure no choking hazards for young students
- **Lost Money:** Establish rule: if play money is lost, store pauses until found (teaches responsibility)
- **Real Money:** Do NOT use real money unless this is connected to actual fundraiser with admin approval

**Item Safety:**
- **Inspect Items:** Check all "products" for safety (no sharp edges, broken parts, small choking hazards)
- **Allergies:** No food items in store unless all allergies are checked and accounted for
- **Ownership:** Clear rules about what happens to items after activity (kept, returned, donated)
- **Disputes:** Have plan for "I changed my mind" after purchase (discuss return policies beforehand)

**Emotional Safety:**
- **Budget Limits:** All students get equal starting money so no one is left out
- **Sold Out Items:** Discuss fairness when popular items run out — first come, first served
- **Peer Pressure:** Monitor that students aren't pressured to buy things they don't want
- **Winning/Losing:** Emphasize learning over competition (no "who sold the most" unless celebrating everyone)

**General:**
- Supervision during all transactions
- Clear start and end times to prevent store "running" during other lessons
- Accessible materials (no climbing to reach items)
- Hand washing if food/snacks involved in celebration

## COMMON CHALLENGES

| Challenge | Why It Happens | Solution |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Money doesn't balance at end | Counting errors; unrecorded sales; lost bills | Pause and recount together. Check logs against money. Teaching moment: this is why cashiers count carefully. |
| Students rush through transactions | Excitement; want to buy everything fast | Use timer per customer (3 min). Model slow, careful counting. Praise accuracy over speed. |
| Transaction logs incomplete | Too much happening; forgot to write | Assign dedicated "accountant" student. Pause after each sale to record. Use simple templates. |
| Popular items sell out immediately | High demand, limited supply | Limit 1 per customer at first. Discuss supply/demand: popular things run out. Restock if possible. |
| Some students never get to be shopkeeper | Uneven rotation; time runs out | Use sign-up board or numbered rotation. Timer per role. Ensure everyone gets a turn. |
| Confusion over who owes change | Both student and shopkeeper uncertain | Model first transaction together. Use "change-making" formula poster on wall. Teacher checks first few. |
| Students want to "steal" items without paying | Testing boundaries; excitement | Clear rules before starting: all items require payment. Store closes if stealing happens. Praise honesty. |
| Prices seem unfair/too high | Student perception of value differs | Discuss: who sets prices? What makes prices fair? Connect to real stores. |
| Arguments over "who's next in line" | No clear system | Use numbered tickets or line formation. First come, first served. Post "Please wait your turn" sign. |

## EXTENSIONS & VARIATIONS

**For Early Finishers:**
1. Count how many items are left in each "department"
2. Draw your favorite item from the store
3. Help restock and organize items
4. Interview customers: "What did you buy? Why did you choose it?"
5. Create advertisement poster for an item that didn't sell

**For Advanced Learners:**
1. Run the accounting department: track ALL transactions and reconcile at end
2. Create data visualization: bar graph of "Items Sold by Category"
3. Calculate profit margin: (Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100 = ____%
4. Research real business: visit a local store owner or interview them via video
5. Write "Store Manager Report" summarizing day's performance and recommendations
6. Design "Sale" promotions to move slow-selling items
7. Calculate: if we ran this store every day for a month, projected revenue?

**For Additional Days/Sessions:**
- **Day 2:** Run store again with different items OR same items at adjusted prices based on Day 1 data
- **Week-long Store:** Open store 10 minutes daily all week, track cumulative revenue and profit
- **Market Day:** Expand to full classroom market with multiple "businesses" competing
- **Seasonal Store:** Rotate inventory monthly (fall: school supplies, winter: gift items, spring: garden seeds)

**Seasonal Variations:**
- **Fall:** School supply store
