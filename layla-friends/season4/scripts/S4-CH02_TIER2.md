## THE BUSINESS PLAN
### Scene 1 (53 words)
The friends gathered at the fort on Monday morning to begin their partnership. "We need a name," said Riley excitedly. Everyone suggested ideas. Benny liked "The Fix-It Four." Riley wanted "Creative Corner." Ellis suggested "Maplewood Makers" because they made things in Maplewood. They voted and "Maplewood Makers" won. Everyone felt now.
### Scene 2 (52 words)
"Let's start selling things today!" said Benny eagerly. Ellis nodded in agreement. But Layla shook her head firmly. "We need a plan first," she said seriously. Riley looked uncertain. "Why do we need a plan?" Layla explained about her journal sales failure. "We can't just start. What if we run out of money?" Riley nodded slowly.
### Scene 3 (53 words)
They walked to Mr. Mason's workshop to ask for advice. The workshop smelled like sawdust and oil. Mr. Mason looked up from his workbench with a warm smile. "We want to start a business together," Ellis explained proudly. Mr. Mason set down his tools. "That's wonderful," he said kindly. "What will it cost you to make these things?"
### Scene 4 (52 words)
Mr. Mason opened his workshop ledger and showed them pages of numbers. "Every piece of wood has a cost," he explained while pointing to entries. "Every hour has value." He showed them what he paid for materials. He showed them what he charged customers. The friends studied the numbers carefully and began to understand.
### Scene 5 (53 words)
Mr. Mason wrote on a piece of paper in large letters: PRICE - COST = PROFIT. "This is the most important formula in business," he said seriously. "If you charge ten dollars and it cost you four dollars to make, your profit is six dollars." They practiced with different examples until everyone understood the formula clearly.
### Scene 6 (52 words)
"What happens when things go wrong?" Mr. Mason asked thoughtfully. He told them about a broken tool that stopped his work. He couldn't finish two jobs on time. "That's why you need an emergency fund," he explained. "Save some money for problems you don't expect." Ellis wrote this down in his notebook carefully.
### Scene 7 (53 words)
Back at the fort, they began listing products they could make. Ellis could build shelves and fix broken things. Riley could make pottery mugs and bowls. Layla could create journals and decorated boxes. Benny could help everyone and deliver finished products. They wrote everything down on a large piece of paper to remember.
### Scene 8 (52 words)
"Let's figure out what a shelf costs to make," Ellis suggested while pulling out his calculator. He listed materials needed. Wood cost four dollars. Screws cost one dollar. Sandpaper cost fifty cents. Stain cost fifty cents. "That's six dollars total in materials," he announced. "Now we need to decide what to charge customers."
### Scene 9 (53 words)
"We should charge eight dollars," said Benny quickly. Layla shook her head. "That's only two dollars profit. We should charge twelve dollars." Ellis thought carefully. "What about fifteen dollars? That covers our time working too." They discussed what felt fair to customers and what made the business worth doing. Finally they agreed on fifteen dollars.
### Scene 10 (52 words)
They decided to visit Frances in her garden for more advice. She was picking vegetables when they arrived. "We're planning our business," Layla explained excitedly. Frances smiled warmly. "That's wonderful planning ahead," she said while placing tomatoes in her basket. "But who are your customers? Who needs what you make?" The friends looked at each other.
### Scene 11 (53 words)
They sat on the garden bench and discussed Frances's question seriously. "Maybe kids who need toys?" suggested Benny. "Or adults who need repairs?" said Ellis thoughtfully. "People who want art?" added Riley hopefully. They debated for several minutes. Finally Layla said, "Anyone who needs something made or fixed." Everyone agreed that made the most sense.
### Scene 12 (52 words)
"We need to assign roles," Layla said back at the fort. "Everyone should do what they do best." They discussed each person's strengths honestly. Layla was organized and good with planning. Ellis was skilled at building and fixing. Riley was creative with pottery and design. Benny loved talking to people and making deliveries.
### Scene 13 (53 words)
Ellis made a roles chart on large paper with four columns. Under Layla's name he wrote: Manager, money tracker, planning. Under his own name: Building, repairs, quality control. Under Riley's name: Pottery, design, custom work. Under Benny's name: Sales, delivery, customer contact. They hung it on the fort wall where everyone could see it clearly.
### Scene 14 (52 words)
"Now let's figure out how much money we need to start," Ellis said while making a new list. They needed clay for pottery, wood for shelves, basic tools, paper for journals, and supplies. Ellis added up every item carefully. "Sixty dollars total," he announced. Everyone checked their savings. They had just enough money from allowances and Chapter 1 earnings.
### Scene 15 (53 words)
They pooled their money together on the fort table. Riley contributed fifteen dollars. Ellis added eighteen dollars. Layla put in twelve dollars. Benny added fifteen dollars. They counted it twice to make sure. Exactly sixty dollars. "This is our startup fund," Layla said seriously. Everyone felt the weight of combining their savings into one shared pile.
### Scene 16 (52 words)
Layla suggested creating a money jar system for tracking. They found three clear jars at her house. She made labels on index cards: COSTS, PROFIT, RESERVE. "Fifty dollars goes to costs for materials," she explained while sorting the money. "Ten dollars goes to reserve for emergencies." The PROFIT jar stayed empty for now.
### Scene 17 (53 words)
"We need an order system," Layla said practically. She designed a simple form on paper. It had spaces for customer name, item requested, price agreed, and deadline. "This way we won't forget anything or mix up orders like Benny did last week." Benny nodded sheepishly, remembering his confused money situation from Chapter 1.
### Scene 18 (52 words)
Ellis created the pricing sheet on a large poster board. He made columns: Product, Materials Cost, Time, Price, Profit. He filled in their main products. Shelf: six dollars materials, two hours, fifteen dollars price, nine dollars profit. Mug: two dollars materials, one hour, eight dollars price, six dollars profit. Everything was organized and clear now.
### Scene 19 (53 words)
They returned to Mr. Mason's workshop to show him their completed plan. He studied their pricing sheet, roles chart, and order forms carefully. "This is excellent work," he said with pride. "You've got a real plan now. Do it carefully and track everything you do." Ellis felt proud of the system they had created together.
### Scene 20 (52 words)
That evening, Layla checked the map in her room. The four separate dots had merged into one bright dot at the fort. Thin lines extended toward Mr. Mason's workshop and Frances's garden. The connections were forming. The map seemed to pulse with energy and anticipation. They were ready to begin. Tomorrow, real business would start.

## FL VOCABULARY
- **business** — an organization that makes or sells things to earn money
- **profit** — the money you keep after paying for costs
- **cost** — how much you pay to make something
- **startup** — the beginning phase when you first start a business
- **emergency fund** — money saved for unexpected problems
