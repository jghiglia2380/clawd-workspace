## THE UNHAPPY CUSTOMER

### Scene 1 (50 words)
A woman at the neighborhood café asked Benny about a decorative platter she wanted as a gift. Benny brought the order back to the fort, where Riley's face lit up with excitement. Platters were her favorite thing to make. "This is going to be my best piece yet," she told the others.

### Scene 2 (50 words)
Riley called the customer to discuss the design. "I want something with flowers," the woman said warmly. "You choose what kind. I trust your eye for this." Riley hung up feeling thrilled. Having creative freedom on a paying job felt like the perfect combination of art and business.

### Scene 3 (50 words)
Riley spent the entire next day at her workstation, shaping clay and painting blue morning glories with delicate green vines curling around the edges. She applied her finest glaze and fired the platter twice for extra shine. When it cooled, she held it up and admired her work. It was beautiful.

### Scene 4 (50 words)
On delivery day, Benny carefully wrapped the platter in a soft cloth and headed to the customer's house. Riley stayed behind at the fort, too nervous to go along. She paced between the money jars and the order board. "She is going to love it," she kept telling Layla with confidence.

### Scene 5 (50 words)
Benny returned to the fort carrying the platter in both hands, his expression serious. "She said it is beautiful work," he told Riley slowly, choosing his words with care. "But it is not what she had in mind." Riley stared at the platter in his hands and felt the air leave her chest.

### Scene 6 (50 words)
The customer had wanted roses, not morning glories. She had pictured warm colors like reds and oranges, not cool blues. When she said "flowers," she assumed Riley would know. But Riley had imagined something very different from what the customer expected. The gap between them was just a misunderstanding, but the platter was already made.

### Scene 7 (50 words)
"But it IS beautiful!" Riley said, clutching the platter protectively against her chest. "I worked so hard on this!" Layla waited a moment, then spoke gently. "It is beautiful, Riley. But we are not making art for ourselves right now. We are making what the customer needs. Those are different things."

### Scene 8 (50 words)
Layla opened her notebook and walked Riley through the numbers. The first platter used eight dollars of materials. A second platter would cost another eight. With revenue fixed at eighteen dollars and total costs now at sixteen, the profit would shrink from ten dollars down to just two. Riley winced at the math.

### Scene 9 (50 words)
The team gathered to decide how to handle the situation. They could refund the money, make a new platter, or try to negotiate. Ellis was the first to speak. "Our reputation matters more than the profit from one job," he said firmly. "If we fix this, she will tell people we are fair."

### Scene 10 (50 words)
Riley took a slow breath and straightened up in her chair. "I will make it again," she said quietly. The decision hurt, but she knew it was the right one. She placed the rejected platter gently on the shelf, pulled out fresh clay, and began preparing for the second attempt.

### Scene 11 (50 words)
That evening, Riley walked to Mr. Mason's workshop to talk. She told him what had happened and how bad she felt. Mr. Mason nodded with understanding. "A customer once returned a table I built," he said. "It taught me to ask more questions before starting. Every good craftsman learns this eventually."

### Scene 12 (50 words)
At the next team meeting, Layla held up a small card she had written and proposed a new rule for the order board. It read: CUSTOM ORDERS OVER TEN DOLLARS — SHOW A SKETCH FIRST. "If we get approval on paper before we start, we avoid paying for a mistake."

### Scene 13 (50 words)
Riley made the second platter with red roses and warm orange tones, following the customer's preferences as closely as she could. The roses looked lovely and the colors glowed. But as Riley painted each petal, something felt missing. She was producing what someone else wanted, not creating from her own vision.

### Scene 14 (50 words)
Benny delivered the second platter, and this time he came back to the fort with a genuine smile. "She is thrilled," he reported. The customer paid the full eighteen dollars without hesitation. Riley felt relief wash through her, though a quiet ache remained. Only two dollars of that payment reached the PROFIT jar.

### Scene 15 (50 words)
The rejected morning glory platter sat on the fort shelf, catching light from the window. Riley found herself glancing at it throughout the week. It was still her finest work, made with care and creative freedom. But no customer had asked for it, and the eight dollars of materials were already spent.

### Scene 16 (50 words)
Frances stopped by the fort and spotted the platter right away. "That is a lovely piece," she said, holding it up to the light. "Why not sell it as an artist's choice item? Some customers want to buy what the artist loves, not what they designed themselves." Riley's eyes brightened at the idea.

### Scene 17 (50 words)
Meanwhile, a separate issue came up when a customer mentioned to Ellis that his birdhouse leaned slightly to the right. Ellis walked over to inspect it right away. The base was not level — a small error in the original construction. "I will come back tomorrow and fix it for you," Ellis promised.

### Scene 18 (50 words)
Ellis returned the next morning with his tools and corrected the birdhouse at no charge. The customer watched with surprise. "You did not have to come back and do this for free," he said. Ellis tightened the last screw. "We stand behind everything we make. If it is wrong, we fix it."

### Scene 19 (50 words)
The birdhouse customer told his neighbors about the free repair, and within days two new orders came in from people on the same street. Doing the right thing had generated more business than the original birdhouse. Layla noted the lesson in her notebook: Goodwill creates more value than it costs.

### Scene 20 (50 words)
Riley studied both platters on the shelf at the end of the week. The red roses sat beside the blue morning glories. She was learning to balance two parts of herself — the artist who created from the heart and the business person who served the customer. Both platters mattered in different ways.

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **rework / redo** (Scenes 8, 10) — Having to make a product again, doubling material costs
2. **profit margin** (Scene 8) — The portion of revenue left after subtracting costs
3. **reputation** (Scene 9) — A business's public image based on customer experiences
4. **goodwill** (Scene 19) — Customer loyalty and trust earned by doing the right thing
5. **artist's choice** (Scene 16) — Products created by the maker's own vision, not custom-ordered
