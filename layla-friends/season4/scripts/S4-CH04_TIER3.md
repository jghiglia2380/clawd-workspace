## THE UNHAPPY CUSTOMER
### Scene 1 (66 words)
A woman from the neighborhood cafe approached Riley on Tuesday morning with a special request. "I heard you make beautiful pottery," she said with genuine interest in her voice. "I'd like to commission a decorative platter for my dining room wall, something that really stands out." Riley's eyes lit up immediately, since decorative pieces were her favorite specialty. "I'd love to make that for you," Riley said eagerly. Custom art projects were the kind of work she enjoyed most.
### Scene 2 (65 words)
"What would you like on it?" Riley asked while pulling out her sketchbook, ready to capture every detail. The woman considered the question for a moment before answering. "Something with flowers. You choose the specific design since you're the artist!" she said warmly. Riley heard those words as creative freedom, which excited her. "I'll make something beautiful," Riley promised with growing confidence. The woman agreed to pay eighteen dollars for the finished piece.
### Scene 3 (66 words)
Riley spent hours in her studio designing the perfect platter with focused dedication. She chose blue morning glories winding gracefully around the rim in an elegant spiral pattern. The glaze turned out perfectly, with deep blue tones contrasting against a warm cream background. She worked carefully on every small detail with the kind of intense focus that only her best projects received. "This is my best work yet," she whispered while examining the finished piece under bright studio light.
### Scene 4 (65 words)
Delivery day arrived on Friday, and Benny carefully carried the wrapped platter to the customer's house while Riley waited anxiously at the fort. She felt proud and confident about the work she had produced. Minutes felt like they stretched into hours as she waited for Benny to return with the customer's feedback. She kept imagining the woman's delighted reaction when she unwrapped the platter and saw the beautiful blue flowers.
### Scene 5 (66 words)
Benny returned to the fort looking somber and uncomfortable, avoiding Riley's eyes as he walked through the door. "She said it's beautiful work," he began slowly, choosing his words with obvious care. Riley's hopeful smile started to fade as she noticed his troubled tone. "But it's not what she wanted," Benny finished quietly, setting the returned platter on the table. Riley's heart sank as confusion and defensiveness flooded through her at the same time.
### Scene 6 (65 words)
"She wanted roses, not morning glories," Benny explained carefully while unwrapping the returned platter for everyone to see. "And she wanted warm colors like red or pink, not cool blue tones." Riley stared at the platter in shock, struggling to understand what had gone wrong. "But she said I could choose the design!" Riley protested, feeling hurt by the rejection. "She assumed you'd know her taste somehow," Benny said with sympathy.
### Scene 7 (66 words)
"But it IS beautiful!" Riley insisted, feeling defensive about the careful work she had poured her heart into. Layla spoke gently but with unmistakable firmness in her voice. "It is beautiful, Riley. Nobody is saying otherwise. But beautiful isn't enough if it doesn't match what the customer actually wanted." Riley felt tears threatening behind her eyes because she'd worked so hard and done her very best. "We have to make what they want, not just what we want," Layla explained.
### Scene 8 (65 words)
They discussed their available options at the fort table, weighing each possibility carefully. "Do we give her money back?" asked Benny uncertainly, not sure what the right move would be. Layla checked their financial records with a serious expression. "We already spent eight dollars on materials for this platter. If we refund the full amount, we lose that money entirely." Ellis thought about the situation carefully before speaking up with a suggestion.
### Scene 9 (66 words)
"We should redo it the way she wants," Ellis suggested after careful consideration. "That keeps the customer happy and protects our reputation going forward." But the rework decision meant Riley needed more clay and more glaze for a second platter. Layla calculated the new costs with concern. "The second platter costs another eight dollars in materials, bringing our total to sixteen dollars for one eighteen dollar order." The profit had nearly vanished.
### Scene 10 (65 words)
Riley visited Mr. Mason's workshop feeling discouraged and questioning her abilities as an artist. "Every woodworker has a piece rejected at some point in their career," Mr. Mason said kindly while pouring her a glass of juice. "It's painful when it happens, but it teaches you something valuable: always ask more questions before you start working." His understanding and experience made Riley feel slightly better about the difficult situation she was navigating.
### Scene 11 (66 words)
Back at the fort, Riley started the second platter with a heavy heart but renewed determination. This time she sketched red and pink roses in warm tones, following the customer's preferences exactly as they had been described. The work felt less creative and more like following someone else's instructions, which was hard for an artist who valued personal expression. But she worked carefully anyway, because customer satisfaction mattered more than her own artistic vision this time.
### Scene 12 (65 words)
While Riley worked on the redo, Ellis discovered a separate quality problem with a birdhouse he had recently built for another customer. When hung on a post, the birdhouse leaned noticeably to one side instead of hanging straight. The customer hadn't complained about it yet, but Ellis spotted the flaw himself during a follow-up visit. "I need to fix this before they notice," he told Layla firmly and without hesitation.
### Scene 13 (66 words)
Ellis fixed the birdhouse for free, using his own time and more materials without charging anything extra. When he delivered the corrected version, the customer was surprised by his initiative. "I hadn't even noticed the problem!" she admitted gratefully. "But I appreciate you taking such care with the quality of your work." She was so impressed that she told three neighbors about Ellis's integrity and commitment to quality that very same afternoon.
### Scene 14 (65 words)
Riley finished the second platter on Wednesday, painting roses in warm tones that matched what the customer had originally envisioned. It was beautiful work, just in a different style than Riley would have chosen personally. Benny delivered it carefully, carrying the platter with extra caution after what happened with the first attempt. This time he returned to the fort with a huge relieved smile. "She loves it!" he announced happily. "She said it's perfect."
### Scene 15 (66 words)
The team gathered for a fort meeting to discuss the lessons they had learned during this challenging week. "We need a new rule for how we handle custom work," Layla said seriously while writing on a fresh card. "For custom orders over ten dollars, we should always show a sketch to the customer first before starting any actual work." Everyone agreed without hesitation. Riley felt relieved that sketches would prevent future misunderstandings.
### Scene 16 (65 words)
Layla hung the new rule card prominently on their order board where everyone could see it before starting any project. "Custom orders $10+ — Sketch First" it read in clear bold letters. "This rule protects both us and our customers from miscommunication," Ellis observed wisely, understanding how important shared expectations were. Riley nodded in agreement, grateful for a system that would prevent anyone from repeating the painful experience of working on wrong assumptions.
### Scene 17 (66 words)
Riley looked at the rejected blue morning glory platter sitting quietly in her studio, wondering what to do with something that was beautiful but unwanted. "What should I do with this?" she asked aloud while studying the delicate flowers she had painted with such care. Layla had an idea that surprised everyone. "What if you keep making pieces you choose, and sell them as 'artist's choice' items?" she suggested thoughtfully. "Some customers might prefer your creative vision."
### Scene 18 (65 words)
The idea excited Riley immediately, sparking new possibilities in her mind. "I could have two separate types of work," she said with growing enthusiasm. "Custom pieces where customers choose everything about the design, and artist's choice pieces where I create freely from my own inspiration." Layla smiled at the elegant solution they had found together. "That way you can still make art your way, but customers always know what they're getting from the start."
### Scene 19 (66 words)
Riley displayed the blue platter at the neighborhood cafe with a handwritten sign reading: "Artist's Choice - $15." Within just two days, a different customer bought it eagerly. "I love that you chose blue!" the woman said with enthusiasm. "It matches my kitchen perfectly." Riley learned that the right customer for every piece existed somewhere out there, and she just needed clear communication and proper labeling to connect her work with the people who would appreciate it most.
### Scene 20 (65 words)
That night, Layla checked the map in her room with quiet attention. The platter customer's line, which had flickered and dimmed after the initial rejection, now glowed steady and bright following the successful redo. The birdhouse customer's line burned even brighter after Ellis's free repair. The map showed clearly that problems handled well could actually be fixed, and that good recovery efforts strengthened relationships and built deeper trust over time.

## FL VOCABULARY
- **satisfaction** — the feeling of being happy with what you received
- **expectations** — what someone hopes or thinks will happen
- **rework** — doing the same job over again to correct a problem
- **quality control** — checking work carefully to make sure it meets standards
- **reputation** — what people think and say about your work when you're not there
