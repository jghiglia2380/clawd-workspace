## THE BIG MISTAKE

### Scene 1 (58 words)
Monday morning at the fort began with the steady rhythm of a well-managed operation. The order board displayed four active jobs, each progressing on schedule with materials accounted for and deadlines clearly marked. Layla reviewed the money jars, recorded the current balances in her notebook, and nodded with quiet confidence. "Solid pace for the week," she said. The calm would not last.

### Scene 2 (58 words)
That afternoon, Benny crossed paths with Mrs. Garcia while cutting through the neighborhood park on his way home. She mentioned needing two custom shelves in her hallway before Friday. "We can handle that!" Benny said without a second thought, his natural enthusiasm running ahead of any caution. He loved the rush of landing new customers. He did not stop to check with the team.

### Scene 3 (58 words)
Tuesday morning during school drop-off, Benny struck up conversations with three separate neighbors who each had projects they needed completed. He said yes to every single request, scribbling orders on whatever scraps of paper he could find and cramming them into his jacket pocket. By the time he reached the fort that afternoon, he was carrying seven fresh commitments and wearing a proud grin.

### Scene 4 (58 words)
At the Wednesday team meeting, Benny pulled out the crumpled papers and read each new order aloud with pride. Seven additional jobs stacked on top of the four already in progress made eleven total. The excitement drained from every face in the room except his. Layla's pen froze mid-stroke. "Benny," she said, measuring her words with care, "we can not do all of this."

### Scene 5 (58 words)
Layla walked to the order board and laid out the numbers with precision. "Our realistic capacity is around six orders per week," she explained, tapping the board with her pen. "You committed us to eleven. That is nearly double what we can handle without sacrificing quality or missing deadlines." Benny's excitement crumbled as the math settled over him. He had never considered the limits.

### Scene 6 (58 words)
"But I already promised every one of them!" Benny said, his voice climbing with rising panic. "I can not call them back and say we changed our minds. They are counting on us!" The room fell into a heavy silence. Nobody wanted to break promises to paying customers, but nobody could find a realistic path through eleven orders in five working days.

### Scene 7 (58 words)
After a long and uncomfortable discussion, they made the difficult decision to push through rather than cancel any commitments. Their reputation as reliable makers meant too much to damage. They would extend their hours and move as fast as their hands allowed to deliver everything by the end of the week. "This is going to be brutal," Ellis warned. His prediction would prove generous.

### Scene 8 (58 words)
Thursday and Friday dissolved into a relentless blur of sawing, painting, glazing, and assembling that left no room for the satisfaction they usually found in their craft. The fort had never been louder or messier. Sawdust coated every surface, wet glaze dripped from the overcrowded workbench onto the floor, and half-finished projects competed for every inch of table space. Nobody took a proper break.

### Scene 9 (58 words)
Riley had a custom platter to complete for Mrs. Davis, the kind of detailed piece she normally devoted eight careful hours to perfecting. With the backlog pressing in from every side, she had only three. The finished platter met the basic requirements but fell well short of her best work. "It is fine," Riley said quietly, though her disappointment was obvious to everyone watching.

### Scene 10 (58 words)
Ellis was juggling two separate projects at once when his focus slipped and he measured a shelf board wrong, cutting it three inches shorter than the plan required. The wood was ruined beyond use. He stared at the wasted piece lying on the floor and shook his head slowly. "I was going too fast," he admitted. Starting over cost both materials and precious time.

### Scene 11 (58 words)
Friday afternoon arrived with two orders still due for delivery. One remained unfinished on the workbench. The customer called asking where her completed work was, and Benny had to deliver the bad news himself. "We need one more day," he said, keeping his voice as steady as he could. "I can bring it first thing tomorrow morning." Her tone carried clear disappointment.

### Scene 12 (58 words)
Saturday became a grueling ten-hour marathon that pushed every member of the team past what they thought possible. All four friends worked from seven in the morning straight through to five in the afternoon without meaningful rest. By the final hour, their hands ached, their eyes felt heavy with fatigue, and conversation had ceased. They delivered the last order as the sun set.

### Scene 13 (58 words)
The late delivery went out on Sunday morning instead of the promised Saturday. The customer accepted her order but gave Benny a look that stayed with him for the rest of the day. "I expected this yesterday," she said in a flat voice that carried more weight than anger. Benny apologized and handed over the finished work. The walk home felt very long.

### Scene 14 (58 words)
Back at the fort, they gathered around the money jars and counted the week's earnings in near silence. One hundred and thirty-two dollars in total revenue — their highest figure since the business began. But the celebration never arrived. Riley sat with her head resting on the table. Ellis rubbed his aching hands. Even Benny, who usually found reason to celebrate anything, stayed quiet.

### Scene 15 (58 words)
Layla flipped to a fresh notebook page and worked through the full accounting with precision. Total hours worked across the team came to fifty-two — thirteen hours per person over the week. Materials wasted from rushing and errors added twelve dollars to their costs. Final profit divided by total hours yielded one dollar and seventy-one cents per hour. Less than before, despite record revenue.

### Scene 16 (58 words)
Mr. Mason stopped by the fort on Sunday afternoon and took in the scene — four exhausted kids surrounded by sawdust and empty supply jars. He settled onto the bench next to Benny. "When you promise more than you can deliver," he said in a calm voice, "you have made two mistakes at once. The promise that breaks, and the work that suffers."

### Scene 17 (58 words)
Layla arrived at the next meeting carrying a homemade card she had designed over the weekend. One side was painted green, the next was yellow, and the last was red. "Benny carries this whenever he talks to customers," she explained, holding it up for the group. "I update it every morning based on how much active work we have sitting on the board."

### Scene 18 (58 words)
They tested the new system right away. Layla examined the current workload and set the card to yellow because six orders were already lined up. Benny practiced his new response out loud: "We are at capacity this week, but I can put you on the waitlist for next week." It felt strange to turn potential work away, but it also felt like responsible management.

### Scene 19 (58 words)
Benny held the capacity card in both hands and studied it for a long moment. "I just wanted to make everyone happy," he said, his voice dropping low. "I hate saying no to people. But this week I said yes too many times, and I made the whole team stressed and tired instead of happy." He looked at his friends. "I am sorry."

### Scene 20 (58 words)
The map on the wall had been flickering and pulsing with anxious orange light throughout the week of chaos. Now, with the capacity card resting on the table and a sustainable workload planned ahead, the lines calmed back to a warm and steady glow. Benny picked up the card and turned it in his hands. "Green means go. Red means grow first."

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **capacity** (Scenes 5, 17, 18) — The maximum amount of work a team can complete within a given time period
2. **overcommitting** (Scenes 5, 6) — Promising more work than the team can realistically deliver without sacrificing quality
3. **profit per hour** (Scene 15) — Total profit divided by total hours worked, measuring earning efficiency over time
4. **waste** (Scenes 10, 15) — Materials or time lost due to errors caused by rushing or working beyond capacity
5. **waitlist** (Scene 18) — A system for queuing future customers when current capacity is fully committed
6. **demand management** (Scenes 17, 18) — The practice of controlling how much work a business takes on to match its available capacity

## NARRATION NOTES

- **Target WPM:** 140 (confident, expressive pace)
- **Pause:** 0.75 seconds between sentences
- **Tone:** Escalating chaos that resolves in honest reflection. The week should feel relentless and physically draining. Benny's good heart causes the problem — he learns that systems protect enthusiasm from becoming a liability.
