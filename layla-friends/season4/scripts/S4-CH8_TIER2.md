## THE PIVOT
### Scene 1 (52 words)
Month two had arrived. The friends gathered in the fort on Saturday morning to review their progress. They had been running Maplewood Makers for five weeks now. Layla opened her notebook. "We should be growing," she said. "But something feels stuck." The others nodded. The numbers had stopped climbing.
### Scene 2 (53 words)
Layla pointed to the scorecard on the wall. Three weeks in a row showed almost the same revenue. Ninety-one dollars. Ninety-four dollars. Ninety-two dollars. "We're working harder than ever," said Ellis. "But we're not earning more." Riley looked worried. "Maybe we need to figure out why," she said. Layla already had an idea.
### Scene 3 (52 words)
That evening, Layla spread every order record across the fort table. She sorted them into four piles. Pottery orders in one pile. Repair orders in another. Birdhouse orders in a third pile. Journal orders in the last pile. Then she counted the money from each pile carefully. The piles told a story.
### Scene 4 (53 words)
Layla made a chart the next morning. She drew four bars on a big piece of paper. Pottery was the tallest bar. It earned sixty cents of every dollar. Repairs earned twenty-five cents. Birdhouses earned ten cents. And journals, the ones Layla made herself, earned only five cents. She stared at that tiny bar.
### Scene 5 (52 words)
She looked deeper into the numbers. Pottery had been growing every week for eight weeks straight. It started small and kept getting bigger. But journals went the other direction. They started with a few sales and then shrank. The last two weeks showed zero journal orders. Nobody was buying them anymore.
### Scene 6 (53 words)
Monday evening, Layla called a meeting in the fort. She pinned her chart to the wall. "Pottery is our winner," she said, pointing to the tall bar. "It brings in most of our money and keeps growing." Everyone could see it clearly. The numbers made the answer obvious. Pottery was what customers wanted most.
### Scene 7 (52 words)
"What about birdhouses?" asked Ellis. Layla showed him the trend. Birdhouse orders had been shrinking for weeks. "We should stop making them ahead of time," she suggested. "If someone orders one, we can still build it. But we shouldn't spend time making extras." Everyone agreed quickly. That decision felt easy.
### Scene 8 (53 words)
Then the room went quiet. Everyone looked at the smallest bar on the chart. Journals. Layla's journals. She had designed them herself back when they first started. She picked the paper, chose the binding, created every cover by hand. These journals were her idea from the beginning. And the market did not want them.
### Scene 9 (52 words)
Riley spoke first. Her voice was gentle. "I know how this feels," she said. "Remember my morning glory platter? I worked so hard on it. It was beautiful. But the customer wanted something different." She looked at Layla with understanding. "Sometimes what we love making isn't what people need to buy."
### Scene 10 (53 words)
Layla's eyes felt hot. "But they're beautiful," she said quietly. "I spent so much time on each one." Ellis put his hand on the table near hers. "The numbers don't measure beauty," he said carefully. "They measure what people choose to spend money on. And people are choosing pottery." That truth stung.
### Scene 11 (52 words)
After the meeting, Layla walked alone to Frances's garden. She found Frances trimming a rose bush. Big healthy branches fell to the ground. "Why are you cutting good branches?" Layla asked. Frances held up her shears. "To make room for great ones," she said. "A plant can't grow everything at once."
### Scene 12 (53 words)
Layla sat on the garden bench. "I have to stop making journals," she said. Her voice cracked on the word "journals." Frances sat beside her. "Pruning hurts," Frances said softly. "You're cutting something alive. Something you grew. But the plant knows what it needs, even when the gardener's heart says otherwise." Layla wiped her eyes. "No more journals."
### Scene 13 (52 words)
Monday morning, Layla walked into the workshop and took the journal orders off the board. She replaced them with extra pottery time slots. The board looked different now. Cleaner. More focused. Only pottery and repairs remained, with a small note for birdhouses by special order only. Layla took a deep breath.
### Scene 14 (53 words)
The time Layla used to spend on journals was now free. She had been spending two or three hours each week cutting paper, binding covers, and stitching pages. Those hours could go to something else. "I can help Riley prep clay and glazes," Layla offered. Riley's face brightened. "That would help so much!"
### Scene 15 (52 words)
With Layla helping prepare materials, Riley could focus entirely on shaping and finishing pottery. She made more pieces each day. Bowls, mugs, and small vases came out of the workshop faster than before. The quality stayed high because Riley wasn't rushing. She just had more time for the work that mattered.
### Scene 16 (53 words)
The first week after the pivot, they counted the money jars on Friday. Revenue had climbed to one hundred and seven dollars. Costs had dropped to twenty-eight dollars because they weren't buying journal supplies or extra birdhouse wood. Profit was seventy-nine dollars. Better than any week before. The pivot was already showing results.
### Scene 17 (52 words)
The second week was even stronger. Revenue reached one hundred and fifteen dollars. Costs fell to twenty-six dollars. Profit hit eighty-nine dollars. Their best number ever. Layla wrote it on the scorecard and circled it twice. The hard decision had been the right decision. Cutting journals made the whole business stronger.
### Scene 18 (53 words)
Ellis checked the data one more time. "Revenue up. Costs down. Profit at a record," he confirmed. "The numbers prove it worked." Layla nodded slowly. She still missed making journals. She probably always would. But the business needed her to follow the data, not her feelings. That was what leaders did.
### Scene 19 (52 words)
Benny mentioned something at lunch. "Did you hear about Neville?" he asked. "He quit his fix-it shop. Too many products, not enough quality." Layla thought about that. "We almost went that direction," she said. "We're still here because we made the hard choice. We pruned before it was too late."
### Scene 20 (53 words)
That evening, they studied the map together. The old journal customer lines had faded away. But the pottery lines glowed brighter than ever, and new ones were appearing. Clusters of pottery customers gathered near the cafe area. Layla traced the bright lines with her finger. "The map shows what works. We just had to be brave enough to follow it."

## FL VOCABULARY
- **pivot** -- changing direction in a business to focus on what works better
- **revenue** -- the total money a business earns before subtracting costs
- **trend** -- a pattern that shows something going up or down over time
- **pruning** -- cutting away parts that aren't helping so better parts can grow
- **opportunity cost** -- what you give up when you choose one thing over another
