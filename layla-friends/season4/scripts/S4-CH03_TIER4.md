## OPEN FOR BUSINESS

### Scene 1 (58 words)
Monday morning arrived with a sense of purpose the friends had never felt before. They gathered at the fort, where Layla had mounted an order board on the wall with three labeled columns: green for ready to take, yellow for in progress, and red for at capacity. Four job cards hung in the yellow column. "Maplewood Makers is officially open for business," Benny said.

### Scene 2 (58 words)
Their very first job was installing a custom shelf for Mrs. Torres. Ellis measured the wall twice, marking each bracket position with careful pencil lines. Riley ferried tools from the front walk to the kitchen. They worked with a level of professionalism that surprised even themselves. Mrs. Torres leaned against the doorframe, watching with her arms folded. "Take your time," she said.

### Scene 3 (58 words)
The finished shelf sat level and sturdy against the kitchen wall. Mrs. Torres ran her fingers along the sanded surface and nodded with satisfaction. "This is exactly what I wanted," she said, and counted out fifteen dollars in cash. Their first official payment as a registered business. Ellis accepted the money with a steady hand and a quiet sense of accomplishment.

### Scene 4 (58 words)
Back at the fort, they gathered around the three glass jars for their first real accounting exercise. Ellis walked them through the numbers. "Our material cost on the shelf was four dollars and fifty cents. We charged fifteen." Layla separated the money with care, placing the cost portion in the COSTS jar and ten dollars and fifty cents into the PROFIT jar.

### Scene 5 (58 words)
That afternoon, Benny set out alone to generate new business. He knocked on Mr. Patel's door and launched into an enthusiastic pitch about birdhouses, describing the woodwork and the painted perch. Mr. Patel listened politely but shook his head. "I appreciate the energy, but I have no need for a birdhouse." Benny walked away with his confidence dented and his pitch clearly broken.

### Scene 6 (58 words)
Benny replayed the conversation on the curb outside Mr. Patel's house, turning his mistake over in his mind. Later, he found Layla at the fort. "I was trying to convince him to want something he did not need," he admitted. "That is not selling. Next time I should ask what he actually needs first." Layla agreed without hesitation.

### Scene 7 (58 words)
Tuesday morning, Ellis headed to Elm Street to tackle a broken garden gate. The latch had pulled free from the post after months of wear, leaving the gate swinging open with every gust of wind. Ellis examined the stripped screw holes, drilled fresh pilot holes half an inch deeper, and secured the latch with longer screws. The repair took thirty focused minutes.

### Scene 8 (58 words)
The homeowner counted out twenty dollars, the amount they had agreed on during the estimate. Then she reached into her wallet again and placed a five-dollar bill on top. "You saved me the cost of calling a professional service," she said. "That extra is well earned." Their first tip — unexpected income that went straight to the profit jar.

### Scene 9 (58 words)
Wednesday morning, Benny tried a new approach. He knocked on a door, introduced himself as a member of Maplewood Makers, and asked one open-ended question instead of delivering a pitch. "Is there anything around your home that needs fixing or building?" The woman considered for a moment. "My mailbox post is leaning badly," she said. Benny wrote it down with a smile.

### Scene 10 (58 words)
By Wednesday evening, the team had completed three jobs and had two more actively in progress. Layla sat at the fort table after dinner, running the numbers in her notebook. The money jars held more than she had projected for just three days of operation. "We are tracking ahead of my estimates," she reported to the group Thursday morning with quiet satisfaction.

### Scene 11 (58 words)
A woman arrived at the fort Thursday morning carrying a photo of a ceramic mug. She wanted a matching set of six as a birthday gift for her sister. Riley's eyes brightened with creative interest. "I can do that," she said with confidence. They agreed on forty-eight dollars for the complete set — their largest single order to date.

### Scene 12 (58 words)
Riley devoted Thursday afternoon and most of Friday to the mug commission, working at the pottery wheel with intense focus. She applied identical glaze to every piece and measured each handle against the first one she had shaped. Consistency across a set required patience that most people underestimated. "They have to look like they belong together," she murmured, inspecting each mug against the light.

### Scene 13 (58 words)
The customer returned Friday afternoon to collect her order. She lifted the lid of the box, peered inside, and her face broke into a wide smile. "These are exactly what I hoped for," she said, turning one mug carefully in her hands. She paid forty-eight dollars on the spot — their largest single transaction. Riley's smile lasted the rest of the afternoon.

### Scene 14 (58 words)
Thursday evening, the friends gathered at the fort to review their running totals for the week. Layla flipped through her notebook, tracing a finger down the column of payments received. She added the figures twice to confirm. "Revenue stands at over eighty dollars," she announced, tapping the total with her pencil. The number made the whole operation feel serious and real.

### Scene 15 (58 words)
Smaller jobs continued filling in around the major ones. Ellis tightened a squeaky door hinge for a neighbor down the block. Benny delivered a repaired shelf two streets over. Layla sold one of her handmade journals, which caught everyone off guard, including her. Each modest payment added another layer to the growing total. The momentum of a functioning business was undeniable.

### Scene 16 (58 words)
Saturday morning, the four friends convened at the fort table for their first formal weekly review. Layla spread a clean sheet of paper in front of her and drew three careful columns, labeling each one in her neatest handwriting: REVENUE, COSTS, and PROFIT. The weekly scorecard would become their standard practice. "Time to see exactly where we stand," she said.

### Scene 17 (58 words)
Layla wrote the final revenue total at the top of the first column. Ninety-three dollars. The number sat on the page for a moment before Benny erupted. "Ninety-three dollars in one week!" He slapped the table and the others joined in with high-fives across the workspace. Nobody had expected their first week of operations to generate that kind of return.

### Scene 18 (58 words)
Layla then itemized the week's expenses. Wood, screws, pottery clay, ceramic glaze, and wood stain totaled twenty-two dollars in direct material costs. She subtracted that figure from the revenue line and wrote the result in the profit column: seventy-one dollars. A margin of over seventy-six percent. They studied the number in the kind of silence that follows something hard-earned.

### Scene 19 (58 words)
But Layla's expression shifted as she circled two specific line items on her sheet. "The gate repair and the mug commission account for almost sixty percent of our total revenue," she said, her pencil tapping the circled numbers. "That is a concentration problem. If we fail to land large orders next week, our income drops by more than half."

### Scene 20 (58 words)
The question settled over the group. They turned toward the map on the wall, where golden lines now traced connections from the fort to customer locations across the neighborhood. The network was real and growing, but much of the map remained dark — territory where Maplewood Makers had not yet reached. Opportunity and risk sat side by side.

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **revenue** (Scenes 14, 16, 17, 19) — Total income received from all sales and services
2. **profit / margin** (Scenes 4, 18) — Revenue minus costs; the percentage of revenue retained
3. **tip** (Scene 8) — Voluntary additional payment from a satisfied customer
4. **commission** (Scene 12) — A custom order created to a customer's specifications
5. **transaction** (Scene 13) — A completed exchange of product or service for payment
6. **revenue concentration** (Scene 19) — The risk of relying on a small number of customers for most income
