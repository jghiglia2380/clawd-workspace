## OPEN FOR BUSINESS
### Scene 1 (105 words)
Monday morning marked the opening of Maplewood Makers as a working business, and the fort felt transformed with their pricing sheet and order board hanging prominently on the wall where everyone could reference them throughout the day. Benny checked the board eagerly, his eyes scanning the color-coded cards that represented actual paying customers who had faith in their abilities. Four orders were positioned in the active section, ready to work on this week. "Let's get started!" he exclaimed with excitement bubbling in his voice. Layla reminded everyone to track their time carefully and save all receipts like Mr. Mason had taught them during their planning sessions. real business was finally starting after weeks of preparation.
### Scene 2 (103 words)
Their first major job was installing a shelf for Mrs. Torres in her kitchen. Ellis and Benny arrived at her house carrying the tools and the wooden shelf that Ellis had carefully built in his workshop the previous afternoon. "This is exactly what I wanted," Mrs. Torres said happily when she examined the craftsmanship, running her hand along the smooth wood. Ellis measured the wall exactly and installed the shelf with careful attention to making it perfectly level. The shelf was sturdy and positioned exactly where it would be most useful. Mrs. Torres was thrilled with the result, admiring how it fit her space.
### Scene 3 (110 words)
"How much do I owe you?" Mrs. Torres asked when Ellis finished installing the shelf successfully and cleaned up the workspace. "Fifteen dollars," Benny said confidently, remembering their carefully calculated pricing sheet that accounted for both materials and labor. Mrs. Torres paid them with a warm smile that made Benny feel proud of their professionalism. Walking home together, Benny felt a surge of pride holding the money they had earned through their own work and skill. Their first real sale felt important and meaningful, marking the transition from practice to actual business. Ellis had done excellent work, and Mrs. Torres was happy with the result. That's what successful business meant—creating value for customers.
### Scene 4 (105 words)
Back at the fort, they performed the money jar routine with careful attention to proper accounting. Layla calculated the profit with precision using her notebook and pencil. Materials for the shelf had cost four dollars and fifty cents total for wood, screws, and finishing supplies. "That means our profit is ten dollars and fifty cents," she announced clearly to everyone gathered around the table. They moved the profit money ceremonially into the PROFIT jar, watching the bills settle at the bottom. It was the very first time that jar had any money in it. Everyone cheered loudly at this big milestone in their business journey.
### Scene 5 (108 words)
"Mrs. Torres told her sister about us!" Benny reported Tuesday morning with excitement in his voice, bouncing slightly on his toes. He had just gotten a phone call about fixing a broken gate on Elm Street for another customer who had heard about their work. "See? That's exactly how it works," Layla said wisely, pleased to see her theory about customer referrals confirmed by real-world evidence. "Good work brings more work because happy customers tell their friends." Ellis nodded understanding how business relationships developed through trust and quality. Word of mouth was already helping their new business grow through the neighborhood network.
### Scene 6 (106 words)
Ellis fixed the broken gate on Elm Street that afternoon with his usual precision and attention to detail. The customer watched him work with growing interest and admiration for his methodical approach. "You're very skilled for your age," the man said respectfully. When Ellis finished the repair successfully, ensuring the gate swung smoothly, the customer paid the agreed twenty dollars plus an unexpected five dollar tip for work that exceeded his expectations. "You earned this with your quality," the customer explained warmly. Ellis had never received a tip before and felt proud of the recognition of his craftsmanship.
### Scene 7 (109 words)
"What do we do with tips?" Ellis asked back at the fort while showing everyone the extra five dollars he'd received unexpectedly from the satisfied customer. They hadn't planned for tips in their business system at all during their planning phase. Layla thought carefully about the question, considering how tips fit into their profit structure and whether they should be treated differently from regular revenue. "Tips are extra money earned for good work, so they should go to profit," she decided after working through the logic. Everyone agreed that approach made perfect sense for their model. They added it happily to the growing PROFIT jar.
### Scene 8 (105 words)
Riley finished the custom mug set Wednesday morning after days of careful work at her pottery wheel. Six perfectly matching mugs in a beautiful deep blue glaze that she'd mixed herself. She had worked hard to make each one perfect in size and finish, checking measurements repeatedly. "These are my best work yet," she said, examining each mug carefully under the light to check for any flaws. Benny would deliver them that afternoon to the waiting customer. The customer had ordered them two weeks ago and paid forty-eight dollars in advance as a deposit.
### Scene 9 (108 words)
Benny carried the complete mug set carefully to the customer's house in a specially padded box to prevent any damage during transport. The woman opened the package slowly and gasped with delight at what she saw inside. "These are beautiful and perfect!" she exclaimed happily while holding each mug up to the light to admire the rich glaze and smooth finish. "Thank you so much for such wonderful work." Benny felt a warm glow inside seeing her joy and appreciation for Riley's craftsmanship. Making customers happy felt just as rewarding as earning money, he realized in that moment.
### Scene 10 (106 words)
Thursday brought an influx of three small jobs that kept everyone busy throughout the day with varied tasks. Ellis repaired a wobbly chair efficiently for eight dollars, tightening joints and ensuring stability. Riley made two small decorative bowls for six dollars each using leftover clay from previous projects. Benny delivered some packages for busy neighbors and earned four dollars for his reliable service and friendly demeanor. "Little jobs really do add up to good money over time," Layla observed wisely while writing everything carefully in her detailed tracking notebook. The business was staying busy with work constantly coming in from various sources throughout the neighborhood.
### Scene 11 (109 words)
Friday afternoon, a nervous customer visited the fort hesitantly, looking uncertain about approaching them with her request. "I need help but I don't have much money to spend," she admitted quietly about her financial situation. Benny listened carefully to her problem with attention and empathy, paying close attention. She needed a small but important repair done urgently for her elderly mother. "We can do it for five dollars," Benny offered kindly after thinking about what seemed fair given the circumstances. The woman's face showed relief and gratitude. Sometimes helping people in need mattered more than maximizing profits on every single job.
### Scene 12 (105 words)
Saturday morning, they gathered to count their week's total earnings together with nervous excitement. Layla had tracked every single dollar carefully in her notebook throughout the week, recording each transaction as it occurred. "Let's add it all up now," she said seriously while pulling out fresh paper for the final calculations. They listed each completed job and its corresponding payment carefully: Mrs. Torres's shelf, the gate repair with the generous tip, the custom mug set, and all the smaller jobs combined. The final total was impressive, better than they expected for the week.
### Scene 13 (108 words)
"Ninety-three dollars total revenue!" Layla announced after adding all the numbers carefully. Everyone immediately cheered loudly at how much they'd earned together through hard work and cooperation. But then Layla's expression shifted to serious as she continued her financial analysis. "Now we have to subtract our costs for materials," she explained to the group, pulling out her expense tracking sheet. They had spent exactly twenty-two dollars on wood, clay, glue, and various supplies throughout the entire week. "That means our profit is seventy-one dollars," she calculated exactly. The PROFIT jar was growing nicely.
### Scene 14 (106 words)
They celebrated their successful week with snacks and drinks in the cozy fort together, enjoying the moment of accomplishment. Seventy-one dollars in profit felt like a fortune to all of them, more money than they'd ever earned before. "We're really good at this!" Benny declared with obvious pride. Ellis agreed while gazing at their three money jars lined up on the shelf, each serving its specific purpose. Riley smiled warmly at their accomplishment. They had worked hard all week. Every job had been done well. Customers were satisfied. The business model was working great.
### Scene 15 (109 words)
But Layla was studying her detailed notebook with a worried expression that concerned the others and stopped the celebration. "I see something potentially problematic," she said slowly while checking her numbers again carefully to make sure her analysis was correct. Everyone immediately stopped celebrating and looked at her with concern about what she'd discovered in the data. "The shelf and the mug set together totaled sixty-three dollars," she explained clearly, pointing to the figures with her pencil. "That's most of our money from just two big jobs." Her voice carried concern about this pattern. The rest came from many small jobs paying much less.
### Scene 16 (105 words)
"Why is that bad?" Benny asked with confusion, since big profitable jobs seemed obviously good to him. Layla tried patiently to explain her concern about revenue concentration to the group, drawing on business concepts Mr. Mason had mentioned. "What if next week we don't get any big jobs at all?" she asked seriously, letting them consider what that meant. "What if we only get small jobs that pay five or eight dollars?" The business needed consistent big customers regularly to maintain good profits week after week, not dependency on occasional large orders that might not materialize.
### Scene 17 (108 words)
Ellis suddenly understood Layla's point with clarity. "It's like only having one best friend," he said thoughtfully, reaching for an analogy they could all understand from their own lives. "If that friend moves away, you're alone." Layla nodded approvingly at his excellent comparison that captured the idea. "Exactly! We need more big customers regularly, not just one or two occasionally," she explained with emphasis on building a solid customer base. Riley looked concerned now too after hearing the explanation clearly. Depending entirely on occasional big orders felt risky when considered carefully, like building on shaky ground.
### Scene 18 (106 words)
"So what should we do about this?" Riley asked earnestly, wanting to solve the problem rather than just worrying about it. Benny thought hard, thinking hard. "Maybe we should tell more people about Maplewood Makers?" he suggested hopefully with growing enthusiasm about the idea. "Try to get more customers who might order big things?" Layla immediately wrote that excellent idea down in her notebook under a new section labeled "Growth Strategies." "Good thinking. More customers means less risk," she agreed approvingly with his smart idea.
### Scene 19 (109 words)
"But seventy-one dollars is still really great!" Benny insisted strongly, not wanting to lose their happy celebratory feeling over one concern about the future. Layla smiled warmly and nodded in agreement with his perspective, agreeing he had a point about appreciating their success. "You're right. We should celebrate what we did well this week," she admitted fairly, putting her notebook aside. Ellis raised his juice box high in the air ceremoniously like a toast. "To our first week!" he proclaimed proudly. They all clinked their drinks together, the sound echoing in the small fort. The celebration felt good despite the one worry.
### Scene 20 (105 words)
That night, Layla checked the mysterious map in her room with her usual curiosity about changes and patterns. Six distinct golden lines now extended clearly from the central fort to various customer houses scattered around the neighborhood. Each glowing line marked where they had completed good work and built new relationships through quality and service. The network was small but growing each day, expanding their reach. Mrs. Torres's line glowed brightest because she had told her sister about their work. Word was spreading slowly but surely through the entire community, one satisfied customer at a time.

## FL VOCABULARY
- **revenue** — total money earned from customers before subtracting costs
- **profit** — money remaining after paying for materials and supplies
- **customer** — a person who pays for products or services
- **tip** — extra money given voluntarily for especially good work
- **word of mouth** — when satisfied customers tell others about your business
- **revenue concentration** — when most income comes from just a few sources, creating risk
- **diversification** — spreading revenue across many customers to reduce risk
