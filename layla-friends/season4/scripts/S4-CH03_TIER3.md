## OPEN FOR BUSINESS

### Scene 1 (50 words)
Monday morning arrived and the friends gathered at the fort with a new energy buzzing between them. Layla had built an order board with three columns: green for ready, yellow for in progress, and red for full capacity. Four cards sat in the yellow column. "Maplewood Makers is officially open," Benny announced proudly.

### Scene 2 (50 words)
Their first real job was installing a shelf for Mrs. Torres. Ellis measured the wall twice to make sure the brackets would sit level. Riley carried the tools from the sidewalk to the kitchen while Ellis marked the spots with a pencil. Mrs. Torres watched from the doorway. "Take your time," she said.

### Scene 3 (50 words)
The finished shelf hung straight and sturdy on the wall. Mrs. Torres ran her hand along the smooth surface and smiled with satisfaction. "This is exactly what I wanted," she said, and handed Ellis fifteen dollars in cash. Their first customer payment. Ellis tucked the bills carefully into his pocket.

### Scene 4 (50 words)
At the fort, they gathered around the money jars for their first real transaction. Ellis walked them through the numbers. "Materials cost four dollars and fifty cents. We charged fifteen." Layla placed the cost portion in the COSTS jar and the rest in the PROFIT jar. Ten dollars and fifty cents of actual profit.

### Scene 5 (50 words)
That afternoon, Benny headed out on his own to drum up more business. He knocked on Mr. Patel's door and launched into a sales pitch about birdhouses. "You really need a birdhouse for your yard," Benny insisted with his biggest smile. Mr. Patel shook his head politely. "I have no interest in a birdhouse."

### Scene 6 (50 words)
Benny sat on the curb outside Mr. Patel's house, replaying the conversation in his head. Later, he told Layla what happened. "I was trying to convince him to want something he did not need," Benny admitted. "Next time I should ask what he needs and go from there." Layla agreed right away.

### Scene 7 (50 words)
On Tuesday morning, Ellis headed to Elm Street to repair a broken gate. The latch had come loose from the post, leaving the gate swinging open whenever the wind blew. Ellis examined the damage, identified the stripped screws, and replaced them with longer ones. The fix took thirty careful minutes.

### Scene 8 (50 words)
The gate customer counted out twenty dollars, the price they had agreed on ahead of time. Then she reached into her wallet again and pulled out a five-dollar bill. "You saved me from calling a professional repair service," she said warmly. "Please keep the extra." Their first tip as a business.

### Scene 9 (50 words)
Wednesday morning, Benny tried a different approach. He knocked on a door, introduced himself as part of Maplewood Makers, and asked a simple question. "Is there anything around your house that needs fixing or making?" The woman paused to think. "My mailbox post is wobbly," she said. Benny smiled and wrote it down.

### Scene 10 (50 words)
By Wednesday evening, three jobs were complete and two more were in progress. Layla tallied the numbers in her notebook after dinner. The money jars held more than she expected for just three days of work. "We are ahead of where I thought we would be," she told the group Thursday morning.

### Scene 11 (50 words)
A woman arrived at the fort Thursday morning with a special request. She wanted six matching mugs as a birthday gift for her sister. Riley's face brightened immediately. "I can definitely make those," she said with confidence. They agreed on forty-eight dollars for the complete set, which was their largest order yet.

### Scene 12 (50 words)
Riley spent Thursday afternoon and most of Friday at the pottery wheel, working with intense focus. She applied the same glaze to every mug and checked each one carefully against the others for size and shape. Consistency mattered more than speed. "They need to look like they belong together," she reminded herself.

### Scene 13 (50 words)
The customer returned Friday afternoon to pick up the mugs. She lifted the lid of the box and peered inside. "Oh my, these are wonderful!" she said, holding one up to the light. She paid forty-eight dollars without hesitation. It was their biggest single transaction, and Riley beamed with pride.

### Scene 14 (50 words)
Thursday evening, the friends gathered at the fort to check the week's running total. Layla flipped through her notebook and added up every payment they had received so far. "Over eighty dollars in revenue," she said, tapping the number with her pencil. The word revenue made everything feel official.

### Scene 15 (50 words)
Smaller jobs continued to come in throughout the week. Ellis tightened a squeaky door hinge. Benny delivered a repaired shelf across the neighborhood. Layla even sold one of her handmade journals, which surprised everyone including her. Each small job added a little more to the total, and the momentum kept building.

### Scene 16 (50 words)
Saturday morning, the four friends sat at the fort table for their first weekly review. Layla spread a fresh piece of paper in front of her and drew three columns. She labeled each one in careful letters: REVENUE, COSTS, and PROFIT. "Let's find out exactly how our first week went," she said.

### Scene 17 (50 words)
Layla wrote the revenue total at the top of the first column. Ninety-three dollars. The number hung in the air for a moment before Benny let out a whoop. "Ninety-three dollars in one week!" They high-fived across the table. None of them had expected to earn that much so quickly.

### Scene 18 (50 words)
Then Layla worked through the costs. Wood, screws, pottery clay, glaze, and stain totaled twenty-two dollars for the week. She subtracted that from the revenue and wrote the final number in the profit column. Seventy-one dollars. Real profit that belonged to Maplewood Makers. They stared at the number in satisfied silence.

### Scene 19 (50 words)
But Layla's expression shifted as she studied the numbers more carefully. She circled the gate repair and the mug order. "These two jobs gave us almost sixty percent of our revenue," she said slowly. "What happens if we do not land big orders like these next week? That worries me."

### Scene 20 (50 words)
The friends sat with Layla's question for a long moment. Then they looked at the map on the wall, where golden lines now connected the fort to a handful of customer homes across the neighborhood. But much of the map remained dark and untouched. Their network was real, but it was still small.

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **revenue** (Scenes 14, 16, 17, 19) — Total money received from all sales and services
2. **profit** (Scenes 4, 18) — Money remaining after subtracting costs from revenue
3. **tip** (Scene 8) — Additional payment from a satisfied customer beyond the agreed price
4. **transaction** (Scene 13) — A completed exchange of goods or services for payment
5. **revenue concentration** (Scene 19, implied) — Risk of depending on a few large orders for most income

- **Target WPM:** 125 (comfortable pace with expression)
- **Pause:** 1 second between sentences
- **Tone:** Builds excitement through the week, shifts to thoughtful concern at the end
