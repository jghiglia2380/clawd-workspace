## OPEN FOR BUSINESS

### Scene 1 (68 words)
Monday morning marked the opening of Maplewood Makers. The fort felt different now with their pricing sheet and order board hanging prominently on the wall where everyone could see them. Benny checked the board eagerly, his eyes scanning the cards. Four orders were ready to work on this week. "Let's get started!" he exclaimed with genuine excitement bubbling in his voice. Layla reminded everyone to track their time carefully and save all receipts like Mr. Mason had taught them. Real business work were finally beginning.

### Scene 2 (66 words)
Their first major job was installing a shelf for Mrs. Torres in her kitchen. Ellis and Benny arrived at her house carrying the tools and the wooden shelf that Ellis had built carefully in his workshop. "This is exactly what I wanted," Mrs. Torres said happily when she examined the craftsmanship. Ellis measured the wall exactly and installed the shelf with careful attention to making it level. The shelf was sturdy and perfectly positioned. Mrs. Torres was thrilled with the result.

### Scene 3 (70 words)
"How much do I owe you?" Mrs. Torres asked when Ellis finished installing the shelf successfully. "Fifteen dollars," Benny said confidently, remembering their carefully calculated pricing sheet. Mrs. Torres paid them with a warm smile that made Benny feel proud. Walking home together, Benny felt a surge of pride holding the money they had earned through their own work. Their first real sale felt important and meaningful. Ellis had done excellent work, and Mrs. Torres was happy with the result. That's what successful business meant.

### Scene 4 (67 words)
Back at the fort, they performed the money jar ceremony with careful attention. Layla calculated the profit with precision using her notebook. Materials for the shelf had cost four dollars and fifty cents total. "That means our profit is ten dollars and fifty cents," she announced clearly to everyone gathered. They moved the profit money ceremonially into the PROFIT jar with satisfaction. It was the very first time that that jar had money sitting inside it. Everyone cheered loudly at this significant milestone.

### Scene 5 (69 words)
"Mrs. Torres told her sister about us!" Benny reported Tuesday morning with barely contained excitement in his voice. He had just gotten a phone call about fixing a broken gate on Elm Street for another customer. "See? That's exactly how it works," Layla said wisely, pleased to see her theory confirmed. "Good work brings more work because satisfied people tell their friends and family." Ellis nodded with growing understanding of how business relationships developed. Word of mouth was already helping their new business grow organically through the neighborhood.

### Scene 6 (68 words)
Ellis fixed the broken gate on Elm Street that afternoon with his characteristic precision. The customer watched him work with growing interest and admiration. "You're very skilled for someone your age," the man said with genuine respect in his tone. When Ellis finished the repair successfully, the customer paid the agreed twenty dollars plus an unexpected five dollar tip for exceptional work. "You earned this with your quality," the customer explained warmly. Ellis had never received a tip before and felt honored by the recognition.

### Scene 7 (70 words)
"What do we do with tips?" Ellis asked back at the fort while showing everyone the extra five dollars he'd received unexpectedly. They hadn't planned for tips in their business system at all. Layla thought carefully about the question, considering how tips fit into their profit structure. "Tips are extra money earned for exceptionally good work, so they should go straight to profit without being split," she decided logically. Everyone agreed that approach made perfect sense. They added it happily to the growing PROFIT jar with satisfaction.

### Scene 8 (67 words)
Riley finished the custom mug set Wednesday morning after days of careful work. Six perfectly matching mugs in a beautiful deep blue glaze that she'd mixed herself. She had worked hard to make each one perfect in size and finish. "These are my best work yet," she said with quiet pride while examining each mug carefully under the light. Benny would deliver them that afternoon to the waiting customer. The customer had ordered them two weeks ago and paid forty-eight dollars in advance.

### Scene 9 (69 words)
Benny carried the complete mug set carefully to the customer's house in a specially padded box to prevent any damage. The woman opened the package slowly and gasped audibly with pure delight at what she saw. "These are beautiful and perfect!" she exclaimed happily while holding each delicate mug up to the afternoon light to admire the glaze. "Thank you so much for such wonderful work." Benny felt a warm glow inside seeing her genuine joy and appreciation. Making customers happy felt just as rewarding as earning money.

### Scene 10 (68 words)
Thursday brought an influx of three small jobs that kept everyone busy throughout the day. Ellis repaired a wobbly chair efficiently for eight dollars. Riley made two small decorative bowls for six dollars each using leftover clay. Benny delivered some packages for busy neighbors and earned four dollars for his reliable service. "Lots of little jobs really do add up over time," Layla observed wisely while writing everything meticulously in her detailed tracking notebook. The business was staying consistently busy with work constantly coming in from various sources.

### Scene 11 (70 words)
Friday afternoon, a nervous customer visited the fort hesitantly, looking uncertain about approaching them. "I need help but I don't have much money to spend," she admitted quietly with obvious embarrassment. Benny listened carefully to her problem with genuine attention and empathy. She needed a small but important repair done urgently. "We can do it for five dollars," Benny offered kindly after thinking about what seemed fair. The woman's face showed immediate relief and deep gratitude. Sometimes helping people in need mattered more than maximizing profits on every single job.

### Scene 12 (67 words)
Saturday morning, they gathered to count their week's total earnings together with anticipation. Layla had tracked every single dollar meticulously in her notebook with careful detail throughout the busy week. "Let's add everything up now," she said seriously while pulling out fresh paper for calculations. They listed each completed job and its corresponding payment methodically. Mrs. Torres's shelf, the gate repair with the generous tip, the custom mug set, and all the smaller jobs combined. The final total was impressive.

### Scene 13 (69 words)
"Ninety-three dollars total revenue!" Layla announced after adding all the numbers carefully on her paper. Everyone immediately cheered loudly at the a lot they'd earned together. But then Layla's expression shifted to serious as she continued her analysis. "Now we have to subtract our costs for materials used," she explained methodically to the group. They had spent exactly twenty-two dollars on wood, clay, glue, and various supplies throughout the entire week. "That means our actual profit is seventy-one dollars," she calculated exactly. The PROFIT jar was growing very nicely indeed.

### Scene 14 (68 words)
They celebrated their successful week with snacks and drinks in the cozy fort together. Seventy-one dollars in profit felt like an absolute fortune to all of them. "We're really good at this business thing!" Benny declared with obvious pride and genuine happiness radiating from his face. Ellis agreed wholeheartedly while gazing at their three money jars lined up on the shelf. Riley smiled warmly at their collective accomplishment and hard work paying off. They had worked diligently hard all week long. Every job had been completed well. Customers were satisfied and happy. The business model was definitely working successfully.

### Scene 15 (70 words)
But Layla was studying her detailed notebook with a distinctly worried expression that concerned the others. "I see something potentially problematic," she said slowly while checking her numbers again carefully to make sure she was correct. Everyone immediately stopped celebrating and looked at her with growing concern about what she'd discovered. "The shelf and the mug set together totaled sixty-three dollars," she explained clearly, pointing to the figures. "That's most of our total money from just two big jobs." Her voice carried unmistakable concern about this concentration.

### Scene 16 (67 words)
"Why is that bad exactly?" Benny asked with genuine confusion, since big profitable jobs seemed obviously good to him. Layla tried patiently to explain her concern about revenue concentration to the group. "What if next week we don't get any big jobs at all?" she asked seriously, letting them consider the implication. "What if we only get small jobs that pay five or eight dollars each time?" The business needed consistent big customers regularly to maintain good profit margins week after week.

### Scene 17 (69 words)
Ellis suddenly understood Layla's point . "It's like only having one best friend in the whole world," he said thoughtfully, reaching for an analogy they could all understand. "If that one friend moves away suddenly, you're alone with nobody." Layla nodded approvingly at his excellent comparison. "Exactly right! We need more big customers regularly, not just one or two occasionally," she explained with emphasis on the word "more." Riley looked concerned now too after hearing the explanation. Depending entirely on occasional big orders felt risky when considered carefully.

### Scene 18 (68 words)
"So what should we actually do about this?" Riley asked earnestly, wanting to solve the identified problem proactively. Benny thought hard, his brow furrowed with concentration. "Maybe we should tell more people in the neighborhood about Maplewood Makers?" he suggested hopefully with growing enthusiasm. "Try to get more customers who might potentially order big things regularly?" Layla immediately wrote that excellent idea down in her notebook. "Good thinking. More diverse customers means way less risk overall," she agreed approvingly with his strategic suggestion.

### Scene 19 (70 words)
"But seventy-one dollars is still really great!" Benny insisted strongly, not wanting to lose their happy celebratory feeling over one concern. Layla smiled warmly and nodded in agreement with his perspective. "You're right about that. We should definitely celebrate what we accomplished well this week," she admitted fairly, recognizing the importance of acknowledging success. Ellis raised his juice box high in the air ceremoniously. "To our very first week in business!" he proclaimed proudly. They all clinked their drinks together enthusiastically. The celebration felt good and deserved despite the small identified worry about concentration.

### Scene 20 (67 words)
That night, Layla checked the mysterious map in her room with her usual curiosity about changes. Six distinct golden lines now extended clearly from the central fort to various customer houses scattered around the neighborhood. Each glowing line marked where they had completed good work and built new relationships. The network was admittedly small but definitely growing steadily each day. Mrs. Torres's line glowed brightest because she had already told her sister enthusiastically. Word was spreading slowly but surely through the entire community.

## FL VOCABULARY

- **revenue** — total money earned from customers before subtracting costs
- **profit** — money remaining after paying for materials and supplies
- **customer** — a person who pays for products or services
- **tip** — extra money given for especially good work
- **word of mouth** — when satisfied customers tell others about your business
