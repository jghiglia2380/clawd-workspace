## THE BUSINESS PLAN

### Scene 1 (68 words)
The friends gathered at the fort on Monday morning to begin their partnership. "We need a name for our business," said Riley while bouncing with excitement. Everyone suggested ideas enthusiastically. Benny liked "The Fix-It Four" because it rhymed, Riley wanted "Creative Corner" because it sounded artistic, and Ellis suggested "Maplewood Makers" because they made things in Maplewood. They voted and "Maplewood Makers" won by majority, making everyone feel now.

### Scene 2 (66 words)
"Let's start selling today!" said Benny eagerly, already imagining customers. Ellis nodded in agreement. But Layla shook her head firmly, remembering her journal failure. "We need a plan first," she said with conviction. Riley looked uncertain. "Why?" she asked. Layla explained her disappointing experience. "We can't just start. What if we run out of money halfway through?" Riley nodded slowly, understanding the wisdom of planning ahead.

### Scene 3 (70 words)
They walked to Mr. Mason's workshop hoping he could guide their planning. The workshop smelled like fresh sawdust and machine oil. Mr. Mason looked up from his workbench with a warm smile. "We want to start a business together," Ellis explained with pride. Mr. Mason set down his tools and gave them his full attention. "That's wonderful," he said kindly. "But tell me, what will it cost you to make these things you want to sell?"

### Scene 4 (67 words)
Mr. Mason opened his workshop ledger and showed them pages filled with careful numbers. "Every piece of wood has a cost that I must account for," he explained while pointing to detailed entries. "Every hour of work has value that deserves compensation." He showed them what he paid for materials like lumber and hardware, then what he charged customers for finished projects. The friends studied the numbers carefully, beginning to see how real businesses tracked money.

### Scene 5 (69 words)
Mr. Mason wrote on paper in large letters: PRICE - COST = PROFIT. "This is the most important formula in business," he said with emphasis. "If you charge ten dollars and it cost you four dollars to make, your profit is six dollars." They practiced with different examples, testing various prices and costs until everyone understood the formula clearly and could figure out profit independently without help from Mr. Mason.

### Scene 6 (68 words)
"But what happens when things go wrong?" Mr. Mason asked thoughtfully. He told them about a broken saw blade that stopped his work . He couldn't finish two important jobs on time, disappointing his customers. "That's why you need an emergency fund set aside," he explained seriously. "Save some money for problems you don't expect." Ellis wrote this crucial advice down in his notebook, recognizing its importance for their business.

### Scene 7 (70 words)
Back at the fort, they began listing products they could make and sell. Ellis could build sturdy shelves and fix broken furniture with skill. Riley could make beautiful pottery mugs and decorative bowls using her valley techniques. Layla could create handmade journals and decorated storage boxes. Benny could help everyone with tasks and deliver finished products to customers. They wrote everything down on large paper, creating a good product catalog to guide their work.

### Scene 8 (67 words)
"Let's figure out what a shelf costs to make," Ellis suggested while pulling out his calculator. He listed all materials needed for construction. Wood cost four dollars at the lumber yard. Screws cost one dollar. Sandpaper cost fifty cents. Wood stain cost fifty cents. "That's six dollars total," he announced with satisfaction. "Now we need to decide what to charge customers for our work and time," he added thoughtfully.

### Scene 9 (69 words)
"We should charge eight dollars," said Benny quickly. Layla shook her head in disagreement. "That's only two dollars profit. We should charge twelve dollars," she countered. Ellis thought carefully, considering both materials and labor. "What about fifteen dollars? That covers our time too, not just materials," he suggested. They discussed what felt fair to customers while also making the business worth their effort. Finally they agreed on fifteen dollars.

### Scene 10 (68 words)
They decided to visit Frances in her garden for additional advice. She was picking ripe vegetables when they arrived. "We're planning our business carefully," Layla explained with excitement. Frances smiled warmly while placing red tomatoes in her basket. "That's wonderful, planning ahead," she said approvingly. "But who are your customers? Who needs what you make?" she asked meaningfully. The friends looked at each other, realizing they hadn't considered this question yet.

### Scene 11 (70 words)
They sat on the garden bench to discuss Frances's question seriously. "Maybe kids who need toys?" suggested Benny thoughtfully. "Or adults who need repairs?" said Ellis, thinking of practical needs. "People who want handmade art?" added Riley hopefully. They debated for several minutes, considering different possibilities. Finally Layla said with clarity, "Anyone who needs something made well or fixed right." Everyone agreed that definition made sense for their diverse skills.

### Scene 12 (67 words)
"We should all have our own jobs," Layla said back at the fort. "Everyone should do what they're good at." They discussed each person's strengths with honesty. Layla was organized and skilled at planning. Ellis was talented at building and fixing things exactly. Riley was creative with pottery and design. Benny loved talking to people and making deliveries. The division of labor was becoming clear to everyone.

### Scene 13 (69 words)
Ellis made a detailed roles chart on large paper with four columns. Under Layla's name he wrote: Manager, money tracker, planning. Under his own name: Building, repairs, quality control. Under Riley's name: Pottery, design, custom work. Under Benny's name: Sales, delivery, customer contact. They hung it prominently on the fort wall where everyone could see it clearly when questions arose about responsibilities, creating accountability for each role.

### Scene 14 (68 words)
"Now let's figure out how much money we need to start," Ellis said while making a new list. They needed clay for Riley's pottery, wood for Ellis's shelves, basic tools, paper for Layla's journals, and various supplies. Ellis added up every item with precision, checking his math twice. "Sixty dollars total," he announced. Everyone checked their savings from allowances and Chapter 1 work. They had just enough money pooled together.

### Scene 15 (70 words)
They pooled their money together on the fort table, creating their initial capital. Riley contributed fifteen dollars from her allowance savings. Ellis added eighteen dollars he'd earned from odd jobs. Layla put in twelve dollars from her careful savings. Benny added fifteen dollars he'd saved. They counted it twice to make sure, touching each bill carefully. Exactly sixty dollars. "This is our startup fund," Layla said seriously. Everyone felt the weight of combining their savings.

### Scene 16 (67 words)
Layla suggested creating a money jar system for tracking funds carefully. They found three clear glass jars at her house. She made neat labels on index cards: COSTS, PROFIT, RESERVE. "Fifty dollars goes to costs for buying materials," she explained while sorting the bills methodically. "Ten dollars goes to reserve for emergencies." The PROFIT jar stayed empty for now, waiting patiently for their first sales to generate earnings.

### Scene 17 (69 words)
"We need something to write down orders so we don't forget," Layla said with practical thinking. She designed a simple form on lined paper with clear spaces for customer name, item requested, price agreed upon, and deadline promised. "This way we won't forget or mix up orders," she explained carefully. Benny nodded sheepishly, remembering his confused money situation from Chapter 1 when he couldn't track who paid what for which errand.

### Scene 18 (68 words)
Ellis created the pricing sheet on large white poster board. He made organized columns with headers: Product, Materials Cost, Time Required, Price, Profit. He carefully filled in their main products with calculations. Shelf: six dollars materials, two hours work, fifteen dollars price, nine dollars profit. Pottery mug: two dollars materials, one hour work, eight dollars price, six dollars profit. Everything was organized and clear now, eliminating confusion about pricing.

### Scene 19 (70 words)
They returned to Mr. Mason's workshop to show him their completed plan proudly. He studied their pricing sheet, roles chart, and order forms with careful attention, reading every detail. "This is excellent work," he said with genuine pride. "You've created a real plan now. Do it carefully and track everything you do," he advised warmly. Ellis felt immense pride in the system they had created together through collaboration.

### Scene 20 (67 words)
That evening, Layla checked the map in her room with curiosity. The four separate dots had merged into one bright dot centered at the fort. Thin lines extended outward toward Mr. Mason's workshop glowing warmly and Frances's garden showing soft green light. New connections were forming. The network was just beginning to take shape. The map seemed to pulse with anticipation. They were ready to begin real business tomorrow.

## FL VOCABULARY

- **business** — an organization that makes or sells products to earn money
- **profit** — the money left after subtracting costs from price
- **cost** — the amount paid for materials to make something
- **startup** — the beginning phase when launching a business
- **emergency fund** — money saved for unexpected problems
