# CHAPTER 2: THE BUSINESS PLAN - TIER 3 (3RD GRADE)

**Character Focus:** Ellis (spotlight)

## THE BUSINESS PLAN

### Scene 1 (50 words)
The friends gathered at the fort on Sunday morning to figure out how to turn their team idea into something real. First, they needed a name. Benny suggested "Benny's Bunch," which made everyone laugh. Riley offered "Art Kids." Then Ellis said "Maplewood Makers," and they all agreed it was perfect.

### Scene 2 (50 words)
Benny jumped to his feet, already looking toward the door. "Let's go find customers!" he said. But Layla held up her hand and shook her head. "We need a plan first," she said firmly. "We tried doing everything without thinking last time, and it fell apart." Riley backed her up right away.

### Scene 3 (50 words)
They walked to Mr. Mason's workshop, where the familiar smell of sawdust and wood stain greeted them at the door. They explained their plan to work together as Maplewood Makers. Mr. Mason listened with his arms folded, nodding as they talked. Then he asked one important question. "What does it cost you to make things?"

### Scene 4 (50 words)
Mr. Mason reached for a thick book on the shelf above his workbench and set it down with a thud. "This is my ledger," he explained, flipping it open to a page full of neat columns. "Every board of wood, every can of stain, and every box of screws gets recorded here."

### Scene 5 (50 words)
Then Mr. Mason picked up a pencil and wrote three words in large letters: PRICE minus COST equals PROFIT. "The price is what your customer pays," he said. "The cost is what you spend to make it. Whatever is left over is your profit. That is the money you actually keep."

### Scene 6 (50 words)
Mr. Mason leaned back on his stool and told them about the time his best saw broke right in the middle of a big project. He had to rush out and buy a replacement, and the unexpected expense wiped out his profit from two separate jobs. "Always save money for surprises," he warned.

### Scene 7 (50 words)
Back at the fort, Ellis opened his notebook with fresh energy. "We need to list everything we can make and sell," he said. Riley said pottery and mugs. Ellis added shelves and repairs. Layla said journals. Benny said deliveries. Ellis wrote each product down and underlined the list with a straight edge.

### Scene 8 (50 words)
Ellis chose the shelf as their first example for pricing. He wrote each cost on its own line. "Wood costs four dollars. Screws cost one dollar. Sandpaper is fifty cents. Stain is fifty cents." He drew a line under the numbers and added them up carefully. "Six dollars total for materials," he announced.

### Scene 9 (50 words)
The pricing debate started immediately. "Charge eight dollars," Benny suggested, thinking that sounded reasonable. Layla shook her head. "Twelve at least. We need more than two dollars of profit." Ellis did the math in his head and said fifteen because their time should count for something too. After discussing it, they settled on fifteen.

### Scene 10 (50 words)
On their way home, the group stopped at Frances's garden. She was watering her sunflowers when they told her about Maplewood Makers. Frances listened with a warm smile, then asked a question they had not considered. "Who are your customers? A garden grows best when you know what the soil needs."

### Scene 11 (50 words)
The question followed them all the way back to the fort. "People who need stuff fixed around their house," Ellis said, thinking out loud. "People who want handmade things they cannot buy at a store," Riley added. Layla pulled out her notebook and wrote down every idea so they would not lose them.

### Scene 12 (50 words)
Assigning roles turned out to be the easiest part of the whole afternoon. Everyone naturally fit a different position on the team. Layla's organized mind made her the perfect manager and money tracker. Ellis would handle building and repairs. Riley would cover pottery and design. Benny was born to be their salesperson.

### Scene 13 (50 words)
Riley spread a large sheet of paper across the table and drew four neat columns. She wrote each person's name at the top of a column and listed their responsibilities underneath. When she finished, she taped the roles chart to the wall by the door so everyone would see it every time they came in.

### Scene 14 (50 words)
Ellis took on the task of calculating their startup costs. He went through every material they would need: wood planks, bags of clay, bottles of paint, sheets of sandpaper, boxes of screws, and cans of stain. The numbers added up quickly, and when he reached the total, he circled it twice. Sixty dollars.

### Scene 15 (50 words)
"We have fifty-eight dollars left from last week," Layla reminded everyone. That covered most of it, but not quite all. Each friend dug into their pockets and added a little from their allowances. When they stacked the bills and coins together, the total came to exactly sixty dollars. Enough to begin.

### Scene 16 (50 words)
The next morning, Layla arrived at the fort carrying three clear glass jars. She taped a hand-written label on each one: COSTS, PROFIT, and RESERVE. Together, they counted out the money and divided it carefully. Fifty dollars went into the costs jar. Ten dollars went into the reserve jar for emergencies.

### Scene 17 (50 words)
Layla had also designed a simple order form the night before. Each form had blank spaces for the customer's name, what they wanted, the agreed price, and the deadline for finishing. She stacked the forms in a neat pile on the table. "This way we keep track of every job," she said.

### Scene 18 (50 words)
Ellis completed their pricing sheet by listing every product they planned to sell. Each row contained the product name, the cost of materials, the estimated time to make it, and the selling price. The final column calculated the profit on each item. Ellis studied the numbers with a satisfied look on his face.

### Scene 19 (50 words)
Mr. Mason stopped by the fort that afternoon and the friends gave him a full tour. They pointed to the pricing sheet on the wall, the roles chart by the door, and the three jars lined up on the shelf. Mr. Mason examined everything carefully. "Solid plan," he said. "Execute it. Track everything."

### Scene 20 (50 words)
After Mr. Mason left, the friends stood together in the fort, surrounded by the systems they had built. The pricing sheet mapped their products. The jars held their capital. The roles chart defined their team. Everything was organized and ready. Layla pulled out the map, and a warm glow pulsed from its center.

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **cost** (Scenes 3, 5, 8, 14) — The money you spend to make something
2. **price** (Scenes 5, 9, 17) — What a customer pays for your product
3. **profit** (Scenes 5, 9, 18) — Money remaining after subtracting costs from price
4. **ledger** (Scene 4) — A record book for tracking business expenses and income
5. **startup cost** (Scene 14) — The total money needed to begin a new business
