## THE PARTNERSHIP

### Scene 1 (33 words)
Saturday morning, Ellen came to the fort. She owned the cafe on the corner. "I have an idea," she said with a smile. The friends were surprised. Nobody visited the fort like this before.

### Scene 2 (32 words)
Ellen looked at Riley's mugs on the shelf. "People at my cafe ask about these," she said. "They want to buy them. Can I sell your pottery at my shop?"

### Scene 3 (33 words)
Benny got excited. "You want to buy our mugs?" he asked. Ellen shook her head. "Not buy them," she said. "Something different. Let's go talk to Mr. Mason. He can explain it."

### Scene 4 (32 words)
At Mr. Mason's workshop, Ellen told him her idea. Mr. Mason smiled. "That's called consignment," he said. "It means Ellen shows your mugs at her cafe. She doesn't buy them first."

### Scene 5 (33 words)
"When a mug sells, Ellen keeps some of the money," Mr. Mason said. "She gives you the rest. If a mug doesn't sell, it comes back to you." That was a new idea.

### Scene 6 (32 words)
"That's different from buying them all at once," Layla said. Mr. Mason nodded. "Right. With consignment, nobody pays until something sells. You share the work and share the money together."

### Scene 7 (33 words)
"How much does Ellen keep?" asked Riley. Ellen said, "I was thinking forty cents from every dollar." Benny thought about it. That seemed like a lot. He wanted to talk about it.

### Scene 8 (32 words)
Benny felt nervous but brave. "What if you kept thirty cents at first?" he asked Ellen. "Then thirty-five cents after the first month?" He held his breath and waited for her answer.

### Scene 9 (33 words)
Ellen thought about it. Then she smiled. "That sounds fair," she said. "You did a good job asking for what you wanted, Benny." Benny felt very proud. They had made a deal together.

### Scene 10 (32 words)
They also agreed on one more thing. If a mug didn't sell in thirty days, it would come back. That way nobody was stuck. Both sides felt good about the plan.

### Scene 11 (33 words)
"Can we write this down?" Layla asked. "If it's not written down, people might forget." Ellen smiled. "That's very smart," she said. Mr. Mason agreed. Good partners write their deals down.

### Scene 12 (32 words)
Layla wrote the rules on paper. She listed the mugs, the money split, and the return rule. Ellen signed it. All four kids signed it too. Now everyone knew the plan.

### Scene 13 (33 words)
Riley picked six of her best mugs. She chose ones with smooth edges and nice handles. "People at the cafe drink coffee," she said. "These handles need to feel good in their hands."

### Scene 14 (32 words)
They brought the mugs to Ellen's cafe on Tuesday. Ellen put them on a shelf near the counter. She made a sign that said "Made by Maplewood Makers." The mugs looked great.

### Scene 15 (33 words)
All week, they waited. Would anyone buy a mug? Riley was nervous. "What if nobody likes them?" she asked. Benny told her, "Ellen believes in us. We have to be patient."

### Scene 16 (32 words)
On Friday, Ellen called. "I sold three mugs!" she said happily. Benny told everyone the news. Riley clapped her hands. The cafe people liked their pottery! The partnership was working.

### Scene 17 (33 words)
Saturday, they visited Ellen. Three mugs sold at twelve dollars each. That was thirty-six dollars total. Ellen kept her share. The friends got twenty-three dollars and forty cents. Ellen shook their hands.

### Scene 18 (32 words)
Back at the fort, Layla made a special envelope. She wrote "Cafe Money" on it. "We keep this separate," she said. "So we know how much comes from Ellen's cafe."

### Scene 19 (33 words)
Benny smiled big. "We made money while we were at school," he said. "The mugs sold without us being there!" That was what a good partnership did. Both sides helped each other.

### Scene 20 (32 words)
That night, Layla looked at the map. A new line glowed between the fort and the cafe. It looked different from the others. Thicker. Golden-green. The business was growing in a new way.

## FL VOCABULARY

- **consignment** -- leaving your things at a friend's shop for them to sell, then sharing the money
- **partnership** -- when two groups work together so both can do better
- **negotiate** -- talking about what's fair for everyone
- **written agreement** -- writing down the rules so nobody forgets
