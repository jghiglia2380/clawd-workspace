# CHAPTER 6: HIDDEN COSTS - TIER 1 (K-1ST GRADE)

**Reading Levels:** D
**Target Narration Speed:** 75 WPM
**Target Word Count:** 600 words
**Episode Duration:** ~8 minutes
**Character Focus:** Layla (spotlight)
**FL Integration:** Strong (overhead, true cost, buying in bulk)
**Setting:** Fort, Mr. Mason's workshop, store

---

## HIDDEN COSTS

### Scene 1 (30 words)
Ellis was cutting wood when his saw blade broke. SNAP! The blade split in half. The shelf was only halfway done. "Oh no," said Ellis. He could not finish without a new blade.

### Scene 2 (30 words)
A new blade cost twelve dollars at the store. That was a lot. They had not planned for it. "This was not on our cost list," said Ellis. He looked worried.

### Scene 3 (30 words)
Riley opened her glaze jar. It was almost empty! She needed it for a new order. "I used it all up," she said. She would have to buy more before she could work.

### Scene 4 (30 words)
At the store, Riley saw two sizes of glaze. A small jar cost eighteen dollars. A big jar cost forty-five dollars but lasted much longer. The big one saved money over time.

### Scene 5 (30 words)
At the fort, the friends looked at the money jars. They had spent thirty extra dollars they did not expect. "Where did this come from?" asked Benny. No one had a good answer.

### Scene 6 (30 words)
Layla looked at their cost list. "We forgot some things," she said. "Tools break. Supplies run out. Those cost money too." They had not counted those costs before.

### Scene 7 (30 words)
They went to see Mr. Mason. He showed them a special list. It had all the costs of running his shop. Tools, power, rent, and more. "These are always there," he said.

### Scene 8 (30 words)
"What about electricity?" Layla asked. Mr. Mason said it cost about twenty dollars a month for the space they used. They had been using his power for free! That was still a cost.

### Scene 9 (30 words)
"These extra costs are called overhead," said Mr. Mason. "They are the cost of having a business, even when you are not making anything." Benny looked confused but tried to understand.

### Scene 10 (30 words)
Mr. Mason showed them how buying more at once saved money. A big pack of sandpaper cost less per sheet than buying one at a time. "Buying big saves money," he said.

### Scene 11 (30 words)
Back at the fort, Layla got out her notebook. "We need to find our real profit," she said. "Not what we thought. The real number." Everyone agreed but looked a little nervous.

### Scene 12 (30 words)
Layla added up the hidden costs. The saw blade wearing down. The glaze Riley could have saved on. The electricity at Mr. Mason's shop. It all added up to sixty-two dollars.

### Scene 13 (30 words)
Their profit had been two hundred and seventeen dollars. Layla took away sixty-two. The new number was one hundred and fifty-five. It was smaller. The room got very quiet.

### Scene 14 (30 words)
"We still made money," Layla said. "One hundred fifty-five dollars is still good. But now we know the real number. That is better than guessing." Ellis nodded slowly.

### Scene 15 (30 words)
They decided to charge a little more for everything to cover hidden costs. Layla changed the prices on the chart. "Now we plan for things wearing out," she said.

### Scene 16 (30 words)
Riley bought the big jar of glaze instead of the small one. It cost more now but would save money later. "Like buying the big box of crackers," said Benny.

### Scene 17 (30 words)
They put extra money into a special jar for tools. When something broke, the money would be ready. "No more surprises," said Ellis. He taped a label that said TOOL FUND.

### Scene 18 (30 words)
Layla made a special page in her notebook called OVERHEAD. She would write down hidden costs every month so they would always know the real numbers from now on.

### Scene 19 (30 words)
Ellis thought about the broken saw blade. "I was mad," he said. "But tools wear out. That is not bad. We just need to plan for it ahead of time."

### Scene 20 (30 words)
They looked at the map. The gold lines still glowed. But now they could see shadows too. The shadows were the hidden costs. They had always been there, just hard to see.
