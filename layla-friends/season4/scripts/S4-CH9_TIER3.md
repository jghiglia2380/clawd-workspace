## THE PARTNERSHIP
### Scene 1 (65 words)
Saturday morning brought an unexpected visitor to the fort. A confident knock at the door made all four friends look up from their weekly planning session. Benny opened the door and found Ellen standing there, the cafe owner from the corner of Maple and Third. She wore her familiar green apron and carried herself with the easy confidence of someone who ran her own business. "Good morning," she said warmly. "I have a business proposition for you."
### Scene 2 (66 words)
Ellen stepped inside the fort and studied the shelves with genuine interest. Riley's pottery collection lined the far wall in organized rows. Mugs with smooth glazes, small decorative bowls, and a few slender vases caught the morning light beautifully. "My cafe customers keep asking about your pottery," Ellen explained directly. "They see these mugs around the neighborhood and want their own. I'd like to sell your work in my cafe."
### Scene 3 (65 words)
The friends exchanged excited glances immediately. Benny leaned forward with enthusiasm. "So you want to buy six mugs from us?" he asked, already imagining a big sale. Ellen shook her head with a patient smile. "Not exactly. I was thinking of a different arrangement called consignment." The word was unfamiliar to all of them. Ellen suggested they visit Mr. Mason together so he could help explain the model properly.
### Scene 4 (66 words)
That afternoon, everyone gathered at Mr. Mason's workshop. Ellen described her proposal while Mr. Mason listened carefully, nodding with recognition. "Consignment is a well-established way for two businesses to work together," Mr. Mason said approvingly. He gestured for the kids to pull their stools closer and settle in for an important lesson. "This is different from how you've been selling directly to customers. Let me walk you through it."
### Scene 5 (65 words)
"With consignment, Ellen doesn't purchase your mugs upfront," Mr. Mason began, speaking clearly so everyone could follow. "Instead, you place your mugs at her cafe on display. She shows them to her customers throughout the week. When a mug sells, Ellen keeps a percentage of the sale price and gives you the remaining portion. If a mug doesn't sell, it comes back to you."
### Scene 6 (66 words)
Layla raised her hand with a thoughtful question. "How is that different from wholesale?" she asked, remembering terminology from earlier discussions. Mr. Mason was pleased by the question. "With wholesale, a buyer purchases your products in bulk at a discounted price. They own the inventory immediately and take on the risk of selling it. With consignment, you retain ownership until the moment of sale. The risk is shared between both parties."
### Scene 7 (65 words)
Riley asked the question everyone was thinking about. "What percentage does Ellen keep from each sale?" Ellen answered directly. "I was thinking forty percent," she said. "So for a twelve-dollar mug, I would keep four dollars and eighty cents, and you would receive seven dollars and twenty cents." Benny's brow furrowed as he calculated silently. Forty percent felt like a significant portion of their hard-earned revenue going to someone else.
### Scene 8 (66 words)
Benny looked at his friends, reading their expressions carefully before speaking up. His stomach fluttered with nerves, but he knew this moment mattered. "What if we started at thirty percent for the first month as a trial?" he proposed carefully, keeping his voice steady despite his racing heart. "Then moved to thirty-five percent after that, once we both see how the partnership performs?" He waited anxiously for Ellen's reaction.
### Scene 9 (65 words)
Ellen considered Benny's counteroffer thoughtfully, taking her time before responding. She glanced at Mr. Mason, who remained deliberately quiet. This negotiation belonged to Ellen and the kids alone. "Thirty percent for the trial month, then thirty-five percent ongoing," Ellen repeated, weighing the terms. Then she extended her hand across the workbench with a genuine smile. "That's a fair and well-reasoned deal, Benny. You negotiate like a real business partner."
### Scene 10 (66 words)
Mr. Mason raised one more consideration the group had not yet addressed. "What happens to mugs that remain unsold after a reasonable period?" he asked pointedly. Ellen and the kids discussed the question together, exploring different scenarios before settling on a thirty-day return policy. Any mug that had not sold within thirty days would be returned to Maplewood Makers in the same condition it was delivered. Both parties agreed the arrangement distributed risk fairly.
### Scene 11 (65 words)
Layla pulled her notebook from the shelf with quiet determination. "Can we write all of this down?" she asked firmly. "If it's not written down, it's just a promise. And promises can be misunderstood." Ellen's eyebrows rose with respect. "That's impressively professional," she said sincerely. Mr. Mason added his own endorsement. "Written agreements protect both sides of a partnership. Every serious business relationship deserves documentation."
### Scene 12 (66 words)
Layla composed the agreement carefully on a clean page, taking her time with each detail. She documented the products covered, the percentage split structure, the thirty-day return policy, and the trial period timeline. She used clear language that left nothing ambiguous or open to interpretation. When she finished, Ellen reviewed every line and nodded with approval. Then Ellen signed, followed by each of the four kids adding their signatures below hers.
### Scene 13 (65 words)
Monday afternoon, Riley conducted a careful selection process, choosing six mugs from their current inventory for the cafe partnership. She evaluated each piece critically, considering the cafe environment and its customers. Every mug she selected had a flawless glaze, a comfortable handle weighted properly for coffee drinking, and colors that would complement a cafe setting. "First impressions matter," Riley said while wrapping each mug securely in protective cloth for transport.
### Scene 14 (66 words)
Tuesday morning, all four friends delivered the mugs to Ellen's cafe together. Ellen had already prepared a prominent wooden shelf near the counter, positioned where every customer would see it while ordering. She arranged the six mugs thoughtfully and placed a handwritten sign beside them reading "Handcrafted by Maplewood Makers." Benny stood back and absorbed the sight of their work displayed in a real business establishment. It felt like a big milestone.
### Scene 15 (65 words)
The waiting period tested their patience throughout the entire week. Each day brought the same anxious question: had anyone purchased a mug from Ellen's shelf? Riley voiced the concern that nagged at all of them. "What if cafe customers have different taste than our regular neighborhood buyers?" she worried aloud. Benny reminded her gently that Ellen understood her own customers. "She chose to partner with us for a reason."
### Scene 16 (66 words)
Friday afternoon, the call they had been waiting for finally arrived. "I sold my first one this morning!" Ellen reported with audible excitement in her voice. Then she paused for emphasis before continuing. "Actually, I sold three total this week. A woman bought two at once for gifts." Benny relayed the news to his friends at full volume. Riley's nervous expression transformed into pure relief and celebration. The partnership was validated.
### Scene 17 (65 words)
Saturday morning, they arrived at the cafe to settle their first week's accounts together. Ellen walked them through the numbers carefully. Three mugs sold at twelve dollars each generated thirty-six dollars in total revenue. Ellen's thirty-five percent share came to twelve dollars and sixty cents. The remaining twenty-three dollars and forty cents belonged to Maplewood Makers. Ellen counted out the money and handed it over with a firm, professional handshake.
### Scene 18 (66 words)
Back at the fort, Layla immediately established a separate tracking system for their new revenue stream. She labeled a small envelope "Cafe Revenue" and placed the twenty-three dollars and forty cents inside with precision. "We need to track this income separately from our direct customer sales," she explained to the group. "Different channels perform differently. Keeping them separate lets us understand which partnerships generate the most value over time."
### Scene 19 (65 words)
Benny settled into his chair with a satisfied expression that reflected a deeper understanding forming in his mind. "We earned money while we were sitting in school," he said slowly, letting the significance of that fact settle over the group. "Those mugs sold when we weren't even there." Layla nodded approvingly. "That's exactly what makes a good partnership valuable. Ellen's effort generates income for both of us at the same time."
### Scene 20 (66 words)
That evening, Layla examined the map and discovered something remarkable. A new line now connected the fort to Ellen's cafe, but it looked distinctly different from every other connection on the map. This line was thicker, pulsing with a steady golden-green glow instead of the familiar pure gold of their customer connections. Smaller lines branched outward from the cafe to new customers they had never reached directly. Their business network was expanding.

## FL VOCABULARY
- **consignment** -- an arrangement where products are placed with a seller who pays you only when items sell
- **wholesale** -- purchasing products in bulk at a discounted price, taking ownership immediately
- **negotiate** -- discussing terms to reach an agreement that benefits both parties fairly
- **written agreement** -- a signed document formalizing the terms, responsibilities, and expectations of a deal
- **passive income** -- revenue generated without direct active involvement at the point of sale
- **revenue stream** -- a distinct source of income within a business
