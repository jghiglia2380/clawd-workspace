# CHAPTER 6: HIDDEN COSTS - TIER 3 (3RD GRADE)

**Reading Levels:** M-N
**Target Narration Speed:** 125 WPM
**Target Word Count:** 1,000 words
**Episode Duration:** ~8 minutes
**Character Focus:** Layla (spotlight)
**FL Integration:** Strong (overhead, true cost accounting, bulk purchasing, tool depreciation, adjusted margins)
**Setting:** Fort, Mr. Mason's workshop, hardware store, craft supply store

---

## HIDDEN COSTS

### Scene 1 (50 words)
Ellis was halfway through cutting a plank for a shelf order when his saw blade snapped with a loud crack. The broken halves clattered to the workbench. Everyone in the fort froze. Ellis held up the useless blade and stared at it. "I was being careful," he said. "It just wore out."

### Scene 2 (50 words)
A trip to the hardware store revealed that a replacement blade cost twelve dollars. Ellis pulled the money from the costs jar with a frown. The saw blade was not on their pricing sheet — they had never planned for tools wearing out. "This came out of nowhere," he said, frustrated.

### Scene 3 (50 words)
The next morning brought another surprise. Riley opened her glaze jar after pulling mugs from the kiln. The jar was nearly empty — barely enough to coat one piece. She had a new order waiting but no glaze to work with. "I go through this faster than I realized," she said.

### Scene 4 (50 words)
At the craft supply store, Riley stood between two options. A small jar of glaze cost eighteen dollars and would last about two weeks. A large container cost forty-five dollars but lasted six weeks or more. She had always bought the small jar. Layla studied both price tags with narrowed eyes.

### Scene 5 (50 words)
That evening at the fort, the team stared at the money jars with worry. Between the saw blade and the glaze, thirty dollars had vanished from costs they never saw coming. "We counted everything so carefully," Benny said, confused. "Where did these extra expenses come from?" Layla already had her notebook open.

### Scene 6 (50 words)
Layla pulled the pricing sheet from the wall and studied each line. "We priced materials for every product," she said slowly. "Wood, clay, screws, stain — all included. But we never accounted for tools wearing out. Or supplies running low between orders. Those are real costs that we missed entirely."

### Scene 7 (50 words)
They walked to Mr. Mason's workshop to ask him about hidden expenses. He reached into a drawer and pulled out a separate tracking sheet. It listed every cost that was not tied to a specific project: rent, electricity, tool replacement, insurance, and supply restocking. The list was longer than they expected.

### Scene 8 (50 words)
Layla pointed to the electricity line on Mr. Mason's sheet. "How much does power cost for the space we use?" she asked. Mr. Mason calculated in his head. "Maybe twenty to thirty dollars a month for what you need." The friends exchanged uneasy glances. They had been using his electricity for free.

### Scene 9 (50 words)
"This is called overhead," Mr. Mason explained, tapping his tracking sheet. "Overhead is what it costs to exist as a business, separate from making any specific product. Rent, power, tool replacement — they are there whether you make one thing or fifty things." Benny frowned. "But we did not pay for electricity." Mr. Mason nodded.

### Scene 10 (50 words)
Mr. Mason opened a supply cabinet and pulled out a large pack of sandpaper. "I buy in bulk whenever I can," he said. "One sheet costs a dollar at the store. But this pack of fifty costs thirty dollars — sixty cents per sheet. Buying more up front saves money over time."

### Scene 11 (50 words)
Back at the fort, Layla sat down with her notebook and a sharp pencil. "We need to recalculate our true profit," she said, her expression serious. "Not the number we reported — the real number, with all the costs we missed." The others agreed, though no one was eager to see the result.

### Scene 12 (50 words)
Layla worked through the adjustments one by one. Tool wear over four weeks came to six dollars based on the saw blade's lifespan. Riley's glaze overspending from buying small jars instead of bulk cost twelve dollars. Mr. Mason's electricity for their share of the workshop added twenty-two dollars. She circled the total.

### Scene 13 (50 words)
Layla wrote the new number on a clean piece of paper and held it up. Their reported profit over four weeks had been two hundred and seventeen dollars. After subtracting sixty-two dollars in overhead, the adjusted profit was one hundred and fifty-five dollars. The fort went silent as the number sank in.

### Scene 14 (50 words)
Layla spoke into the silence with a steady voice. "We are still profitable," she said. "One hundred and fifty-five dollars is still real money that we earned. But the old number was wrong. Knowing the truth is better than being surprised later." Riley nodded. "It is like fixing a crooked shelf — better straight."

### Scene 15 (50 words)
They decided to add fifteen percent to their future prices to cover ongoing overhead expenses. Layla updated each line on the pricing sheet with the adjusted numbers. "This way, we are planning for things to wear out and run low," she explained. "We will not get caught off guard like this again."

### Scene 16 (50 words)
Riley placed an order for the large container of glaze instead of her usual small jar. The upfront cost was higher, but the per-mug expense dropped by nearly a third. "Buying in bulk feels weird at first because you spend more," she said. "But the math says it saves money in the long run."

### Scene 17 (50 words)
They added ten dollars to the reserve jar with a new label taped to the front: TOOL FUND. When a saw blade broke or a brush wore out, the money would already be set aside. "No more scrambling for twelve dollars we did not expect to spend," Ellis said with clear relief.

### Scene 18 (50 words)
Layla created a fresh page in her notebook titled OVERHEAD. She drew columns for tools, space, supplies, and miscellaneous costs. Every month they would update the page and add the totals into their profit calculations. "This is the page that shows the whole picture," she said, underlining the header twice.

### Scene 19 (50 words)
Ellis leaned against the wall and thought about the snapped saw blade. "I was angry when it broke," he admitted. "But Mr. Mason is right — tools wear out from regular use. That is part of doing business. The difference is whether you plan for it or let it surprise you."

### Scene 20 (50 words)
The friends looked at the map on the wall. The golden customer lines still glowed strong, but faint shadows now appeared beneath them — a reminder of costs that had always been running under the surface. "The shadows were always there," Riley said softly. "We just could not see them before."

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **overhead** (Scene 9) — Business costs not tied to any specific product, like rent, utilities, and tool replacement
2. **bulk purchasing** (Scenes 10, 16) — Buying supplies in larger quantities to reduce cost per unit
3. **depreciation / tool wear** (Scenes 1, 12) — The gradual reduction in a tool's value through regular use
4. **adjusted profit** (Scene 13) — Profit recalculated to include previously untracked costs
5. **true cost accounting** (Scene 6, implied) — Capturing all expenses, visible and hidden, in business calculations
