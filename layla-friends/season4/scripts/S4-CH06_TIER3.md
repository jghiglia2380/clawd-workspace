## HIDDEN COSTS
### Scene 1 (66 words)
Ellis was halfway through cutting a shelf plank when a sharp metallic snap echoed through Mr. Mason's workshop. His saw blade had broken clean in half, the two jagged pieces resting on the workbench like evidence of some invisible failure. Ellis stared at them with visible frustration building in his expression. "I was being so careful!" he protested. Mr. Mason walked over calmly and examined the broken blade with experienced eyes. "Tools wear out, Ellis. That's not failure. That's use."
### Scene 2 (65 words)
Ellis needed a replacement blade immediately since the shelf order was due Thursday. They walked to the hardware store together, and Ellis found the right blade on the pegboard wall. Twelve dollars. He paid from the COSTS jar, but his face remained tight with frustration the entire walk back. "We didn't plan for this in any of our budgets," he said with concern. Twelve dollars had appeared out of nowhere.
### Scene 3 (66 words)
Back at the fort that afternoon, Riley opened her kiln to discover three beautifully finished mugs sitting inside. The blue glaze had turned out perfectly smooth and even, exactly the way the customer wanted. She felt a surge of pride as she examined each one carefully. Then she reached for her glaze jar and tilted it toward the light. Nearly empty. Her satisfaction evaporated instantly. "I need more glaze before the next order," she said quietly.
### Scene 4 (65 words)
Riley and Layla went glaze shopping together at the ceramics supply store across town. The small jar Riley usually purchased cost eighteen dollars and lasted about two weeks. But sitting right beside it on the shelf was a large container priced at forty-five dollars. "The big one lasts about three times longer than the small," the shop owner explained helpfully. Riley had never stopped to compare the value before.
### Scene 5 (66 words)
At the fort meeting that afternoon, the mood was noticeably more serious than usual. Ellis placed his twelve dollar hardware store receipt on the table. Riley set her eighteen dollar glaze receipt beside it. "That's thirty dollars in unexpected costs this week alone," Layla said, studying her notebook with concern evident in her expression. "Where exactly does this money come from in our budget?" The question hung in the air without an answer.
### Scene 6 (65 words)
Layla pulled out their carefully constructed pricing sheet and examined every single line with analytical precision. Materials for each specific product were listed accurately. But saw blades appeared nowhere on any product list. Glaze refills weren't allocated to individual items either. "We priced the materials that go into each thing we make," Layla said slowly, realization dawning in her voice. "But what about tools that gradually wear out? Supplies that need constant restocking?"
### Scene 7 (66 words)
They walked to Mr. Mason's workshop together to ask about these perplexing surprise costs that seemed to materialize from nowhere. He listened to their concerns thoughtfully, then opened his desk drawer and pulled out a detailed sheet of paper covered in neat columns of categorized numbers. "This is my overhead tracking sheet," he explained with the patience of someone who had learned this lesson years ago. "Every business has costs beyond materials. I track mine religiously every single month."
### Scene 8 (65 words)
Layla scanned Mr. Mason's overhead sheet with widening eyes as she absorbed each category listed there. Rent for the workshop space. Monthly electricity payments. Tool replacement funds. Sandpaper and consumable supplies. Workshop insurance premiums. "You have to pay for all of this separately?" she asked with genuine surprise at the scope. Mr. Mason nodded matter-of-factly. "The lights cost money. The heat costs money. Even the broom I sweep with eventually wears out and needs replacing."
### Scene 9 (66 words)
Layla felt an uncomfortable realization forming as she studied the numbers further. "What about us?" she asked carefully, almost reluctantly. "We use your workshop regularly. Your electricity powers our tools. Your lights illuminate our workspace." Mr. Mason smiled gently at the question. "I don't mind sharing my space with you. But yes, electricity runs about thirty to forty dollars monthly for the area you occupy." He paused meaningfully. "Overhead is what it costs to exist as a business, entirely separate from making any specific product."
### Scene 10 (65 words)
Mr. Mason walked them over to his supply cabinet and demonstrated his purchasing strategy with a concrete example. He held up a large bulk box of sandpaper. "Fifty sheets for fifteen dollars. That works out to thirty cents per sheet." Then he held up a small retail pack from the same shelf. "Or ten sheets for eight dollars. That's eighty cents per sheet." The unit price difference was dramatic and immediately obvious to everyone watching.
### Scene 11 (66 words)
Walking back to the fort through the afternoon sunshine, the friends were unusually quiet as they processed everything they had just learned from Mr. Mason. Benny finally broke the extended silence with a reasonable objection. "But we didn't actually spend any money on electricity. We used Mr. Mason's for free." Layla shook her head firmly at the logic. "We didn't pay, but someone certainly did. If we grow larger, we'll eventually need our own space. We should count these costs honestly right now."
### Scene 12 (65 words)
At the fort, Layla proposed something she knew would be difficult for everyone to accept, including herself. "We need to recalculate our true profit," she said with quiet determination. "Going back over the entire four weeks of operation." The team exchanged nervous glances around the table. Their PROFIT jar currently showed two hundred seventeen dollars, a number they had celebrated with pride. Nobody wanted to watch it change. But Layla was right. They needed honest numbers.
### Scene 13 (66 words)
Layla carefully listed each previously hidden cost on a fresh sheet of paper, working through the categories one by one. Estimated saw blade wear over four weeks of regular use: six dollars. Riley's glaze purchased in small quantities instead of cost-efficient bulk: twelve dollars in lost savings over the same period. Mr. Mason's electricity for the workshop space they occupied for four weeks: twenty-two dollars estimated. She added the column carefully. "Sixty-two dollars in overhead we never counted," she announced to the silent room.
### Scene 14 (65 words)
Then came the painful part that everyone had been dreading since Layla first proposed recalculation. She reached into the PROFIT jar with steady hands and counted out exactly sixty-two dollars. Bill by bill, she transferred the money slowly and deliberately into the COSTS jar while everyone watched in heavy silence. The PROFIT jar visibly shrank from two hundred seventeen dollars to one hundred fifty-five dollars. The fort felt quieter than it had in weeks.
### Scene 15 (66 words)
Benny stared at the diminished jar with obvious concern written across his face. "Did we actually lose money?" he asked, needing reassurance that their weeks of hard work still meant something tangible. Layla shook her head firmly and with genuine conviction. "No. We are still profitable. One hundred fifty-five dollars is real, honest profit we actually earned." She tapped her notebook emphatically. "Before today, we believed we made two hundred seventeen. But that number included money we had already spent without realizing it. Now we know the truth."
### Scene 16 (65 words)
The team made concrete decisions about moving forward with this new understanding of their complete cost structure. Layla calculated that overhead added about fifteen percent to their actual operating costs beyond raw materials. "We need to factor fifteen percent overhead into all our future prices," she explained clearly while showing her calculations. "That covers tools wearing out through normal depreciation, electricity usage, and supply restocking." They updated the pricing sheet together, adjusting each product price upward by the appropriate amount.
### Scene 17 (66 words)
Riley made a firm decision about her glaze purchasing strategy going forward, applying the bulk purchasing lesson directly. "I'm ordering the large container this time," she announced with confidence. The forty-five dollar upfront cost felt steep compared to the familiar eighteen dollar small jar. But it would last three times as long, covering way more production. "That saves about thirty percent over time on a per-unit basis," Layla confirmed after verifying the mathematics. Higher cost now translated directly into lower cost later.
### Scene 18 (65 words)
They established a dedicated new jar in their financial system: the RESERVE jar. Ten dollars went in immediately, allocated for inevitable tool replacement and equipment maintenance expenses. "When Ellis needs his next saw blade, the money will already be set aside and waiting," Layla explained with satisfaction at the improved system. Ellis looked visibly relieved by this structural change to their planning. His saw blade breaking would never again represent an unplanned emergency expense.
### Scene 19 (66 words)
Layla created a new dedicated tracking page in her notebook, separate from their existing product-cost records. She titled it carefully: "Costs That Don't Go in Products." Beneath the heading she established four distinct categories: Tools and Equipment, Space and Electricity, Bulk Purchase Savings, Other. "We'll review and update this carefully every month going forward," she stated. Ellis nodded with genuine acceptance. "I was frustrated about the saw blade breaking. But Mr. Mason was right. Tools wear out. We just have to plan for it."
### Scene 20 (65 words)
That night, Layla studied the map in her room with fresh eyes shaped by everything she had learned today. The bright golden customer lines still glowed strong and warm, representing every relationship they had built through honest work. But now she noticed something new: dim gray shadows running beneath those golden lines like underground streams. Costs that had always existed but remained invisible until today. The shadows didn't diminish the golden lines. They revealed the complete, honest picture at last.

## FL VOCABULARY
- **overhead** -- indirect costs of running a business that aren't tied to making one specific product
- **depreciation** -- the gradual loss of value in tools and equipment through normal use over time
- **true cost accounting** -- calculating the complete cost of business work including hidden expenses
- **bulk purchasing** -- buying larger quantities to reduce the per-unit price of supplies
- **reserve fund** -- money deliberately set aside for anticipated future expenses
