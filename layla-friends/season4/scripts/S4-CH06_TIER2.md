## HIDDEN COSTS

### Scene 1 (53 words)
Ellis was cutting wood for a shelf when a sharp snap filled the workshop. His saw blade had broken clean in half. He stared at the two pieces with frustration. "I was being careful!" he said. Mr. Mason looked at the broken blade calmly. "Tools wear out, Ellis. That's not failure. That's use."

### Scene 2 (52 words)
Ellis needed a new blade right away. They walked to the hardware store together. A replacement blade cost twelve dollars. Ellis paid from the COSTS jar, but his face was tight. "We didn't plan for this," he said. Twelve dollars wasn't in the budget for any specific project. It just happened.

### Scene 3 (53 words)
Back at the fort, Riley opened her kiln to find three perfect mugs inside. She smiled at the smooth blue glaze. Then she picked up her glaze jar and tilted it. Almost empty. Her smile faded. "I need more glaze before the next order," she said quietly. Another cost they hadn't expected this week.

### Scene 4 (52 words)
Riley and Layla went glaze shopping. The small jar cost eighteen dollars. But next to it sat a large container for forty-five dollars. "The big one lasts three times longer," the shop owner explained. Riley always bought small. She never thought about whether bigger might save money over time.

### Scene 5 (53 words)
At the fort meeting that afternoon, the mood was serious. Ellis held up his receipt for twelve dollars. Riley held up hers for eighteen dollars. "That's thirty dollars in costs we didn't plan for," Layla said, studying her notebook. "Where does this money come from in our budget?" Nobody had a good answer.

### Scene 6 (52 words)
Layla pulled out their pricing sheet and studied every line. Materials for each product were listed carefully. But saw blades weren't on any product list. Glaze refills weren't either. "We priced materials for each item we make," Layla said slowly. "But what about tools that wear out? Supplies that run out?"

### Scene 7 (53 words)
They visited Mr. Mason's workshop to ask about these surprise costs. He pulled a sheet of paper from his desk drawer. It was covered in neat columns of numbers. "This is my overhead tracking sheet," he said. "Every business has costs beyond materials. I track mine every month." The friends gathered around to look.

### Scene 8 (52 words)
Layla scanned the sheet with wide eyes. Rent, electricity, tool replacement, sandpaper, insurance. "You pay for all of this?" she asked with surprise. Mr. Mason nodded. "The lights in this workshop cost money. The heat costs money. Even the broom I sweep with eventually needs replacing." Layla hadn't thought about electricity before.

### Scene 9 (53 words)
"What about us?" Layla asked carefully. "We use your workshop. Your electricity. Your lights." Mr. Mason smiled gently. "I don't mind sharing. But yes, electricity costs about thirty to forty dollars a month for this space." He paused. "Overhead is what it costs to exist as a business, separate from making specific products."

### Scene 10 (52 words)
Mr. Mason showed them his sandpaper supply. "I buy the big pack," he said, holding up a large box. "Fifty sheets for fifteen dollars. That's thirty cents each." Then he held up a small pack. "Or ten sheets for eight dollars. That's eighty cents each." Buying bigger saved real money.

### Scene 11 (53 words)
Walking back to the fort, the friends were quiet. Benny broke the silence. "But we didn't spend money on electricity. We used Mr. Mason's." Layla shook her head. "We didn't pay, but someone did. If we grow, we'll need our own space someday. We should count these costs now."

### Scene 12 (52 words)
At the fort, Layla proposed something hard. "We need to recalculate our true profit," she said. "Going back four weeks." The team looked nervous. Their profit jar showed two hundred seventeen dollars. That number felt good. Nobody wanted it to change. But Layla was right. They needed honest numbers.

### Scene 13 (53 words)
Layla listed each hidden cost on paper. Saw blade wear over four weeks: six dollars estimated. Riley's glaze, bought small instead of bulk: twelve dollars in lost savings. Mr. Mason's electricity for four weeks: twenty-two dollars estimated. She added them up carefully. "Sixty-two dollars in overhead we never counted," she announced quietly.

### Scene 14 (52 words)
Then came the hard part. Layla reached into the PROFIT jar. She counted out sixty-two dollars and moved it slowly to the COSTS jar. Everyone watched in silence. The PROFIT jar shrank from two hundred seventeen dollars to one hundred fifty-five dollars. The number felt smaller. The room felt heavier.

### Scene 15 (53 words)
Benny stared at the jar. "Did we lose money?" he asked. Layla shook her head firmly. "No. We're still profitable. One hundred fifty-five dollars is real profit." She tapped her notebook. "Before, we thought we made two hundred seventeen. But that wasn't the true number. Now we know the real one."

### Scene 16 (52 words)
They decided on changes going forward. Layla calculated that overhead added about fifteen percent to their actual costs. "We need to add fifteen percent to our prices," she explained. "That covers tools wearing out, electricity, and supplies." They updated the pricing sheet together, adding a small amount to each product price.

### Scene 17 (53 words)
Riley made a decision about glaze. "I'm ordering the large container," she said. It cost forty-five dollars upfront instead of eighteen. But it lasted three times longer. "That saves thirty percent over time," Layla confirmed after checking the math. Higher cost now meant lower cost later. Riley was learning to think ahead.

### Scene 18 (52 words)
They created a new jar: the RESERVE jar. Ten dollars went in for tool replacement. "When Ellis needs a new blade, the money is already set aside," Layla said. Ellis looked relieved. His saw blade breaking wouldn't be a surprise expense anymore. Planning ahead felt better than scrambling.

### Scene 19 (53 words)
Layla made a new tracking page in her notebook. She titled it: "Costs That Don't Go in Products." She listed categories: Tools and Equipment, Space and Electricity, Bulk Purchase Savings, Other. "We'll update this every month," she said. Ellis nodded. "I was mad about the saw blade. But Mr. Mason was right. We just have to plan."

### Scene 20 (52 words)
That night, Layla studied the map. The bright golden customer lines still glowed strong. But now she noticed dim gray shadows beneath them. Costs that were always there but invisible until today. The shadows didn't replace the golden lines. They completed the picture. "It's the whole story now," she whispered.

## FL VOCABULARY

- **overhead** -- costs of running a business that aren't tied to making one specific product
- **depreciation** -- tools and equipment wearing out and losing value through normal use
- **true cost** -- the full price of doing business, including hidden expenses
- **bulk purchasing** -- buying larger amounts to pay less per item
- **reserve** -- money set aside for future expected expenses
