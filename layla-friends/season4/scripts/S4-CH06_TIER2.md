## HIDDEN COSTS

### Scene 1 (45 words)
Ellis was cutting wood for a shelf when his saw blade snapped in half. The sharp crack made everyone jump. Ellis stared at the broken blade in his hand. The job was only halfway done and now the saw was useless. "I was being careful," he said.

### Scene 2 (45 words)
They went to the hardware store for a new blade. It cost twelve dollars. Ellis pulled the money from the costs jar and frowned. "We did not plan for this," he said. The blade was not on their pricing sheet. It was a cost that came out of nowhere.

### Scene 3 (45 words)
The next morning, Riley opened her jar of glaze after pulling mugs from the kiln. The jar was nearly empty. She needed glaze for a new order, but there was barely enough left to coat one mug. "I go through this stuff faster than I thought," she said.

### Scene 4 (45 words)
At the craft supply store, Riley found two choices. A small jar of glaze cost eighteen dollars. A large one cost forty-five dollars but lasted three times as long. She had always bought the small jar. "The big one costs more up front but saves money over time," Layla noticed.

### Scene 5 (45 words)
At the fort that evening, the team looked at the money jars. Between the saw blade and the glaze, they had spent thirty dollars they had not planned for. "Where did this come from?" Benny asked. "We counted all our costs before." Layla shook her head slowly.

### Scene 6 (45 words)
Layla pulled the pricing sheet off the wall and studied it closely. "We priced the materials for each product," she said. "But we never counted the cost of tools wearing out. Or supplies running low. Those are real costs too, and we missed them."

### Scene 7 (45 words)
They walked to Mr. Mason's workshop to ask him about it. He pulled a sheet of paper from a drawer and showed it to them. It listed all the costs of running his shop that were not tied to any one project. Rent, power, tool replacement, and more.

### Scene 8 (45 words)
Layla pointed at one line on Mr. Mason's sheet. "What does electricity cost?" she asked. Mr. Mason thought for a moment. "For the space you kids use, maybe twenty to thirty dollars a month." The friends looked at each other. They had been using his power for free.

### Scene 9 (45 words)
"This is called overhead," Mr. Mason explained. "It is what it costs to run a business even when you are not making anything. Rent, power, tool wear — they are always there." Benny scratched his head. "But we did not pay for any of that." Mr. Mason smiled.

### Scene 10 (45 words)
Mr. Mason opened a drawer and showed them a large pack of sandpaper. "I buy in bulk," he said. "One sheet costs a dollar. But this pack of fifty costs thirty dollars. That is sixty cents each." He tapped the pack. "Buying more at once saves money."

### Scene 11 (45 words)
Back at the fort, Layla sat down with her notebook and a serious look on her face. "We need to figure out how much profit we really made," she said. "Not what we thought we made. The real number." The others nodded, even though the idea made them nervous.

### Scene 12 (45 words)
Layla listed the hidden costs. The saw blade had been wearing down for weeks — about six dollars of wear. Riley could have saved twelve dollars buying glaze in bulk. Mr. Mason's electricity was about twenty-two dollars over four weeks. The list kept growing.

### Scene 13 (45 words)
Layla did the math and wrote the new number on a fresh piece of paper. Their reported profit had been two hundred and seventeen dollars. After subtracting the overhead costs, the adjusted profit was one hundred and fifty-five dollars. The room went quiet. That was sixty-two dollars less.

### Scene 14 (45 words)
Layla looked at her friends and spoke carefully. "We are still making money," she said. "One hundred and fifty-five dollars is still good. But now we know the real number. That is better than thinking we earned money we did not actually earn." Ellis nodded slowly.

### Scene 15 (45 words)
They decided to add fifteen percent to their prices going forward to cover overhead costs. Layla updated the pricing sheet with the new numbers. "This way, we plan for tools breaking and supplies running out before it happens," she said. The new prices felt more honest.

### Scene 16 (45 words)
Riley ordered the large container of glaze instead of the small jar. It cost more now, but over time each mug used less money in glaze. "It is like buying the big box of crackers instead of little bags," Benny said. Everyone laughed because he was right.

### Scene 17 (45 words)
They also put ten extra dollars into the reserve jar, but this time they labeled it TOOL FUND. When a saw blade broke or a brush wore out, the money would be ready. "No more surprises," Ellis said, taping the label to the jar. It felt good to be prepared.

### Scene 18 (45 words)
Layla created a new page in her notebook called OVERHEAD. She drew columns for tools, space, supplies, and other costs. Every month they would add up these hidden costs so nothing would sneak past them again. "This is the page that tells the whole truth," she said.

### Scene 19 (45 words)
Ellis sat on the bench and thought about the broken saw blade. "I was mad when it snapped," he said. "But Mr. Mason is right. Tools wear out from use. That is not failing. We just need to plan for it instead of being surprised by it."

### Scene 20 (45 words)
They looked at the map. The golden lines to their customers still glowed bright. But now they could see faint shadows beneath the lines — the hidden costs that had always been there. Riley said, "It is like seeing roots under a flower. They were there all along."

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **overhead** (Scene 9) — Costs of running a business that are not tied to any one product
2. **bulk purchasing** (Scenes 10, 16) — Buying in larger amounts to save money per item
3. **adjusted profit** (Scene 13) — Profit after accounting for all costs, including hidden ones
4. **tool replacement** (Scenes 1, 17) — The cost of replacing tools that wear out from use
