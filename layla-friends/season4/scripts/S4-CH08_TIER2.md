## THE PIVOT

### Scene 1 (45 words)
Five weeks had passed since the business opened. The calendar showed a new month. Layla looked at the order board and the money jars. Everything was running fine, but something felt off. They were working harder than before, but the numbers were not growing.

### Scene 2 (45 words)
Layla flipped through her notebook and found the weekly totals. Ninety-one dollars. Then ninety-four. Then ninety-two. Three weeks in a row, almost the same number. They were not losing money, but they were not making more either. "We are stuck," Layla said to herself quietly.

### Scene 3 (45 words)
That night, Layla spread every order record across her bedroom floor and sorted them into piles. One pile for pottery orders. One for repairs. One for birdhouses. One for journals. She wanted to see where the money came from, not where she guessed it came from.

### Scene 4 (45 words)
The numbers told a clear story. Pottery made up sixty percent of their revenue. Repairs brought in twenty-five percent. Birdhouses added ten percent. Journals, the product Layla had created herself, made up only five percent. She stared at the journal pile. It was the smallest stack on the floor.

### Scene 5 (45 words)
Layla pulled out records from the first weeks and compared them to recent ones. Pottery had grown from forty percent to sixty percent. Journals had dropped from twelve percent to five percent. Nobody was reordering journals. The trend was going in only one direction, and it was down.

### Scene 6 (45 words)
At the next fort meeting, Layla taped a chart to the wall showing revenue by product. Pottery had the tallest bar. Journals had the shortest. "This is where our money comes from," she said. The team studied the chart in silence. The pattern was impossible to miss.

### Scene 7 (45 words)
"Birdhouses are only ten percent," Ellis said, pointing at the chart. "Should we stop making them?" Layla shook her head. "We stop making them ahead of time. If someone orders one, we build it. But we do not keep extras on the shelf." Everyone agreed. That was easy.

### Scene 8 (45 words)
Then the room got very quiet. Everyone knew what came next. Journals sat at five percent. No one had reordered. Layla had spent hours designing the covers, cutting paper, and stitching bindings. They were beautiful. But the chart did not care about that. Layla stared at her hands.

### Scene 9 (45 words)
Riley was the first to speak. "I know how this feels," she said softly. She looked at Layla with understanding. "Remember my platter that the customer sent back? I loved it, but she did not want it. Sometimes what we love is not what people need."

### Scene 10 (45 words)
"But I worked so hard on them," Layla said. Her voice was thin and tight. "Every journal has my handwriting inside the cover." Ellis put his hand on the table. "The work was real," he said gently. "But the market tells us what people want. Not what we want."

### Scene 11 (45 words)
After the meeting, Layla walked alone to Frances's garden. Frances was trimming roses. "I have to stop making journals," Layla said. Frances set down her clippers. "Come look at this." She pointed to a rose bush. "I cut away good branches so the best ones grow stronger."

### Scene 12 (45 words)
Layla sat on the garden bench and let the decision settle. It hurt. Journals were the first product she had made all by herself. But the numbers were clear and the team needed her time. "No more journals," she said. Her voice cracked, but her eyes stayed steady.

### Scene 13 (45 words)
Monday morning, the order board looked different. Pottery orders filled most of the space. Repairs sat in a neat row beside them. The birdhouse section was marked FOR ORDERS ONLY. The journal section was gone. Layla had peeled off the label herself before anyone else arrived at the fort.

### Scene 14 (45 words)
The hours Layla used to spend on journals were now free. She started helping Riley prepare clay and mix glazes in the mornings. Two extra hours a day made a real difference. "This is so much faster with help," Riley said, working beside Layla at the pottery bench.

### Scene 15 (45 words)
With Layla's help, Riley finished three extra platters that week. Orders that would have taken until Friday were done by Wednesday. The team moved on to new work ahead of schedule for the first time. The fort felt lighter and more focused than it had in a while.

### Scene 16 (45 words)
At the end of the first week after the change, Layla counted the money. One hundred and seven dollars in revenue. Costs dropped to twenty-eight dollars because they were not buying journal supplies anymore. Profit came to seventy-nine dollars. Better than any normal week they had ever had.

### Scene 17 (45 words)
The second week was even stronger. Revenue reached one hundred and fifteen dollars. Costs held at twenty-six dollars. Profit hit eighty-nine dollars, their best number yet. Layla recorded it carefully in her notebook and circled the total twice. The pivot was working, and the numbers proved it.

### Scene 18 (45 words)
Layla sat with her notebook open and looked at the trend lines she had drawn. Revenue climbing. Costs falling. Profit growing. "The hard choice was the right choice," she said. Ellis nodded. "The data told us what to do. You just had to be brave enough to listen."

### Scene 19 (45 words)
Benny mentioned that Neville had shut down his repair shop. He had taken on too much work, let quality slip, and lost his customers one by one. "We are still here because we make hard choices," Layla said. "Neville tried to do everything. We chose to do less, better."

### Scene 20 (45 words)
The map on the wall had changed. The journal lines had faded away. But the pottery lines glowed brighter than ever, and new ones appeared near the café. Layla traced a bright cluster with her finger. "The map shows what works. We follow it."

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **pivot** (Scenes 12, 13, 17) — Changing what a business focuses on based on what the data shows
2. **revenue by category** (Scenes 4, 6) — Sorting income by product to see where the money comes from
3. **opportunity cost** (Scene 14) — The work you miss out on because your time is spent on something else
4. **data-driven** (Scenes 3, 18) — Using numbers instead of feelings to make business decisions
