**Episode Duration:** ~8 minutes
**Character Focus:** Ellis (spotlight)
**FL Integration:** Strong (competition, competitive advantage, differentiation, quality premium, guarantee)
**Setting:** Fort, hardware store, Mr. Mason's workshop, neighborhood

## THE COMPETITION

### Scene 1 (50 words)
Benny ran to the fort after school with an expression that told the others something was wrong. "Some older kid is doing repairs around the neighborhood," he said, still catching his breath. "People on Elm Street say he charges way less than we do." A worried silence fell over the group.

### Scene 2 (50 words)
The next morning, Mrs. Chen told Layla that she had hired someone else to build a shelf for her living room. "That other boy only charged ten dollars," she explained. Layla felt her confidence slip. Their shelves cost fifteen dollars. If customers could get the same thing cheaper, Maplewood Makers was in trouble.

### Scene 3 (50 words)
The friends called an emergency meeting at the fort that evening. Ellis paced by the order board while the others sat around the table. "Who is this kid and why is he undercutting us?" Ellis asked. Nobody had answers yet, but the threat felt real. Riley looked at the others nervously.

### Scene 4 (50 words)
Ellis spotted the competitor at the hardware store on Saturday morning. He was older, maybe twelve, with a confident stride and a bag of supplies. "Hey, I'm Neville," the boy said with a wide grin. "Starting a fix-it business. Should be fun." Ellis introduced himself politely but felt uneasy the entire walk home.

### Scene 5 (50 words)
Over the next few days, they gathered information about Neville's operation. His shelves cost ten dollars. Repairs were twelve. Birdhouses were only eight. Every price undercut Maplewood Makers by at least three dollars. Neville had even taped a flyer to the telephone pole near the park advertising his low rates.

### Scene 6 (50 words)
Ellis stood at the fort table, gripping the edge. "We have to drop our prices right now," he insisted. "If Neville charges less and people pick him, we lose everything we built." His voice had an edge of panic to it. Layla shook her head calmly and said, "Wait. Do the math first."

### Scene 7 (50 words)
Layla pulled out the pricing sheet from the wall and spread it flat on the table. "Our shelf costs six dollars in materials," she said, tracing the numbers with her pencil. "If we match Neville at ten dollars, we keep four. Split four ways, that is one dollar each for two hours of work."

### Scene 8 (50 words)
Ellis sank into his chair as the math settled over him. One dollar each for two hours of work meant fifty cents an hour — even less than what they earned during their disastrous solo week. "We can not survive at those prices," he admitted. Matching Neville would mean running the business at a loss.

### Scene 9 (50 words)
They walked to Mr. Mason's workshop after school. He listened to the full story, then set down his sanding block and looked at them. "Not everyone wants the cheapest option," he said. "Some people want the best option. You can not be both at once. Choose which customer you want to serve."

### Scene 10 (50 words)
Back at the fort, the team brainstormed what made Maplewood Makers different from Neville's operation. Custom designs. Careful craftsmanship. Personal service. Quality materials. They listed every advantage on a fresh sheet of paper. "These are the things Neville does not offer," Layla said. "This is our competitive advantage. We should lean into it."

### Scene 11 (50 words)
After a long discussion, they made a decision that felt both brave and scary. They would not lower their prices to match Neville. Instead, they would raise the shelf price from fifteen to eighteen dollars to signal higher quality. "If our work is worth more, our price should show it," Ellis said.

### Scene 12 (50 words)
Ellis proposed one more move to set them apart. "We should guarantee our work," he said. "If anything we build breaks within a month, we fix it for free." Layla wrote it on a card: MAPLEWOOD MAKERS QUALITY GUARANTEE — 30 DAYS. Every customer would receive a copy going forward.

### Scene 13 (50 words)
The first week tested their nerve. Three customers chose Neville because his prices were lower, and each lost order stung. The fort felt quieter than it had since their first week of business. Riley kept glancing at the order board, where too many green cards sat unclaimed. "Did we make a mistake?" she asked.

### Scene 14 (50 words)
Thursday afternoon, the phone at the fort rang. Mrs. Lee was on the other end, sounding upset. "I had that other boy build me a shelf last week," she said. "It fell off the wall this morning and broke my picture frame. Can you come fix it?" Ellis grabbed his toolbox immediately.

### Scene 15 (50 words)
At Mrs. Lee's house, Ellis inspected the damage and found the cause within seconds. Neville had used the wrong type of screws and skipped wall anchors entirely. The shelf had been hanging by friction alone. "He cut corners on the parts that matter most," Ellis said, shaking his head at the sloppy work.

### Scene 16 (50 words)
Ellis repaired the shelf properly using the right hardware and solid anchors. He tested it with weight before stepping back. Mrs. Lee watched the whole process with relief. "I should have come to you first," she said, paying twenty dollars for the repair. "Your work is worth every penny of the difference."

### Scene 17 (50 words)
Mrs. Lee wasted no time telling her neighbors about the experience. She showed them the repaired shelf and the guarantee card Ellis had left on her counter. "Neville's shelf lasted one week," she said plainly. "These kids build things that hold." The story spread down the block faster than any flyer could.

### Scene 18 (50 words)
The very next day, Mr. Patel arrived at the fort. "I heard you fixed Mrs. Lee's shelf after that other boy made a mess of it," he said. "I want a shelf too, and I want it done right from the start." He asked about the guarantee before he asked about the price.

### Scene 19 (50 words)
Saturday morning, the team gathered at the fort feeling steadier than they had all week. The panic from Monday had been replaced by clarity. They did not have to be the cheapest option — they just had to be the best one. "People pay more for work they can trust," Ellis said firmly.

### Scene 20 (50 words)
They studied the map on the wall. A new dot marked where Neville operated, but its customer lines were thin and flickering. The Maplewood Makers network glowed with steady, warm light. Quality and trust had built something that low prices alone could not create. The difference was visible right there on the wall.

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **competition** (Scenes 1, 5) — Another business offering similar products or services in the same market
2. **competitive advantage** (Scene 10) — The qualities that make one business better or different from another
3. **profit margin** (Scenes 7, 8) — The money remaining after subtracting costs from the selling price
4. **guarantee / warranty** (Scene 12) — A promise to repair or replace a product if it fails
5. **quality premium** (Scene 11, implied) — Charging more because the product is made to a higher standard

---

## NARRATION NOTES

- **Target WPM:** 125 (comfortable pace with expression)
- **Pause:** 1 second between sentences
- **Tone:** Builds tension, then resolves through strategy and validation. Neville is a competitor, not a villain.
