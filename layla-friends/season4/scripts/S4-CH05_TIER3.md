## THE COMPETITION

### Scene 1 (66 words)
Benny rushed into the fort on Tuesday morning with a troubled expression. "I heard something bad while delivering packages yesterday," he said, catching his breath. "Some older kid has been doing fix-it jobs around the neighborhood, charging way less than us." The friends exchanged worried glances across the table as the implications became clear. Competition had arrived in Maplewood, and nobody had anticipated or prepared for this challenge that now threatened everything they had built together.

### Scene 2 (65 words)
The news grew worse that afternoon when Mrs. Chen stopped by during her daily walk. "I hired that other boy for a shelf last week," she mentioned casually. "He only charged me ten dollars." Their shelves cost fifteen dollars, which meant someone was undercutting their established prices. Ellis felt his stomach tighten as the reality sank in deeper. They were already losing customers to this unknown competitor who had appeared seemingly overnight in their neighborhood market.

### Scene 3 (66 words)
Ellis called an emergency meeting at the fort right after dinner, recognizing that time was critical. All four friends gathered around the wooden table with serious expressions reflecting the gravity of the situation. "Who is this kid?" Riley asked nervously, fidgeting with her ribbon. "Why can he afford to charge so much less than us?" Nobody had answers yet since Benny had only heard scattered rumors. They needed solid information before developing an effective response to this unexpected competition.

### Scene 4 (65 words)
The following day, Ellis discovered the answer at the hardware store while picking up supplies. A tall boy, probably around twelve years old, was loading wood into a red wagon with impressive speed. He wore a confident grin suggesting everything was going according to plan. "I'm Neville," the boy announced when Ellis introduced himself. "Just launched my own fix-it business last week, and business is booming already!" His confidence was unmistakable, and that confidence concerned Ellis more than he wanted to admit.

### Scene 5 (66 words)
Ellis investigated further throughout the week by talking to various neighbors about Neville's prices. Shelves for ten dollars, repairs for twelve, and birdhouses for only eight. Every product and service was priced significantly cheaper than Maplewood Makers had established. Ellis carefully wrote every number down and brought the detailed comparison back to the fort. The team stared at the list in stunned silence as the reality sank in. Neville was undercutting every single price they had set, making the competitive threat both serious and immediate.

### Scene 6 (64 words)
"We have to match his prices immediately!" Ellis declared, pacing around the fort with panic rising in his voice. "If we don't lower our prices right now, every customer will choose Neville instead!" Riley nodded in agreement, looking frightened by the prospect of losing their business. "He's already taking our best customers." Ellis wanted to slash their shelf price to ten dollars that afternoon before they lost anyone else. Desperation was overtaking his usually careful, methodical approach to problem-solving.

### Scene 7 (66 words)
But Layla held up her hand firmly, stopping everyone from spiraling into panic mode. "Wait," she said with calm authority that cut through the tension. She pulled out the pricing sheet they had created back in Chapter Two. "Before we panic and make hasty decisions, let's examine the actual math." She pointed to the carefully calculated numbers. "Our shelf costs six dollars in materials alone, so if we charge ten like Neville, our profit shrinks to four dollars total, and divided among four partners, that's only one dollar each."

### Scene 8 (65 words)
Layla continued her analysis, letting the numbers speak for themselves. "One dollar each for two full hours of work means we'd be earning fifty cents per hour for our labor." She looked directly at Ellis with steady eyes. "We would be working for almost nothing." The room fell into heavy silence as the reality settled in. Ellis had been so consumed by panic that he had forgotten to consider profit margins. Competing purely on price would be a race to the bottom that nobody could win.

### Scene 9 (66 words)
Seeking guidance from someone with more experience, they visited Mr. Mason's workshop that weekend to explain their competition problem. He listened patiently to their entire story without interrupting or rushing them. "Here's something important about business," Mr. Mason said thoughtfully. "Not every customer wants the cheapest option available, even though it might seem that way. Some customers want the best quality and are willing to pay for it. You cannot be both the cheapest and the greatest because those strategies require different approaches. You must choose which type of customer you want to serve." That perspective changed everything.

### Scene 10 (65 words)
Back at the fort, energized by Mr. Mason's wisdom, they brainstormed their competitive advantage together. "What specific things do we offer that Neville doesn't or can't provide?" Layla asked, writing ideas on their whiteboard. Ellis thought carefully before answering with growing conviction. "Custom designs tailored to each customer's individual needs," he said. "I build exactly what each person wants, not just standard items." Riley contributed next: "Superior craftsmanship and quality construction that lasts for years. Our work is built to stand the test of time." Benny added his strength: "Personal service and friendly relationships with every customer. People enjoy working with us." They had distinctive strengths separating them from the competition.

### Scene 11 (66 words)
After extensive discussion that stretched past sunset, they reached a bold and counterintuitive decision against their initial instincts. They would not lower their prices to compete with Neville on cost alone. Instead, they would raise them strategically to signal superior quality to customers. Their shelf price increased from fifteen dollars to eighteen dollars. "Higher prices communicate clearly that our work is worth the investment and will last," Layla explained confidently. It felt terrifying to raise prices during a competitive threat, but their differentiation strategy meant distinguishing themselves through demonstrated excellence and reliability, not cheaper pricing that would undermine their value.

### Scene 12 (64 words)
Ellis contributed an innovative idea that would become their strongest differentiator in the competitive landscape. "What if we guarantee every piece of our work with a written promise?" he suggested with enthusiasm. Together they designed a small professional card to accompany every project they completed. It read in clear language: "Maplewood Makers guarantees our work unconditionally. If anything breaks within one month of installation, we will fix it free of charge with no questions asked." Neville offered no such guarantee, which was a significant weakness. This quality guarantee set them apart dramatically, proving to customers that they stood behind their craftsmanship and believed in what they built.

### Scene 13 (66 words)
The first week under their new strategy proved difficult and tested their resolve in unexpected ways. Three separate customers chose Neville over them because his prices were lower and seemed more attractive. Ellis felt serious doubt creeping into his thoughts like shadows lengthening across a room. "Maybe we made the wrong choice with our pricing decision," he whispered anxiously to Layla during a quiet moment when the others weren't listening. The order board looked emptier than it had in previous weeks. Riley stared at it without speaking, her worry evident in her expression. Fear and doubt are natural companions when competition intensifies and the path forward seems uncertain.

### Scene 14 (65 words)
Then everything shifted dramatically when the phone rang on Thursday afternoon, breaking the worried silence. It was Mrs. Lee, calling with frustration and disappointment clear in her voice. "I purchased a shelf from that other boy last week because it seemed cheaper," she explained with regret. "It fell off my wall this morning without warning, and everything on it crashed to the floor. Several valuable items broke into pieces." Ellis stared at Layla with wide eyes across the fort, processing what this meant. The callback they had secretly hoped for had arrived sooner than anyone expected.

### Scene 15 (66 words)
Ellis and Benny traveled to Mrs. Lee's house that afternoon to inspect the damage firsthand. The shelf had pulled completely out of the drywall, leaving ugly holes where it had been mounted incorrectly. Ellis examined Neville's work with a careful eye, noting every mistake that had been made. The screws were wrong for the job, far too short and thin to bear any meaningful weight safely. No wall anchors had been used, which was a critical oversight for drywall installation. The installation was rushed and fundamentally flawed in multiple ways demonstrating poor understanding of basic principles. It was never going to hold up over time.

### Scene 16 (65 words)
Ellis repaired and reinstalled the shelf properly and methodically, demonstrating exactly why their work commanded a higher price point. He selected the correct heavy-duty screws for the job, inserted proper wall anchors into each mounting point, and verified that everything was level and secure before stepping back. The process took additional time and care because doing quality work always requires patience, precision, and attention to detail. Mrs. Lee paid the full repair price of twenty dollars gratefully. "I should have come to you first instead of trying to save a few dollars," she said with sincere appreciation.

### Scene 17 (66 words)
Mrs. Lee wasted no time telling her neighbors exactly what had happened with both shelves. "The cheap shelf fell off my wall after just one week of normal use," she reported to anyone who would listen. "Maplewood Makers came the same day and fixed it properly with the right materials and techniques." Word spread quickly through the neighborhood as neighbors talked over fences and during walks. People throughout the community began discussing the meaningful difference between cheap work and good work. Neville's low prices had appeared to be a wonderful bargain initially when compared side by side, but the questionable quality failed to hold up over time.

### Scene 18 (64 words)
A brand new customer contacted them the very next morning, validating their strategic approach. Mr. Patel wanted custom bookshelves built for his home study and was willing to pay a quality premium for dependable work. "I heard you repaired Mrs. Lee's disaster beautifully and professionally," he said with respect. "I specifically want the team who does the job correctly the first time instead of having to fix problems later." He deliberately chose Maplewood Makers despite their higher prices, proving their theory about quality-focused customers. Their established reputation for quality craftsmanship attracted this valuable new customer through positive word-of-mouth referral.

### Scene 19 (66 words)
The entire team gathered at the fort that afternoon feeling relieved and justifiably proud of what they had accomplished through patience and strategic thinking. Their decision to compete on quality rather than price had proven successful beyond their initial hopes. "We didn't have to become the cheapest option available to attract and retain loyal customers who value our work," Ellis said with newly earned confidence. Layla smiled warmly at his growth through the challenging experience. "Our competitive advantage is caring deeply about doing excellent work and standing behind it with our guarantee." They had firmly established their price positioning as the premium quality choice in their local market.

### Scene 20 (65 words)
That evening, Layla examined the neighborhood map with fresh eyes and noticed something revealing about the competitive landscape. A new dot had appeared on the map, positioned separate and isolated from their established network. It represented Neville's operation as a single dot with thin flickering lines extending weakly toward a few customers. Several of his customer connections had already visibly broken and faded away, which spoke volumes. Meanwhile, the Maplewood Makers web continued glowing steady and strong across the neighborhood with bright connections. Their network was built on enduring trust and proven quality that customers could depend on, not merely on low prices that attracted people initially but failed to retain them.

## FL VOCABULARY

- **competition** — when another business offers the same products or services in the same market
- **competitive advantage** — the specific strengths that allow one business to outperform another
- **differentiation** — making your product or service distinctly different from competitors
- **price positioning** — strategically choosing where your price sits relative to competitors to signal value
- **quality premium** — the additional amount customers willingly pay for superior workmanship and reliability
- **race to the bottom** — when competitors keep lowering prices until nobody can make a profit
