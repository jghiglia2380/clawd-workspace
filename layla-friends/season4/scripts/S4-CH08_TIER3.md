## THE PIVOT

### Scene 1 (50 words)
Five weeks had passed since the business opened, and month two was beginning. Layla looked at the order board and the money jars with a feeling she could not quite name. Everything was running. Orders came in and went out on time. But the numbers had stopped climbing, and that bothered her.

### Scene 2 (50 words)
Layla reviewed the last three weekly totals in her notebook. Ninety-one dollars, then ninety-four, then ninety-two. Nearly identical. They were putting in more effort than the early weeks, but the revenue line had gone flat. She tapped her pencil against the page. "We are working harder and earning the same," she said quietly.

### Scene 3 (50 words)
That evening, Layla spread every order record across her bedroom floor and sorted them into four careful piles. Pottery in one corner, repairs in another, birdhouses in a third, and journals in the last. She wanted to see where their revenue was actually coming from, not where she assumed it was.

### Scene 4 (50 words)
The piles told a story the weekly totals had hidden. Pottery orders made up sixty percent of their revenue and the stack kept growing. Repairs held steady at twenty-five percent. Birdhouses sat at ten percent. Journals, the product Layla had created and designed herself, accounted for only five percent. The smallest pile of all.

### Scene 5 (50 words)
Layla pulled out records from the first two weeks and laid them beside the recent ones. Pottery had climbed from forty percent to sixty percent over eight weeks. Journals had fallen from twelve percent to five. Not a single customer had come back for a second journal. The trend pointed in only one direction.

### Scene 6 (50 words)
At the next team meeting, Layla taped a hand-drawn chart to the fort wall showing revenue broken down by product. Pottery had the tallest bar. Journals had the shortest. Riley leaned forward. Ellis folded his arms and studied it. "This is where our money comes from," Layla said. "And where it does not."

### Scene 7 (50 words)
Ellis pointed at the birdhouse bar. "Ten percent and holding flat. Should we stop making them?" Layla had already thought about it. "We stop building inventory. If someone orders one, we make it. But we do not keep extras sitting around." The team nodded. The birdhouse decision was practical and painless.

### Scene 8 (50 words)
Then silence settled over the room like a weight nobody wanted to carry. Journals sat at five percent with zero reorders. Everyone knew what the chart was saying. Layla had spent hours cutting paper, stitching bindings, and writing messages inside each cover. The journals were beautiful. But the chart did not measure beauty.

### Scene 9 (50 words)
Riley broke the silence first. "I know how this feels," she said softly, looking directly at Layla. "Remember when my platter came back? I loved that piece. I thought it was my best work. But the customer wanted something different." She paused. "Sometimes what we love is not what people need."

### Scene 10 (50 words)
"But I worked so hard on every one of them," Layla said. Her voice was thin and strained. "Every journal has my handwriting inside the cover." Ellis leaned forward gently. "The work was real," he said. "But the market tells us what people value. Not what we wish they valued."

### Scene 11 (50 words)
After the meeting, Layla walked alone to Frances's garden. Frances was trimming her rose bushes with practiced cuts. Layla told her about the journals. Frances set down her clippers and pointed at a branch she had just removed. "That was a good branch. I cut it so the best ones grow stronger."

### Scene 12 (50 words)
Layla sat on the garden bench and let the decision take shape inside her chest. Journals were the first product she had made on her own. They represented her creativity before the business even existed. But the data was clear, and the team needed her hours elsewhere. "No more journals," she said quietly.

### Scene 13 (50 words)
Monday morning, the order board reflected the new direction. Pottery orders filled most of the space. Repairs sat in a tidy row beside them. The birdhouse section was labeled FOR ORDERS ONLY. The journal section was gone. Layla had peeled the label off the board herself before anyone else arrived that morning.

### Scene 14 (50 words)
The two to three hours Layla had spent each week on journals were now free. She started helping Riley with clay preparation and glaze mixing every morning. The shift was immediate. "I can not believe how much faster this goes with two people," Riley said, pulling a finished bowl from the wheel.

### Scene 15 (50 words)
With the extra help, Riley completed three additional platters that week. Orders that normally stretched to Friday were finished by Wednesday. The team moved on to new work ahead of schedule for the first time in weeks. The fort had a different energy now, focused and purposeful instead of spread thin across too many products.

### Scene 16 (50 words)
Friday arrived and Layla counted the money. One hundred and seven dollars in revenue, up from the plateau of ninety-two. Costs dropped to twenty-eight dollars because they no longer purchased journal supplies or stocked birdhouse materials. Profit came to seventy-nine dollars. It was the best single week they had recorded yet.

### Scene 17 (50 words)
The second week after the pivot was even stronger. Revenue climbed to one hundred and fifteen dollars. Costs held steady at twenty-six. Profit reached eighty-nine dollars. Layla wrote the number in her notebook and circled it twice with a slow, deliberate line. "Record profit," she said. "And we are working fewer hours."

### Scene 18 (50 words)
Layla sat with her open notebook and traced the trend lines she had drawn. Revenue rising. Costs falling. Profit growing week over week. "The hard choice was the right choice," she said. Ellis looked at the chart on the wall. "The data told us what to do. You were brave enough to follow it."

### Scene 19 (50 words)
Benny mentioned that Neville had closed his repair shop for good. He had taken on too many products, let quality drop, and lost customers one by one. "We are still here because we make hard choices," Layla said. "Neville tried to do everything. We chose to do fewer things and do them well."

### Scene 20 (50 words)
The map on the wall had shifted. The thin lines where journal customers once connected had faded and disappeared. But the pottery lines glowed brighter than before, and new lines appeared in a cluster near the café. Layla traced a glowing path with her finger. "The map shows what works. We follow it."

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **pivot** (Scenes 12, 17) — A strategic change in what a business focuses on, based on market data
2. **revenue by category** (Scenes 4, 6) — Breaking down income by product type to see where the money comes from
3. **opportunity cost** (Scene 14) — The value of what you give up when you spend time on something else instead
4. **data-driven** (Scenes 3, 18) — Making business decisions based on numbers and evidence rather than feelings
5. **pruning** (Scene 11) — Cutting away good things to make room for the best things to grow

## NARRATION NOTES

- **Target WPM:** 125 (comfortable pace with expression)
- **Pause:** 1 second between sentences
- **Tone:** Emotional weight balanced with analytical clarity. Layla's sacrifice is the heart of the chapter, validated by post-pivot results.
