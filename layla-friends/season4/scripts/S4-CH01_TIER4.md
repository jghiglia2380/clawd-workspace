## GOING SOLO

### Scene 1 (58 words)
Three weeks had passed since the triumphant Makers' Faire, and the friends gathered in their fort on a cool Saturday morning to discuss what came next. Ellis opened his notebook to a page filled with careful handwriting and read through a list of fourteen separate requests. "All these people want us to make things or fix things for them," he said.

### Scene 2 (58 words)
Riley suggested they divide the requests so each person could focus on their strongest skill. She would handle the pottery commissions, Ellis would manage the repair jobs, Layla would try selling her handmade journals at the park, and Benny would take on neighborhood errands. The plan sounded clean and efficient, so they agreed and headed home to prepare for the week ahead.

### Scene 3 (58 words)
Riley arrived at Mrs. Patterson's door Monday morning, eager to land her first commission. "I'd like six matching mugs for my kitchen shelf," Mrs. Patterson explained, describing the size she had in mind. Riley agreed without hesitation, wanting to seem professional. But halfway home, a worry crept in — she had forgotten to ask what "matching" actually meant.

### Scene 4 (58 words)
Ellis showed up at Mr. Chen's house Monday afternoon to repair a broken garden gate, his toolbox heavy in one hand. He assessed the damage, identified the loose hinge and the cracked crossbar, and got to work. But halfway through, he discovered the crossbar needed a finer saw blade than anything he had brought. He would have to return the next day.

### Scene 5 (58 words)
Layla arranged her handmade journals on a bright blanket at the park on Tuesday afternoon, positioning each one so the hand-stitched spines and painted covers faced outward. Neighbors strolled past and paused to admire the craftsmanship, turning the pages and running their fingers along the binding. But after two full hours under the warm sun, not one person had opened their wallet.

### Scene 6 (58 words)
Benny threw himself into errands on Wednesday, darting across the neighborhood from one task to the next. He picked up groceries for Mrs. Williams, mailed a package for Mr. Patel, and returned library books for Mrs. Chen. By evening he felt proud of a productive day — until he realized he had no record of who paid him what.

### Scene 7 (58 words)
The friends gathered at the fort Thursday evening for a quick check-in, settling onto the wooden benches as the last light of the day faded. "How is everything going?" Layla asked, hoping for good news. Everyone said "fine," but their faces told a different story — Riley's hands were caked with dried clay, and Ellis still had sawdust in his hair.

### Scene 8 (58 words)
Riley's commission trouble deepened as the week wore on. Mrs. Patterson studied the first set of mugs and asked for them taller. Riley rebuilt the entire batch. Then Mrs. Patterson decided she preferred a different handle shape, so Riley started over a third time. "I just want her to be happy with them," she told Benny, her voice cracking.

### Scene 9 (58 words)
A frustrating pattern emerged midweek when Ellis mapped out his repair schedule. He would fix a door on Maple Street, walk twenty minutes across town to repair a shelf, then walk twenty minutes back for another Maple Street job. "I'm spending more time walking between houses than on the actual repairs," he told his dad over dinner.

### Scene 10 (58 words)
Friday brought Layla her first sale — a teacher bought a journal for eight dollars and complimented the binding. Layla glowed with pride as she handed it over. But at home that evening, she sat at the kitchen table and did the math. Four hours of cutting, stitching, and painting had gone into that journal. Two dollars an hour. Her pride wilted.

### Scene 11 (58 words)
Benny's lack of record-keeping caught up with him Friday afternoon. After collecting payment from Mrs. Chen for her grocery delivery, he got confused about which bills belonged to which errand and accidentally handed Mrs. Chen's money to Mr. Patel. When Mrs. Chen came looking for her change, Benny had nothing left to give her. He dug into his own allowance to make it right.

### Scene 12 (58 words)
That evening, Layla walked to Frances's garden, needing a quiet place to sort through the week's disappointments. Frances was kneeling among the tomato plants, pulling ripe fruit from the vines. She noticed Layla's slumped shoulders and set down her basket. Layla told her about the journals. Frances nodded. "One tomato plant feeds a family. A whole garden feeds the neighborhood."

### Scene 13 (58 words)
By Thursday, Ellis reached capacity. Two new repair requests came in from neighbors who needed help, and he had to turn them both down. "I'm sorry, I've got too much on my plate right now," he told Mr. Rodriguez, who nodded with understanding. Ellis felt a knot in his stomach — he hated saying no, but one person could only stretch so far.

### Scene 14 (58 words)
Riley's breaking point arrived Friday afternoon when she delivered the mugs to Mrs. Patterson for the fourth time, each one reshaped and smoothed to meet the latest round of feedback. Mrs. Patterson examined them one by one, turning each mug slowly in her hands, then set them down with a sigh. "These still aren't quite right," she said gently. Riley blinked back tears.

### Scene 15 (58 words)
Friday night, each friend sat alone in their room and counted the money they had earned over the course of a long, exhausting week. Riley had twenty-two dollars from pottery. Ellis earned nineteen from his repair jobs. Layla had twelve from her single journal sale. Benny was left with five dollars after covering his payment mistake. The numbers felt painfully small.

### Scene 16 (58 words)
They reconvened at the fort Saturday morning, but a heavy silence settled over the group. Nobody wanted to share their numbers first. Finally Riley placed her crumpled bills on the table. One by one, the others followed. Fifty-eight dollars sat in a small pile between them. "That looks decent," Benny offered hopefully. Then Layla leaned forward with the question that mattered.

### Scene 17 (58 words)
"How many hours did we each put in this week?" Layla asked, pulling a pencil from behind her ear. They worked it out together — Riley had logged twelve hours, Ellis ten, Layla seven, and Benny six. Thirty-five hours combined. "Fifty-eight dollars for thirty-five hours," Ellis said, the math settling over him like a cold blanket. The fort went quiet.

### Scene 18 (58 words)
Mr. Mason appeared at the fort entrance just then, returning a borrowed clamp, and paused when he noticed the small pile of money and four discouraged faces. "How did the solo experiment go?" he asked, setting the clamp down. Ellis walked him through the numbers. Mr. Mason considered for a moment. "That comes to less than two dollars an hour," he observed.

### Scene 19 (58 words)
Layla studied each of her friends around the table — Riley's clay-stained fingers, Ellis's tired eyes, Benny's crooked scarf, and the meager pile of bills that represented thirty-five hours of combined effort. "What if these aren't four separate problems?" she said, the idea forming as she spoke. "What if it's one problem? We're each trying to do everything alone."

### Scene 20 (58 words)
Riley sat up straighter. "So what if we worked together — like one real team?" Ellis considered the idea for a long moment, then nodded slowly. "That could work way better than this." Benny broke into a grin. "Let's try it for one week!" They shook on it, and for the first time since Monday, nobody in the fort felt tired.

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **commission** (Scene 3) — A paid request to create something specific for a customer
2. **revenue** (Scene 15, implied) — The total money earned before subtracting costs
3. **capacity** (Scene 13) — The maximum amount of work one person can handle
4. **per hour** (Scenes 10, 18) — Earnings divided by the number of hours worked
5. **division of labor** (Scene 2, implied) — Splitting work based on each person's strengths
6. **opportunity cost** (Scene 10) — What you give up when you spend time on one thing instead of another
