## GOING SOLO

### Scene 1 (105 words)
Three weeks had passed since the triumphant Makers' Faire, and the friends gathered in their secret fort on Saturday morning to discuss the unexpected consequences of their success. Ellis, who had been meticulously tracking every request in his organized notebook, opened it carefully and showed them a detailed list written in his precise handwriting. "I wrote down everything people asked for at the faire," he explained, running his finger down the page with each entry. "Fourteen separate people want us to either make things or fix things for them." Everyone felt a mixture of excitement about the opportunity and nervousness about managing so much work with their limited experience and resources.

### Scene 2 (103 words)
"That's way too much work for just four people," said Layla, studying the impressively long list with growing concern about their capacity. Riley, characteristically quick to propose solutions when faced with challenges, nodded thoughtfully as she considered their options. "We should divide them up based on what each of us does best," she suggested practically. "I can handle all the pottery commissions since I learned those techniques at the valley. Ellis can manage the repairs because he's naturally skilled with tools and problem-solving. Layla can create the journals, and Benny can run neighborhood errands." Everyone agreed readily, assuming that working independently would be simpler and more efficient than the complex coordination required for teamwork.

### Scene 3 (110 words)
Riley visited Mrs. Patterson first thing Monday morning, eager to launch her pottery commission business with confidence. "I'd like six matching mugs for my kitchen counter," Mrs. Patterson said with a warm, welcoming smile that immediately put Riley at ease. Riley, not wanting to appear inexperienced or unprofessional to her first real customer, said yes immediately and enthusiastically without pausing to ask any clarifying follow-up questions. But as she walked home with the commission secured, a nagging worry began forming in her mind. She suddenly realized she had forgotten to ask what "matching" actually meant in Mrs. Patterson's vision. Did she want identical colors? The same precise size? Matching decorative patterns? Riley understood with growing concern that she should have asked far more specific questions before confidently agreeing to the project.

### Scene 4 (105 words)
Ellis approached Mr. Chen's property on Monday afternoon to repair his broken gate, carrying his heavy toolbox and feeling confident about completing the straightforward job efficiently. He carefully assessed the structural damage, identified the problem with professional competence, and began working with focused determination. However, exactly halfway through the repair process, he made a frustrating discovery. He needed a different type of specialized saw blade that he hadn't anticipated bringing with him from his workshop. "I don't have that blade in my current toolbox," he told Mr. Chen apologetically, feeling his confidence deflate. He would have to return the following day to finish the job, which bothered him because it represented wasted time and diminished efficiency.

### Scene 5 (108 words)
Layla set up her carefully arranged display at the park on Tuesday afternoon with her beautifully handmade journals, positioning them strategically on a colorful blanket to attract maximum attention from passersby. She felt genuine pride in her meticulous craftsmanship as people walked by and paused to admire her work with appreciative expressions. Some curious visitors picked up the journals, examined the careful hand-stitching closely, ran their fingers over the textured covers, and smiled with what appeared to be genuine appreciation of the quality. But after sitting there patiently for two full hours under the warm sun, not a single person had actually purchased anything despite their apparent interest. Layla packed up feeling deeply disappointed, gaining the hard lesson that creating quality products was considerably easier than convincing potential customers to actually spend their money.

### Scene 6 (106 words)
Benny enthusiastically started running errands for various neighbors on Wednesday morning, eager to be helpful to everyone in the close-knit community who had supported their faire. Mrs. Williams needed specific groceries purchased from the corner store. Mr. Patel wanted an important package mailed promptly at the post office before the afternoon deadline. Mrs. Chen asked him to return several overdue library books that she'd been meaning to return for weeks. Benny felt simultaneously helpful and productively busy throughout the entire day, enjoying the sense of purpose. Unfortunately, when evening finally arrived and he tried to reconcile his finances, he discovered he couldn't remember who had paid him what specific amount for which service because he had carelessly forgotten to write any of the financial transactions down in any kind of organized record book.

### Scene 7 (109 words)
The friends reconvened at the fort Thursday evening for a quick informal check-in to assess how everyone's independent week was progressing with their various solo ventures. "How's your stuff going so far?" asked Layla hopefully, wanting to hear positive progress reports from her friends. Everyone responded with a quick, automatic "fine," but their exhausted faces and slumped postures told an entirely different story that contradicted their optimistic words. Riley's usually clean hands were covered in stubborn dried clay that had worked into her fingernails. Ellis had sawdust thoroughly stuck in his normally neat hair. Layla yawned repeatedly from bone-deep exhaustion. Benny's signature green plaid scarf hung noticeably crooked around his neck. Despite the evidence, nobody wanted to be the first person to admit they were struggling with the challenges.

### Scene 8 (105 words)
Riley's escalating customer problem grew progressively worse as the frustrating week continued without resolution. When she proudly delivered the first complete set of mugs to Mrs. Patterson on Wednesday, expecting praise, the woman examined them carefully before frowning slightly with polite disappointment. "Can you possibly make them a bit taller to better match my cabinet dimensions?" she asked apologetically. Riley remade them entirely with painstakingly adjusted dimensions. Then Mrs. Patterson thoughtfully decided she actually preferred different-shaped handles that would be more comfortable to grip. Riley, growing increasingly frustrated with the endless revisions, made them a third time. "I just want her to be happy with them," Riley told Benny later that evening, fighting back hot tears of mounting frustration and self-doubt.

### Scene 9 (108 words)
Ellis came to a troubling realization that he was wasting precious, valuable time inefficiently traveling between scattered jobs instead of working productively on actual repairs that generated income. He would conscientiously fix a broken door on Maple Street, then walk all the way across town to repair a wobbly shelf for another customer, then journey back to Maple Street for yet another repair job in the same neighborhood he'd already left. "I'm spending way more time walking between jobs than actually doing repairs," he complained to his dad over dinner that evening with mounting frustration. His dad nodded knowingly with sympathetic understanding, having faced identical efficiency challenges in his own carpentry work throughout his career.

### Scene 10 (106 words)
On Friday afternoon, Layla finally achieved her first sale when she sold one of her meticulously handmade journals at her park display. A friendly elementary teacher purchased it for eight dollars, and Layla felt proud of successfully making her very first independent sale as an entrepreneur. However, when she sat down to figure out the actual economics at her kitchen table that evening, her initial pride quickly transformed into serious concern about the business model's viability. The journal had required four solid hours to create from start to finish. Eight dollars divided by four hours of skilled labor meant she had effectively earned only two dollars per hour for her detailed craftsmanship, which seemed like woefully inadequate compensation for work requiring such concentration and skill.

### Scene 11 (110 words)
Benny's most significant and embarrassing mistake of the entire challenging week occurred Friday afternoon when his disorganized financial tracking system finally collapsed under its own chaotic weight. He had successfully collected exact payment from Mrs. Chen for efficiently delivering her requested groceries to her doorstep. But then, thoroughly confused about which specific money belonged to which that transaction in his muddled mental accounting, he accidentally gave that exact same money to Mr. Patel as change for a different errand involving the post office. When Mrs. Chen politely asked for her correct change later that afternoon, Benny discovered with horror that he didn't have it anymore because he'd already given it away. He had to dip into his own carefully saved allowance money to correct the mortifying mistake, which felt terrible and highlighted his lack of organization.

### Scene 12 (105 words)
That evening, feeling discouraged by the disappointing journal sales, Layla visited Frances in her flourishing garden because she needed quiet time to think things through carefully and process her frustration. Frances was methodically picking ripe, red tomatoes from her healthy, productive vines with practiced efficiency. "You look exhausted and troubled," Frances observed kindly, immediately noticing Layla's slumped shoulders and disappointed expression. Layla explained her discouraging experience attempting to sell journals despite their obvious quality. Frances smiled with warm understanding born from years of experience. "One single tomato plant produces some tomatoes for one family," she said thoughtfully, gesturing to her abundant garden. "But a whole interconnected garden feeds the entire neighborhood." Layla pondered the deeper meaning of that agricultural metaphor.

### Scene 13 (108 words)
By Thursday afternoon, Ellis found himself in the uncomfortable and guilt-inducing position of having to turn down two new repair requests from neighbors who needed his help. "I'm really sorry, but I'm too busy right now to take on more work," he explained reluctantly to Mr. Rodriguez, who looked disappointed but understanding. Ellis felt guilty about saying no to people in his community who needed assistance. However, he was already actively juggling four different simultaneous projects at various stages of completion. He couldn't physically or mentally do more work entirely by himself without burning out. There simply weren't enough hours in a single day for one person to handle everything alone, regardless of how efficient or dedicated they tried to be.

### Scene 14 (106 words)
Riley's emotional breaking point arrived suddenly on Friday afternoon when she brought Mrs. Patterson the extensively revised mugs for the fourth exhausting time. She had spent countless additional hours perfecting them meticulously based on all the detailed feedback and specific requests. Mrs. Patterson examined them carefully with a critical eye, rotating each one slowly to check every angle, then frowned apologetically with genuine regret. "These still aren't quite what I had in mind," she said gently, trying to soften the rejection. Riley felt tears threatening to spill from sheer frustration and disappointment. "I don't want to do customer commissions anymore," she told Ellis later that evening with finality. "It's too emotionally draining constantly trying to satisfy other people's ever-changing expectations."

### Scene 15 (109 words)
Friday night, each friend privately counted their modest week's earnings alone in their respective bedrooms, reflecting soberly on the considerable efforts they had invested. Riley had earned twenty-two dollars from her pottery work and multiple revisions. Ellis had made nineteen dollars from his various repair jobs around the neighborhood. Layla had earned only twelve dollars total from her single journal sale. Benny had merely five dollars remaining after using his own money to fix his embarrassing payment mistake with the neighbors. They all felt disappointed and deflated by the modest amounts that seemed inadequate. The money seemed insignificant and almost insulting compared to all the exhausting hours of hard work they had invested throughout the demanding week.

### Scene 16 (105 words)
Saturday morning, they reconvened at their familiar fort for their regular weekly meeting with heavy hearts. Nobody wanted to be the first person to share their disappointing financial results and admit failure. Finally, Riley gathered her courage and decisively placed her crumpled dollar bills on the worn wooden table with a soft thud. "Twenty-two dollars," she announced quietly, unable to meet anyone's eyes directly. The others slowly added their own earnings one by one in a small, unimpressive pile. Together, they had fifty-eight dollars total from a week of work. "That actually seems like quite a lot when you see it all together," said Benny hopefully, trying to find optimism. Then Layla asked the crucial question that would change everything about their perspective.

### Scene 17 (108 words)
"How many hours did we each work this week?" Layla asked seriously, pulling out paper and a pencil to figure out the important mathematics accurately. They figured out the honest numbers together carefully, no one wanting to exaggerate or minimize their efforts. Riley had worked approximately twelve hours on pottery and revisions. Ellis had invested ten hours in repairs and travel. Layla had worked seven hours creating and attempting to sell journals. Benny had contributed six hours of errand-running effort for the community. That totaled exactly thirty-five hours of combined labor from four people. "Fifty-eight dollars earned from thirty-five hours of dedicated work," Ellis said slowly, doing the troubling division calculation in his head while everyone waited anxiously for the disappointing result.

### Scene 18 (106 words)
Mr. Mason stopped by the fort unexpectedly to return a borrowed tool and immediately noticed the small pile of money and universally exhausted expressions on young faces. "How did your first entrepreneurial week go?" he asked gently with genuine curiosity, settling onto a bench to hear their story. Ellis explained honestly about earning fifty-eight dollars collectively across thirty-five hours of combined work from four people. "That works out to less than two dollars per hour per person," Mr. Mason observed quietly and carefully, letting the harsh economic calculation hang in the still air without additional commentary. The friends absorbed the disappointing reality of their inefficient solo approach, understanding now why they felt so exhausted for such minimal financial compensation.

### Scene 19 (109 words)
Layla looked carefully and thoughtfully at each of her close friends gathered around the table, noticing all the obvious signs of struggle written on their faces and bodies. She saw Riley's frustrated expression and clay-stained hands, Ellis's tired eyes with dark circles underneath, Benny's characteristically crooked scarf hanging loosely, and the disappointingly small pile of money representing their collective week's exhausting efforts. "What if these aren't four separate problems?" she said thoughtfully as a new realization formed clearly in her mind. "What if it's really one problem? We're all trying to do everything alone." Everyone sat up noticeably straighter, suddenly listening intently to her important insight.

### Scene 20 (105 words)
"What if we worked together as one business instead of working by ourselves?" Layla proposed clearly, gaining confidence as the idea crystallized in her thinking. "We could support each other and share the workload instead of struggling separately." Riley's eyes lit up immediately with renewed hope and possibility. "You mean like a real team?" Ellis nodded slowly and deliberately, his analytical mind considering all the practical possibilities and advantages. "That might work way better than what we're doing now." Benny grinned enthusiastically, his natural optimism returning. "Let's try it for a week!" They all agreed unanimously and eagerly to attempt genuine partnership, hoping cooperation would succeed where isolation had clearly failed.

## FL VOCABULARY

- **commission** — a paid request to create something specific for a customer
- **transaction** — an exchange of money, goods, or services between parties
- **entrepreneurial** — related to starting and running a business venture
- **compensation** — payment received in exchange for work or services provided
- **partnership** — a business relationship where people work together toward shared goals
- **revenue** — the total money earned before subtracting costs
- **capacity** — the maximum amount of work someone can handle
- **viability** — whether something can work successfully over time
