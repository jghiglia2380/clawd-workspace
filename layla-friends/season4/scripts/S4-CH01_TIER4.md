## GOING SOLO
### Scene 1 (105 words)
Three weeks had passed since the triumphant Makers' Faire, and the friends gathered in their secret fort on Saturday morning to discuss what happened next. Ellis had been tracking every request in his notebook. He opened it carefully and showed them a detailed list written in his neat handwriting. "I wrote down everything people asked for at the faire," he explained, running his finger down the page with each entry. "Fourteen separate people want us to either make things or fix things for them." Everyone felt excited about the opportunity but also nervous about managing so much work with their limited experience and resources.
### Scene 2 (103 words)
"That's way too much work for just four people," said Layla, studying the long list with concern. Riley nodded thoughtfully as she considered their options. She always had ideas when they faced problems. "We should divide them up based on what each of us does best," she suggested. "I can handle all the pottery commissions since I learned those techniques at the valley. Ellis can manage the repairs because he's good with tools and problem-solving. Layla can create the journals, and Benny can run neighborhood errands." Everyone agreed readily, assuming that working alone would be simpler and more efficient than trying to coordinate as a team.
### Scene 3 (110 words)
Riley visited Mrs. Patterson first thing Monday morning, eager to start her pottery commission business. "I'd like six matching mugs for my kitchen counter," Mrs. Patterson said with a warm smile that put Riley at ease. Riley didn't want to seem inexperienced to her first real customer, so she said yes right away without asking any questions. But as she walked home with the commission secured, a worry began forming in her mind. She suddenly realized she had forgotten to ask what "matching" actually meant. Did Mrs. Patterson want identical colors? The same exact size? Matching patterns? Riley understood with concern that she should have asked way more questions before agreeing to the project.
### Scene 4 (105 words)
Ellis went to Mr. Chen's property on Monday afternoon to repair his broken gate, carrying his heavy toolbox and feeling confident about the job. He carefully looked at the damage, identified the problem, and began working with determination. However, halfway through the repair, he made a frustrating discovery. He needed a different type of saw blade that he hadn't thought to bring from his workshop. "I don't have that blade in my toolbox," he told Mr. Chen, feeling embarrassed. He would have to return the following day to finish the job, which bothered him because it meant wasted time and he'd be less efficient than he'd planned.
### Scene 5 (108 words)
Layla set up her display at the park on Tuesday afternoon with her handmade journals, placing them on a colorful blanket to attract attention from people walking by. She felt proud of her careful craftsmanship as people paused to admire her work with appreciation. Some curious visitors picked up the journals, examined the hand-stitching closely, ran their fingers over the textured covers, and smiled with what seemed like real appreciation of the quality. But after sitting there patiently for two full hours under the warm sun, not a single person actually bought anything despite their interest. Layla packed up feeling deeply disappointed, learning the hard lesson that creating quality products was much easier than convincing customers to spend their money.
### Scene 6 (106 words)
Benny started running errands for neighbors on Wednesday morning, eager to be helpful to everyone in the community who had supported their faire. Mrs. Williams needed groceries from the corner store. Mr. Patel wanted a package mailed at the post office before the afternoon deadline. Mrs. Chen asked him to return several overdue library books that she'd been meaning to return for weeks. Benny felt helpful and busy throughout the entire day, enjoying the sense of purpose. Unfortunately, when evening arrived and he tried to figure out his money, he discovered he couldn't remember who had paid him what amount for which service. He had forgotten to write any of the transactions down in his notebook.
### Scene 7 (109 words)
The friends met at the fort Thursday evening for a quick check-in to see how everyone's week was going with their solo ventures. "How's your stuff going so far?" asked Layla hopefully, wanting to hear good news from her friends. Everyone responded with a quick "fine," but their exhausted faces and slumped postures told an entirely different story. Riley's usually clean hands were covered in stubborn dried clay that had worked into her fingernails. Ellis had sawdust stuck in his normally neat hair. Layla yawned repeatedly from exhaustion. Benny's green plaid scarf hung crooked around his neck. Despite the obvious signs of struggle, nobody wanted to be the first person to admit they were having a hard time.
### Scene 8 (105 words)
Riley's customer problem grew worse as the week continued. When she delivered the first complete set of mugs to Mrs. Patterson on Wednesday, hoping for praise, the woman examined them carefully before frowning slightly. "Can you make them a bit taller to match my cabinet dimensions?" she asked. Riley remade them entirely with adjusted dimensions. Then Mrs. Patterson decided she actually preferred different-shaped handles that would be more comfortable to grip. Riley, growing frustrated with the endless revisions, made them a third time. "I just want her to be happy with them," Riley told Benny later that evening, fighting back tears of frustration and doubt.
### Scene 9 (108 words)
Ellis came to a troubling realization that he was wasting precious time traveling between scattered jobs instead of working on actual repairs that made him money. He would fix a broken door on Maple Street, then walk all the way across town to repair a wobbly shelf for another customer, then walk back to Maple Street for yet another repair job in the same neighborhood he'd already left. "I'm spending way more time walking between jobs than actually doing repairs," he complained to his dad over dinner that evening with frustration. His dad nodded with understanding, having faced the same efficiency challenges in his own carpentry work throughout his career.
### Scene 10 (106 words)
On Friday afternoon, Layla finally made her first sale when she sold one of her handmade journals at her park display. A friendly elementary teacher bought it for eight dollars, and Layla felt proud of making her very first sale as an entrepreneur. However, when she sat down to figure out the actual numbers at her kitchen table that evening, her pride quickly turned into concern about whether this could actually work. The journal had taken four solid hours to create from start to finish. Eight dollars divided by four hours of work meant she had earned only two dollars per hour for her craftsmanship, which seemed like way too little money for work that required such concentration and skill.
### Scene 11 (110 words)
Benny's biggest and most embarrassing mistake of the entire week happened Friday afternoon when his disorganized money tracking system finally fell apart. He had collected payment from Mrs. Chen for delivering her groceries to her doorstep. But then, confused about which money belonged to which transaction in his head, he accidentally gave that same money to Mr. Patel as change for a different errand involving the post office. When Mrs. Chen asked for her correct change later that afternoon, Benny discovered with horror that he didn't have it anymore because he'd already given it away. He had to use his own saved allowance money to fix the embarrassing mistake, which felt terrible and showed how disorganized he'd been.
### Scene 12 (105 words)
That evening, feeling discouraged by the journal sales, Layla visited Frances in her garden because she needed quiet time to think things through and process her frustration. Frances was picking ripe, red tomatoes from her healthy vines. "You look exhausted and troubled," Frances said kindly, noticing Layla's slumped shoulders and disappointed expression. Layla explained her discouraging experience trying to sell journals despite their quality. Frances smiled with understanding. "One tomato plant produces some tomatoes for one family," she said thoughtfully, gesturing to her abundant garden. "But a whole garden feeds the entire neighborhood." Layla thought about what Frances meant.
### Scene 13 (108 words)
By Thursday afternoon, Ellis found himself in the uncomfortable position of having to turn down two new repair requests from neighbors who needed his help. "I'm really sorry, but I'm too busy right now to take on more work," he explained to Mr. Rodriguez, who looked disappointed but understanding. Ellis felt guilty about saying no to people in his community who needed help. However, he was already juggling four different projects at various stages. He couldn't do more work by himself without burning out. There simply weren't enough hours in a day for one person to handle everything alone, no matter how efficient or dedicated they tried to be.
### Scene 14 (106 words)
Riley's breaking point came on Friday afternoon when she brought Mrs. Patterson the revised mugs for the fourth time. She had spent countless hours perfecting them based on all the detailed feedback and requests. Mrs. Patterson examined them carefully, rotating each one slowly to check every angle, then frowned. "These still aren't quite what I had in mind," she said gently, trying to soften the rejection. Riley felt tears threatening to spill from frustration and disappointment. "I don't want to do customer commissions anymore," she told Ellis later that evening. "I'm so tired of redoing stuff when she keeps changing her mind."
### Scene 15 (109 words)
Friday night, each friend counted their week's earnings alone in their bedrooms, thinking about how much effort they had put in. Riley had earned twenty-two dollars from her pottery work and multiple revisions. Ellis had made nineteen dollars from his various repair jobs around the neighborhood. Layla had earned only twelve dollars total from her single journal sale. Benny had merely five dollars remaining after using his own money to fix his embarrassing payment mistake with the neighbors. They all felt disappointed by the small amounts. The money seemed tiny and almost insulting compared to all the exhausting hours of hard work they had put in throughout the week.
### Scene 16 (105 words)
Saturday morning, they met at their familiar fort for their weekly meeting with heavy hearts. Nobody wanted to be the first person to share their disappointing results and admit failure. Finally, Riley gathered her courage and placed her crumpled dollar bills on the worn wooden table with a soft thud. "Twenty-two dollars," she said quietly, unable to meet anyone's eyes. The others slowly added their own earnings one by one in a small pile. Together, they had fifty-eight dollars total from a week of work. "That actually seems like quite a lot when you see it all together," said Benny hopefully. Then Layla asked the question that would change everything.
### Scene 17 (108 words)
"How many hours did we each work this week?" Layla asked seriously, pulling out paper and a pencil to figure out the math. They worked out the honest numbers together carefully, no one wanting to exaggerate or downplay their efforts. Riley had worked about twelve hours on pottery and revisions. Ellis had put in ten hours in repairs and travel. Layla had worked seven hours creating and trying to sell journals. Benny had done six hours of errand-running for the community. That totaled exactly thirty-five hours of combined work from four people. "Fifty-eight dollars earned from thirty-five hours of work," Ellis said slowly, doing the division in his head while everyone waited for the disappointing result.
### Scene 18 (106 words)
Mr. Mason stopped by the fort to return a borrowed tool and noticed the small pile of money and exhausted looks on their faces. "How did your first entrepreneurial week go?" he asked gently with curiosity, settling onto a bench to hear their story. Ellis explained about earning fifty-eight dollars across thirty-five hours of combined work from four people. "That works out to less than two dollars per hour per person," Mr. Mason said quietly, letting the math sink in. The friends absorbed the disappointing reality of their solo approach, understanding now why they felt so exhausted for such little money.
### Scene 19 (109 words)
Layla looked at each of her friends gathered around the table, noticing all the obvious signs of struggle on their faces and bodies. She saw Riley's frustrated expression and clay-stained hands, Ellis's tired eyes with dark circles underneath, Benny's crooked scarf hanging loosely, and the disappointingly small pile of money representing their week's exhausting efforts. "What if these aren't four separate problems?" she said thoughtfully as a new idea formed in her mind. "What if it's really one problem? We're all trying to do everything alone." Everyone sat up straighter, suddenly listening carefully to her insight.
### Scene 20 (105 words)
"What if we worked together as one business instead of working by ourselves?" Layla said, gaining confidence as the idea became clearer in her mind. "We could support each other and share the workload instead of struggling separately." Riley's eyes lit up with hope. "You mean like a real team?" Ellis nodded slowly, his mind considering all the possibilities and advantages. "That might work way better than what we're doing now." Benny grinned, his natural optimism returning. "Let's try it for a week!" They all agreed to try working as real partners, hoping teamwork would succeed where working alone had clearly failed.

## FL VOCABULARY
- **commission** — a paid request to create something specific for a customer
- **transaction** — an exchange of money, goods, or services between parties
- **entrepreneurial** — related to starting and running a business venture
- **compensation** — payment received in exchange for work or services provided
- **partnership** — a business relationship where people work together
- **revenue** — the total money earned before subtracting costs
- **capacity** — the maximum amount of work someone can handle
- **whether it could work** — whether something can work successfully over time
