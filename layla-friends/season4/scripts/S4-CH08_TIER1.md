## THE PIVOT

### Scene 1 (30 words)
A new month started. The team had been in business for five weeks. They were still making money, but the number was the same every week. It was not going up.

### Scene 2 (30 words)
Layla looked at her notebook. Last week they made ninety-one dollars. The week before, ninety-four. The week before that, ninety-two. "We are stuck," said Layla. The numbers were not growing.

### Scene 3 (30 words)
Layla spread out all the orders and put them in piles. One pile for pottery. One for repairs. One for birdhouses. One for journals. She wanted to see where the money came from.

### Scene 4 (30 words)
Pottery was the biggest pile. It made most of the money. Journals was the smallest pile. It made the least. Layla made the journals herself. That part made her feel sad.

### Scene 5 (30 words)
Layla looked at the old records. Pottery was growing. More people wanted it every week. Journals were shrinking. Fewer people bought them each time. Nobody came back for a second journal.

### Scene 6 (30 words)
At the meeting, Layla showed a chart. Pottery had a tall bar. Journals had a tiny bar. "This is where our money comes from," she said. Everyone could see the difference.

### Scene 7 (30 words)
"Should we stop making birdhouses?" asked Ellis. "Only make them when someone asks," said Layla. "No more keeping extras." That was an easy choice. Everyone agreed right away.

### Scene 8 (30 words)
Then it got quiet. Journals were next. Layla had made every journal by hand. She loved them. But almost nobody bought them. The chart was clear. Layla looked down at the table.

### Scene 9 (30 words)
"I know how this feels," said Riley. "My platter got sent back. I loved it, but the customer did not want it. Sometimes what we love is not what people need."

### Scene 10 (30 words)
"But I worked so hard on them," Layla said quietly. Ellis put his hand on the table. "The numbers tell us what people want," he said. "Not what we want."

### Scene 11 (30 words)
Layla went to see Frances in her garden. "I have to stop making journals," Layla said. Frances showed her a rose bush. "I cut good branches so the best ones grow."

### Scene 12 (30 words)
Layla sat on the garden bench. It hurt to let go. Journals were her first product. But the numbers were clear. "No more journals," she said. Her voice cracked a little.

### Scene 13 (30 words)
On Monday, the order board looked different. Pottery took up most of the space. Repairs sat in a row beside it. Birdhouses said FOR ORDERS ONLY. The journal spot was gone.

### Scene 14 (30 words)
Now Layla had extra time. She helped Riley get clay ready and mix glazes. "This is so much faster with help!" said Riley. They worked side by side at the pottery bench.

### Scene 15 (30 words)
Riley made three extra platters that week. Work that used to take until Friday was done by Wednesday. The fort felt calmer. Everyone had more time and less to worry about.

### Scene 16 (30 words)
At the end of the week, Layla counted the money. One hundred and seven dollars! Costs went down because they stopped buying journal supplies. Profit was seventy-nine dollars. That was the best yet.

### Scene 17 (30 words)
The next week was even better. They made one hundred and fifteen dollars. Costs stayed low. Profit was eighty-nine dollars. Layla circled the number in her notebook. The change was working!

### Scene 18 (30 words)
"The hard choice was the right choice," said Layla. She looked at the numbers in her notebook. Revenue was going up. Costs were going down. The data told the story.

### Scene 19 (30 words)
Benny said that Neville closed his shop. He tried to do too much and his work got bad. "We are still here because we made hard choices," said Layla.

### Scene 20 (30 words)
The map changed. The journal lines faded away. But the pottery lines glowed brighter than ever. New ones appeared near the café. Layla smiled. "The map shows what works."

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **pivot** (Scene 12) — Changing what your business makes based on what the numbers show
2. **data-driven** (Scene 18) — Using numbers to help you decide what to do
