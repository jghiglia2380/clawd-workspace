## THE PARTNERSHIP

### Scene 1 (58 words)
Saturday morning at the fort, an unexpected visitor appeared in the doorway. It was Ellen, the woman who owned the corner café. She stepped inside and scanned the shelves of finished pottery, the order board on the wall, and the row of labeled money jars. "I have a business idea for you," she said, folding her arms with a confident smile.

### Scene 2 (58 words)
Ellen explained that customers at her café asked about the pottery mugs almost every week. "I would like to display and sell your pottery at my café," she said. "People notice the mugs when I serve their coffee, and they want to know where they come from." Riley sat up straight in her chair. A real café wanted to carry her work.

### Scene 3 (58 words)
Benny leaned forward with immediate interest. "So you want to buy a batch of mugs from us?" Ellen shook her head. "Not exactly. I do not want to purchase them up front. I want to display them on a shelf. When a customer buys one, we split the money between us." The team exchanged glances. This was a model they had never considered.

### Scene 4 (58 words)
They walked over to Mr. Mason's workshop so he could help them understand the arrangement. Ellen came along and described the idea again. Mr. Mason listened carefully and nodded. "That is called consignment," he said. "Ellen does not purchase the mugs. She displays them. When a customer buys one, she keeps a percentage and sends the rest to you."

### Scene 5 (58 words)
"How is that different from just selling them to her?" Ellis asked. Mr. Mason drew two simple columns on a scrap of wood with chalk. "If she bought them outright, that would be wholesale. She pays you now and takes full ownership. With consignment, you keep ownership until the customer buys. Ellen only pays you after a sale actually happens."

### Scene 6 (58 words)
The team discussed the offer back at the fort while Ellen waited at the café. Consignment meant Ellen carried no financial risk on unsold items. If the mugs did not sell, they returned to the team. "That is fair," said Layla. "She gives us shelf space and introduces us to her customers. That exposure has real value even before a single mug sells."

### Scene 7 (58 words)
Ellen had proposed keeping forty percent of each sale as her share. Benny turned the number over in his head. Forty percent of a twelve-dollar mug meant Ellen would keep four dollars and eighty cents while they received seven dollars and twenty cents. Was that the right split? He was not sure, but he knew better than to accept the first offer without thinking.

### Scene 8 (58 words)
That evening, the team gathered at the fort and ran the numbers across three possible splits. At forty percent, Ellen kept four dollars and eighty cents per mug. At thirty percent, three dollars and sixty cents. At thirty-five, four dollars and twenty cents. Benny studied the columns Layla had written on the board. "I think we can land somewhere between thirty and thirty-five."

### Scene 9 (58 words)
The next day, Benny walked to the café to present his counter-offer. His palms were damp and his voice carried a slight tremor he worked to control. "What if you kept thirty percent for the first month as a trial period?" he said. "Then thirty-five percent going forward after that?" Ellen considered the terms for a quiet moment. "That works for me."

### Scene 10 (58 words)
Back at the fort, Layla added one final condition. "We need to put this in writing," she said with quiet firmness. "If it is not on paper, it is just a verbal agreement between friends. Verbal agreements can be remembered differently by both sides." Everyone nodded. A signed document would protect the partnership and keep expectations clear for both parties.

### Scene 11 (58 words)
Layla drafted a simple one-page agreement at the table. It listed the products covered, the percentage split for each period, the return policy for unsold items, and the terms for ending the arrangement. Mugs not sold within thirty days came back to the team. Layla and Ellen both signed at the bottom. Riley stared at the two signatures. "This is a real business contract."

### Scene 12 (58 words)
Riley selected six mugs from the shelf with careful attention. She chose warm earth tones and rich glazes that would catch the eye against the café's wooden interior. She wiped each one and wrapped them in soft cloth for transport. "People I have never spoken to are going to see my work on that shelf," she said, balancing nerves and excitement in her voice.

### Scene 13 (58 words)
Tuesday morning, they delivered the mugs to the café. Ellen arranged them on a display shelf near the front counter and placed a small sign beside them reading MADE BY MAPLEWOOD MAKERS. Benny stood back and studied the arrangement in the warm light of the café. Their pottery sat on a real shelf in a real business. It felt like a milestone.

### Scene 14 (58 words)
The rest of the week passed with a slowness that tested every member of the team. Each afternoon, Benny detoured past the café on his walk home and peered through the window to check the shelf. Were all six mugs still there? Had any moved? He could never tell from outside. By Thursday the waiting felt harder than the negotiation.

### Scene 15 (58 words)
Friday afternoon, Ellen called the fort with news that sent Benny's heart racing. "I sold one mug this morning," she said. "And two more earlier in the week. Three total out of six in the first week." Benny relayed the numbers and the fort erupted. Three sales from a shelf they did not have to tend. The partnership was producing results.

### Scene 16 (58 words)
Saturday morning, they walked to the café to collect the first payment. Three mugs at twelve dollars each came to thirty-six dollars in total sales. Ellen kept her thirty percent — ten dollars and eighty cents. That left twenty-five dollars and twenty cents for the team. Benny counted every bill and coin, then counted again. The numbers matched and the money was real.

### Scene 17 (58 words)
At the fort, they placed the café earnings into a small jar Layla had labeled CAFÉ REVENUE. "We track this separately from direct sales," she explained. "That way we always know how much revenue comes through the café channel and how much comes from our own orders." The jar was small, but the revenue stream it represented carried real significance.

### Scene 18 (58 words)
Benny sat back in his chair and let a thought settle that changed how he understood their business. "We made money while we were sitting in class," he said slowly. "The mugs sold when we were not even present. Ellen handled the customers, the display, and the sale." Layla smiled. "That is what people call passive income. Both sides win."

### Scene 19 (58 words)
Frances stopped by the fort that afternoon and noticed the new jar and the signed agreement pinned to the wall. "Partnerships succeed when both parties receive something they need," she said. "Ellen gets unique products that draw customers to her café. You get access to buyers you could never reach on your own. A fair exchange like that strengthens both businesses."

### Scene 20 (58 words)
The map on the wall displayed a new connection unlike any before it. Thicker than a customer line and glowing golden-green instead of plain gold, it pulsed with a steady rhythm linking the fort to the café. Smaller lines branched from it, reaching customers who bought mugs through Ellen. The network had grown in a direction they never planned but were glad to follow.

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **consignment** (Scenes 4, 5) — An arrangement where products are displayed by a partner who pays the maker only after a customer purchase
2. **partnership** (Scenes 18, 19) — A business relationship where two parties collaborate so both benefit from the arrangement
3. **negotiation** (Scenes 8, 9) — The process of discussing and adjusting terms to reach an agreement both sides consider fair
4. **written agreement** (Scenes 10, 11) — A signed document recording the terms of a deal to prevent misunderstanding
5. **passive income** (Scene 18) — Revenue earned without direct involvement in every transaction
6. **percentage-based compensation** (Scenes 7, 8) — Payment calculated as a share of each sale rather than a fixed amount
