## THE PIVOT

### Scene 1 (58 words)
Five weeks into the business, the calendar turned to a new month and Layla noticed something that troubled her. The order board looked healthy, the money jars held steady balances, and deliveries went out on time. But when she reviewed her notebook, the revenue line had gone flat. They were working harder than ever with nothing new to show for it.

### Scene 2 (58 words)
Layla reviewed the last three weekly totals with growing concern. Ninety-one dollars. Ninety-four. Ninety-two. Three consecutive weeks hovering around the same figure despite the team investing more hours and handling more orders. The effort was increasing but the results were not. She tapped her pencil against the open page. "We are stuck," she told herself, "and I need to find out why."

### Scene 3 (58 words)
That evening, Layla carried every order record to her bedroom and spread them across the floor in careful rows. She sorted each one into product categories — pottery in one corner, repairs in another, birdhouses in a third, and journals in the last. She wanted to see where their revenue was actually coming from, not where she assumed it was.

### Scene 4 (58 words)
The sorted piles revealed a story that the weekly totals had been hiding. Pottery orders accounted for sixty percent of their total revenue and the pile had visibly grown over recent weeks. Repairs held steady at twenty-five percent. Birdhouses sat at ten percent. Journals, the product Layla had designed and crafted herself, represented only five percent. The thinnest pile on the floor.

### Scene 5 (58 words)
Layla pulled out records from their opening weeks and arranged them beside the recent ones for comparison. The shift was clear. Pottery had climbed from forty percent of revenue to sixty percent across eight weeks of operation. Journals had fallen from twelve percent to five, with zero repeat customers. Not one person had returned to buy a second journal.

### Scene 6 (58 words)
At the next team meeting, Layla taped a hand-drawn bar chart to the fort wall showing revenue broken down by product category. Pottery stood tall. Repairs held firm. Birdhouses barely registered. Journals was the shortest bar on the chart. "This is where our money comes from," Layla said, letting the visual do the work, "and where it does not."

### Scene 7 (58 words)
Ellis pointed at the birdhouse column. "Ten percent and flat. Are we keeping that?" Layla had already worked through the question. "We stop building inventory ahead of orders. If a customer requests one, we build it. But we do not invest materials or time in stock that sits on the shelf waiting." The team agreed without debate. That decision cost nobody anything personal.

### Scene 8 (58 words)
Then the room settled into a silence heavier than the conversation before it. Journals sat at five percent with no repeat buyers. Everyone at the table understood what the data was suggesting, but nobody wanted to say it first. Layla had spent hours cutting paper, stitching bindings, and writing messages inside each cover. The journals were beautiful. The chart did not measure beauty.

### Scene 9 (58 words)
Riley was the one who broke the silence. "I know what this feels like," she said, her voice low and steady as she looked directly at Layla. "My platter came back in Chapter Four. I thought it was perfect. But the customer wanted something else." She paused. "Sometimes what we love making is not what people need to buy."

### Scene 10 (58 words)
"But I worked so hard on every single one of them," Layla said, her voice tight and strained in a way the team had rarely heard from her. "Every journal has my handwriting inside the front cover." Ellis leaned forward with care in his expression. "The work was real. But the market tells us what people value. Not what we wish they valued."

### Scene 11 (58 words)
After the meeting, Layla walked alone to Frances's garden. Frances was trimming her rose bushes with practiced, unhurried cuts. Layla told her about the journals. Frances set down her clippers and pointed at a branch she had just cut. "That was a good branch. I removed it so the best ones grow stronger."

### Scene 12 (58 words)
Layla sat on the garden bench and let the decision settle in her chest. Journals were the first product she had made before the business even existed. They represented her creativity at its most personal. But the data was clear and every hour spent on journals was an hour not spent on pottery. "No more journals," she said. Her voice cracked slightly.

### Scene 13 (58 words)
Monday morning, the order board reflected their new direction. Pottery orders filled most of the available space. Repairs sat in a tidy row beside them. The birdhouse section carried a new label reading FOR ORDERS ONLY. The journal section was gone entirely. Layla had peeled the label from the board herself before anyone else arrived, standing alone in the quiet of the empty fort.

### Scene 14 (58 words)
The two to three hours Layla had devoted to journals each week were now open. She redirected that time toward helping Riley with clay preparation and glaze mixing every morning. The effect was immediate and visible. "I can not believe how much faster this goes with two people working side by side," Riley said, pulling a finished piece from the wheel with both hands.

### Scene 15 (58 words)
With the added support, Riley completed three additional platters that week alone. Orders that normally stretched to Friday were finished by Wednesday afternoon, freeing the team to begin new projects ahead of schedule. The fort carried a different energy now — focused, directed, and purposeful rather than scattered across four product lines pulling attention in competing directions at once.

### Scene 16 (58 words)
Friday arrived and Layla counted the week's earnings with careful attention. One hundred and seven dollars in revenue, a clear jump from the plateau weeks of ninety-two. Costs dropped to twenty-eight dollars because they were no longer purchasing journal supplies or stocking birdhouse materials in advance. Profit reached seventy-nine dollars. Their best single week since the business had opened its doors.

### Scene 17 (58 words)
The second week following the pivot delivered even stronger results. Revenue climbed to one hundred and fifteen dollars. Costs held at twenty-six. Profit reached eighty-nine dollars — their highest margin on record. Layla wrote the number in her notebook and traced a slow, deliberate circle around it twice. "Record profit," she said to the team. "And we are working fewer total hours."

### Scene 18 (58 words)
Layla sat with her notebook open and studied the trend lines she had drawn connecting each week's figures. Revenue climbing steadily upward. Costs bending downward. Profit expanding between the two curves like a widening path. "The hard choice was the right choice," she said. Ellis looked at the chart. "The data told us what to do. You were brave enough to follow it."

### Scene 19 (58 words)
Benny mentioned that Neville had closed his repair shop for good. He had tried to offer every possible service, spread himself too thin, let quality suffer, and lost his customers one by one. "We are still here because we make hard choices when the data tells us to," Layla said. "Neville tried to do everything. We chose focus over variety."

### Scene 20 (58 words)
The map on the wall had shifted in ways that told the whole story. The thin lines where journal customers once connected had faded away. But the pottery lines glowed brighter than before, and new ones had appeared in a clear cluster near the café. Layla traced a bright path with her finger. "The map shows what works. We follow it."

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **pivot** (Scenes 12, 17) — A strategic shift in what a business focuses on, guided by market data and performance trends
2. **revenue by category** (Scenes 4, 6) — Breaking income down by product type to identify where money is actually coming from
3. **opportunity cost** (Scene 12) — The value lost when time and resources go toward one activity instead of a more productive one
4. **data-driven** (Scenes 3, 18) — Making business decisions based on numbers and evidence rather than personal feelings or assumptions
5. **pruning** (Scene 11) — Deliberately cutting away good offerings to concentrate resources on the strongest performers
6. **focus vs diversification** (Scene 19) — Choosing to do fewer things well rather than many things poorly
