# CHAPTER 1: GOING SOLO - TIER 3 (3RD GRADE)

**Reading Levels:** M-N
**Target Narration Speed:** 125 WPM
**Target Word Count:** 1,000 words
**Episode Duration:** ~8 minutes
**Character Focus:** Ensemble — all four friends
**FL Integration:** Medium (entrepreneurship, opportunity cost, division of labor)
**Setting:** Secret fort and neighborhood

---

## GOING SOLO

### Scene 1 (50 words)
Three weeks had passed since the Makers' Faire, and the friends gathered at their fort on Saturday morning to figure out their next move. Ellis opened his notebook and read a list of fourteen requests from people in the neighborhood. "They all want us to make or fix things," he said.

### Scene 2 (50 words)
Riley suggested they divide the work so each person could focus on what they did best. She would handle pottery orders, Ellis would take on repairs, Layla would sell her handmade journals at the park, and Benny would run errands around the neighborhood. They all agreed and headed home to get started.

### Scene 3 (50 words)
Riley visited Mrs. Patterson on Monday morning to talk about her first commission. "I'd like six matching mugs for my kitchen shelf," Mrs. Patterson said with a smile. Riley agreed right away, excited about her first paying customer. But walking home, she realized she had forgotten to ask what "matching" actually meant.

### Scene 4 (50 words)
Ellis arrived at Mr. Chen's house Monday afternoon to repair a broken gate. He set out his tools and got to work with confidence. But halfway through the job, he discovered he needed a different saw blade. He told Mr. Chen he would have to come back the next day to finish.

### Scene 5 (50 words)
Layla set up a display at the park on Tuesday, arranging her handmade journals on a bright blanket. Several neighbors stopped to admire the careful stitching and colorful covers. But after sitting there for two full hours in the afternoon sun, not a single person had bought one.

### Scene 6 (50 words)
Benny spent Wednesday running errands for neighbors all over the community. He picked up groceries for Mrs. Williams, mailed a package for Mr. Patel, and returned library books for Mrs. Chen. He felt helpful and busy the entire day, but he forgot to write down any of the payments.

### Scene 7 (50 words)
The friends gathered at the fort Thursday evening to check in on how their week was going. Layla asked hopefully whether things were going well. Everyone answered "fine," but their exhausted faces told a different story. Riley had dried clay on her hands and Ellis had sawdust stuck in his hair.

### Scene 8 (50 words)
Riley's mug problem kept getting worse as the week went on. Mrs. Patterson wanted the mugs taller, so Riley remade the entire set. Then Mrs. Patterson decided she preferred different handles. Riley had to start over a third time. "I just want her to like them," she told Benny with frustration.

### Scene 9 (50 words)
Ellis realized he was spending too much time traveling between jobs instead of working on them. He would fix something on one street, walk across town for a second job, then walk right back again. "I spend more time walking than working," he told his dad at dinner.

### Scene 10 (50 words)
Layla finally made her first sale on Friday when a teacher bought one of her journals for eight dollars. She felt proud at first, but her excitement faded when she calculated the real numbers. The journal had taken four hours to create, which meant she earned only two dollars per hour.

### Scene 11 (50 words)
Benny's biggest mistake happened Friday afternoon when his messy system for tracking payments fell apart. He collected money from Mrs. Chen for her groceries, but got confused and gave that same money to Mr. Patel for a different errand. He had to use his own allowance to fix the mistake.

### Scene 12 (50 words)
That evening, Layla visited Frances in her garden because she needed a quiet place to think. Frances was picking tomatoes from her vines when she noticed Layla's tired expression. Layla told her about the disappointing sales. Frances nodded. "One tomato plant gives you some tomatoes. A whole garden feeds everyone."

### Scene 13 (50 words)
By Thursday, Ellis had reached his limit and had to turn down two new repair requests from neighbors. He told Mr. Rodriguez that he was sorry but had too much on his plate already. He felt guilty about saying no, but one person could only handle so much work on their own.

### Scene 14 (50 words)
Friday brought Riley to her breaking point when she delivered the mugs to Mrs. Patterson one last time, hoping they were finally right. Mrs. Patterson examined each one carefully, turning them over in her hands, then shook her head. "These still aren't what I had in mind," she said gently.

### Scene 15 (50 words)
On Friday night, each friend sat alone in their room and counted the money they had earned during the long week. Riley had twenty-two dollars from pottery. Ellis earned nineteen from repairs. Layla had twelve from her single journal sale. Benny had just five dollars left after paying back his mistake.

### Scene 16 (50 words)
Saturday morning, they returned to the fort for their weekly meeting, but nobody wanted to be the first to share disappointing numbers. After an uncomfortable silence, Riley finally placed her crumpled bills on the table. The others added theirs one by one. Fifty-eight dollars total sat between them in a small pile.

### Scene 17 (50 words)
Then Layla asked the question that changed everything. "How many hours did we each work this week?" They calculated together. Riley worked twelve hours, Ellis worked ten, Layla worked seven, and Benny worked six. That added up to thirty-five hours. "Fifty-eight dollars for thirty-five hours of work," Ellis said slowly, frowning.

### Scene 18 (50 words)
Mr. Mason stopped by the fort to return a borrowed tool and noticed the small pile of money and four exhausted faces. He asked how their week had gone. Ellis told him the numbers. Mr. Mason paused. "That works out to less than two dollars an hour," he said quietly.

### Scene 19 (50 words)
Layla studied her friends, noticing tired eyes, clay-stained hands, and the small pile of money that represented a whole week of work. "What if these aren't four separate problems?" she said thoughtfully. "What if it's one problem? We're each trying to do everything alone. What if we worked together instead?"

### Scene 20 (51 words)
Riley's face lit up with sudden hope. "You mean like a real team?" Ellis nodded slowly, thinking it through. "That could work way better than what we've been doing on our own." Benny grinned. "Let's try it for one week!" They all agreed, and for the first time all week, nobody felt tired.

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **commission** (Scene 3) — A paid request to create something specific for a customer
2. **errand** (Scenes 2, 6) — A short trip to do a task for someone
3. **per hour** (Scenes 10, 18) — The amount earned for each hour of work
4. **opportunity cost** (Scene 10, implied) — What you give up when you spend your time one way
