**Target Narration Speed:** 125 WPM
**Target Word Count:** 1,300 words
**Episode Duration:** ~10 minutes
**Character Focus:** Ensemble — all four friends learning through struggle
**FL Integration:** Medium (entrepreneurship, opportunity cost, division of labor)
**Setting:** Secret fort and neighborhood

## GOING SOLO
### Scene 1 (68 words)
Three weeks had passed since the Makers' Faire, and the friends gathered in their fort on Saturday morning to discuss what to do next. Ellis, who had been tracking every interaction, opened his notebook and showed them a detailed list. "I wrote down everything people asked for at the faire," he explained, "and fourteen people want us to either make things or fix things for them." Everyone felt both excited and nervous.
### Scene 2 (66 words)
"That's a lot of work for just four people," said Layla as she studied the long list with concern. Riley, who was ready with practical solutions, nodded thoughtfully while considering their options. "We should divide them up based on what each of us does best," she suggested. "I can handle the pottery commissions, Ellis can manage the repairs, Layla can create the journals, and Benny can run errands." Everyone agreed readily.
### Scene 3 (70 words)
Riley visited Mrs. Patterson on Monday morning, eager to start her pottery commission business. "I'd like six matching mugs for my kitchen counter," Mrs. Patterson said with a warm smile. Riley, not wanting to appear inexperienced to her first customer, said yes immediately without asking any follow-up questions. But as she walked home, she suddenly realized she had forgotten to ask what "matching" actually meant. Riley understood she should have asked more specific questions.
### Scene 4 (67 words)
Ellis went to Mr. Chen's property on Monday afternoon to repair his broken gate, carrying his toolbox and feeling confident about the job. After assessing the damage and identifying the problem, he began working with determination. However, halfway through the repair when he was making good progress, he discovered he needed a different saw blade. "I don't have that blade in my toolbox," he told Mr. Chen, which meant he would have to return the following day.
### Scene 5 (69 words)
Layla set up her display at the park on Tuesday afternoon with her handmade journals, placing them on a colorful blanket. She felt pride in her craftsmanship as people walked by, paused to admire her work, and made kind comments. Some visitors picked up the journals with interest, examined the hand-stitching closely, and smiled with appreciation. But after sitting there for two hours, not a single person had purchased anything, which left Layla feeling deeply disappointed.
### Scene 6 (68 words)
Benny started running errands for neighbors on Wednesday morning, eager to be helpful to everyone in the community. Mrs. Williams needed groceries from the corner store, Mr. Patel wanted a package mailed at the post office, and Mrs. Chen asked him to return overdue library books. Benny felt helpful and busy throughout the day, but when evening arrived, he discovered he couldn't remember who had paid him what because he had forgotten to write anything down.
### Scene 7 (70 words)
The friends met at the fort Thursday evening for a check-in to see how everyone's week was going. "How's everything going with your work so far?" asked Layla hopefully, wanting to hear progress reports. Everyone responded with a quick "fine," but their exhausted faces and slumped postures told a different story. Riley's hands were covered in dried clay, Ellis had sawdust stuck in his hair, Layla yawned repeatedly from exhaustion, and Benny's scarf hung crooked around his neck.
### Scene 8 (67 words)
Riley's customer problem grew worse as the week continued without resolution. When she delivered the first set of mugs to Mrs. Patterson on Wednesday hoping for praise, the woman examined them carefully before frowning. "Can you make them a bit taller?" she asked, which meant Riley had to remake them entirely. Then, after Riley delivered the second version, Mrs. Patterson decided she preferred different-shaped handles. "I just want her to be happy with them," Riley told Benny later while fighting back tears.
### Scene 9 (69 words)
Ellis realized he was wasting valuable time traveling between jobs instead of working on repairs. He would fix a broken door on Maple Street, then walk across town to repair a wobbly shelf for another customer, then return to Maple Street for another repair job. "I'm spending more time walking between locations than working on the repairs," he complained to his dad over dinner, and his dad nodded with understanding.
### Scene 10 (68 words)
On Friday afternoon, Layla finally made her first sale when she sold one of her handmade journals at her park display. A teacher purchased it for eight dollars after admiring the craftsmanship, and Layla felt proud of making her first sale. However, when she calculated the economics at her kitchen table that evening, her pride quickly transformed into concern. The journal had required four hours to create, which meant she had earned only two dollars per hour.
### Scene 11 (70 words)
Benny's biggest mistake of the week occurred Friday afternoon when his disorganized financial tracking system collapsed. He had collected payment from Mrs. Chen for delivering her groceries to her doorstep. But then, confused about which money belonged to which transaction, he accidentally gave that same money to Mr. Patel as change for a different errand. When Mrs. Chen asked for her change later, Benny discovered he didn't have it, so he had to use his own saved allowance money.
### Scene 12 (67 words)
That evening, feeling discouraged by the journal sales, Layla visited Frances in her garden because she needed time to think. Frances was picking ripe tomatoes from her healthy vines. "You look exhausted and troubled," Frances observed kindly while pausing in her work. Layla explained her experience trying to sell journals despite their quality. Frances smiled with understanding. "One tomato plant produces some tomatoes," she said thoughtfully, "but a whole garden feeds the entire neighborhood."
### Scene 13 (69 words)
By Thursday afternoon, Ellis found himself having to turn down two new repair requests from neighbors who needed his help. "I'm sorry, but I'm too busy right now to take on more work," he explained to Mr. Rodriguez, who looked disappointed but understanding. Ellis felt guilty about saying no to people in his community who needed help, but he was already juggling four different projects. He couldn't do more work by himself.
### Scene 14 (68 words)
Riley's breaking point arrived on Friday afternoon when she brought Mrs. Patterson the revised mugs for the fourth time, hoping they would finally meet expectations. She had spent hours perfecting them based on the feedback. Mrs. Patterson examined each mug carefully, rotating each one slowly, then frowned. "These still aren't quite what I had in mind," she said gently. "I don't want to do commissions anymore," Riley told Ellis later. "It's too draining."
### Scene 15 (70 words)
Friday night, each friend counted their week's earnings alone in their bedrooms, reflecting on the efforts they had invested. Riley had earned twenty-two dollars from her pottery work and the revisions. Ellis had made nineteen dollars from his repair jobs around the neighborhood. Layla had earned only twelve dollars from her journal sale. Benny had merely five dollars remaining after using his own money to fix his payment mistake. They all felt disappointed because the money seemed so small compared to the work.
### Scene 16 (67 words)
Saturday morning, they met at their fort for their weekly meeting with heavy hearts. Nobody wanted to be the first to share their disappointing results and admit failure. Finally, after an uncomfortable silence, Riley gathered her courage and placed her crumpled dollar bills on the wooden table. "Twenty-two dollars," she announced quietly. The others slowly added their own earnings one by one until they had fifty-eight dollars total. "That seems like quite a lot," said Benny hopefully.
### Scene 17 (69 words)
Then Layla asked the question that would change everything. "How many hours did we each work this week?" she asked seriously while pulling out paper and a pencil to figure out. They figured out the numbers together carefully, with no one wanting to exaggerate their efforts. Riley had worked twelve hours on pottery and revisions, Ellis had worked ten hours on repairs and travel, Layla had worked seven hours making and selling journals, and Benny had worked six hours running errands. That totaled thirty-five hours of combined labor.
### Scene 18 (68 words)
"Fifty-eight dollars from thirty-five hours of work," Ellis said slowly while doing the division in his head. Mr. Mason stopped by the fort to return a borrowed tool and noticed the small pile of money and exhausted expressions on their faces. "How did your entrepreneurial week go?" he asked gently with curiosity. Ellis explained about earning fifty-eight dollars across thirty-five hours. "That works out to less than two dollars per hour," Mr. Mason observed quietly, and the number hung in the air.
### Scene 19 (70 words)
Layla looked at each of her friends gathered around the table, noticing all the signs of struggle on their faces. She saw Riley's frustrated expression and clay-stained hands that showed her revisions, Ellis's tired eyes with dark circles underneath, Benny's crooked scarf, and the small pile of money representing their week's efforts. "What if these aren't four separate problems?" she said thoughtfully as a realization formed in her mind. "What if it's one problem? We're all trying to do everything alone."
### Scene 20 (67 words)
"What if we worked together as one business instead of working alone?" Layla proposed while gaining confidence as the idea crystallized. "We could support each other and share the workload instead of struggling separately." Riley's eyes lit up with hope about the future. "You mean like a real team?" Ellis nodded slowly while his mind considered the possibilities. "That might work better than our current system." Benny grinned. "Let's try it for one week!" They all agreed.

## FL VOCABULARY
- **commission** — a request to create something specific for payment
- **transaction** — an exchange of money or goods between people
- **entrepreneurial** — related to starting and running a business
- **compensation** — payment received for work or services
- **partnership** — working together with others toward a shared goal
