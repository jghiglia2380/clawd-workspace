## THE SLOW SEASON

### Scene 1 (52 words)
The new year arrived. January first felt fresh and hopeful. The team gathered at the workshop, ready for another strong week. Layla checked the order board. One card in the yellow section. Just one. "Huh," she said. "Maybe people are still tired from the holidays."

### Scene 2 (53 words)
Friday afternoon came. Time to count the week's earnings. Riley brought out the money jar. They counted twice to be sure. Eighteen dollars. Total. For the whole week. In December, they'd made that much in a single day. Ellis stared at the coins and bills. "Wait. Is that all?"

### Scene 3 (52 words)
"Is our business over?" Ellis asked. His voice was quiet. The holiday rush seemed like it happened years ago, not weeks ago. Layla looked at the nearly empty jar. "No," she said. "January is just slow. People spent all their money in December. Now they're being careful."

### Scene 4 (54 words)
Ellis still looked worried. Layla stood up and walked to the shelf. She brought down a different jar, one they'd labeled RESERVE in December. She set it on the table. Seventy-three dollars. Money saved from their best weeks. "This is why we saved," Layla said. "For weeks like this one."

### Scene 5 (53 words)
The next day, they visited Frances's garden. Everything looked dead. Brown stems. Empty beds. No flowers. No vegetables. Just dirt and dried leaves. "Your garden looks sad," Benny said. Frances smiled. "Does it? Look closer. The roots are growing underground. You just can't see them yet."

### Scene 6 (55 words)
Frances walked them through the winter garden. She pointed to where tulip bulbs waited beneath the soil. "January isn't about big visible growth," she said. "It's about building capacity you can't see yet. Better systems. Better skills. Stronger relationships. When spring comes, you'll be ready because of what you built now."

### Scene 7 (52 words)
The team's thinking shifted. Instead of asking "why aren't we growing?" they started asking "what can we build right now?" The slow season wasn't a problem to panic about. It was time they could use differently. Time to get better at things they didn't have time for in December.

### Scene 8 (56 words)
Ellis started a project in Mr. Mason's workshop. He built a better workbench for pottery production. Measured and sanded each piece of wood. Added storage shelves underneath for tools and supplies. "I'm not making money this week," he told Mr. Mason. "But I'm making our workspace better. That counts for something, right?"

### Scene 9 (54 words)
Layla proposed using some reserve money for an investment. Better packaging materials for spring orders. Nicer boxes. Tissue paper. Small cards they could write thank-you notes on. Twelve dollars from the reserve jar. Everyone agreed. It felt good to spend the saved money on something that would help them later.

### Scene 10 (53 words)
Riley used the quiet time to build inventory. She made a spring collection. Mugs with pastel glazes. Bowls with tiny painted flowers. Pieces that would be perfect for spring gifts. No one was ordering them yet, but when demand came back, they'd have stock ready to sell immediately.

### Scene 11 (55 words)
Benny couldn't sit still. He tried everything to drum up business. Posted on community boards. Visited shops to ask about carrying their products. Called past customers. Some conversations went well. Others didn't. He learned that sometimes you can't force people to buy when they're not ready. Demand comes when it comes.

### Scene 12 (52 words)
Week two finished. Twenty-two dollars in revenue. A little better than week one, but still very slow. The reserve jar money helped cover costs and keep them from panicking. Layla updated the order board. Three yellow cards this week instead of one. Slow progress, but progress.

### Scene 13 (56 words)
One bright spot stayed steady. Ellen's café kept ordering mugs and small bowls. Fifteen to twenty dollars per week, even in January. The business-to-business relationship proved its worth. While individual customers slowed down, the café partnership continued. "This is why relationships matter," Layla said. "They're reliable even when things get quiet."

### Scene 14 (52 words)
Tuesday afternoon in the third week of January. The phone rang. Ellis answered. A woman's voice. "Hi, my name is Ms. Rivera. I'm looking for someone who can make custom pottery pieces for a wedding. Are you taking orders?" Ellis's heart jumped. "Yes. Yes, we are."

### Scene 15 (53 words)
Ms. Rivera explained how she found them. Her friend Mrs. Torres had ordered pottery gifts last spring. The pieces were beautiful. The service was wonderful. When Ms. Rivera started planning wedding favors, Mrs. Torres gave her the team's number without hesitation. "She said you do excellent work and you're very reliable."

### Scene 16 (55 words)
The order details came together. Thirty custom pottery pieces. Small dishes with the couple's initials and wedding date. Nice packaging. Due in four weeks. Ms. Rivera's budget: one hundred eighty dollars. The biggest single order the team had ever received. Ellis wrote down every detail carefully, his hand shaking a little.

### Scene 17 (53 words)
After the call ended, the team sat in silence for a moment. Then Ellis spoke. "We did good work for Mrs. Torres eight months ago. We were careful and kind. She remembered. She told her friend. Now we have this." Past work was still paying off through reputation.

### Scene 18 (52 words)
They spent the rest of the week planning the wedding order. No rush. Plenty of time to do it right. Riley sketched designs. Ellis calculated clay and glaze needs. Benny planned the packaging presentation. Layla made a production timeline. The slow season gave them space to be thoughtful.

### Scene 19 (54 words)
January ended. They counted the final week's earnings and added up the whole month. Fifty-eight dollars in regular revenue. Not much compared to December's totals. But Ms. Rivera had also paid a ninety-dollar deposit on the wedding order. And the café partnership stayed solid. The reserve fund worked exactly as planned.

### Scene 20 (54 words)
Layla looked at the map one last time that month. The winter-blue lines seemed less dim than before. The wedding order appeared as a new line, glowing warm and branching from Mrs. Torres's older line. Frances was right all along. The roots had been growing underground. Spring was coming.
