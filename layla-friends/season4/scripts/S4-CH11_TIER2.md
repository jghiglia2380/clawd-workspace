## THE SLOW SEASON

### Scene 1 (45 words)
January arrived. The new year felt quiet and still. The order board that had been overflowing in December now showed a single card. One order for the whole week. Ellis stared at the board. "Where did everyone go?" he said. The holiday rush was over.

### Scene 2 (45 words)
Friday came and Layla counted the money. Eighteen dollars in revenue for the entire week. The team sat in silence. In December they had earned almost one hundred dollars a week. Now it was eighteen. "Is our business over?" asked Benny. Nobody knew what to say.

### Scene 3 (45 words)
Ellis felt the frustration building inside him. "We worked so hard last month," he said. "Did we do something wrong?" Layla shook her head. "No. January is just slow. People spent their money in December. They do not need gifts anymore. It is the season."

### Scene 4 (45 words)
Layla reached for the jar labeled RESERVE. She held it up so everyone could see. Seventy-three dollars sat inside. "This is why we saved," she said. "We do not need to panic. We planned for this." The jar Benny argued against now felt like the smartest decision they had made.

### Scene 5 (45 words)
The team walked to Frances's garden after the meeting. Everything looked bare. The bushes had no leaves. The flower beds were empty dirt. "It looks dead," said Benny. Frances smiled. "It is not dead. The roots are growing underground where you can not see them."

### Scene 6 (45 words)
Frances knelt beside a bed of soil. "In winter, the garden does not show off. It stores energy. It builds strength. By spring, everything comes back stronger." She looked at the team. "Your business is doing the same thing. You are building what you can not see yet."

### Scene 7 (45 words)
Something shifted in Ellis after the garden visit. He stopped asking why they were not growing. Instead he started asking what they could build while things were slow. "If we can not make money this week, we can make ourselves better," he said. The team agreed.

### Scene 8 (45 words)
Ellis spent three days at Mr. Mason's workshop building a new workbench for the fort. He measured twice and cut with care. When he finished, the new bench was sturdy, level, and wide enough for two people to work side by side. Better tools would lead to better work.

### Scene 9 (45 words)
Layla used twelve dollars from the reserve jar to buy better packaging materials. Tissue paper, small boxes, and printed labels. "When spring comes, our products will look more professional," she said. They were spending a little now to earn more later. The team liked the sound of that.

### Scene 10 (45 words)
Riley spent the slow days at her wheel making new pieces. She tried pastel colors and flower designs for spring. No one had ordered them yet, but they would be ready when demand came back. "I am building inventory," she said. The shelf filled up one piece at a time.

### Scene 11 (45 words)
Benny tried harder to find customers. He walked the neighborhood and knocked on doors. A few people said maybe later. Nobody said yes right now. He came back to the fort looking tired. "You can not force demand," said Layla gently. "Sometimes people are just not buying."

### Scene 12 (45 words)
The second week brought in twenty-two dollars. A small improvement, but still far below their December numbers. Ellis did not let the number bother him. "We are getting better at what we do," he said. "When people start buying again, we will be ready." That thought kept him going.

### Scene 13 (45 words)
The café partnership kept a small, steady stream of money coming in. Ellen sold two mugs that week for fifteen dollars in revenue to the team. Even in the slowest month, the café line on the map stayed lit. "That is the value of a partnership," said Layla.

### Scene 14 (45 words)
On a Tuesday afternoon, the fort phone rang. A woman named Ms. Rivera was on the line. "I need pottery favors for a wedding," she said. "Thirty custom pieces." The room went silent. Thirty pieces was the biggest single order they had ever been offered.

### Scene 15 (45 words)
Ms. Rivera explained that she was a friend of Mrs. Torres from way back in the fall. Mrs. Torres had told her about the quality of their work. "She said you are the best in the neighborhood," Ms. Rivera said. That one recommendation had traveled for months before arriving.

### Scene 16 (45 words)
The wedding order was worth one hundred and eighty dollars. Due in four weeks. They would collect a ninety-dollar deposit up front and the balance on delivery. Ellis's hands were shaking when he hung up the phone. "One hundred and eighty dollars," he whispered. "From one call."

### Scene 17 (45 words)
Ellis sat down and thought about the chain of events. "We did good work for Mrs. Torres months ago. She told her friend. Her friend called us." He looked at the team. "We did not advertise. We did not knock on doors. Our reputation did the work for us."

### Scene 18 (45 words)
They used the slow week to plan the wedding order carefully. No rush. No cutting corners. They mapped out the design, the timeline, and the materials. For the first time, the quiet pace felt like a gift. They had time to do it right.

### Scene 19 (45 words)
January ended. Total revenue for the month came to fifty-eight dollars from regular orders plus a ninety-dollar deposit on the wedding. Cash flow had improved because of a relationship that started months before anyone could see its value. The reserve jar still had sixty-one dollars in it.

### Scene 20 (45 words)
The map had gone quiet during January. Most lines were pale blue instead of gold. But the café line stayed lit. And one new line appeared — warm and bright — connecting to the wedding customer. It branched from Mrs. Torres's old line. The roots were showing.

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **seasonal slowdown** (Scenes 3, 14) — A period when customer demand drops because of the time of year
2. **reserve** (Scene 4) — Saved money that protects a business when income is low
3. **maintenance mode** (Scenes 7, 8) — Using slow time to improve tools, skills, and systems instead of chasing sales
4. **referral** (Scenes 15, 17) — When a satisfied customer recommends your business to someone they know

---

## NARRATION NOTES

- **Target WPM:** 112 (moderate pace)
- **Pause:** 1.5 seconds between sentences
- **Tone:** Quiet and reflective, moving from worry to patience to surprise reward. Ellis learns that business has seasons and slow time has value.
