## THE PIVOT
### Scene 1 (33 words)
It was month two. The friends met at the fort. Layla looked at her notebook. "We are working hard," she said. "But we are not making more money." That did not feel right.
### Scene 2 (32 words)
Layla showed them the scorecard. Three weeks in a row. Ninety-one dollars. Ninety-four dollars. Ninety-two dollars. Almost the same every time. "Why isn't it going up?" asked Riley. Nobody knew yet.
### Scene 3 (33 words)
Layla looked at all their orders. She put them in piles. Pottery in one pile. Repairs in one pile. Birdhouses in one pile. Journals in one pile. She counted the money in each pile.
### Scene 4 (32 words)
Layla made a chart with four bars. Pottery was the tallest bar. It made the most money. Journals was the tiniest bar. It made almost no money. Layla stared at the small bar.
### Scene 5 (33 words)
Pottery was getting bigger every week. More people wanted it. But journals was getting smaller. Fewer people wanted them. The last two weeks, nobody bought any journals at all. Zero sales. That was bad.
### Scene 6 (32 words)
Layla showed the chart at the next meeting. "Pottery is winning," she said. She pointed to the tall bar. Everyone could see it. The numbers told them what to do. Pottery was the answer.
### Scene 7 (33 words)
"What about birdhouses?" asked Ellis. The birdhouse bar was shrinking too. "Let's stop making extra ones," said Layla. "We can make them if someone asks." Everyone agreed. That was an easy choice.
### Scene 8 (32 words)
Then everyone got quiet. They all looked at the journals bar. Layla's journals. She made them herself. She picked the paper. She chose the covers. They were her special thing. But nobody was buying them.
### Scene 9 (33 words)
Riley spoke softly. "I know this is hard," she said. "Remember my platter? I made it so pretty. But the customer did not want it." Riley understood how Layla felt. "Sometimes people want something different."
### Scene 10 (32 words)
"But they're beautiful," said Layla. Her voice was small. "I worked so hard." Ellis said gently, "People aren't buying them. They are buying pottery instead." Layla felt sad. She knew he was right.
### Scene 11 (33 words)
Layla walked to Frances's garden alone. Frances was cutting branches off a rose bush. "Why are you cutting good branches?" Layla asked. "To make room for great ones," said Frances. "Plants can't grow everything."
### Scene 12 (32 words)
Layla sat on the bench. "I have to stop making journals," she said. Her voice wobbled. Frances sat with her. "Cutting is hard," Frances said. "But it helps what's left grow stronger." Layla wiped her eyes.
### Scene 13 (33 words)
On Monday, Layla took the journal cards off the order board. She put pottery cards up instead. The board looked different now. Cleaner. Simpler. Only pottery and repairs. Layla took a deep breath. It was done.
### Scene 14 (32 words)
Now Layla had extra time. She used to spend hours making journals. "I can help you get clay ready," she told Riley. Riley smiled. "Yes! That would help a lot!"
### Scene 15 (33 words)
Riley made more pottery with Layla helping. Bowls and mugs and vases came out faster. The pottery was still good because Riley was not rushing. She just had more time to work.
### Scene 16 (32 words)
After one week, they counted the jars. One hundred and seven dollars! Costs went down to twenty-eight dollars because they stopped buying journal supplies. Profit was seventy-nine dollars. Their best week yet!
### Scene 17 (33 words)
The second week was even better. One hundred and fifteen dollars came in. Costs were only twenty-six dollars. Profit was eighty-nine dollars! Layla circled the number on the scorecard. The hard choice was working.
### Scene 18 (32 words)
"It worked," said Ellis. "More money coming in. Less money going out." Layla nodded. She still missed her journals. But the business was stronger now. Following the numbers was the right thing to do.
### Scene 19 (33 words)
Benny told them about Neville. "He quit his shop," Benny said. "He tried to sell too many things." Layla thought about that. "We're still here because we made the hard choice," she said.
### Scene 20 (32 words)
They looked at the map together. The journal lines had faded away. But the pottery lines glowed bright. New ones were growing near the cafe. "The map shows what works," Layla said. "We followed it."
