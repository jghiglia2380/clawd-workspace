## THE PARTNERSHIP

### Scene 1 (45 words)
Saturday morning at the fort, a woman appeared in the doorway. It was Ellen, the owner of the café on the corner. She looked at the pottery shelves and smiled. "I have a business idea for you," she said. The team set down their tools and listened.

### Scene 2 (45 words)
Ellen explained that her café customers often asked about the pottery mugs she used. "I would like to sell your pottery at my café," she said. "People see them every day and ask where they come from." Riley's eyes went wide. A store selling her work!

### Scene 3 (45 words)
Benny jumped in. "So you want to buy mugs from us?" Ellen shook her head. "Not exactly. I do not want to buy them up front. I want to show them. When they sell, we split the money." The team looked at each other. That was new.

### Scene 4 (45 words)
They walked to Mr. Mason's workshop for help. Ellen came along. Mr. Mason listened to the idea and nodded. "That is called consignment," he said. "Ellen does not buy the mugs. She shows them. When one sells, she keeps part of the money and you get the rest."

### Scene 5 (45 words)
"How is that different from just selling them to her?" asked Ellis. Mr. Mason explained. "If she bought them, she pays you right now. That is wholesale. With consignment, you keep owning the mugs until a customer buys one. Ellen only pays you after a sale happens."

### Scene 6 (45 words)
The team talked about it. Consignment meant Ellen did not take any risk. If the mugs did not sell, they came back. "That is fair," said Layla. "She is giving us space in her café and telling her customers about us. That is worth something on its own."

### Scene 7 (45 words)
Ellen said, "I was thinking I would keep forty percent of each sale." Benny looked at his friends. He was not sure if that was fair. "Can we think about it?" he asked. Ellen nodded. "Take your time. I want this to work for both of us."

### Scene 8 (45 words)
That night, the team talked numbers. A mug sold for twelve dollars. At forty percent, Ellen would keep four dollars and eighty cents. At thirty percent, she would keep three dollars and sixty cents. Benny wrote both numbers on the board. "I think we can find something in between."

### Scene 9 (45 words)
The next day, Benny met Ellen at the café. His hands were a little sweaty. "What if you kept thirty percent for the first month?" he said. "Then thirty-five percent after that?" Ellen thought about it for a moment. "That works for me," she said. They shook hands.

### Scene 10 (45 words)
Back at the fort, Layla had one more idea. "We should write this down," she said. "If we do not put it on paper, it is just a promise. Promises can get mixed up." Everyone agreed. A written deal was better than just a handshake.

### Scene 11 (45 words)
Layla wrote a simple agreement on one page. It listed the products, the percentages, and the rules. If mugs did not sell in thirty days, they came back. Layla and Ellen both signed it. "This is real," said Riley, staring at the paper with two signatures at the bottom.

### Scene 12 (45 words)
Riley picked six of her best mugs from the shelf. She chose warm colors for the café. She wiped each one clean and wrapped them in soft cloth. "These are going on display," she said. "People I have never met are going to see my work."

### Scene 13 (45 words)
Tuesday morning, they brought the mugs to the café. Ellen set them on a shelf near the counter with a small sign that read MADE BY MAPLEWOOD MAKERS. Benny stood back and looked at the display. Their pottery in a real store. It did not feel real yet.

### Scene 14 (45 words)
The rest of the week crawled by. Every afternoon, Benny walked past the café and peeked through the window. Were the mugs still there? Had anyone picked one up? He could not tell. "What if nobody buys them?" he said to Riley on Thursday. "They will," she said.

### Scene 15 (45 words)
Friday afternoon, Ellen called. "I have news," she said. "I sold one mug this morning. And two more yesterday." Benny nearly dropped the phone. Three mugs sold in one week! He ran to the fort and told everyone. The smiles around the table said more than any words could.

### Scene 16 (45 words)
Saturday morning, the team went to the café to pick up the payment. Three mugs at twelve dollars each made thirty-six dollars total. Ellen kept thirty percent, which was ten dollars and eighty cents. That left twenty-five dollars and twenty cents for the team. Benny counted it twice.

### Scene 17 (45 words)
Back at the fort, they put the café money into a small jar labeled CAFÉ REVENUE. "We should keep this separate," said Layla. "So we know how much comes from the café and how much comes from our regular orders." The jar was small but felt important.

### Scene 18 (45 words)
Benny sat back in his chair and let a thought settle. "We made money while we were at school," he said slowly. "The mugs sold when we were not even there." Layla smiled. "That is what a partnership does. Both sides win even when you are not watching."

### Scene 19 (45 words)
Frances stopped by the fort that afternoon. She noticed the new jar and the agreement on the wall. "Partnerships work when both people get what they need," she said. "Ellen gets products for her café. You get customers you could not reach alone. That is a fair deal."

### Scene 20 (45 words)
The map on the wall showed a new line. It was thicker than the customer lines and glowed golden-green instead of plain gold. It connected the fort to the café. Smaller lines branched off from it, reaching people they had never met. The network was growing.

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **consignment** (Scenes 4, 5) — Leaving your products with someone to sell; you get paid when they sell
2. **partnership** (Scenes 18, 19) — Two businesses working together so both of them benefit
3. **negotiation** (Scenes 8, 9) — Talking about a deal to find terms that are fair for everyone
4. **written agreement** (Scenes 10, 11) — A signed paper that puts the rules of a deal in writing
