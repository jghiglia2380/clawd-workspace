## THE COMPETITION
### Scene 1 (120 words)
Benny rushed into the fort on Tuesday morning with a troubled expression that got everyone's attention. Everyone could tell from his face that something was wrong. "I heard something bad while I was delivering packages around Elm Street yesterday afternoon," he said, still catching his breath from running. "Some older kid has started doing fix-it jobs and repairs all around the neighborhood, and from what people are saying, he's charging way less than us for the exact same kinds of work." The friends exchanged worried glances across the table, the comfortable mood disappearing. Competition had arrived unexpectedly in Maplewood, and none of them had expected it.
### Scene 2 (118 words)
Things got worse that very afternoon when Mrs. Chen stopped by the fort during her regular walk through the neighborhood. "I hired that other boy for a shelf installation last week," she mentioned casually. "He only charged me ten dollars for the whole thing, and he finished quite quickly." Their carefully calculated shelf price was fifteen dollars, a number they had determined carefully in Chapter Two. Someone was undercutting their prices across every category of service they offered. Ellis felt his stomach clench tight with a mixture of fear and frustration. They were already losing customers to a competitor they hadn't even identified yet.
### Scene 3 (122 words)
Ellis called an emergency meeting at the fort immediately after dinner that evening, unable to wait any longer. All four friends gathered around the wooden planning table looking serious. "Who exactly is this kid, and where did he come from?" Riley asked nervously, twisting her ribbon between anxious fingers as she spoke. "How is he able to charge so much less than us for the same services?" Nobody had concrete answers yet, only bits and pieces. Benny had picked up scattered rumors from customers and neighbors, but nothing enough to make a plan. They needed real information and hard numbers before they could figure out what to do about this competition.
### Scene 4 (120 words)
The following day at the hardware store, Ellis finally discovered who it was. A tall boy, probably around twelve years old and clearly older than any of them, was loading rough-cut planks of wood into a battered red wagon quickly, grabbing boards without checking them for warping or knots. He wore a supremely confident grin. "I'm Neville," the boy announced with casual authority when Ellis worked up the courage to introduce himself. "Just launched my own fix-it business about two weeks ago, and honestly, business is already booming. People love a good deal." He seemed very confident, though there was nothing mean about him. He simply believed that cheaper prices guaranteed success.
### Scene 5 (119 words)
Over the next several days, Ellis carefully investigated and compiled a good list of all Neville's prices to compare with their own established rates. Shelves installed for just ten dollars against their fifteen. General repairs for twelve dollars compared to their twenty. Birdhouses for a mere eight dollars versus their twelve. Every single product and service Neville offered was priced way below what Maplewood Makers charged, making a big difference that customers noticed. Ellis documented every number carefully and brought the detailed comparison back to the fort for the team to analyze together. They stared at the side-by-side figures in stunned silence, Everyone got quiet. Neville was carefully undercutting every established price point they had. The competitive threat was serious.
### Scene 6 (121 words)
"We have to match his prices immediately or we're finished!" Ellis declared desperately, pacing back and forth. He was panicking. "If we don't slash our prices down to his level right now, every single customer in this neighborhood will abandon us and choose Neville instead. It's simple math!" Riley nodded vigorously in agreement, her own fear amplifying his alarm. "He's already stealing our customers one by one." Ellis was ready to drop their shelf price to ten dollars that very afternoon, convinced that speed was their only weapon against this threat. He was too worried to think clearly.
### Scene 7 (120 words)
But Layla held up her hand calmly. "Wait," she said firmly. "Before we decide, let's examine what the math actually tells us." She reached into their records shelf and retrieved the detailed pricing sheet they had constructed so carefully during Chapter Two, spreading it on the table. "Our shelf costs six dollars in materials alone," she said, pointing exactly to the figures they had calculated together. "If we drop our price to match Neville at ten dollars, our total profit per shelf shrinks to just four dollars. Split equally among four partners, that leaves each of us with exactly one dollar per shelf."
### Scene 8 (119 words)
Layla continued her pricing analysis with methodical precision, letting each number build upon the last to construct an argument that panic alone could never dismantle. "One dollar each for about two hours of skilled labor means we would each be earning fifty cents per hour," she stated, meeting each friend's eyes in turn to make sure they understood. "We would essentially be working for nothing, and our business would be financially unsustainable within weeks." The fort fell silent as the math made the panic fade that had been driving the conversation. Ellis, who had been moments away from slashing every price on their board, suddenly recognized what he had almost done. Competing purely on price without considering profit margins would have been a race to the bottom nobody could win.
### Scene 9 (122 words)
Seeking experienced guidance, they visited Mr. Mason's workshop that Saturday morning and laid out the entire competitive situation before him honestly, including their initial panicked impulse to match Neville's prices. Mr. Mason listened patiently to every detail. When they finished, he set down his chisel carefully and spoke confidently. "Here's something important," Mr. Mason said. "Not every customer is searching for the cheapest available option. Many customers seek the best quality and pay more for it. You simply cannot be both the cheapest and the best at the same time. You must choose which customers you want to serve."
### Scene 10 (120 words)
Returning to the fort with Mr. Mason's advice helping them see things differently, they started brainstorming to figure out what made them special over Neville. "What concrete things do we offer that Neville simply cannot match?" Layla asked, uncapping a marker at their whiteboard. Ellis, feeling calmer and clearer now, spoke first. "Custom designs made for each customer's exact needs," he said. "I don't build generic shelves. I measure the space, discuss what they need, and construct exactly what fits." Riley added her perspective: "Superior craftsmanship and lasting quality. Our work doesn't cut corners." Benny added: "Personal service and genuine relationships. Our customers know us and trust us." They had real strengths.
### Scene 11 (121 words)
After talking for a long time, they made a decision that felt both bold and terrifying. They would not lower their prices to compete with Neville on cost. Instead, with a strategy that took courage, they would actually raise their prices to show the higher value and quality of their work. Their shelf price increased from fifteen dollars to eighteen dollars, showing they were better than Neville instead of competing on price. "Higher prices communicate something important to customers," Layla explained, confidently. "They signal that our work is worth the investment because it lasts." The decision felt scary. But their strategy meant they had to stand out through quality, not price.
### Scene 12 (118 words)
Ellis then had an idea that showed how much his thinking had changed since the panic of a few days ago. "What if we guarantee every single piece of work we produce?" he suggested, his eyes bright with conviction. Working together, they designed a simple but professional card. It stated clearly: "Maplewood Makers guarantees our work. If anything breaks within one month of installation, we will repair it free of charge." Neville offered no comparable guarantee. This guarantee showed every customer that Maplewood Makers stood behind their work.
### Scene 13 (120 words)
The first week operating under their new premium pricing strategy tested them in ways they hadn't expected. Three separate customers,  chose Neville over Maplewood Makers because his prices were a lot lower. Ellis felt genuine doubt creeping into his thoughts. "Maybe we calculated wrong and made a terrible mistake," he whispered anxiously to Layla during a quiet afternoon. The order board displayed noticeably fewer cards than in previous weeks, each empty space amplifying their uncertainty. Riley stared at the board without speaking, her expression unreadable. Fear and doubt came naturally during competition, but they needed courage to push through.
### Scene 14 (119 words)
Then everything changed when the phone rang at the fort on Thursday afternoon. It was Mrs. Lee, and she sounded frustrated and upset. "I hired that other boy just last week," she explained. "This morning it pulled away from the wall and collapsed. Everything sitting on it, my grandmother's vase, three framed photographs, a stack of books, crashed to the floor and shattered." She paused to compose herself before continuing. "Can you come look at it and repair it properly?" Ellis looked at Layla with wide eyes. The problem they'd talked about had happened sooner than they expected.
### Scene 15 (122 words)
Ellis and Benny traveled to Mrs. Lee's house that same afternoon to inspect the damage and see what went wrong with Neville's installation. The shelf had torn free from the drywall, leaving ragged holes and scattered debris across the floor beneath. Ellis examined Neville's workmanship with a critical eye. He saw the problems immediately. The screws Neville had selected were wrong for the job, too short and too thin to hold weight. Not a single wall anchor had been installed at any mounting point. The brackets looked like they'd been measured carelessly. The entire installation had been rushed, done too quickly without proper care.
### Scene 16 (121 words)
Ellis reinstalled and properly secured the shelf with careful attention to every technical detail, showing why their work cost more. He used heavy-duty screws for the wall type. He installed proper expansion anchors at each mounting point, drilling pilot holes with precise measurements. He verified the shelf was perfectly level using his bubble level, making tiny adjustments until it was perfect. He tested it by pressing hard before calling it done. The entire process required over thirty minutes, because quality work takes time. Mrs. Lee paid their full established repair price of twenty dollars without hesitation. "I should have come to you from the very beginning," she said.
### Scene 17 (120 words)
Mrs. Lee proved to be happy to share her story with neighbors throughout the week that followed. "The cheap shelf literally collapsed off my wall after barely one week," she reported to neighbors. "Maplewood Makers came the same day I called, diagnosed every problem Neville's installation had, and fixed it properly with the right materials and real craftsmanship." Word of her experience spread quickly through the neighborhood. People in the neighborhood started talking about the difference between cheap work and good work. Neville's aggressively low prices had seemed like great deals, but his rushed work wasn't holding up.
### Scene 18 (118 words)
A new customer contacted Maplewood Makers the next morning after hearing about Mrs. Lee's experience. Mr. Patel wanted custom bookshelves designed and built for his home study, and he was willing to pay their higher price because he understood why it cost more. "I heard the complete story of how you diagnosed and repaired Mrs. Lee's shelf disaster," he said . "I want people who do it right the first time, even if it costs more." He chose Maplewood Makers despite their higher pricing. Their reputation for quality work had brought this customer through word of mouth.
### Scene 19 (121 words)
The entire team gathered at the fort that afternoon feeling relieved, proud, and wiser about how quality matters in business. Their strategic decision to compete through quality and differentiation rather than engaging in a destructive price war had proven successful better than they'd hoped. "We didn't have to become the cheapest option available to attract and retain loyal customers," Ellis said with quiet confidence. "We just had to be the best option, and let our work prove it." Layla smiled warmly, proud of how much his thinking had changed. "Our advantage is doing excellent work that lasts. That's what makes us special, and nobody can compete with that just by lowering prices."
### Scene 20 (119 words)
That evening, Layla examined the neighborhood map and noticed something interesting. A new dot had appeared on the map, positioned separate and isolated from their established and growing network. It showed Neville's business as a single dot with thin, weak lines to a few customers. Several of those connections had already faded away. Meanwhile, Maplewood Makers' web glowed steadily across the neighborhood, each connection thick and stable, showing relationships built on trust. Their network was built on trust and quality, not low prices. That made all the difference.

## FL VOCABULARY
- **competition** — when another business offers the same products or services, creating pressure on pricing and market share
- **competitive advantage** — the specific, defensible strengths that allow one business to consistently outperform rivals
- **differentiation** — the strategy of making your product or service distinctly different from competitors in ways customers value
- **price positioning** — carefully choosing where your price sits relative to competitors to communicate quality and value
- **quality premium** — the more amount customers willingly pay for superior workmanship, reliability, and durability
- **race to the bottom** — a destructive competitive dynamic where businesses repeatedly lower prices until profitability becomes impossible
- **market segmentation** — recognizing that different customers want different things, and choosing which group to serve
