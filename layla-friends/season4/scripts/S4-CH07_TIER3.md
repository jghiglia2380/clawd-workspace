## THE BIG MISTAKE

### Scene 1 (65 words)
Monday morning opened with the kind of calm the friends had learned to appreciate. Layla's order board displayed four yellow cards in a neat row, each representing a job in progress. Ellis organized his woodworking tools while Riley sketched an elaborate platter design she'd been planning for Mrs. Davis. Benny adjusted his green plaid scarf, grabbed his delivery bag, and headed out to check on their neighborhood customers. Everything felt balanced.

### Scene 2 (66 words)
Benny ran into Mrs. Garcia while she was checking her mailbox on Elm Street. "My kitchen shelves are getting wobbly," she mentioned casually. "Could your team build me two new ones?" Benny's face lit up immediately. "! We can have them done by Friday!" Mrs. Garcia beamed at the quick response. Benny loved that feeling of making someone happy. He didn't pause to check the order board or consult with his team first.

### Scene 3 (65 words)
Tuesday morning before school, Benny struck up conversations with three more neighbors during the walk to the bus stop. Mr. Rodriguez wanted a birdhouse for his backyard. Mrs. Kim needed a raised garden box. Mr. Torres asked about a small bench for his front porch. Benny promised each of them delivery by the weekend, grinning with every handshake. Three more commitments in a single morning, and nobody on the team knew.

### Scene 4 (66 words)
Wednesday afternoon, Benny burst through the fort door radiating pride. "Incredible news!" he announced, spreading his arms wide. "I landed us seven brand-new orders this week!" He paused, waiting for celebration. The fort went silent instead. Layla's hand froze over the order board. Riley slowly set down her sketching pencil. Ellis stopped mid-stroke on his chisel sharpening. "Seven new orders?" Layla repeated carefully. "On top of the four we already have?"

### Scene 5 (65 words)
Layla pulled out her notebook and started calculating with focused intensity. "Four current orders plus seven new ones gives us eleven total commitments this week." She tapped her pencil against the table rhythmically while the number sank in. "Our realistic capacity is about six orders per week. Eleven is nearly double what we can handle." She met Benny's eyes directly. "We physically cannot deliver all of this."

### Scene 6 (66 words)
Benny's confident expression crumbled. "But I already gave my word to every single one of them," he said, his voice dropping to barely above a whisper. "Mrs. Garcia is depending on those shelves for her kitchen renovation. Mr. Rodriguez already told his wife the birdhouse was coming." He searched his friends' faces for understanding. "I can't go back on my promises now. Our reputation would suffer." The silence that followed felt heavy.

### Scene 7 (65 words)
Ellis rubbed his forehead, thinking through the dilemma. "He has a valid point about reputation. If we cancel commitments, customers lose confidence in us." Layla nodded reluctantly. "Then we have to attempt everything. But I want everyone to understand how difficult this will be." Riley glanced at her platter sketch for Mrs. Davis, the custom piece requiring eight careful hours of detailed work. She already knew those hours wouldn't materialize.

### Scene 8 (66 words)
Thursday through Saturday collapsed into one exhausting blur. Ellis began cutting lumber before sunrise each morning. Riley threw clay onto her pottery wheel before breakfast, skipping her usual warm-up routine. Layla tracked orders so frantically that her normally precise handwriting became nearly illegible. Benny sprinted between delivery locations and the workshop without pausing. Nobody stopped for lunch. Nobody cracked jokes or laughed. The fort that normally buzzed with creative energy now hummed with desperate, grinding pressure.

### Scene 9 (65 words)
Friday morning, Riley confronted the impossible math of Mrs. Davis's custom platter. She had exactly three hours before the scheduled pickup. This that design demanded eight hours of careful attention to get the glazing and detail work right. She rushed through the edges, eliminated the decorative flourishes she loved, and simplified every element. When she held up the finished piece, it looked acceptable. Just acceptable. "I made her a platter," Riley said quietly. "But not my best platter."

### Scene 10 (66 words)
While juggling simultaneous shelf and birdhouse construction, Ellis measured a board for Mrs. Garcia's kitchen shelves, got momentarily distracted calculating dimensions for a separate order, and cut the lumber two full inches too short. "No!" he groaned, staring at the ruined piece. Unusable. He grabbed a fresh board and started the measurements again from scratch. That single distracted moment cost them four dollars in wasted materials and forty-five precious minutes of irreplaceable production time.

### Scene 11 (65 words)
Friday afternoon arrived with devastating clarity. Two orders were scheduled for five o'clock delivery. One was complete and packaged. The other sat on the workbench barely half-finished. Benny had to make the call nobody wanted to make. His hands trembled as he dialed the customer's number. "Would it be possible to deliver tomorrow morning instead?" A long, uncomfortable pause. "I suppose that works," she responded flatly. Benny hung up feeling physically ill.

### Scene 12 (66 words)
Saturday became an endurance test. All four friends arrived at the fort by seven in the morning and worked without meaningful breaks until five in the afternoon. Ten grueling hours straight. Ellis, hands shaking from fatigue, dropped a completed birdhouse and cracked the decorative roof panel. More waste, more money lost. Riley's fingers cramped painfully from rushing her pottery. Layla's eyes burned from staring at her tracking spreadsheet. Benny's scarf dragged through sawdust, and he didn't even notice.

### Scene 13 (65 words)
Sunday morning, Benny carried the final order to the last customer's doorstep. The woman who answered looked pointedly at her watch. "This was supposed to arrive yesterday," she said without warmth. Benny apologized profusely, twice, offering genuine regret. She accepted the shelf without thanking him and closed the door. Walking home alone, Benny kicked a stone down the empty sidewalk. He had wanted to make everyone happy. He'd accomplished the opposite.

### Scene 14 (66 words)
Back at the fort, they emptied the money jar and counted every bill and coin. One hundred thirty-two dollars. Their highest revenue total ever recorded. "Record-breaking week!" Benny announced, attempting a celebratory tone that convinced nobody. The celebration fell flat. Riley sat massaging her aching hands. Ellis had his forehead pressed against the wooden table. Layla yawned so deeply her jaw produced an audible crack. The impressive pile of money sat on the table, untouched and unappreciated.

### Scene 15 (65 words)
Layla opened her analysis notebook with methodical determination. "Let's figure out what we actually earned per hour of effort." She tallied the labor investment: thirteen hours per person, times four workers, equaling fifty-two total hours. Revenue of one hundred thirty-two dollars minus forty-three dollars in supply costs, which now included twelve dollars of materials ruined by rushing. Eighty-nine dollars in actual profit. Divided across fifty-two hours. "One dollar and seventy-one cents per hour," she announced.

### Scene 16 (66 words)
"Our average over previous weeks has been between two-fifty and three dollars per hour," Layla continued, flipping back through her records for confirmation. "This week, despite generating our highest revenue ever, we earned way less per hour." Ellis raised his head from the table slowly. "We worked nearly twice as hard and earned less per hour?" Layla confirmed it. "The rushing created waste. The waste consumed our profit margin. We actually moved backward financially."

### Scene 17 (65 words)
Mr. Mason appeared at the fort Sunday afternoon to return a borrowed clamp. He surveyed the scene: four visibly exhausted children slumped around a table with money they were too drained to appreciate. "Looks like a demanding week," he observed gently. Benny nodded miserably. "I committed us to too much." Mr. Mason settled onto the bench beside them. "When you promise more than you can deliver, Benny, you've made two mistakes. The promise, and the delivery."

### Scene 18 (66 words)
That evening, Layla proposed a practical solution. "What if we gave Benny a real-time signal showing our current workload capacity?" She grabbed a blank index card from the supply shelf. She colored one side green, the reverse side yellow, and wrote RED on a third fold. Green meant room for two or three more orders. Yellow meant nearly full, perhaps one small job remaining. Red meant booked, waitlist only. "Check this card before making any commitment," she told Benny.

### Scene 19 (65 words)
They ran through practice scenarios immediately. Layla positioned the card to display yellow, indicating near-capacity status. Benny role-played a neighbor approaching to request a custom shelf. He glanced deliberately at the card, drew a steadying breath, and delivered the new response: "We're at capacity this week, but I'd love to put you on our list for next week." The words felt uncomfortable leaving his mouth. But they also felt responsible and honest.

### Scene 20 (66 words)
Benny lifted the capacity card from the table and tucked it carefully into his scarf pocket, where he'd feel it every time he reached inside. "I wanted to make every single person happy," he said, his voice carrying genuine regret. "Instead, I made every single person stressed." Riley placed a supportive hand on his shoulder. "We love that you want to help everyone. The card helps you do it without breaking us." Benny nodded firmly. "Green means go. Red means grow the business first."

## FL VOCABULARY

- **capacity** -- the maximum amount of work a team can handle in a given time period
- **overcommitting** -- promising to deliver more than your resources can support
- **waste** -- materials, time, or money lost due to errors caused by rushing or fatigue
- **profit per hour** -- the actual earnings calculated by dividing profit by hours worked
- **waitlist** -- an ordered queue where customers wait for their turn when capacity is full
- **demand management** -- controlling how much work you accept to maintain quality
