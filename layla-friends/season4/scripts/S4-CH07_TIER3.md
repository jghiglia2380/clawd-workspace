# CHAPTER 7: THE BIG MISTAKE - TIER 3 (3RD GRADE)

**Target Narration Speed:** 125 WPM
**Target Word Count:** 1,000 words
**Episode Duration:** ~8 minutes
**Character Focus:** Benny (spotlight)
**FL Integration:** Medium (capacity limits, overcommitting, profit per hour, demand management, waste)
**Setting:** Fort, neighborhood, customer homes

## THE BIG MISTAKE

### Scene 1 (50 words)
Monday morning at the fort started like any other week. The order board showed four jobs in progress, all on schedule. Layla checked the money jars, updated her notebook, and nodded with satisfaction. "Solid pace for the week," she said. The team felt calm and organized. Nobody suspected what was coming.

### Scene 2 (50 words)
That afternoon, Benny ran into Mrs. Garcia while walking through the park. She mentioned needing two new shelves for her hallway by Friday. "We can handle that!" Benny said without hesitation. He loved the feeling of landing a new order. He did not stop to check with the team first.

### Scene 3 (50 words)
Tuesday morning at school drop-off, Benny talked to three more neighbors who each needed something made or repaired. He said yes to every one of them, scribbling orders on scraps of paper and stuffing them into his jacket pocket. By the time he reached the fort, he was carrying seven new commitments.

### Scene 4 (50 words)
At the Wednesday team meeting, Benny proudly pulled out the crumpled papers and read each order aloud. Seven new jobs on top of their existing four made eleven total. The smiles vanished from every face in the room except his. Layla's pen stopped moving. "Benny, we can not do all of this."

### Scene 5 (50 words)
Layla walked to the order board and pointed at the numbers. "Our realistic capacity is about six orders per week," she explained. "You committed us to eleven. That is nearly double what we can handle." Benny's excitement deflated as he stared at the board. He had never thought about the math.

### Scene 6 (50 words)
"But I already promised all of them!" Benny said, his voice rising with panic. "I can not call them back and say we changed our minds. They are counting on us!" The room fell silent. Nobody wanted to break promises to customers, but nobody could see a path through eleven orders either.

### Scene 7 (50 words)
After a long discussion, they decided to push through rather than cancel. Their reputation mattered too much. They would work extended hours and move as fast as possible to deliver everything by the end of the week. "This is going to be brutal," Ellis warned. He did not know how right he was.

### Scene 8 (50 words)
Thursday and Friday turned into a chaotic blur of sawing, painting, glazing, and assembling. The fort was louder and messier than it had ever been. Sawdust coated every surface, wet glaze dripped from the workbench onto the floor, and half-finished projects competed for table space. Nobody took a proper break all day.

### Scene 9 (50 words)
Riley had a custom platter to complete for Mrs. Davis. She normally spent eight hours on a piece like this, but with the backlog pressing in, she had only three. The finished platter was acceptable but nowhere near her best. "It is fine," Riley said quietly, but her disappointment was obvious to everyone.

### Scene 10 (50 words)
Ellis was juggling two projects at once when he measured a shelf board wrong and cut it three inches too short. The wood was ruined. He stared at the wasted piece on the floor and shook his head. "I was going too fast," he admitted. Starting over cost materials and time.

### Scene 11 (50 words)
Friday afternoon arrived with two orders still due. One was not finished. The customer called asking where her delivery was. Benny had to break the news. "We need one more day," he said carefully. "I can bring it first thing tomorrow morning." She agreed, but her voice carried clear disappointment.

### Scene 12 (50 words)
Saturday became a ten-hour marathon. All four friends worked from seven in the morning straight through to five in the afternoon. By the final hour, their hands ached, their eyes felt heavy, and conversation had stopped entirely. They delivered the last order just as the setting sun painted the sky orange.

### Scene 13 (50 words)
The late delivery went out on Sunday morning. The customer accepted her order but gave Benny a look that stayed with him all day. "I expected this yesterday," she said flatly. Benny handed over the finished work and apologized. Walking home, he carried a knot in his chest that would not loosen.

### Scene 14 (50 words)
Back at the fort, they gathered around the money jars and counted. One hundred and thirty-two dollars in revenue — their highest total ever. But the celebration never came. Riley sat with her head resting on the table. Ellis rubbed his sore hands. Even Benny stayed silent. They were too exhausted.

### Scene 15 (50 words)
Layla flipped to a fresh page and worked through the numbers. Total hours worked: fifty-two — thirteen hours per person. Materials wasted from rushing added twelve dollars to their costs. Final profit divided by hours came to one dollar and seventy-one cents per hour. Less than before, despite a record revenue week.

### Scene 16 (50 words)
Mr. Mason stopped by the fort on Sunday afternoon and took in the scene — four tired kids surrounded by sawdust and empty glaze jars. He sat down next to Benny. "When you promise more than you can deliver," he said gently, "you have made two mistakes. The promise and the work."

### Scene 17 (50 words)
Layla came to the next meeting with an idea and a homemade card. One side was green, the next was yellow, and the last was red. "Benny carries this when he talks to customers," she said. "I update it every morning based on how much work we have on the board."

### Scene 18 (50 words)
They tested the system right away. Layla set the card to yellow because six orders were already in progress. Benny practiced his new response: "We are at capacity this week, but I can put you on the list for next week." It felt strange to turn work away, but it also felt responsible.

### Scene 19 (50 words)
Benny held the capacity card and stared at it for a while. "I just wanted to make everyone happy," he said quietly. "I hate saying no. But this week I said yes too many times, and I made the whole team stressed instead of happy." He looked at his friends. "I am sorry."

### Scene 20 (50 words)
The map on the wall had been flickering and pulsing with stressed orange light all week. Now, with the capacity card sitting on the table and a sustainable workload ahead, the lines calmed back to a warm, steady glow. Benny picked up the card. "Green means go. Red means grow first."

## FL VOCABULARY INTRODUCED

1. **capacity** (Scenes 5, 17, 18) — The maximum amount of work a team can complete in a given period
2. **overcommitting** (Scenes 5, 6) — Promising more work than the team can realistically deliver
3. **profit per hour** (Scene 15) — Total profit divided by total hours worked, measuring earning efficiency
4. **waste** (Scenes 10, 15) — Materials or time lost due to errors caused by rushing
5. **waitlist** (Scene 18) — A system for queuing future customers when current capacity is full

## NARRATION NOTES

- **Target WPM:** 125 (comfortable pace with expression)
- **Pause:** 1 second between sentences
- **Tone:** Escalating chaos that resolves in honest reflection. The week should feel relentless.
