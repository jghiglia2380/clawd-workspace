## THE PARTNERSHIP
### Scene 1 (53 words)
Saturday morning, someone knocked on the fort door. The friends looked at each other with surprise. Nobody knocked on the fort door. Benny opened it and found Ellen, the cafe owner from the corner. She wore her green apron and a friendly smile. "Good morning," she said. "I have a business idea for you."
### Scene 2 (52 words)
Ellen stepped inside and looked around at their shelves. Riley's pottery lined one wall. Mugs, bowls, and small vases sat in neat rows. "My customers ask about your pottery," Ellen said. "They see your mugs around the neighborhood and want some too. I'd like to sell your pottery in my cafe."
### Scene 3 (53 words)
The friends got excited right away. Benny's eyes went wide. "So you want to buy six mugs from us?" he asked eagerly. Ellen shook her head gently. "Not exactly," she said. "I was thinking of something different. It's called consignment." Nobody knew that word. Ellen suggested they talk to Mr. Mason first.
### Scene 4 (52 words)
They walked to Mr. Mason's workshop together that afternoon. Ellen explained her idea to him. Mr. Mason nodded with understanding. "Consignment is a smart way for businesses to work together," he said. He pulled two stools close and asked the kids to sit down. "Let me explain how it works."
### Scene 5 (53 words)
"With consignment, Ellen doesn't buy the mugs from you," Mr. Mason began. "Instead, you leave the mugs at her cafe. She displays them for her customers to see. When a mug sells, Ellen keeps part of the money. She gives you the rest. If it doesn't sell, the mug comes back to you."
### Scene 6 (52 words)
"That's different from buying them outright," Layla said thoughtfully. Mr. Mason nodded. "Exactly. When someone buys your product in bulk at a lower price, that's called wholesale. With consignment, nobody pays anything upfront. Ellen shares the risk with you. She doesn't lose money if the mugs don't sell."
### Scene 7 (53 words)
"How much does Ellen keep?" Riley asked. That was the big question. Ellen spoke up. "I was thinking forty percent," she said. "That means for every dollar a mug earns, I keep forty cents and you keep sixty cents." Benny scrunched his nose. That sounded like a lot going to the cafe.
### Scene 8 (52 words)
Benny looked at his friends. He felt nervous but wanted to be fair. "What if we started at thirty percent for the first month?" he suggested carefully. "Then thirty-five percent after that? That way you get a trial period and we keep a little more." His heart was beating fast.
### Scene 9 (53 words)
Ellen thought about Benny's offer. She looked at Mr. Mason, who stayed quiet. This was for the kids and Ellen to work out together. "Thirty percent for the first month, then thirty-five ongoing," Ellen repeated slowly. She smiled. "That's a reasonable deal. You negotiated well, Benny." Benny felt proud of himself.
### Scene 10 (52 words)
Mr. Mason spoke up about one more detail. "What happens to mugs that don't sell?" he asked. Ellen and the kids discussed it together. They agreed that any unsold mug would come back after thirty days. Nobody would be stuck with something they didn't want. Both sides felt the deal was fair.
### Scene 11 (53 words)
"Can we write this down?" Layla asked. She pulled out her notebook. "If it's not written down, it's just a promise. Promises can be forgotten." Ellen looked impressed. "That's very professional," she said with respect. Mr. Mason agreed. "A written agreement protects both sides. Good business partners always put deals in writing."
### Scene 12 (52 words)
Layla wrote the agreement on a clean page. She listed the products, the percentage split, and the thirty-day return rule. She wrote it in clear words anyone could understand. When she finished, Ellen read it over and nodded. Then Ellen signed it. All four kids signed it too. The deal was.
### Scene 13 (53 words)
Riley spent Monday choosing the best mugs for Ellen's cafe. She picked six of her favorites. Each one had a smooth glaze and a comfortable handle. "Cafe customers drink coffee," Riley said. "These handles need to feel right." She wrapped each mug carefully in cloth. These mugs represented their whole business.
### Scene 14 (52 words)
Tuesday morning, they delivered the mugs to Ellen's cafe. Ellen placed them on a wooden shelf near the counter. She made a small sign that said "Made by Maplewood Makers." The mugs looked beautiful next to the coffee cups and pastries. Benny stood back and felt proud seeing their work displayed.
### Scene 15 (53 words)
The week felt long. Every day they wondered if anyone had bought a mug. Riley kept checking her phone. "What if cafe customers don't like them?" she asked nervously. These were different customers than the neighbors they already knew. Benny tried to stay patient. "Ellen believes in our work," he reminded her.
### Scene 16 (52 words)
Friday afternoon, the fort phone rang. It was Ellen. "I sold one this morning!" she said happily. Then she paused. "Actually, I sold three total this week." Benny shouted the news to everyone. Three mugs sold! Riley clapped her hands. The cafe customers liked their pottery after all.
### Scene 17 (53 words)
Saturday morning, they visited Ellen to collect their share. Three mugs at twelve dollars each meant thirty-six dollars total. Ellen's thirty-five percent was twelve dollars and sixty cents. The friends received twenty-three dollars and forty cents. Ellen handed over the cash with a handshake. "Good first week, partners," she said warmly.
### Scene 18 (52 words)
Back at the fort, Layla set up a new system. She labeled a small envelope "Cafe Revenue" and put the twenty-three dollars and forty cents inside. "We need to track this separately," she explained. "This money came from a different channel than our regular customers. That helps us see which partnerships work."
### Scene 19 (53 words)
Benny sat back and smiled. "We made money while we were at school," he said slowly. "Those mugs sold when we weren't even there." Layla nodded. "That's what a good partnership does. Ellen works and we earn at the same time." Frances had been right. Real partnerships meant both people win.
### Scene 20 (52 words)
That evening, Layla checked the map. A new line connected the fort to Ellen's cafe. But this line looked different. It was thicker than the customer lines and glowed golden-green instead of gold. Smaller lines branched from the cafe to Ellen's customers. Their business had grown in a brand new direction.

## FL VOCABULARY
- **consignment** -- leaving your products with another business to sell, splitting the money when items sell
- **wholesale** -- buying products in bulk at a lower price to resell
- **negotiate** -- talking to find a deal that works fairly for both sides
- **written agreement** -- a signed document that records the terms of a deal
- **partnership** -- when two businesses work together for shared benefit
- **passive income** -- earning money even when you are not actively working
