## THE MEDITERRANEAN FISHING VILLAGE

### Scene 1
Mr. Mason lifted a tiny fishing net from the trunk with great care in his deliberate movements. The threads were delicate and woven beautifully with incredible skill by experienced hands that worked long ago. "This is from Greece," he said with a fond smile spreading across his weathered face. "From a small fishing village by the Mediterranean Sea that I visited many years ago during my travels. It taught me that timing matters greatly in business and in life itself. Time can be your greatest enemy or your greatest ally."

### Scene 2
The village was small and absolutely beautiful with white houses covering every hillside around them like terraces. Blue doors and shutters dotted the whitewashed buildings throughout the settlement along narrow winding cobblestone streets. Every morning the fishermen went out early before the sun rose over the distant horizon in their boats. They returned at eight o'clock sharp every single day with their fresh catch from the night's hard work at sea. It was a rhythm as old as time itself in that place.

### Scene 3
Young Mr. Mason met a fisherman named Dimitri one morning at the busy docks where boats came in with their catches. His fishing boat was painted bright blue and very well maintained after many years of constant use on the sea. He had fished for forty years in these productive waters with great success throughout his entire life. One morning Dimitri invited young Mr. Mason to help sell the catch at the market. "Come with me, young friend," he said kindly with a warm smile and genuine hospitality. "I will show you something very important about business and time management today. You will learn a valuable lesson about perishable goods."

### Scene 4
They spread the fresh catch out on ice at the dock market stall carefully and with attention to display. Beautiful silver fish glistened and sparkled in the morning light brightly for all the customers. Dimitri set up his wooden table and arranged everything appealingly for the people who would arrive. "How much do you charge for the fish you catch each day?" young Mr. Mason asked with curiosity. Dimitri pointed to his handwritten sign displayed clearly for everyone passing by.

### Scene 5
"Fresh fish at eight in the morning costs ten euros per kilogram," Dimitri explained clearly to his helper standing beside him. Customers came early to buy from him because they knew his excellent reputation for quality fish caught fresh every night. They bought quickly without hesitation or haggling with him about the prices he posted. The fish looked absolutely perfect in the morning light shining down on the ice. The high quality was obvious to everyone who looked at the glistening silver catch displayed attractively before them on the table.

### Scene 6
By ten o'clock in the morning, half the fish had already sold to satisfied customers. But customers stopped coming as frequently now as they had before in the early morning hours. "The morning market is ending now," Dimitri said to his young helper beside him. "All the housewives are home cooking lunch for their families. The dock is growing quiet." The market activity slowed down noticeably around them as time passed.

### Scene 7
Young Mr. Mason watched carefully as Dimitri changed his price sign at eleven o'clock sharp on the dot. The price dropped from ten euros to eight euros per kilogram now for all customers. "Why lower the price when the fish are still perfectly fresh?" he asked with genuine confusion about the strategy. "Watch closely and you will understand the reason," Dimitri said with a knowing smile and patient demeanor. "Time is my worst enemy in this business always. Every hour that passes, my product loses value steadily."

### Scene 8
Dimitri explained the concept while arranging the remaining fish for display. "These fish are what we call perishable products in the fishing trade," he said seriously to the young man. "They spoil very quickly in the Mediterranean heat and sun beating down all day. I cannot keep them until tomorrow's market. They will go completely bad overnight and smell terrible and be worthless by morning."

### Scene 9
At noon exactly on the clock, the price lowered again to six euros per kilogram for the remaining fish. "The fish are still perfectly good quality to eat right now for your family," Dimitri said to customers passing by the stall. "But I absolutely need to sell them today before closing time this evening at five o'clock sharp. Tomorrow morning they will be completely worthless to me and to everyone who might want them! I must sell everything now while they're still good and fresh and safe to eat tonight."

### Scene 10
By two o'clock in the warm afternoon, fewer customers came to browse the market stalls along the dock. Dimitri had ten kilograms left unsold sitting there on the melting ice that was getting watery. "Five euros only for excellent fresh fish caught last night in deep waters!" he called out loudly to people passing by on the street. Some morning customers came back to buy more when they heard the significantly lower prices being offered. The prices were dropping steadily throughout the day exactly as Dimitri had expected and planned.

### Scene 11
Young Mr. Mason asked if this constant price dropping made Dimitri angry or frustrated at all. "You're charging half the morning price now!" he said with concern for the fisherman's profits and income. But Dimitri smiled wisely at the young man's worry and anxiety about the situation. "It's better to get five euros than zero tomorrow morning when they're spoiled," he explained with calm wisdom and acceptance. "Tomorrow these fish will be garbage that nobody wants to buy at any price whatsoever! Better to sell cheap than not sell at all."

### Scene 12
"So the clock is working against you constantly?" young Mr. Mason asked, understanding better now. "Exactly right, my young friend," Dimitri said while rearranging his dwindling stock carefully. "Every hour that passes, my fish become less valuable to customers. Time is running out constantly all day long. I race against spoilage every single day in this business."

### Scene 13
"At four o'clock in the afternoon, five kilograms remained unsold on the table," Mr. Mason continued his story to the friends. "Three euros now for the entire remaining lot on the table together. An old woman dressed in traditional black clothing came to the stall with her wicker basket. She had waited patiently all day on purpose for this exact moment to arrive. 'I knew you would drop the price eventually!' she said with satisfaction and a knowing smile. She understood the economics of perishable goods perfectly well."

### Scene 14
"That doesn't seem fair to Dimitri at all," Benny said with frustration clearly. "She should pay the same price as everyone else this morning!" "I thought exactly the same thing when I saw it happen," Mr. Mason said honestly. "But then Dimitri explained something wise that changed my view completely."

### Scene 15
"She's being smart about business economics and market dynamics," Dimitri had told young Mr. Mason calmly without any resentment. "She knows very well that fish are perishable products with strict time limits that cannot be extended. She knows I absolutely must sell everything today before closing. She uses her knowledge cleverly to save money for her family budget. That's actually clever business thinking on her part entirely. I respect her strategy even though it costs me profit."

### Scene 16
"By five o'clock at closing time, two kilograms remained unsold on the table," Mr. Mason said to the attentive friends. "One euro now for both kilograms together as a final offer. But even at that incredibly low bargain price, no customers came by to purchase them. He gave them to a friendly neighbor for completely free as a gift. It was better someone eats them tonight for dinner than they go to complete waste in the trash!"

### Scene 17
"So for our Community Market," Ellis said thoughtfully while processing the lesson in his mind. "We really need to identify carefully what products are perishable like fresh food or cut flowers. They need completely different pricing plans than other things! They absolutely cannot wait until tomorrow without spoiling."

### Scene 18
"Exactly right, you understand perfectly!" Mr. Mason said with obvious pride at his understanding of the concept. "Compare fish to something like a boat for a good example of contrast. A boat doesn't spoil at all over time sitting in the harbor. You can sell it next week or next month or even years later. You can wait patiently for exactly the right price from interested buyers who want it! Time is not your enemy with durable goods like boats or tools."

### Scene 19
"Fresh lemonade is perishable too," Layla realized suddenly with new insight. "If we don't sell it the same day it's made fresh, it goes bad quickly. We might need to lower our prices later in the day as time passes. Just exactly like Dimitri did with all his fish throughout the entire day!"

### Scene 20
Mr. Mason held the delicate fishing net up in the attic light for all of them to see. "This special net reminds me every time I look at it," he said warmly to the friends. "Some products are truly trapped by time and cannot escape the ticking clock. You must sell them quickly before they spoil and lose their value completely. Be flexible with your prices as time passes throughout the day!" The friends understood the important lessons about urgency.
