## THE MOROCCAN SPICE MARKET

### Scene 1
The friends gathered closely around the old wooden trunk in Mr. Mason's dusty attic, excited for another adventure story. He reached inside carefully with both hands and pulled out a small leather pouch tied with a faded red ribbon. The rich smell of exotic spices immediately filled the air around them, making everyone breathe deeply. "Let me tell you about my trip to Morocco when I was young," he said with a mysterious twinkle in his eye. Everyone leaned forward eagerly, ready to listen and learn.

### Scene 2
Mr. Mason opened the ancient pouch slowly and gently let each friend smell the precious contents inside. The wonderful scent of sweet cinnamon, precious saffron, and earthy cumin mixed together beautifully in the quiet attic air around them. "These spices are from a famous market in Marrakech, Morocco," he explained with obvious fondness in his voice. "It was one of the most colorful, busy, overwhelming places I've ever seen in my entire life." His eyes looked distant and happy as the vivid memory came flooding back.

### Scene 3
"Let me paint a picture for you," Mr. Mason began, settling comfortably into his best storytelling voice. "Imagine narrow winding streets covered completely by colorful fabric awnings stretched overhead to block the desert sun. Hundreds of wooden stalls lined up in seemingly endless rows on both sides of every street you walked down. Each stall absolutely overflowing with spices, fabrics, pottery, or shiny metalwork." He gestured with his hands to show the size. "The colors were almost blinding in the bright sunlight."

### Scene 4
"The sounds were just as incredible and overwhelming as all the sights," Mr. Mason continued with growing enthusiasm and energy. "Merchants calling out constantly to anyone and everyone passing by their stalls every single moment. 'Best saffron in all of Morocco!' one would shout very loudly at the top of his voice. 'Finest cumin, best price in the market!' another would yell back just as loudly in fierce competition." He smiled broadly at the wonderful memory. "Everyone competed loudly and energetically for customers' attention."

### Scene 5
"I was very young then, maybe twenty years old and traveling alone for the first time," Mr. Mason explained to them thoughtfully. "I'd saved up money for months working odd jobs to afford this special trip to Africa. My mother had asked me to bring back real saffron for her special cooking. 'Get the good stuff,' she'd told me with a knowing smile, trusting my judgment." He paused for effect. "I wanted desperately to find the very best quality saffron I could possibly afford."

### Scene 6
"I walked through the enormous market feeling completely overwhelmed by everything around me," Mr. Mason admitted honestly to the friends. "There were dozens and dozens of stalls selling spices of every imaginable kind and color! How could I possibly choose the right one? How would I know if the price being offered was truly fair?" He looked directly at each friend seriously. "I had absolutely no experience negotiating prices in foreign markets at that point in my young life."

### Scene 7
"Finally, after wandering for nearly an hour feeling lost, I saw a stall with beautiful saffron displayed in neat, carefully arranged piles," Mr. Mason continued his story. "The merchant spotted me immediately and could probably tell I was a foreign tourist from my clothes. 'You want saffron?' he asked in broken English. 'Best quality, best price!' I nodded nervously. 'How much does it cost?' I asked hesitantly. 'Fifty dirhams,' he said with complete confidence." Mr. Mason paused dramatically. "That seemed quite expensive to me."

### Scene 8
Layla interrupted politely with an important question. "Did you just pay exactly what he asked for?" Mr. Mason shook his head firmly with a knowing smile. "No! Something deep inside told me to wait and not rush this decision. I'd seen other tourists getting better prices by walking away from stalls. So that's exactly what I decided to do. I thanked him politely and started walking deliberately toward the next stall at a slow, measured pace."

### Scene 9
"That's when things got really interesting in the negotiation," Mr. Mason said, leaning forward closer to the group. "Wait, wait, my friend!" the merchant called after me somewhat desperately. I turned around slowly and calmly. "For you, very special price! Forty dirhams!" I smiled politely but kept walking steadily away." The friends watched with wide eyes, completely absorbed. "Then what happened next?" Riley asked breathlessly, unable to contain her curiosity about the outcome.

### Scene 10
"Thirty-five dirhams, final price!" the merchant shouted after me with growing desperation in his voice. Mr. Mason acted out the scene with his hands and body language. "I stopped walking at that point. I turned around slowly and walked back to his stall at an unhurried pace. Now we were both ready to negotiate seriously for real. The game had officially begun between us."

### Scene 11
"But I still wasn't completely sure what a truly fair price should be," Mr. Mason explained honestly. "So I said carefully, 'Let me look at the other stalls first and compare quality.' The merchant nodded, understanding my position completely. 'You look, you look around,' he said generously. 'But you come back here. My saffron is the absolute best quality in the whole market, I promise you.'"

### Scene 12
"I spent the next hour walking slowly through the entire spice section," Mr. Mason said. "I watched other customers buying saffron at different stalls throughout the marketplace carefully. I saw prices ranging anywhere from twenty-five to forty dirhams depending on quality. Most people seemed to pay around thirty dirhams for good quality saffron." He smiled with satisfaction. "That knowledge gave me real confidence."

### Scene 13
"I went back to the first merchant with new confidence," Mr. Mason continued. "He smiled warmly when he saw me returning. 'Ah, you came back! I knew you would!' he said happily. I nodded calmly. 'I'll give you twenty-five dirhams,' I offered boldly." The merchant's friendly face changed quickly. "Twenty-five? No, no, my friend. This is the highest quality available anywhere!"

### Scene 14
"We negotiated back and forth for several minutes in a friendly way," Mr. Mason explained. "He came down to thirty-two dirhams. I went up to twenty-seven. He countered with thirty. I offered twenty-eight firmly." Benny interrupted. "That sounds tiring!" Mr. Mason chuckled. "Actually, it was fun! Like playing a respectful game between two people who understood the rules."

### Scene 15
"Finally, the merchant smiled widely at me," Mr. Mason said. "Thirty dirhams, and I throw in cumin absolutely free. Final offer.' I thought about it very carefully for a moment. Based on everything I'd seen, thirty was actually quite fair for the quality." Mr. Mason paused. "So I agreed to his deal."

### Scene 16
"We shook hands firmly, both smiling," Mr. Mason said warmly. "The merchant carefully wrapped my saffron in brown paper with great care, then placed it gently in this very pouch. As he handed it to me respectfully, he said something I never forgot: 'A good deal makes both people happy.'"

### Scene 17
Ellis spoke up thoughtfully. "So negotiation wasn't about fighting? It was about finding a price that worked for both of you?" Mr. Mason nodded approvingly. "Exactly! If the price was too low, he couldn't support his family. Too high, and I couldn't afford other things I needed."

### Scene 18
"That brings us to supply and demand," Mr. Mason said. "That year, there had been a bad saffron harvest in the region. Less supply meant merchants could charge higher prices. But there was still plenty of demand because people wanted it for cooking."

### Scene 19
"The next day, I needed black pepper," Mr. Mason continued. "Every stall had mountains of it! When supply is very high, prices drop dramatically. I got excellent pepper for just a few dirhams."

### Scene 20
Layla had been thinking quietly. "For our Community Market, we need several things," she said slowly. "Know what things are worth. Set fair starting prices. Be willing to negotiate respectfully. Make sure both buyer and seller feel happy." Mr. Mason beamed. "Now you understand!"

3. **supply** — How much of something exists
4. **demand** — How much people want something
