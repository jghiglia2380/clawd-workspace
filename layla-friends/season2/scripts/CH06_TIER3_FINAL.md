## THE TOKYO STREET FOOD VENDOR

### Scene 1
Mr. Mason reached into the old trunk and carefully pulled out a delicate paper fan. It had beautiful colorful designs painted all across it in traditional style. "This came all the way from Tokyo, Japan," he said with a warm, nostalgic smile spreading across his face. "A street food vendor named Yuki gave it to me many years ago when I visited. She taught me one of the most important business lessons I've ever learned about choosing the right location."

### Scene 2
"Yuki sold takoyaki from a small mobile cart that she pushed," Mr. Mason began fondly with affection in his voice. "Takoyaki are octopus balls cooked in special round pans with little dimples in them. They're a very popular Japanese street food that people of all ages love. Round and crispy on the outside with special toppings like sauce and bonito flakes. Soft and fluffy inside. Absolutely delicious when fresh and hot!"

### Scene 3
"I met Yuki on a quiet side street in the heart of Tokyo," Mr. Mason continued with the memory. "She'd been set up at her spot for two whole hours already that morning. But only three people had stopped to buy food from her cart during that time. Just three customers in two full hours! She looked tired, frustrated, and clearly worried about her poor sales for the day."

### Scene 4
"I walked up to her cart and asked if her takoyaki was any good," Mr. Mason said. "She smiled warmly and offered me a free sample to try. It was absolutely amazing! Perfectly cooked and seasoned with just the right toppings. 'This is delicious!' I told her honestly with enthusiasm. 'So why isn't anyone else buying from you? Your food is excellent!'"

### Scene 5
Yuki sighed deeply and looked down the nearly empty street with clear frustration. "My location is the real problem here," she explained with disappointment in her voice. "This particular street is just too quiet for successfully selling food. Not enough people walk by here during the busy daytime hours. I desperately need more foot traffic passing by. More customers walking by to see my cart and smell the delicious food cooking!"

### Scene 6
"Why don't you move to a busier street then?" young Mr. Mason asked her with genuine curiosity. "She explained the difficulty to me," old Mr. Mason told the listening friends. "Busy spots with lots of foot traffic cost much more money to rent each single day. She had to pay the city government for official permission to sell in any location at all throughout the entire city of Tokyo."

### Scene 7
"The quiet spot cost one thousand yen per day to rent," Mr. Mason explained clearly to them. "The busy spot with lots of people cost five thousand yen per day for the same size space. That's five times more expensive! But was it really worth paying that much more money each and every day? That was the critical question we both wondered about."

### Scene 8
"It depends on how many extra customers she can attract there," Benny said thoughtfully, considering the math. "Exactly right!" Mr. Mason replied with enthusiasm and obvious approval. "That's exactly what I told her too when she asked my opinion! Yuki decided to test the busy spot and find out for herself. I helped her carefully push her heavy cart all the way across town to the new location."

### Scene 9
"The busy street was absolutely incredible compared to the quiet one," Mr. Mason continued with excitement in his voice. "Hundreds of people walking past every few minutes constantly throughout the day! Office workers rushing around on their limited lunch breaks from nearby buildings. Tourists exploring the interesting city and taking photos of everything. Students heading home from school with their backpacks. The constant foot traffic was truly amazing to witness!"

### Scene 10
"Within the first hour on the busy street," Mr. Mason said with clear amazement, "Yuki sold an impressive thirty servings of takoyaki to hungry customers passing by! Thirty servings in just one single hour! At three hundred yen each serving, that was nine thousand yen in revenue already! She could hardly believe the dramatic difference in sales between the two locations."

### Scene 11
"On the quiet street all morning long," Mr. Mason explained carefully for comparison, "she'd only made nine hundred yen total in three full hours of hard work. The difference was absolutely remarkable! She sold ten times more in just one hour on the busy street. Location truly changed everything about her business that day!"

### Scene 12
Ellis was calculating the numbers quickly in his active mind. "So the busy location costs four thousand yen more per day to rent," he said out loud for everyone to hear. "But she made eight thousand yen more in just the first hour alone! That's already four thousand yen extra profit right there! And the day wasn't even over yet!"

### Scene 13
"Location isn't only about the total foot traffic numbers passing by," Yuki had explained to young Mr. Mason very carefully that important day. "It's about having the right foot traffic for your specific product you're selling. Office workers buy lunch because they're hungry and have limited time at work. Tourists buy interesting snacks while they explore new places in the city. Students buy cheap food because of their small budgets. Different types of customers have completely different needs."

### Scene 14
"By the end of that long day," Mr. Mason said with a smile, "Yuki had sold an impressive one hundred twenty servings total to customers! That's thirty-six thousand yen in total revenue for just one day! She was absolutely thrilled with the dramatic results from simply changing her location to a better spot."

### Scene 15
"After paying the five thousand yen daily location fee to the city government," Mr. Mason continued with his story, "she still had thirty-one thousand yen in profit remaining for herself. That was an excellent and very successful day of business for her small mobile cart! She was extremely happy with her smart decision to move."

### Scene 16
"Now compare that to the quiet street where she started," Mr. Mason said for clear perspective. "She might sell only about fifteen servings all day there on a typical day. That's four thousand five hundred yen in total revenue for the entire day. After paying one thousand for the location fee, only three thousand five hundred profit. The difference is huge and important!"

### Scene 17
Riley's eyes widened with sudden understanding of the important lesson. "So the expensive location made almost nine times more profit!" she said with clear amazement in her voice. "Even after paying much higher rent for the better spot! The busy location was definitely worth every single yen she spent!"

### Scene 18
"Yuki taught me three critical rules about choosing good business locations," Mr. Mason said seriously. "First important rule: visibility matters greatly. People need to see your business clearly and easily from a distance. Second rule: foot traffic counts enormously. More people walking by means more potential customers. Third rule: attract the right people who actually want your specific product."

### Scene 19
Layla was thinking very carefully about their upcoming Community Market now. "So where we set up really matters for our success!" she said with clear understanding. "We need to choose a place where lots of people naturally walk by every single day. Where they can see us clearly and access us easily without obstacles. The park is perfect for all those reasons!"

### Scene 20
Mr. Mason held up the delicate paper fan very carefully in his hands. "Yuki gave me this beautiful fan to help me remember the important lesson forever," he said warmly with emotion. "She told me that good products absolutely need good locations to succeed. Even the best food in the entire world won't sell if nobody can see it! Location, location, location!" The friends all understood the valuable lesson completely now.
