## THE JAPANESE FISH MARKET

### Scene 1
Mr. Mason reached into his old trunk and pulled out a carved wooden fish with care. The details were amazing to see up close. Every scale was visible and perfectly carved. "This is from the Tsukiji fish market in Tokyo," he said proudly. "The biggest fish market in the entire world!"

### Scene 2
"I visited at four in the morning," Mr. Mason began his story with energy. "That's when the tuna auction happens every day without fail. Hundreds of massive frozen tuna were lined up in perfect rows on the floor. Each one weighed hundreds of pounds and looked huge!"

### Scene 3
"Restaurant owners came from all over Tokyo to bid," Mr. Mason continued eagerly. "They inspected each fish very carefully before the bidding started each morning. Looking closely at the color of the meat with bright flashlights they brought. Checking the fat content inside. Each fish could be worth thousands of dollars to the right buyer!"

### Scene 4
"How do they decide who gets which fish?" Ellis asked. Mr. Mason smiled warmly. "That's the interesting part! They use an auction to sell each fish fairly. The fish goes to whoever offers the most money."

### Scene 5
"The auctioneer stood on a platform," Mr. Mason explained. "He spoke fast in Japanese. Ringing a bell to signal bids. Pointing at bidders around the room. Everything happened in seconds! It was like watching a fast dance!"

### Scene 6
"One tuna caught everyone's attention," Mr. Mason said with excitement. "It was perfect for making sushi. Beautiful deep red color. Excellent fat marbling. A famous restaurant owner wanted it badly for his wealthy customers."

### Scene 7
"The bidding started at ten thousand yen," Mr. Mason continued with energy. "That's about one hundred dollars. But immediately others raised their hands to bid higher. Fifteen thousand yen! Twenty thousand! The price climbed very quickly!"

### Scene 8
Layla looked confused. "Why didn't they just set one price?" Mr. Mason explained patiently. "Because different buyers value the same fish differently. A fancy restaurant can charge customers more for premium sushi. So they can afford to pay more than small neighborhood restaurants."

### Scene 9
"The bidding reached fifty thousand yen," Mr. Mason said with energy. "Most bidders dropped out at that point. Only three determined buyers remained. They were serious restaurant owners with successful businesses. The fish was worth the high price to them."

### Scene 10
"Sixty thousand! Seventy thousand! Eighty thousand!" Mr. Mason acted out the scene with energy. "Finally, at ninety thousand yen, one restaurant owner won! That's about nine hundred dollars for one fish! The crowd clapped loudly for the winner who smiled with pride."

### Scene 11
"That seems like money!" Benny said in clear amazement at the price. Mr. Mason nodded at him knowingly with a wise smile. "But that restaurant owner knew something very important about his business and customers. He could make twenty perfect portions from that one fish he purchased. Sell each portion for sixty dollars to his customers who paid well. That's twelve hundred dollars total revenue from selling all the portions!"

### Scene 12
Ellis was doing quick math in his head now. "So he pays nine hundred dollars to buy the fish at auction from the seller. Makes twelve hundred dollars by selling all the portions to customers at his restaurant. His profit is three hundred dollars after costs!" Mr. Mason smiled proudly at Ellis's thinking and math. " right! He knew the fish's true value to his successful business perfectly."

### Scene 13
"But why auction?" Riley asked the obvious question curiously and thoughtfully. "Why not just sell it for nine hundred dollars directly to someone who wants it?" Mr. Mason explained the key point here and. "The auction finds the true market price automatically without guessing wrong. The person who values it most in the entire room wins the bidding."

### Scene 14
"If the seller just guessed a price," Mr. Mason continued teaching them wisely from experience, "they might guess too low and leave extra money on the table they could have earned. Or guess too high and not sell anything to anyone there. Auctions solve this difficult pricing problem perfectly for both buyer and seller in the market."

### Scene 15
"I watched many different fish sell that busy morning," Mr. Mason said fondly with nostalgia and happiness. "Some went for twenty thousand yen to smaller family restaurants with modest budgets. Some sold for forty thousand to medium-sized places with regular customers. Some for ninety thousand to top premium establishments serving wealthy clients. Each buyer paid what they personally thought was fair value for their situation."

### Scene 16
"The auction system is very efficient," Mr. Mason explained to the group listening. "It's fast and gets things done quickly without delay or wasted time. It's fair to everyone involved in the process too without favoritism. Everyone can see what's happening openly and in front of them. The highest bidder wins every single time. No secrets or favoritism."

### Scene 17
Benny had a sudden realization about their project excitedly now. "So for our Community Market, we could auction special items too! Things that might be worth different amounts to different people depending on their interest level!"

### Scene 18
"Brilliant idea!" Mr. Mason said with obvious pride in them all together. "Maybe someone donates a rare toy to sell at the market for charity. Or a special collectible book from an attic somewhere. Or handmade art that Riley created herself with care and skill. An auction lets people decide the real value together through competitive bidding among interested buyers."

### Scene 19
"Remember the important rules," Mr. Mason said seriously and to them all. "Start with a minimum price first as a baseline. Take bids in clear order from the crowd gathered. Announce each bid loudly so everyone hears. When bidding stops, declare the winner right away. It must be fair for everyone."

### Scene 20
Mr. Mason held up the carved wooden fish for them all to see in the light now. "This fish reminds me that value isn't fixed or permanent in markets or economies. Different people see different worth in the same exact thing based on their needs and circumstances. Auctions find that true value through open, fair competition among buyers." The friends understood auctions now and felt ready to try running one themselves!
