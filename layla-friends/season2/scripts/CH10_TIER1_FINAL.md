## THE GERMAN CHRISTMAS MARKET

### Scene 1
Mr. Mason held up wooden star. Gold paint shimmered bright. "This from Germany," he said smiling. "From Christmas market long ago!" Riley leaned in close. Snow fell outside the window. So pretty and magical!

### Scene 2
"Market was magical place," Mr. Mason began. "Lights twinkled everywhere bright. Snow covered the stalls. Smell of cinnamon filled air. Roasted nuts cooking hot. People smiled and laughed. Children played games. So beautiful and happy!"

### Scene 3
"I saw woman selling ornaments," Mr. Mason said. "Each carved by hand carefully. Stars like this one. Pretty angels with wings. Delicate snowflakes too. Every piece was unique! No two looked the same!"

### Scene 4
"How much for star?" I asked her. "Fifteen euros," she said kindly. Toy store sold similar ones. Only three euros each there. I wondered a lot. Why hers cost so much more? I was confused!

### Scene 5
"She saw my confused face," Mr. Mason said. "She invited me sit down. 'Let me show you something,' she said. Picked up plain wood piece. 'This how I start making,' she showed."

### Scene 6
"First, sand wood smooth," she explained slowly. "Takes thirty minutes hard work. Then carve design carefully. One whole hour working. One mistake ruins everything! Must start over again. Must be very careful!"

### Scene 7
"Then paint it gold," she continued explaining. "Mix special gold paint. Recipe from my grandmother. Two thin coats carefully. Each must dry completely. Another hour more. Then add hanging string! Finally sign my name back!"

### Scene 8
"Each star takes three hours?" Riley asked amazed. "Yes!" Mr. Mason said nodding. "She'd been carving twenty years! Skill took long time learn! Practiced for many years. Made thousands before getting perfect!"

### Scene 9
"If charge fifteen euros," woman explained clearly, "and took three hours make, I earn five per hour. Fair payment for work! My time has value. Must also cover my costs!"

### Scene 10
"But toy store stars three euros," I said confused. She smiled at me. "Look closely at both. Which made better quality? Which truly unique piece? Compare them carefully now."

### Scene 11
Toy store star made by machine. Thousands looked exactly same. Paint was uneven in spots. Fine but very ordinary. Her star was perfect! Every line was careful. Gold paint glowed bright! Each one was different!

### Scene 12
"People value different things," woman said wisely. "Some want cheap and fast. Some want handmade with care. Some support local artists. All choices are fine! But handmade takes real time. Takes special skills too!"

### Scene 13
"So handmade costs more," Ellis said understanding. "Because takes more time! Because maker has skills! Skills took years learn!" "Exactly right!" Mr. Mason said proudly.

### Scene 14
"Woman explained other costs," Mr. Mason said. "Wood costs money buy. Tools wear out over time. Must replace them often. Market booth costs rent. Must pay every day. Must charge enough cover everything! Must run good business!"

### Scene 15
"I bought the star," Mr. Mason said happily. "Fifteen euros felt fair. I knew the story now. Every time I look, remember her skill! Remember her care. Remember the beautiful market!"

### Scene 16
"For our market," Benny asked thinking, "if Riley makes crafts by hand, can charge more? Because time and skill?" "Yes!" Mr. Mason said smiling. "She should charge fairly!"

### Scene 17
"But must explain value," Mr. Mason said seriously. "Tell people it's handmade. Tell how long took. Help them understand why. Why costs more than stores! Most people appreciate quality work!"

### Scene 18
"German woman taught me else," Mr. Mason said. "'Every handmade item carries maker's heart inside.' Those her exact words. Store-bought is fine too. Nothing wrong with that. But handmade is special!"

### Scene 19
Riley looked at her hands. Turned them over slowly. "So when make crafts, think about time spent. Skills I have. Materials I use. Then set fair price! Not too low. Not too high either!"

### Scene 20
"Perfect!" Mr. Mason said proudly. Held star up high. Gold caught the light. "This reminds me always. Handmade deserves fair prices. Unique and special. Takes real time. Carries maker's care!" Friends all understood clearly!
