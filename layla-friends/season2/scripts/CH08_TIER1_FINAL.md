## THE MEDITERRANEAN FISHING VILLAGE

### Scene 1
Mr. Mason pulled a tiny fishing net from his trunk. "This is from Greece," he said. "A fishing village taught me about timing!"

### Scene 2
"The village was small and beautiful," Mr. Mason began. "White houses. Blue doors. Every morning fishermen went out to sea. They came back at eight with fish."

### Scene 3
"I met fisherman Dimitri," Mr. Mason said. "He had a bright blue boat. Fished forty years in those waters. One day he asked me to help sell."

### Scene 4
"We spread fish on ice at the dock," Mr. Mason said. "Beautiful silver fish shining in the sun! Dimitri set up his table. 'How much?' I asked him."

### Scene 5
"Ten euros per kilogram," Dimitri told young Mr. Mason. "Fresh fish at eight morning costs this much." Customers came early to buy. They bought very fast. The fish looked perfect!

### Scene 6
By ten o'clock, half the fish sold. Customers stopped coming to the stall. "Morning market done," Dimitri said. "Housewives home cooking." The dock grew quiet.

### Scene 7
"Does he drop the price then?" Ellis asked Mr. Mason. "Yes!" Mr. Mason said with a nod. "At eleven, Dimitri changed his sign. Eight euros now. The fish still very fresh!"

### Scene 8
"Why drop the price?" Layla asked with a confused look on her face. "Fish are perishable," Mr. Mason explained to all the friends. "That means they go bad fast. Can't keep till tomorrow. They would spoil!"

### Scene 9
At noon, the price dropped again to six euros per kilogram now. "Fish still good," Dimitri said to people walking by the stall. "But must sell today. Tomorrow they worth nothing. No one will buy them!"

### Scene 10
By two in the afternoon, fewer customers came to buy fish. Dimitri had ten kilograms left on the ice still. "Five euros!" he called out to people passing by. Some morning customers came back. Prices kept dropping all day!

### Scene 11
"I asked if he was angry," Mr. Mason said to the friends. "Half the morning price now! But Dimitri smiled at me. 'Better five euros than zero tomorrow,' he said. 'Tomorrow these fish garbage!'"

### Scene 12
"So sell fast?" Riley asked to understand the lesson better now. "Yes!" Mr. Mason said to Riley with energy in his voice. "Clock was his enemy. Every hour, fish worth less money. Time running out for him!"

### Scene 13
"At four, five kilograms left," Mr. Mason continued telling his story. "Three euros now for each kilogram of fish. Old woman came to buy. She waited all day. 'I knew price would drop!' she said."

### Scene 14
"That's not fair," Benny said with an upset look. "She should pay same as everyone this morning!" "I thought so too," Mr. Mason said to Benny. "But Dimitri taught me something about business then."

### Scene 15
"She's smart about business," Dimitri told young Mr. Mason that day. "She knows fish perishable and go bad fast. She knows I must sell today. She uses this to save money. Good business thinking!"

### Scene 16
"By five, two kilograms left," Mr. Mason said to the friends. "One euro now for each kilogram of fish left. No one came to buy any more. He gave them to neighbor free. Better eat than waste!"

### Scene 17
"So for our market," Ellis said while thinking hard, "we need know what's perishable. Like food and flowers. They need different prices than toys or books!"

### Scene 18
"Right!" Mr. Mason said with pride in his voice for Ellis. "Compare fish to a boat. Boat doesn't spoil ever. Can sell next week. Next month. Can wait for right price!"

### Scene 19
"Lemonade perishable too," Layla said with understanding now in her eyes. "If don't sell same day, goes bad fast. Might need drop prices later in the day. Just like Dimitri did!"

### Scene 20
Mr. Mason held the fishing net up in the light. "Some things trapped by time," he said to all the friends. "Must sell fast before they spoil. Be ready to change prices!" Friends learned timing matters.
