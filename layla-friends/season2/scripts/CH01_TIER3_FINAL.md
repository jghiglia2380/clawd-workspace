# CHAPTER 1: THE SWAP MEET - TIER 3 (3RD GRADE)

**Reading Levels:** M-P
**Target Narration Speed:** 130 WPM
**Target Word Count:** 1,300 words
**Episode Duration:** ~10 minutes
**Character Focus:** All four friends
**FL Integration:** Fair trade, value varies
**Setting:** Community center

---

## THE SWAP MEET

### Scene 1
Layla walked through the neighborhood on a sunny Saturday morning. The warm air felt fresh and pleasant on her face. She noticed something colorful posted on a telephone pole ahead of her. As she got closer, she could see it clearly and read the words. It was a hand-painted sign with bright, cheerful letters in many colors. She stopped to read it with growing interest and curiosity.

### Scene 2
"Community Swap Meet — Today!" the sign announced with bright enthusiasm. Below it said in smaller letters: "Bring what you don't need. Find what you do!" Layla read the details carefully and thoughtfully. Ten o'clock at the community center, just two blocks away from here. Her eyes lit up with genuine excitement about the idea. "That sounds like our Trading Post!" she said aloud to herself. "But way bigger and better than ours!"

### Scene 3
Layla ran to Riley's house as fast as she could and knocked loudly. Riley answered quickly, still wearing her comfortable pajamas. "You have to see this right now!" Layla said breathlessly. She told Riley all about the swap meet happening today. Riley's face brightened immediately with interest. "A swap meet?" Riley said with building excitement. "We should bring our old stuff!" She grabbed her shoes.

### Scene 4
The friends hurried to the park to find the others. Ellis and Benny were already there, tossing a ball back and forth. "Guess what's happening at the community center today?" Layla called out enthusiastically. She explained the whole swap meet concept to them. Ellis looked very interested and curious. "What kind of stuff should we bring?" he asked. Everyone started planning immediately.

### Scene 5
Layla searched her room carefully for good trading items. She opened her closet wide and looked through her toy box. She found something perfect — a puzzle she had completed many times before. The pieces were all there, carefully organized in the box. But she knew every corner and pattern by heart. "Maybe someone else would really enjoy this," she decided wisely.

### Scene 6
Riley looked through her art supplies at home very carefully. She found colored pencils that had gotten short from use but still worked. She also discovered stickers she didn't want anymore in a drawer. "These pencils still work perfectly," she reasoned aloud to herself. "Someone might really want them for their own projects." She placed them thoughtfully in a small cloth bag.

### Scene 7
Ellis organized his room carefully before selecting items to bring. He was very careful and thoughtful about choosing what to offer. He picked a mystery book he had read twice already. He also found a toy car he had outgrown recently. "I really loved this book," he thought quietly to himself. "Time for someone else to discover it and enjoy the story."

### Scene 8
Benny dug through his belongings with great enthusiasm and energy. He looked under his bed and deep in his closet. He found a bright bouncy ball and a toy drum. He hadn't played with either one in ages and months. "I completely forgot I had these!" he said cheerfully. They were in good condition. Someone else could enjoy them now.

### Scene 9
The friends met on the sidewalk at ten o'clock sharp. Each one carried their carefully selected swap items. They walked together toward the community center down the street. The morning sun warmed their backs pleasantly. They chatted excitedly about what they might find there. "I hope someone has art books," Riley said hopefully. The center appeared ahead.

### Scene 10
Tables filled the community center's main room from wall to wall completely. Neighbors arranged boxes of items on every available surface. Mrs. Patterson stood near the entrance with a clipboard and pen. "Welcome to the swap meet!" she called out warmly. The room buzzed with activity and friendly conversation. The friends looked around with wide, amazed eyes.

### Scene 11
Mrs. Patterson walked over to greet them with a warm smile. "The rule is simple and easy," she explained clearly to them. "Make fair trades with other people. If both people are happy with the exchange, it works!" She gestured toward an empty table section nearby. "You can set up your items over there." The friends thanked her and headed over.

### Scene 12
They arranged their items neatly on the table in an organized way. The puzzle sat next to the colored pencils and stickers. The mystery book stood beside the toy car carefully. The bouncy ball and drum completed their display nicely. Ellis organized everything by type to make it look good. Layla was already looking at other tables. "Let's see what everyone brought!" she said.

### Scene 13
The friends spread out to browse the tables around the room with interest. One displayed old toys in good condition and clean shape. Another had picture books, kitchen items, and decorations carefully arranged by type. One table had board games with all pieces included in the boxes. Another offered stuffed animals and dolls of many different sizes. The amazing variety surprised them completely. People of all ages wandered between tables, examining items very carefully and thoughtfully.

### Scene 14
Riley spotted something special at a craft table in the corner. An elderly woman sat behind a set of watercolor paints. The paints were used but had plenty of color remaining. Riley's eyes sparkled with interest and excitement. "Are you interested in trading?" the woman asked kindly. Riley nodded eagerly, her heart beating faster with anticipation.

### Scene 15
"What do you have to offer?" the woman asked with interest. Riley showed her the worn pencils and stickers carefully. The woman's face brightened with a warm smile. "My grandchildren love to color and create," she said happily. "These are perfect for their small hands." They exchanged items right there. Riley couldn't believe it — real watercolors!

### Scene 16
The friends gathered back at their table soon after browsing. Riley showed her watercolors proudly to the whole group. Ellis had traded his book for a magnifying glass already. Benny watched a young boy walk away happily, banging the drum. "My pencils were perfect for her grandkids," Riley explained proudly. "One person's old thing is another person's treasure."

### Scene 17
Benny smiled as he watched the boy with the drum play. The boy seemed so happy and excited with his new toy. "I forgot that drum was in my room," he said. "But listen to how happy he is now!" The drumming echoed across the room loudly. Something Benny had ignored brought someone else joy. That felt wonderful and satisfying.

### Scene 18
A young boy and his parent approached their table with interest. The boy's eyes grew wide when he spotted the colorful puzzle. "I've never seen this before!" he said excitedly to his parent. Layla smiled warmly at him. She had solved that puzzle countless times already. But for him, it was completely new and exciting. She agreed to trade for his kaleidoscope happily.

### Scene 19
The swap meet wound down as afternoon arrived at the center. The friends sat together on benches outside in the shade. Golden sunlight warmed them as they examined their treasures carefully. "We didn't spend any money today," Layla realized suddenly. "But everyone got something they wanted." Ellis nodded thoughtfully. "Value isn't about the thing itself," he said wisely.

### Scene 20
Layla's mind started working on a bigger idea for the future. "Our Trading Post was just us four friends," she said thoughtfully. "But this was the whole neighborhood together!" She looked at her friends with growing excitement in her eyes. "What if we did something even bigger? Like a real market where everyone shares what they make?" The idea took root. Something wonderful was beginning.
