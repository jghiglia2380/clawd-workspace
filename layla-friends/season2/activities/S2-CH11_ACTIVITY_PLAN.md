# CHAPTER 11: EL MERCADO DE LOS MUERTOS - ACTIVITY PLAN **Chapter Theme:** Recognizing intrinsic vs monetary value **Story Connection:** Friends honor loved ones at Day of the Dead market, learning some values transcend money **Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10) **Activity Type:** Single-session art/cultural expression project **Season Theme:** Exchange, Supply & Demand --- ## ACTIVITY TITLE **"Value Beyond Price: Memory Art Project"** An art and cultural expression project where students create artwork honoring something or someone priceless to them, exploring the concepts of intrinsic value, emotional worth, and items that cannot be measured in money. --- ## STORY CONNECTION **Explicit Scene Links:** - **Scenes 1-5:** Friends visit El Mercado de los Muertos (Day of the Dead market) - **Scenes 6-10:** Learning that some offerings are priceless—photos of loved ones, treasured memories, cultural traditions - **Scenes 11-15:** Understanding that emotional and cultural value exceeds any monetary amount - **Scenes 16-19:** Creating offerings that honor people and memories that are irreplaceable - **Scenes 19-20:** Reflection on what truly matters beyond money and possessions **Character Moments:** - Layla honors her grandmother with a photo that's "priceless"—not worth money, but worth everything to her - Carmen creates an altar with family heirlooms that carry emotional value far beyond monetary worth - Mateo realizes that relationships and memories cannot be bought or sold - Diego learns that cultural traditions and connections to ancestors have intrinsic value **FL Connections from Story:** - Intrinsic value exists beyond market value - Some things cannot be bought: love, memories, relationships, cultural heritage - Emotional worth is real and valid - What matters most often has no price tag - Cultural traditions carry value that transcends economics --- ## LEARNING OBJECTIVES ### Financial Literacy Objectives: 1. **Intrinsic Value:** Understand value that exists independent of price or market worth 2. **Priceless vs Worthless:** Distinguish between "can't put a price on it" and "has no value" 3. **Emotional Value:** Recognize that personal meaning creates real value 4. **Cultural Value:** Understand traditions and heritage carry worth beyond money 5. **Value Hierarchy:** Learn that not all value is economic; some things matter more than money ### Academic Objectives: 1. **Art Expression:** Create meaningful artwork expressing personal values 2. **Written Communication:** Articulate why something is priceless 3. **Critical Thinking:** Analyze different types of value 4. **Cultural Awareness:** Explore Day of the Dead traditions and meaning 5. **Personal Reflection:** Identify what matters most personally ### SEL Objectives: 1. Identify and honor what matters most to you 2. Respect others' values and treasures 3. Appreciate cultural diversity and traditions 4. Express emotions through art 5. Understand that self-worth and relationships transcend money --- ## THREE TIME OPTIONS ### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION: "Draw Something Priceless" **Best For:** K-1st grade, introduction to concept, quick activity **Materials Needed:** - Paper - Crayons, markers, or colored pencils - Pencils for writing - Simple sentence frame worksheet **Activity Steps:** 1. **Introduction (4 minutes)** - Show two items: a dollar bill and a family photo - Ask: "Which can you buy? Which is priceless?" - Explain: "Priceless means so special you could never buy it" - Share: "Today we'll draw something priceless to you" 2. **Drawing Activity (10 minutes)** - Students draw something priceless to them: * A person they love (family member, friend, teacher, pet) * A special memory (birthday party, family trip, special day) * Something they treasure (not for its cost, but for meaning) - Encourage detail and color - Circulate and ask: "Why is this priceless to you?" 3. **Writing Component (4 minutes)** - Simple sentence frame: * **K:** Dictate to teacher: "My _____ is priceless because _____" * **1st:** Copy and complete: "_____ is priceless to me." 4. **Sharing Circle (2 minutes)** - Volunteers share drawings with class - "This is priceless to me because..." - Celebrate diversity of what people treasure **Discussion Questions:** - "Can you buy love at a store?" - "Is a hug worth money? Why is it important anyway?" - "What's something you love that doesn't cost anything?" **Success Indicator:** Students can name and illustrate something priceless to them and state that it's valuable even though you can't buy it. --- ### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION: "Mixed Media Memory Art" **Best For:** 1st-2nd grade, balanced depth, cultural connection **Materials Needed:** - Card stock or construction paper - Art supplies (crayons, markers, colored pencils) - Magazines or printed images for collage - Glue sticks - Scissors - "Museum card" template (title, description, why priceless) - Optional: printed family photo if families consent **Activity Steps:** 1. **Cultural Introduction (5 minutes)** - Brief introduction to Day of the Dead tradition (age-appropriate): * "A holiday where families remember and honor loved ones" * "They create altars with photos and special items" * "These items are priceless—they can't be bought" - Show example of ofrenda (offering/altar) with priceless items - Connect: "Today you'll create art honoring something priceless to you" 2. **Art Creation (17 minutes)** - Students create mixed media artwork: * **Option A:** Draw/paint/collage a person who is priceless to them * **Option B:** Represent a priceless memory or tradition * **Option C:** Create artwork representing what matters most - Use multiple materials: drawing, magazine images, colors - Encourage creativity and personal meaning 3. **Museum Card (5 minutes)** - Students complete museum-style information card: ``` TITLE: _________________ Created by: _____________ This artwork represents: _______________ It is priceless to me because: _______________________________ _______________________________ If I could put a price on this, I would say: □ Priceless (can't be bought) □ Worth more than all the money in the world □ Worth everything to me ``` 4. **Gallery Walk & Appreciation (3 minutes)** - Display artwork on desks or walls with museum cards - Students walk through "gallery" viewing others' priceless treasures - Practice respectful appreciation: "I notice... I appreciate..." **Discussion Questions:** - "Why can't you buy the thing you drew?" - "If someone offered you a million dollars for your [family/pet/memory], would you sell? Why not?" - "How is priceless different from expensive?" **Success Indicator:** Students create meaningful artwork, articulate why their subject is priceless (beyond monetary value), and demonstrate understanding that emotional/intrinsic value is real and distinct from market value. --- ### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION: "Full Memory Art Gallery with Cultural Connection" **Best For:** 2nd-4th grade, comprehensive cultural and FL integration **Materials Needed:** - Quality art paper, card stock, or small canvases - Variety of art supplies (paints, markers, colored pencils, pastels) - Collage materials (magazines, fabric scraps, decorative paper) - Frames or decorative borders (can be hand-made) - Professional museum card templates - Display materials (easels, stands, or wall space) - Optional: printed photos (with family permission) - Cultural resources: images of Day of the Dead altars, book about tradition **Activity Steps:** 1. **Cultural & Concept Introduction (7 minutes)** - Read short book or show images about Day of the Dead tradition - Discuss key concept: "People honor loved ones with priceless offerings—photos, favorite foods, treasured items" - Introduce FL concept: **Intrinsic Value** * "Value that exists inside something, not based on money" * "Things that matter for what they ARE, not what they cost" - Examples of priceless things: * People you love (can't buy family) * Memories (can't purchase experiences after they happen) * Cultural traditions (can't buy heritage) * Feelings (love, joy, connection) - Students brainstorm: "What's priceless to you?" 2. **Art Creation (22 minutes)** - Students create artwork honoring something priceless: * **Theme A:** Person who is priceless to me (family, friend, teacher, mentor) * **Theme B:** Memory that's priceless (special day, tradition, experience) * **Theme C:** Value that matters more than money (kindness, love, honesty, family) - Use mixed media for depth: * Drawing/painting base * Collage elements * Written words incorporated * Symbolic representations - Quality encouraged: this is honoring something precious - Teacher circulates, asks guiding questions: "Why is this priceless? What does it represent?" 3. **Museum Card Creation (8 minutes)** - Students complete detailed museum card: ``` TITLE: _________________________ Artist: ________________________ Medium: ________________________ ARTIST'S STATEMENT: This artwork honors: ____________________ This is priceless to me because: ____________________________________ ____________________________________ REFLECTION ON VALUE: Market value (what you could buy it for): $_____ or ☑ Cannot be bought Intrinsic value (what it's worth to me): ____________________ Emotional value: _______________________ If someone offered me $1,000,000 for this, I would: □ Never sell—it's priceless □ Consider it, but probably say no □ Explain why you can't put a price on it: _______________ ``` 4. **Gallery Setup (3 minutes)** - Arrange classroom as art gallery - Display artwork with museum cards - Create respectful viewing environment (quiet, thoughtful) - Optional: Add battery-operated candles, flowers for Day of the Dead altar aesthetic 5. **Gallery Walk & Reflection (5 minutes)** - Students walk through gallery silently, reading museum cards - Observe what classmates treasure - Notice diversity of priceless things - Appreciation activity: Place sticky notes with positive messages **Extension Activities:** - Write paragraph: "Why Intrinsic Value Matters" - Create class definition of "priceless" - Research cultural traditions that honor intrinsic value (Day of the Dead, Memorial Day, family heirlooms) - Discuss: "Can you put a price on nature? Clean air? Friendship?" **Success Indicator:** Students create meaningful artwork honoring something priceless, articulate clear distinction between market value and intrinsic/emotional value, complete sophisticated reflection on value types, and demonstrate cultural awareness of Day of the Dead tradition's connection to intrinsic value. --- ## THREE BUDGET TIERS

These photos are priceless—not because they're expensive, but because the people in them cannot be replaced. A photo might cost 50¢ to print, but the grandmother in the photo? Irreplaceable. Infinite intrinsic value. **Activity Application:** Students create artwork honoring something with high intrinsic value (family member, treasured memory, cultural tradition). They distinguish between market value (could buy frame for $5) and intrinsic value (the subject honored is priceless). **Real-World Connections:** - Natural resources (clean air, water, biodiversity) - Human rights (freedom, dignity—cannot be bought) - Cultural heritage (languages, traditions, ancestral knowledge) - Relationships (family, friendship, love) - Health (priceless but not for sale) - Education (inherent value beyond earning potential) **Progressive Understanding:** - **K-1:** Some things are special even if they don't cost money - **2nd:** You can't buy everything; people and memories are priceless - **3rd:** Intrinsic value means something is valuable for what it is, not for its price - **4th:** Can analyze multiple value types; understand that a healthy society values more than money --- ### Secondary FL Concept: **Priceless vs Worthless** **Definition:** Clarifying that "priceless" means "so valuable you can't put a price on it" NOT "has no value" **Child-Friendly:** "Priceless means it's worth EVERYTHING, not worth nothing" **Common Misconception:** Young students sometimes confuse "no price" with "no value"—clarify that priceless = infinite value. **Activity Application:** Students explore that their artwork subject is priceless = invaluable, not valueless. --- ### Tertiary FL Concept: **Cultural Value** **Definition:** Worth that exists in traditions, heritage, and cultural practices **Child-Friendly:** "Our traditions and culture are valuable treasures that connect us to who we are" **Activity Application:** Through Day of the Dead cultural lens, students see how traditions carry value beyond economics—they preserve memory, honor ancestors, build community. **Real-World Connections:** - Indigenous knowledge systems - Languages and dialects - Family recipes and traditions - Cultural celebrations - Ancestral lands and sacred sites --- ### Quaternary FL Concept: **Emotional Value** **Definition:** Worth based on feelings, memories, and personal meaning **Child-Friendly:** "Something is valuable to you because of how it makes you feel or what it reminds you of" **Activity Application:** Students recognize their artwork honors something with high emotional value to them personally. **Real-World Connections:** - Heirlooms and keepsakes - Gifts from loved ones - Photos and mementos - First artwork, baby blankets, childhood toys (high emotional value, low market value) --- ## ASSESSMENT OPTIONS ### Formative Assessment (During Activity): **Observation Checklist:** -
