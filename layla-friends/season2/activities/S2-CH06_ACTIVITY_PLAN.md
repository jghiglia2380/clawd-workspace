## ACTIVITY TITLE **"Going, Going, Gone! Classroom Auction"**
 ---

 - **Scenes 1-4:**

 Buyers compete for best fish; prices rise based on quality and desire → Activity demonstrates bidding drives prices up - **Scenes 10-13:**
 Auction bell rings; highest bidder wins → Activity uses auction format with "sold!" moments - **Scenes 14-17:**

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES 1. **Financial Literacy:**
 Understand how auctions work and that competition drives prices up 2. **Budget Management:**
 Practice spending within limits and prioritizing purchases 3. **Economics Concepts:**
 Learn that market value is determined by what people will pay, not just what something costs 4. **Strategic Thinking:**
 Decide when to bid aggressively and when to save money 5. **Opportunity Cost:**
 Recognize that spending on one item means you can't buy another 6. **Self-Regulation:**
 Manage excitement and make thoughtful decisions under pressure ---
 

## THREE TIME OPTIONS 

### 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity) **Best for:**
 Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling **Total time:**
 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes **Activity:**
 Quick 5-Item Auction **Materials Needed:**
 - 5 desirable classroom items (stickers, pencils, bookmarks, small prizes) - Play money ($20 per student - simple denominations) - Paper for tracking spending **Steps:**
 1. **Setup (2 min):**
 Each student gets $20 in play money. Display 5 items. 2. **Auction Rules (1 min):**
 "I'll hold up an item. You raise your hand to bid. I'll say 'Going once, going twice, sold!' Highest bidder wins." 3. **Quick Auction (5 min):**
 Auction 5 items, starting each at $1. Students bid by raising hands and calling out amounts. 4. **Reflection (2 min):**
 "Who spent all their money? Who saved some? What happened to prices when everyone wanted something?" **Discussion Questions:**
 - "What item sold for the most? Why?" - "Did you spend your money wisely?" - "What happens when lots of people want the same thing?" ---
 


### 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity) **Best for:**
 Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling **Total time:**
 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes **Activity:**
 Budget-Managed Auction with Tracking **Materials Needed:**
 - 10 auction items (mix of desirable and less popular) - Play money ($50 per student in various denominations) - Budget tracking sheets - Auction paddles (numbered craft sticks) - optional - Bell or gavel for "sold!" **Steps:**
 1. **Auction Introduction (3 min):**
 - Each student gets $50 budget - Display all 10 items so students can plan - Explain bidding: "Start at $5. You can bid higher. Highest bidder wins." - Introduce budget tracking sheet: record each purchase 2. **First Half: Visible Budget (7 min):**
 - Auction 5 items - After each sale, pause: "How much money do you have left?" - Students record purchases on tracking sheet 3. **Mid-Auction Analysis (2 min):**
 "What have you noticed? What's your strategy for the remaining items?" 4. **Second Half: Strategic Bidding (6 min):**
 - Auction final 5 items (include some "surprise" items - mystery bags) - Watch for strategic behavior: saving vs. spending 5. **Post-Auction Reflection (2 min):**
 - Count: Who spent all money? Who saved some? - Discuss: Which items went for highest prices? Why? - Graph: Actual sale prices vs. teacher's guess **Discussion Questions:**
 - "What was your strategy?" - "Did you run out of money before getting something you wanted?" - "What would you do differently next time?" - "How did competition affect prices?" **Extension:**
 Students calculate "satisfaction rating" for purchases - did high-priced items make them happier? ---
 


### 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity) **Best for:**
 Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration **Total time:**
 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes **Activity:**
 Complete Auction Economics Simulation **Materials Needed:**
 - 15+ auction items (variety: highly desirable, medium, and less popular) - Play money ($100 per student in realistic denominations) - Auction paddles with student numbers - Budget worksheets with planning section - Auction catalog (list of all items with descriptions) - Bell/gavel - Poster board for price tracking - Optional: wrapped mystery items - Optional: bonus "auction bucks" for good behavior before auction **Steps:**
 1. **Pre-Auction Planning (8 min):**
 - Distribute $100 budget and auction catalog - Students review all items and plan strategy - Budget worksheet: rank items by desire, set maximum bid for each - Discuss: opportunity cost ("If you spend $40 on Item 1, what can't you buy?") - Introduce roles: some students will be bidders, others will be auctioneer's assistants (tracking prices) 2. **Auction Round 1: Learning Phase (8 min):**
 - Auction first 5 items - Teacher as auctioneer: "Do I hear $5? $10? Going once, going twice, SOLD to bidder #7!" - Assistants record winning bids on chart - After each sale, brief pause for students to update budgets 3. **Mid-Auction Economic Analysis (4 min):**
 - Chart first 5 prices - Discuss: "What patterns do you see? Which items went high? Why?" - "How many of you are running low on money?" - Introduce concepts: bidding war, market value, reserve price 4. **Auction Round 2: Mystery Items (6 min):**
 - Include 3 mystery bags/boxes (unknown contents) - Students must bid blind - Discuss: "How do you decide value when you don't know what's inside?" - Unwrap after bidding - discuss willingness to take risks 5. **Auction Round 3: Student Auctioneers (6 min):**
 - Volunteer students take over as auctioneers - Final items auctioned - Observe strategic behavior: desperation bidding, saving, bargain hunting 6. **Comprehensive Post-Auction Analysis (3 min):**
 - Create bar graph: all items and their final prices - Calculate: average price, highest price, lowest price - Discuss findings: - "Did expensive items make people happier?" - "What determined final price: actual value or how much people wanted it?" - "What's the difference between what something costs and what it's worth?" - "Who managed their budget best? How?" **Discussion Questions:**
 - "What was your bidding strategy? Did it work?" - "Did you change your strategy during the auction? Why?" - "What happens when people compete for the same item?" - "Is the highest-priced item always the best? Why or why not?" - "How does an auction determine the 'true' value of something?" - "When might auctions be better than fixed prices? When might fixed prices be better?" - "What emotions did you feel during bidding?" - "How did you decide when to stop bidding on something?" **Extension Ideas:**
 - Students sell their own items in a second auction - Research real auctions (art, antiques, livestock, online) - Calculate: if you could return items for refund, which would you return? - Write reflection: "The most important thing I learned about money and value" - Compare auction prices to "store prices" - which would you choose and why? ---

## FL INTEGRATION **Chapter 6 Financial Literacy Concepts:**
 1. **Competitive Pricing Through Auctions**
 - Core Lesson: Auctions let buyers compete; highest bid wins; prices not fixed - Activity Connection: Direct experience of bidding and winning/losing - Age-Appropriate: Concrete participation in auction dynamics 2. **Market Value vs. Cost**
 - Core Lesson: Something is worth what someone will pay, not what it cost to make/buy - Activity Connection: Items sell above or below "expected" prices based on demand - Age-Appropriate: Observable through price variations 3. **Budget Management and Scarcity**
 - Core Lesson: Limited money requires choices; can't buy everything - Activity Connection: Students must prioritize within budget - Age-Appropriate: Real decisions with consequences (run out of money) 4. **Opportunity Cost**
 - Core Lesson: Buying one thing means giving up ability to buy another - Activity Connection: Spending early means missing later items - Age-Appropriate: Experiential learning through regret/satisfaction **Non-Moralistic Approach:**
 - Activity presents auctions as neutral pricing mechanism - No shame for "overpaying" or "losing" items - Acknowledges emotional component of bidding (excitement!) - Focuses on strategy and learning, not judgment - Celebrates both spenders and savers **Connection to Season Arc:**
 - Builds on scarcity concepts from CH5 - Demonstrates another pricing mechanism (auction vs. negotiation vs. fixed price) - Introduces competitive market forces - Sets up later discussions of fair pricing and value ---

## TEACHER TIPS **Preparation:**
 - Choose auction items strategically: mix highly desirable, medium, and less popular - Test play money distribution - ensure enough small bills for incremental bidding - Practice auctioneer cadence: "Do I hear $5? $10? Going once, going twice, SOLD!" - Consider seating: can all students see items clearly? - Prepare for emotional responses (disappointment, excitement, regret) **Classroom Management:**
 - Set clear bidding rules before starting - Establish signal for bidding (raised hand, paddle, voice) - Control pace: don't rush or drag - Monitor for students excluded due to insufficient funds (have "late items" with lower starting bids) - Have plan for ties (quick re-bid or coin flip) - Volume control: auctions get loud! **Common Challenges & Solutions:**
 | Challenge | Solution | |---
---
---
--|---
---
---
-| | Bidding wars get out of control | Set maximum bid increments ($5 at a time, no jumping to $100) | | Students run out of money early | Include late-auction items with lower starting bids; discuss strategy after | | "That's not fair!" when outbid | Teach: "Auctions mean someone willing to pay more wins. That's how they work." | | Impulsive bidding without thinking | Pause between items: "Check your budget before next item" | | Students bidding beyond their budget | Monitor closely; stop bids that exceed remaining money | | Some students never win anything | Ensure some low-competition items; OR everyone wins at least one thing (design accordingly) | | Emotional meltdowns | Validate feelings; remind it's practice with play money; offer alternative (help auction) | | Timing: auction runs too long or too short | Adjust number of items; speed up/slow down based on energy | **Auctioneer Tips:**
 - Start each item at reasonable price (not $1 for everything) - Build excitement: "This is a POPULAR one!" - Use dramatic pauses: "Going once... going twice..." - Celebrate winners: "SOLD to bidder #5!" - Keep energy high but controlled **Safety & Emotional Management:**
 - Remind students: it's practice with play money, not real - Normalize disappointment: "It's okay to feel sad when you don't win" - Redirect fixation: "There are other great items coming" - Watch for students who might be triggered by scarcity/competition - Have quiet alternative role (price tracker, item holder) for overwhelmed students ---

## SAFETY NOTES **Emotional Safety:**
 - **Competition Stress:**
 Some students find competitive environments stressful - **Disappointment:**
 Not getting desired item can be upsetting - **Embarrassment:**
 Running out of money or making "mistakes" can feel bad - **Equity:**
 Ensure all students have equal starting funds (no advantage) **Solutions:**
 - Frame as learning experience with play money - Normalize all outcomes: "Some people spent all their money, some saved. Both are fine!" - Offer alternative roles for students overwhelmed by competition - Debrief emotions: "How did you feel during the auction?" **Physical Safety:**
 - **Excitement:**
 Students may stand, jump, shout in excitement - set boundaries - **Movement:**
 Retrieving won items requires organized system to avoid chaos **General:**
 - Clear expectations about behavior during auction - Respectful treatment of items (won't all get handled gently in excitement) - Plan for storage of items students win - What happens to items no one bids on? (Teacher keeps, donate, raffle) ---
