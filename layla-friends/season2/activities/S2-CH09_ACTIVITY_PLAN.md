# CHAPTER 9: LA VECINA'S GARDEN - ACTIVITY PLAN **Chapter Theme:** Planning community resources and shared investment **Story Connection:** Friends learn about gardens as shared resources requiring group investment **Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10) **Activity Type:** Single-session design challenge **Season Theme:** Exchange, Supply & Demand --- ## ACTIVITY TITLE **"Our Community Garden Blueprint"** A collaborative planning and design challenge where students learn about community resources, shared investment, and fair distribution of benefits from group projects. --- ## STORY CONNECTION **Explicit Scene Links:** - **Scenes 1-5:** La Vecina invites neighbors to create a community garden together - **Scenes 6-10:** Everyone contributes seeds, tools, time, and labor—shared investment - **Scenes 11-15:** Planning who gets which garden plots and how to share the harvest - **Scenes 16-19:** Understanding that everyone's contribution matters for the garden to succeed - **Scenes 19-20:** Reflection on community cooperation and shared returns on investment **Character Moments:** - Layla suggests a fair way to divide garden plots based on contribution - Carmen offers to share her family's extra seeds with neighbors who have less - Mateo calculates how much each family needs to contribute for supplies - Diego designs the garden layout to maximize shared space **FL Connections from Story:** - Everyone invests something (money, seeds, time, tools) - Shared resources benefit the whole community - Fair distribution based on contribution - Return on investment when harvest comes - Community cooperation reduces individual cost --- ## LEARNING OBJECTIVES ### Financial Literacy Objectives: 1. **Shared Investment:** Understand that people can pool resources for mutual benefit 2. **Return on Investment (ROI):** Learn that investments can grow and produce returns 3. **Fair Distribution:** Explore equitable ways to share costs and benefits 4. **Community Resources:** Recognize resources that serve many people 5. **Cost Sharing:** Understand how group projects reduce individual expense ### Academic Objectives: 1. **Collaborative Planning:** Work together to design shared project 2. **Spatial Reasoning:** Plan garden layout with measurements and plots 3. **Resource Allocation:** Decide how to distribute limited space 4. **Data Collection:** Track contributions and expected returns 5. **Problem-Solving:** Address challenges in fair distribution ### SEL Objectives: 1. Cooperate with peers toward shared goal 2. Consider multiple perspectives in resource distribution 3. Contribute ideas respectfully to group planning 4. Understand community interdependence 5. Practice compromise and fairness --- ## THREE TIME OPTIONS ### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION: "Garden Plot Designer" **Best For:** K-1st grade, quick introduction, individual design **Materials Needed:** - Paper with grid (or blank paper and ruler) - Crayons or colored pencils - Simple garden plot template (4-6 squares) - Picture cards of vegetables to "plant" **Activity Steps:** 1. **Introduction (3 minutes)** - Show picture of community garden - Explain: "Many families share one big garden" - Everyone helps, everyone gets vegetables 2. **Individual Design (10 minutes)** - Each student gets grid paper (4-6 plots) - Draw or place picture cards to "plant" garden - Color different plots for different families - Label with family names or numbers 3. **Gallery Walk (5 minutes)** - Post designs around room - Students walk and observe others' gardens - Notice different ways to organize plots 4. **Quick Discussion (2 minutes)** - "Why is sharing a garden helpful?" - "What if you don't have a yard? Could a community garden help?" - Connect to La Vecina's garden story **Success Indicator:** Students create a simple garden plan with at least 3 plots and can explain that sharing helps people without individual gardens. --- ### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION: "Shared Investment Planning" **Best For:** 1st-2nd grade, balanced depth, group collaboration **Materials Needed:** - Large poster paper or chart paper - Grid overlay or ruler for sections - Seed catalog pictures (cut from magazines or printed) - Play money ($20 per student) - Contribution tracking sheet - Markers, glue sticks **Activity Steps:** 1. **Setup & Scenario (5 minutes)** - Divide class into groups of 4-5 students - Scenario: "Your neighborhood wants a community garden, but gardens cost money" - Costs: * Garden plot: $40 * Seeds: $20 * Tools: $20 * Soil: $20 - Each student has $20 to contribute 2. **Group Investment Decision (8 minutes)** - Groups discuss: "Should we each make our own tiny garden, or pool our money for one big garden?" - Calculate: * Individual: $20 buys very little * Pooled: $80-100 buys full garden with all supplies - Make group decision (guided toward pooling for lesson) 3. **Garden Design (12 minutes)** - Groups draw garden on poster paper - Divide into plots (one per student) - Add paths, shared tool area, water source - Select seeds/plants from catalog - Glue pictures in each plot 4. **Sharing Plan (3 minutes)** - Decide: "If tomatoes grow in one plot, does only that person get them?" - Discuss fair sharing since everyone contributed - Record sharing agreement 5. **Presentation (2 minutes)** - Each group shows garden design - Shares one thing about their sharing plan **Discussion Questions:** - "Why did pooling money help more than individual spending?" - "How should you share the vegetables when they grow?" - "What if someone contributed seeds instead of money—should they still get vegetables?" **Success Indicator:** Groups successfully pool resources, create collaborative garden design, and establish fair sharing plan that considers all contributors. --- ### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION: "Full Community Garden Simulation" **Best For:** 2nd-4th grade, comprehensive project-based learning **Materials Needed:** - Large grid paper or poster board (one per group) - Detailed contribution tracking sheets - Play money ($30 per student in mixed bills) - Resource cards (seeds, tools, soil, water access, time/labor) - Seed catalog or printed plant images - Measuring tools (rulers, tape measures for scale) - Investment contract templates - Colored pencils, markers, scissors, glue **Activity Steps:** 1. **Scenario Introduction (5 minutes)** - Present community garden challenge: * Neighborhood has empty lot (12' x 16' = 192 sq ft) * Could become community garden if neighbors invest - Show cost breakdown: * Fence: $50 * Soil (all plots): $40 * Seeds: $30 * Shared tools: $30 * Water hookup: $50 * **Total needed: $200** - Class has combined $180 in cash (6 students × $30) - Gap: $20—someone needs to contribute non-monetary resources 2. **Investment Planning (10 minutes)** - Students receive varied resources: * 4 students: $30 cash each * 1 student: $20 cash + seed packets (worth $10) * 1 student: $10 cash + tools to share (worth $20) - Complete contribution worksheet: * Name * Cash contributed: $_____ * Non-cash contributed: _____ * Total value: $_____ - Pool all resources in center table - Calculate if goal met ($200 total) 3. **Garden Design Phase (15 minutes)** - Groups (6-8 students) design garden layout: * Use grid paper (each square = 2 sq ft) * Divide into individual plots (4-6 sq ft per person) * Include shared spaces: - Tool shed area - Compost bin - Pathways - Water access point - Assign plots fairly: * Option A: Equal sizes for all * Option B: Larger plots for larger investors * Group decides which is "fair" - Select plants for each plot from catalog - Label everything clearly 4. **Return on Investment Calculation (8 minutes)** - Use provided data for expected harvest: * Tomatoes: 20 lbs per plot * Lettuce: 10 heads per plot * Carrots: 15 bunches per plot - Students calculate: * Their investment: $______ * Their expected harvest value (at market price): $______ * ROI: (Harvest value - Investment) ÷ Investment - Example: Invested $30, harvest worth $60 → ROI = 100% (doubled money) 5. **Sharing Agreement (5 minutes)** - Create written agreement: * "We agree that vegetables will be shared by: _____" * Options: equally, by contribution, by need, combination - All group members sign - Post agreements with garden designs 6. **Gallery Walk & Reflection (2 minutes)** - View other groups' designs - Notice different solutions to fair distribution **Investment Tracking Sheet Format:** ``` COMMUNITY GARDEN INVESTMENT TRACKER Group Name: _____________________ CONTRIBUTIONS: Name Cash Seeds Tools Time TOTAL VALUE ____________ $___ $___ $___ $___ $______ ____________ $___ $___ $___ $___ $______ ____________ $___ $___ $___ $___ $______ ____________ $___ $___ $___ $___ $______ TOTAL GROUP INVESTMENT: $______ GOAL: $200 ✅ Met / ❌ Short by $____ PLOT ASSIGNMENTS: Name Plot Size (sq ft) Reason for Size _____________ ________ ______________ _____________ ________ ______________ SHARING AGREEMENT: We agree to share the harvest by: □ Equal shares for everyone □ Proportional to investment □ Based on need □ Other: _______________ Signed: _________________ _________________ _________________ _________________ _________________ _________________ ``` **Discussion Questions:** - "Is it fair to give bigger plots to bigger investors, or should everyone get equal space?" - "What if someone contributed tools instead of money—should they get less vegetables?" - "How does a community garden help people who can't afford their own garden?" - "What's the return on investment if you invest $30 and get $60 worth of vegetables?" - "Could this idea work for other community projects? What else?" **Extension Activities:** - Calculate exact ROI percentages for each student - Research real community gardens in your area - Write proposal for a school community garden - Design seasonal rotation plan (spring, summer, fall crops) - Create budget for Year 2 of the garden **Success Indicator:** Students complete investment tracking, create detailed garden design with fair plot distribution, calculate basic ROI, and articulate principles of shared investment and community resources. --- ## THREE BUDGET TIERS ### Tier 1: FREE ($0/student) **Standard Classroom Supplies Only:** - Individually, each family couldn't afford a full garden. Together, their combined $200 creates a thriving community resource that benefits everyone. **Activity Application:** Students experience pooling $20 each ($100-120 total) to afford supplies that would be impossible individually. They see that $20 alone = tiny garden, but $20 × 6 people = full professional garden. **Real-World Connections:** - Co-ops (food co-ops, housing co-ops) - Community centers funded by many families - Crowdfunding (Kickstarter for projects) - Public parks funded by taxes (everyone contributes a little) - School fundraisers (many small contributions = big result) **Progressive Understanding:** - **K-1:** Working together helps everyone - **2nd:** Pooling money lets us buy more than alone - **3rd:** Shared investment reduces individual cost and increases total benefit - **4th:** Can calculate ROI, understand cooperative economics, compare shared vs individual investment outcomes --- ### Secondary FL Concept: **Return on Investment (ROI)** **Definition:** The value you get back compared to what you put in **Child-Friendly:** "If you spend $20 and get $40 worth of vegetables, you doubled your money!" **Activity Application:** Students invest $20-30, receive harvest worth $50-60 (in market value). ROI = 100-200%. They learn investments can grow. **Real-World Connections:** - Planting seeds (invest $5, harvest $50 of vegetables) - Savings accounts (invest money, earn interest) - Education (invest time/money, earn higher income later) - Business (invest in supplies, sell for profit) **Formula (for 3rd-4th):** ROI = (Value Received - Investment) ÷ Investment × 100% Example: ($60 - $30) ÷ $30 = 100% ROI --- ### Tertiary FL Concept: **Fair Distribution** **Definition:** Equitable ways to share costs and benefits **Child-Friendly:** "Making sure everyone gets a fair amount based on what they gave" **Activity Application:** Groups must decide: - Equal plots for all (regardless of contribution)? - Proportional to investment (bigger contributors get more)? - Based on need (those with less get more)? - Combination approach? No single "right" answer—explores fairness concepts. **Real-World Connections:** - Stock ownership (more shares = more dividends) - Housing co-ops (different ownership percentages) - Group projects (equal credit vs contribution-based grading) - Tax systems (progressive vs flat) --- ### Quaternary FL Concept: **Community Resources** **Definition:** Shared resources available to all community members **Child-Friendly:** "Things many people can use together" **Examples in Activity:** - Shared tool area in garden - Water access point - Pathways everyone uses - Compost bin for all plots **Real-World Connections:** - Parks, playgrounds - Libraries - Community centers - Public pools, beaches - Roads, sidewalks --- ## ASSESSMENT OPTIONS ### Formative Assessment (During Activity): **Observation Checklist:** - [ ] Contributes ideas to group planning - [ ] Participates in resource pooling activity - [ ] Understands that sharing reduces individual cost - [ ] Engages in fair distribution discussions - [ ] Uses FL vocabulary appropriately - [ ] Completes assigned role in group work **Anecdotal Notes:** - Record reasoning about fairness - Note misconceptions about sharing - Identify students who grasp investment concepts - Document collaboration skills --- ### Summative Assessment (Post-Activity): **Tier 1 (K-1):** - **Drawing Task:** Draw a community garden with at least 3 plots labeled for different families - **Verbal Explanation:** "Why do people share gardens?" - **Concept Check:** "Does sharing help people who don't have yards?" **Tier 2 (1st-2nd):** - **Short Answer:** "Why is it helpful to pool money for a community garden?" - **Scenario:** "You have $10.
