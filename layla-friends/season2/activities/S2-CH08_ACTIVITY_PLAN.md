# CHAPTER 8: THE MORNING MARKET - ACTIVITY PLAN **Chapter Theme:** Time affects value of perishable goods **Story Connection:** Friends learn that fresh items spoil and timing matters in selling **Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10) **Activity Type:** Single-session classroom activity **Season Theme:** Exchange, Supply & Demand --- ## ACTIVITY TITLE **"Fresh or Not? The Perishable Challenge"** A sorting game and market simulation where students learn that timing affects value with perishable goods, and experience strategic decision-making under time pressure. --- ## STORY CONNECTION **Explicit Scene Links:** - **Scenes 1-5:** The morning market bustles at dawn as vendors sell fresh fish and produce - **Scenes 6-10:** Layla and friends learn that fresh items spoil by afternoon—timing is critical for vendors - **Scenes 11-15:** Understanding that value drops dramatically as perishable items age throughout the day - **Scenes 16-19:** Witnessing strategic decisions about when to sell perishables vs. holding non-perishables - **Scenes 19-20:** Reflection on time pressure in business and the concept of depreciation **Character Moments:** - Layla observes fishmongers pricing aggressively early in the morning - Mateo notices produce vendors dropping prices as the sun rises higher - Carmen learns why her tía sells bread at a discount in the afternoon - Diego discovers the difference between perishable and non-perishable inventory **FL Connections from Story:** - Fresh fish loses value hour by hour - Strategic timing: sell early vs. risk spoilage - Price adjustments based on time of day - Understanding depreciation in real-world context --- ## LEARNING OBJECTIVES ### Financial Literacy Objectives: 1. **Depreciation Concept:** Understand that perishable goods lose value over time 2. **Strategic Timing:** Learn when to sell depreciating items for maximum return 3. **Inventory Management:** Balance holding items vs. selling quickly 4. **Time Pressure:** Experience urgency in business decision-making 5. **Pricing Strategy:** Understand why prices drop for aging perishables ### Academic Objectives: 1. **Classification Skills:** Distinguish perishable from non-perishable goods 2. **Time Concepts:** Understand how time affects physical and economic states 3. **Strategic Thinking:** Make decisions weighing multiple factors 4. **Value Comparison:** Compare changing values across time periods 5. **Data Analysis:** Track and compare outcomes of different strategies ### SEL Objectives: 1. Handle disappointment when items lose value 2. Make strategic decisions under pressure 3. Understand real-world business constraints 4. Learn from failed strategies without self-blame --- ## THREE TIME OPTIONS ### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION: "Sort and Identify" **Best For:** K-1st grade, quick introduction, minimal prep time **Materials Needed:** - 10-15 picture cards showing various items (food and non-food) - Two sorting areas (circles on floor or desk sections) - Visual labels: "SPOILS FAST" and "LASTS LONG" **Activity Steps:** 1. **Introduction (3 minutes)** - Show students a banana: "Fresh today, brown tomorrow—why?" - Explain some things last, others don't - Introduce sorting challenge 2. **Sorting Activity (10 minutes)** - Students take turns selecting a card - Place in correct category: Spoils Fast or Lasts Long - Class votes if unsure - Discuss each placement 3. **Quick Game (5 minutes)** - Teacher calls out items - Students point to correct category - Speed increases for challenge 4. **Discussion (2 minutes)** - "Would you sell fish today or save for tomorrow? Why?" - "What happens if you wait to sell milk?" - Connect to morning market story **Items to Include:** - Spoils Fast: fish, milk, bread, lettuce, bananas, strawberries - Lasts Long: toys, books, clothes, pencils, rocks, jewelry **Success Indicator:** Students correctly sort 7+ out of 10 items and can explain one reason why timing matters. --- ### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION: "3-Round Trading with Price Drops" **Best For:** 1st-2nd grade, balanced depth, standard class activity **Materials Needed:** - Item cards (3 per student: 1 perishable, 2 non-perishable) - Price chart showing rounds and values - Play money ($20 per student) - Timer (visual countdown) - Trading area marked on floor **Activity Steps:** 1. **Setup & Explanation (5 minutes)** - Each student receives: * 1 perishable card (fish, bread, milk, fruit) * 2 non-perishable cards (toys, books, tools, clothes) - Explain three trading rounds - Show price chart on board 2. **Round 1: Morning Market (7 minutes)** - Timer set for 3 minutes - Prices: Perishables $10, Non-perishables $5 - Students can sell to "market buyer" (teacher/volunteer) - Buyers walk around making offers - Record what each student sells 3. **Round 2: Midday Market (7 minutes)** - Timer set for 2 minutes - Prices: Perishables DROP to $5, Non-perishables stay $5 - Students who held perishables see value decrease - More selling happens - Visual: Update price chart dramatically 4. **Round 3: Afternoon Market (7 minutes)** - Timer set for 1 minute - Prices: Perishables DROP to $1, Non-perishables stay $5 - Last chance to sell - Those who held perishables feel real impact 5. **Count & Compare (4 minutes)** - Students count final money - Graph results on board - Who made the most? What strategy did they use? - Who sold perishables early? Late? **Price Chart Example:** ``` ROUND 1 (Morning): Fish $10 | Bread $10 | Toys $5 | Books $5 ROUND 2 (Midday): Fish $5 | Bread $5 | Toys $5 | Books $5 ROUND 3 (Afternoon): Fish $1 | Bread $1 | Toys $5 | Books $5 ``` **Discussion Questions:** - "Who sold fish in Round 1? Why?" - "Who waited? How did that work out?" - "What would you do differently next time?" - "Why don't toy prices drop like fish prices?" **Success Indicator:** Students recognize that selling perishables early yields more money, and can explain why non-perishables don't lose value the same way. --- ### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION: "Full Market Simulation" **Best For:** 2nd-4th grade, comprehensive experience, project-based **Materials Needed:** - Market stalls (tables or designated areas) - Mixed inventory cards (10 items per student: 4 perishable, 6 non-perishable) - Timer with audible signals - Price tracking sheets (one per student) - Play money ($50 per student as starting cash) - "Market Buyer" badges for rotating buyers - Graph paper for results analysis **Activity Steps:** 1. **Market Setup (8 minutes)** - Students set up stalls with inventory - Receive starting cash $50 - Review price tracking sheet - Assign initial "buyers" (rotating role) - Practice using tracking sheet 2. **Period 1: Dawn Market (10 minutes)** - Timer: 5 minutes of active trading - Starting prices: * Perishables: $8 each (fish, fruit, milk, bread) * Non-perishables: $5 each (toys, books, tools, clothes, crafts, jewelry) - Buyers visit stalls, negotiate, purchase - Sellers mark sales on tracking sheet - Rotate buyer badges after 3 minutes 3. **Period 2: Mid-Morning Market (10 minutes)** - Timer: 4 minutes of active trading - Price changes announced dramatically: * Perishables: DROP to $4 each * Non-perishables: STAY $5 each - Discussion pause: "Notice anything about your fish value?" - Continue trading - Sellers see depreciation impact - Rotate buyers again 4. **Period 3: Late Morning Market (10 minutes)** - Timer: 3 minutes of active trading - Final price changes: * Perishables: DROP to $2 each * Non-perishables: STAY $5 each - Urgent selling begins - Some students stuck with near-worthless perishables - Close market 5. **Analysis & Graphing (7 minutes)** - Students calculate total revenue - Create bar graphs showing: * Starting inventory value * Final revenue * Difference (profit/loss) - Class data: graph average revenue by strategy - Identify patterns: early sellers vs. holders **Price Tracking Sheet Format:** ``` Name: ________________ Stall #: ____ INVENTORY: Perishables: [ ] Fish [ ] Fruit [ ] Milk [ ] Bread Non-perishables: [ ] Toy [ ] Book [ ] Tool [ ] Clothes [ ] Craft [ ] Jewelry PERIOD 1 (Perish=$8, Non-perish=$5): Sold: ____________ Revenue: $______ PERIOD 2 (Perish=$4, Non-perish=$5): Sold: ____________ Revenue: $______ PERIOD 3 (Perish=$2, Non-perish=$5): Sold: ____________ Revenue: $______ TOTAL REVENUE: $______ Starting inventory value: $______ Profit/Loss: $______ ``` **Discussion Questions:** - "What strategy worked best? Why?" - "Who sold all perishables in Period 1? How did that turn out?" - "Who held onto fish until Period 3? What happened?" - "Would you change your strategy if we played again?" - "Why do real markets work this way?" - "What items in real life work like our perishables?" **Extension Activities:** - Write a "Market Strategy Guide" for next year's students - Graph class data showing correlation between early perishable sales and revenue - Create visual timeline showing value depreciation - Research real perishable markets (fish markets, farmers markets) **Success Indicator:** Students can articulate the inverse relationship between time and perishable value, demonstrate strategic decision-making, and analyze their own results to identify optimal strategies. --- ## THREE BUDGET TIERS ### Tier 1: FREE ($0/student) **Standard Classroom Supplies Only:** - By afternoon, the same fish sells for much less—or can't be sold at all. The fish hasn't changed hands; it's simply gotten older. Time = value loss. **Activity Application:** Students experience this directly: Round 1 fish = $10, Round 3 fish = $1. Same fish card, different value based solely on time elapsed. **Real-World Connections:** - Bakery bread discounted at closing time - Grocery store markdowns on near-expiration items - Flower shops pricing down day-old flowers - Farmers markets at end of day **Progressive Understanding:** - **K-1:** Some things go bad; sell them fast - **2nd:** Value goes down over time for perishables - **3rd:** This is called depreciation; it's predictable and affects business decisions - **4th:** Can calculate depreciation rate, model optimal sell times, understand opportunity cost --- ### Secondary FL Concept: **Strategic Timing** **Definition:** Choosing the right moment to act for best outcome **Child-Friendly:** "Picking the perfect time to sell or buy" **Activity Application:** Students must decide: sell perishables immediately for $10, or wait hoping for better opportunity but risk price drop to $5 or $1. Introduces risk/reward analysis. **Real-World Connections:** - Selling concert tickets (early vs last-minute) - Yard sales (morning shoppers vs afternoon discounts) - Seasonal sales (winter coats in January) --- ### Tertiary FL Concept: **Inventory Management** **Definition:** Deciding what to keep vs sell **Child-Friendly:** "Choosing what to keep in your store and what to sell now" **Activity Application:** Students manage mixed inventory—must prioritize perishables while protecting non-perishable value. --- ## ASSESSMENT OPTIONS ### Formative Assessment (During Activity): **Observation Checklist:** - [ ] Correctly sorts perishable vs non-perishable items - [ ] Recognizes price drops across rounds - [ ] Makes strategic decisions (sells perishables early) - [ ] Uses FL vocabulary appropriately (spoil, value, depreciate) - [ ] Tracks money/revenue accurately - [ ] Participates in trading appropriately **Anecdotal Notes:** - Record student reasoning during decision points - Note misconceptions for addressing later - Identify students who grasp concept quickly for extensions --- ### Summative Assessment (Post-Activity): **Tier 1 (K-1):** - **Sorting Task:** Give 8 new items; sort with 7/8 accuracy - **Verbal Explanation:** "Why sell fish today, not tomorrow?" - **Drawing:** Illustrate fresh fish vs old fish, label which is worth more **Tier 2 (1st-2nd):** - **Short Answer:** "Explain why the fish price dropped from $10 to $1" - **Scenario:** "You have milk and a toy.
