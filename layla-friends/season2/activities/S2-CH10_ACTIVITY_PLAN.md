# CHAPTER 10: THE CHRISTMAS MARKET - ACTIVITY PLAN **Chapter Theme:** Understanding value of handmade items **Story Connection:** Friends create crafts for market and learn about labor cost and craftsmanship **Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10) **Activity Type:** Single-session craft workshop **Season Theme:** Exchange, Supply & Demand --- ## ACTIVITY TITLE **"Handmade Holiday Workshop"** A craft production and value comparison activity where students create handmade items, track their time and effort, and compare handmade value to mass-produced goods—learning about labor cost, craftsmanship, and sentimental value. --- ## STORY CONNECTION **Explicit Scene Links:** - **Scenes 1-5:** Friends prepare handmade crafts to sell at the Christmas market - **Scenes 6-10:** Discovering that handmade items take time, skill, and effort to create - **Scenes 11-15:** Learning why handmade items often cost more than store-bought versions - **Scenes 16-19:** Understanding that handmade gifts carry special sentimental value beyond price - **Scenes 19-20:** Reflection on the worth of time, effort, and personal touch in creations **Character Moments:** - Layla spends hours making beaded ornaments, realizes time = value - Carmen creates personalized cards, understands sentimental value exceeds material cost - Mateo calculates cost of materials plus time to determine fair price - Diego experiments with different techniques, learns quality craftsmanship takes practice **FL Connections from Story:** - Labor is a cost (time spent making = money in business) - Handmade items often cost more due to time investment - Sentimental value can exceed market value - Quality craftsmanship justifies higher prices - Mass production reduces cost but removes personal touch --- ## LEARNING OBJECTIVES ### Financial Literacy Objectives: 1. **Labor as Cost:** Understand that time spent making something has monetary value 2. **Handmade vs Mass-Produced:** Compare value and pricing of handmade vs factory-made items 3. **Pricing for Labor:** Learn to price products including both materials and time 4. **Sentimental Value:** Recognize non-monetary value in personal, handmade creations 5. **Quality Premium:** Understand why quality craftsmanship commands higher prices ### Academic Objectives: 1. **Time Measurement:** Track time spent on tasks accurately 2. **Cost Calculation:** Add materials cost + labor cost 3. **Comparison Skills:** Analyze differences between handmade and mass-produced 4. **Fine Motor Skills:** Practice crafting with various materials 5. **Quality Assessment:** Evaluate craftsmanship and quality ### SEL Objectives: 1. Take pride in personal creation and effort 2. Appreciate others' work and time investment 3. Understand value beyond money (sentimental, personal) 4. Develop patience and attention to detail 5. Celebrate unique, individual creations vs uniformity --- ## THREE TIME OPTIONS ### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION: "Quick Craft & Time Talk" **Best For:** K-1st grade, introduction to concept, minimal materials **Materials Needed:** - Paper or cardstock - Scissors (safety scissors for K-1) - Crayons, markers, or colored pencils - String or ribbon - Simple timer or clock **Activity Steps:** 1. **Introduction (3 minutes)** - Show two items: handmade card vs store-bought card - Ask: "Which took more time to make?" - Explain: "Today we'll make something and see how long it takes" 2. **Quick Craft: Paper Ornament (12 minutes)** - Each student makes a simple paper ornament or card - Teacher starts timer when they begin - Students: * Cut paper into shape (circle, star, tree) * Decorate with drawings, coloring * Add string for hanging or write message inside - Call out time updates: "5 minutes so far!" 3. **Time Discussion (3 minutes)** - Stop timer, announce total time: "15 minutes!" - Ask: "How long did it take to make your ornament?" - Discuss: "If you sold this, should you charge only for paper, or also for your time?" 4. **Show & Appreciate (2 minutes)** - Students show their creations to a partner - "Your time made this special!" **Discussion Questions:** - "How long did you work on this?" - "Would a machine make it faster?" - "Is your handmade ornament special? Why?" **Success Indicator:** Students understand that making things takes time, and that time has value. Can articulate that handmade items take longer than machine-made ones. --- ### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION: "Craft, Track, & Compare" **Best For:** 1st-2nd grade, balanced activity with time tracking **Materials Needed:** - Craft materials (paper, felt scraps, beads, pipe cleaners, glue) - Time tracking worksheet (one per student) - Timer or clock visible to all - Sample store-bought item to compare (e.g., beaded bracelet from dollar store) - Pencils for tracking **Activity Steps:** 1. **Setup & Introduction (5 minutes)** - Show handmade bracelet and store-bought bracelet side-by-side - Discuss differences: "Which is unique? Which looks exactly like others?" - Introduce concept: "Today you'll make something AND track how long it takes" - Explain time tracking sheet 2. **Craft Creation (15 minutes)** - Students choose a simple craft project: * Beaded bracelet or necklace * Felt bookmark * Decorated card or frame - As they work, they track time on worksheet: * Start time: _____ * End time: _____ * Total minutes: _____ - Teacher calls out 5-minute intervals 3. **Cost Calculation (5 minutes)** - Materials cost discussion: * "Our beads cost $3 for 100. You used 20 beads = about 60¢" * "If your time is worth $6/hour and you worked 15 minutes, that's $1.50 of labor" - Students add: Materials + Labor = Total Cost - Example: 60¢ + $1.50 = $2.10 4. **Comparison Activity (3 minutes)** - Show store-bought bracelet: "This costs $1 at the store" - Handmade bracelet costs $2.10 to make - Discuss: "Why might handmade cost more?" * More time * Unique design * Personal touch * Quality materials - "But does your handmade bracelet have special value to you?" 5. **Sharing & Reflection (2 minutes)** - Partner share: "Show your creation and say what makes it special" - Celebrate uniqueness of each handmade item **Time Tracking Worksheet Format:** ``` NAME: ________________ DATE: __________ MY HANDMADE CREATION: _________________ START TIME: _____ : _____ END TIME: _____ : _____ TOTAL TIME: _____ minutes MATERIALS I USED: □ Paper □ Beads □ String □ Felt □ Other: _____ COST CALCULATION: Materials cost: $ _____ (about 60¢) Labor cost: $ _____ (time × $6/hour) TOTAL COST: $ _____ Store-bought similar item costs: $ _____ My handmade item is special because: _________________________________ ``` **Success Indicator:** Students successfully track their crafting time, understand basic cost calculation (materials + labor), and can explain why handmade items might cost more than mass-produced versions. --- ### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION: "Full Holiday Craft Workshop" **Best For:** 2nd-4th grade, comprehensive experience with multiple crafts **Materials Needed:** - Multiple craft stations (3-4 different crafts) - Detailed time & cost tracking sheets - Sample store-bought comparison items - Timer at each station - Price tags and markers - Display area for finished products - Calculators for older students **Craft Station Options:** 1. **Beaded Jewelry Station:** Bracelets, necklaces, keychains 2. **Card-Making Station:** Holiday cards with decorations 3. **Ornament Station:** Felt or paper ornaments 4. **Gift Tag Station:** Personalized gift tags **Activity Steps:** 1. **Workshop Introduction (5 minutes)** - Introduce scenario: "You're craft vendors at a holiday market!" - Explain stations and tracking system - Show sample finished products - Demonstrate time tracking: start timer when you begin, stop when done 2. **Craft Production - Round 1 (15 minutes)** - Students choose one station - Create one item, carefully tracking: * Materials used * Time spent * Quality level (self-assessment) - Aim for quality over speed - Teacher circulates, provides support 3. **Cost Analysis (8 minutes)** - Students calculate total cost of their creation: * **Materials cost:** Use provided price list - Beads: 3¢ each - Felt squares: 25¢ each - Ribbon: 10¢ per foot - Paper/cardstock: 5¢ per sheet - Glue: 5¢ * **Labor cost:** Time spent × $6/hour (or $0.10/minute) - Example: 10 minutes × $0.10 = $1.00 labor cost * **Total cost:** Materials + Labor - Record on worksheet 4. **Pricing Strategy (5 minutes)** - Introduce pricing concepts: * **Cost:** What it cost you to make * **Price:** What you charge customers (must be higher than cost!) * **Profit:** Price - Cost - Students decide on selling price: * Must cover costs * Add profit margin * Consider quality and uniqueness - Create price tag for their item 5. **Comparison & Discussion (8 minutes)** - Compare handmade items to store-bought equivalents: * Handmade beaded bracelet: $3.50 (took 12 minutes) * Store-bought bracelet: $1.00 (factory made in 30 seconds) - Discuss questions: * "Why does the handmade one cost more?" * "Which would you rather receive as a gift? Why?" * "What's the difference in quality?" * "Does the handmade one have special value beyond the price?" 6. **Gallery Display & Reflection (4 minutes)** - Set up mini "market display" of all creations - Students walk through, observe variety and uniqueness - Reflection: "Every item is one-of-a-kind because you made it!" **Detailed Tracking Sheet Format:** ``` HANDMADE HOLIDAY WORKSHOP - COST & PRICING SHEET Maker's Name: __________________ Date: __________ Item Created: ___________________ Station: ______ TIME TRACKING: Start Time: ____ : ____ End Time: ____ : ____ Total Minutes: ______ MATERIALS USED & COST: Item Quantity Unit Cost Total _________ ____ _____ $_____ _________ ____ _____ $_____ _________ ____ _____ $_____ MATERIALS TOTAL: $_____ LABOR COST: Time worked: _____ minutes Rate: $6/hour = $0.10 per minute Labor cost: _____ minutes × $0.10 = $_____ TOTAL COST (Materials + Labor): $_____ PRICING DECISION: My selling price: $_____ Profit per item: $_____ (Price - Cost) COMPARISON: Similar store-bought item costs: $_____ Difference: $_____ Why my handmade item is worth the price: □ Unique, one-of-a-kind design □ Higher quality materials □ Personal touch & care □ Time and skill invested □ Sentimental value for recipient □ Other: _________________ REFLECTION: The hardest part of making this was: _________________ I'm proud of this because: _________________ If I made 10 of these, it would take _____ hours! ``` **Extension Activities:** - Calculate bulk pricing: "If you made 10 bracelets, would materials cost less per item?" - Time efficiency: "Make a second bracelet—does it take less time with practice?" - Market research: "Survey classmates—what would they pay for your item?" - Quality analysis: "How does your craftsmanship compare to factory quality?" **Success Indicator:** Students successfully track time and materials, calculate total cost including labor, set appropriate selling price above cost, and articulate multiple dimensions of value in handmade items (quality, uniqueness, sentimental value, time invested). --- ## THREE BUDGET TIERS ### Tier 1: FREE ($0/student)

If you spend an hour making something, that hour should count as a cost." **Story Example:** In THE CHRISTMAS MARKET, Layla spends 2 hours making beaded ornaments. She realizes that even though beads only cost $2, her 2 hours of work are valuable. If her time is worth $6/hour, that's $12 of labor + $2 materials = $14 total cost. **Activity Application:** Students track their crafting time and calculate labor cost (time × hourly rate). They see that a bracelet with 50¢ of beads but 20 minutes of work actually costs $2.50 to make. **Real-World Connections:** - Hourly wages (babysitting, retail jobs) - Freelance work (charge for time + materials) - Service businesses (haircuts, tutoring = paying for time/skill) - Etsy sellers pricing their handmade goods **Progressive Understanding:** - **K-1:** Making things takes time; time is valuable - **2nd:** Count minutes worked; understand time as a cost - **3rd:** Calculate labor cost: minutes × rate - **4th:** Understand opportunity cost of time; compare time vs money tradeoffs --- ### Secondary FL Concept: **Handmade Premium** **Definition:** Handmade items often cost more due to time, quality, uniqueness, and personal touch **Child-Friendly:** "Handmade things cost more because someone spent their time making it special just for you" **Activity Application:** Compare student-made bracelet ($3 cost) to dollar-store bracelet ($1). Discuss why handmade costs more: time invested, unique design, quality materials, one-of-a-kind. **Real-World Connections:** - Etsy vs Amazon prices - Local bakery vs grocery store cookies - Custom-made clothes vs fast fashion - Artist-made jewelry vs mall jewelry --- ### Tertiary FL Concept: **Sentimental Value** **Definition:** Personal meaning and emotional connection that exceeds monetary worth **Child-Friendly:** "Something can be priceless to you because of who made it or what it reminds you of, even if it didn't cost much money" **Activity Application:** Students recognize their handmade card to grandma is "priceless" even though materials cost 25¢—sentimental value exceeds market value. **Real-World Connections:** - Family heirlooms - Handmade gifts from loved ones - Children's artwork to parents - Wedding rings (value beyond gold cost) --- ### Quaternary FL Concept: **Quality & Craftsmanship** **Definition:** Skill and care in making something affects value and justifies higher price **Child-Friendly:** "When something is made really well with care and skill, it's worth more" **Activity Application:** Compare quickly-made, sloppy bracelet to carefully-crafted one. Discuss how quality affects value. **Real-World Connections:** - Handmade furniture vs IKEA - Artisan cheese vs processed cheese - Custom-tailored suit vs off-the-rack - Fine art vs poster print --- ## ASSESSMENT OPTIONS ### Formative Assessment (During Activity): **Observation Checklist:** -
