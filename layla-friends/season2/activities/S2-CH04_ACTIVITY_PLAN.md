# CHAPTER 4: MR. MASON'S TRUNK - ACTIVITY PLAN **Chapter Theme:** Cultural heritage and global exchange **Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where Mr. Mason opens an old trunk full of treasures from his travels to world markets, launching the friends into memories of global exchange **Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10) **Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency) --- ## ACTIVITY TITLE **"My Family's Travel Trunk"** --- ## STORY CONNECTION **Explicit Scene Links:** - **Scenes 1-4:** Mr. Mason opens mysterious old trunk; friends see items from around the world → Activity begins with creating personal cultural trunk - **Scenes 5-9:** Each item has a story from a different market or place → Activity includes storytelling about family heritage items - **Scenes 10-13:** Mr. Mason explains how goods traveled through trade routes → Activity maps where family items originate - **Scenes 14-17:** Friends realize their families have traveled/traded items too → Activity connects personal heritage to global exchange - **Scenes 18-20:** Creating memories through objects that carry meaning beyond price → Activity emphasizes cultural value vs. monetary value --- ## LEARNING OBJECTIVES 1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand that goods travel from around the world through trade 2. **Cultural Awareness:** Appreciate diverse heritages and traditions in the classroom 3. **Value Recognition:** Distinguish between cultural/personal value and monetary value 4. **Geography:** Learn about global connections through trade and family heritage 5. **Storytelling:** Practice sharing family stories and traditions 6. **Empathy:** Appreciate that everyone has unique cultural background and treasures --- ## THREE TIME OPTIONS ### 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity) **Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling **Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes **Activity:** Draw Your Family Treasures **Materials Needed:** - Paper - Crayons, markers - Pencils **Steps:** 1. **Introduction (2 min):** "Mr. Mason had a trunk with special items from his travels. What special items does your family have?" 2. **Think & List (2 min):** Think of 3 things that represent your family or culture (could be foods, celebrations, places, traditions, items) 3. **Draw (5 min):** Draw the 3 items on paper. Simple pictures are fine. 4. **Partner Share (1 min):** Turn to neighbor, show drawings, explain one item: "This is ___. It's special because ___." **Discussion Questions:** - "Where does this item come from?" - "Why is it special to your family?" - "Did you have to buy it or is it something your family has always had?" --- ### 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity) **Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling **Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes **Activity:** Paper Bag Travel Trunk **Materials Needed:** - Paper bag for each student (lunch size or larger) - Markers, crayons, colored pencils - Index cards or paper for item cards - Scissors - Optional: decorative materials (stickers, fabric scraps) **Steps:** 1. **Trunk Creation (6 min):** Decorate paper bag as your "travel trunk." Draw or write family name, designs representing heritage 2. **Item Cards (8 min):** - Create 3-5 item cards (one per card) - Each card includes: drawing of item, name of item, where it comes from, why it matters to your family - Items can be real objects, foods, traditions, places 3. **World Map Connection (3 min):** On classroom world map, students mark (with sticky dots or pins) where their items originate 4. **Small Group Presentations (3 min):** Groups of 3-4. Each student shares 1-2 items from their trunk. **Discussion Questions:** - "How many different countries/places are represented in our class?" - "What surprised you about someone else's trunk?" - "How are our trunks similar? How are they different?" - "How do items from other places get to us?" (Trade, travel, immigration) **Extension:** Display trunks and map showing global connections --- ### 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity) **Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration **Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes **Activity:** Complete Cultural Heritage Trunk with Presentation **Materials Needed:** - Shoebox or small box for each student (can be decorated box or plain) - Decorative materials (markers, fabric scraps, stickers, ribbon, paper) - Item cards with detailed template - String and pushpins for world map connections - Large world map for classroom - Optional: actual items from home (if students bring them) - Optional: photos of family items/places **Steps:** 1. **Trunk Design & Construction (10 min):** - Decorate box as family "travel trunk" - Include family name, cultural symbols, colors representing heritage - Can include flag elements, traditional patterns, meaningful decorations 2. **Item Documentation (12 min):** - Create 3-5 detailed item cards including: - Name of item - Country/place of origin - How it came to family (bought on trip, inherited, traditional, homemade) - Why it's valuable (personal story, tradition, memory) - Estimated store price vs. personal value ("Priceless" is an option!) - Draw or describe each item 3. **World Map Display (5 min):** - Each student places item card on world map at origin point - Use string to connect student's location to item origins - Visual web shows global connections 4. **Presentation Round (8 min):** - Students present 1-2 items to class - Explain: what it is, where it's from, why it matters - Class asks questions: "How did your family get this?" "What's your favorite thing about it?" **Discussion Questions:** - "Look at our class map. How many countries are represented?" - "What's the farthest place any item came from?" - "Did anyone have items from the same place? What were they?" - "How did items travel from those places to here?" (Trade routes, immigration, online shopping, family trips) - "Is something worth more because of its story or its price?" - "What's the difference between buying a souvenir and inheriting a family treasure?" - "How does trade connect people around the world?" **Extension Ideas:** - Invite family members to visit and share their heritage stories - Research trade routes connecting represented countries - Create class "Global Heritage Museum" - Write family interview: "Tell me about something important from your heritage" - Map how one item (like chocolate or spices) travels from origin to local store --- ## THREE BUDGET TIERS ### Tier 1: FREE ($0/student) **Standard Classroom Supplies Only:** - Paper (any type available in classroom) - Pencils, crayons, or markers (classroom supply) - Scissors (classroom supply) - Glue sticks or tape (classroom supply) - Rulers (if applicable, classroom supply) **Total Cost:** $0 per student --- ### Tier 2: LOW COST (Under $1/student) **Bulk Additions (split across class):** - Shoebox (ask families to donate) or small craft boxes - Basic craft supplies for decoration - Printed item card templates - Large world map poster - Pushpins and string for connections **Total Cost:** Under $1 per student (bulk items shared across class) --- ### Tier 3: PREMIUM ($1-3/student max)

**Additional Materials:**
- Decorative wooden or sturdy boxes
- Laminated world map (large, classroom size)
- Photo printing for family items
- Cultural decorative materials (fabric squares, beads, stamps)
- Display stands for trunk exhibition set

**Total Cost:** $1-3 per student max

## MATERIALS SUMMARY **Required (All Tiers):** - Container for "trunk" (bag or box) - Decorating materials - Paper for item cards - World map (printed or poster) - Writing/drawing implements **Note:** This activity relies heavily on students' personal contributions (ideas, stories, heritage) rather than purchased materials --- ## DISCUSSION QUESTIONS **Pre-Activity (Connect to Story):** - "What did Mr. Mason keep in his trunk?" - "Why were those items special even though they were old?" - "What did each item remind him of?" - "How did Mr. Mason get items from so many places?" **During Activity (K-1):** - "What's this a picture of?" - "Where does it come from?" - "Why do you like it?" **During Activity (2-4):** - "Where was your family born? Where do they live now?" - "What foods, celebrations, or traditions are special to your family?" - "How did these items get from there to here?" - "What makes something valuable even if it doesn't cost money?" **Post-Activity (Reflection):** - "What did you learn about your classmates' families?" - "How many different countries/places are represented in our class?" - "What surprised you about someone's trunk?" - "How are we all connected through trade and travel?" **Financial Literacy Deepening (3-4):** - "How do items from other countries get to stores here?" (Trade routes, shipping, import/export) - "Is a family heirloom more valuable than something you buy new? Why?" - "What's the difference between cultural value and money value?" - "How does trade connect people around the world?" - "If you couldn't buy something at a store, how else might you get it?" (Make it, trade for it, inherit it, receive as gift) - "Why do people travel to buy things from specific places?" --- ## DIFFERENTIATION NOTES **By Reading Level (Tier):** **Tier 1 (Levels A-D / K-1):** - Draw items instead of writing descriptions - Teacher scribes family stories - Simple identification: "This is ___. It's from ___." - Focus on sharing and listening - Vocabulary: family, special, from, country **Tier 2 (Levels E-J / 1st-2nd):** - Combination of drawing and simple sentences - Sentence frames: "This is ___. It comes from ___. It's special because ___." - Basic map reading (pointing to continents) - Vocabulary: heritage, culture, tradition, value, travel **Tier 3 (Levels K-M / 2nd Grade):** - Written descriptions with some detail - Independent map work (finding countries) - Understanding of trade and travel concepts - Vocabulary: import, export, trade route, cultural value, heirloom **Tier 4 (Levels N-O-P / 3rd-4th):** - Detailed written descriptions and stories - Research about countries/items - Understanding of global trade systems - Advanced vocabulary: commerce, cultural heritage, immigration, ancestral, provenance **For English Language Learners:** - Celebrate bilingual/multilingual backgrounds (huge asset!) - Bilingual item cards (item name in both languages) - Partner with fluent speaker for presentation - Visual emphasis (drawings, photos) - This activity centers their experience and expertise **For Students with Learning Differences:** - Reduce number of items (1-2 instead of 3-5) - Provide pre-made trunk template to decorate - Allow verbal presentation instead of written cards - Option to bring actual item instead of drawing - Pair with supportive partner **For Advanced Students:** - Research trade routes connecting item origins to current location - Calculate distance items traveled - Write detailed family heritage story - Lead map activity for class - Create "museum placard" with comprehensive information **For Students from Immigrant Families:** - This activity centers their experience - Option to highlight family immigration journey - Celebrate multilingual abilities - Share food, music, or traditions from heritage culture **For Students Without Strong Cultural Connection:** - Frame as "family traditions" or "things we do" - Items can be: favorite family foods, places visited, hobbies, local traditions - Emphasis that ALL families have unique cultures - Teacher models with own example --- ## STANDARDS ALIGNMENT **Common Core ELA Standards:** - **Relationship Skills:** Sharing personal stories, listening to others' experiences - **Self-Management:** Pride in heritage, handling emotions around identity --- ## FL INTEGRATION **Chapter 4 Financial Literacy Concepts:** 1. **Global Trade Connects Communities** - Core Lesson: Items in our homes come from around the world through trade networks - Activity Connection: Mapping item origins shows global interconnection - Age-Appropriate: Concrete visualization of where things come from 2. **Cultural Value vs. Monetary Value** - Core Lesson: An item's worth isn't only its price; cultural/emotional value matters - Activity Connection: Family heirlooms are "priceless" regardless of store cost - Age-Appropriate: Students identify items valuable for reasons beyond money 3. **Trade Routes and Commerce History** - Core Lesson: For centuries, people have traded goods across long distances - Activity Connection: Items traveled from origin points to students' families - Age-Appropriate: Basic understanding of how trade works geographically 4. **Inheritance and Non-Purchased Value** - Core Lesson: Not all valuable things are bought; some are given, made, or inherited - Activity Connection: Many trunk items weren't purchased by student's family - Age-Appropriate: Recognition that acquisition happens through multiple means **Non-Moralistic Approach:** - Activity celebrates all cultures and backgrounds equally - No hierarchy of "better" or "more interesting" cultures - Acknowledges that some students have strong cultural ties, others don't (both valid) - Focuses on sharing and appreciation, not comparison - Recognizes diverse family structures and backgrounds **Connection to Season Arc:** - Bridges from local trade (CH1-3) to global markets (CH4-8) - Introduces concept of goods traveling long distances - Sets up later chapters exploring specific world markets - Personal connection before abstract economic concepts --- ## ASSESSMENT OPTIONS **Observational (During Activity):** - Prepare your own trunk as model - Have world map ready and tested (can students reach it?) - Plan for students who may not have strong cultural connections - Research pronunciation of countries/places students might mention **Classroom Management:** - Create safe space for sharing personal/family information - Establish respect rules: listen, appreciate, ask kind questions - Be sensitive to students from diverse family structures - Allow students to pass if uncomfortable sharing - Monitor for students who might feel "less than" if their heritage seems "ordinary" - Celebrate ALL backgrounds equally **Common Challenges & Solutions:** | Challenge | Solution | |-----------|----------| | "My family is just normal/boring" | Reframe: everyone has culture! Favorite foods, holidays, family traditions all count | | Student doesn't know family heritage | Focus on "things my family does" instead of "where we're from" | | Sensitive family situations (adoption, foster care, etc.) | Broaden to "important to me" instead of only family heritage | | Student shares something very personal/emotional | Thank them for sharing; validate gently; move forward with care | | Other students make insensitive comments | Pause, teach: "We appreciate all cultures. Kind questions only." | | Some students have elaborate heritage, others minimal | Emphasize: all contributions are valuable; quality not quantity | | Map is too small for whole class | Use digital map projected, OR small groups take turns at map | | Students can't find their country | Teacher assists; use this as geography learning opportunity | **Cultural Sensitivity:** - Research pronunciations ahead of time - Don't put students on the spot as "representative" of a culture - Avoid stereotypes ("All people from X are...") - Celebrate multilingual students (ask them to teach a word!) - Acknowledge that culture is complex and diverse WITHIN countries too - Be aware of global events that might affect student emotions **Time Management:** - Trunk decoration can expand to fill time - set clear boundaries - Presentations take longer than expected - use timer or limit to 1 item each - Have extension for fast finishers (add more items, help others, create museum labels) - Can extend over multiple days: Day 1 = create trunk, Day 2 = share --- ## PARENT COMMUNICATION **Sample Take-Home Note:** *Dear Families,* *We're creating "Family Travel Trunks" inspired by our story about Mr. Mason's treasures from around the world! **Sensitive Topics:** Family heritage can involve immigration, loss, complex emotions - **Inclusivity:** All family structures are valid (adopted, foster, blended, etc.) - **Privacy:** Students choose what to share; no pressure for deeply personal info - **Respect:** Establish ground rules about kind questions and appreciation **Physical Safety:** - **If students bring items from home:** Handle with care, create safe display area - **Decorating materials:** Supervise glue guns, scissors, small decorative items **Cultural Safety:** - **Avoid stereotypes:** Research cultures respectfully - **Pronunciation:** Learn correct pronunciation of places and names - **Current events:** Be aware of global situations affecting students' heritage countries - **Avoid assumptions:** Don't assume student knows about or practices heritage culture **General:** - Create safe, respectful environment for sharing - Have tissues available (stories can be emotional) -
