# CHAPTER 2: THE LEMONADE PROBLEM - ACTIVITY PLAN **Chapter Theme:** Supply and demand dynamics **Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where friends learn that when supply exceeds demand, prices drop and everyone loses — but collaboration and differentiation help everyone succeed **Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10) **Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency) --- ## ACTIVITY TITLE **"Lemonade Stand Showdown"** --- ## STORY CONNECTION **Explicit Scene Links:** - **Scenes 1-4:** All four friends set up lemonade stands on the same street → Activity begins with competing businesses - **Scenes 5-9:** Customers are confused by identical stands; price war begins → Activity demonstrates oversupply problem - **Scenes 10-13:** Prices keep dropping as stands undercut each other; no one makes profit → Activity simulates race to bottom - **Scenes 14-17:** Mr. Mason suggests differentiation instead of competition → Activity introduces unique selling propositions - **Scenes 18-20:** Each stand adds something different; all find success → Activity concludes with variety benefiting everyone --- ## LEARNING OBJECTIVES 1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand supply and demand basics 2. **Economics Concepts:** Learn that oversupply decreases value; differentiation increases it 3. **Competition vs. Collaboration:** Recognize when cooperation serves everyone better than competition 4. **Pricing Strategy:** Understand how to price products in a competitive market 5. **Business Differentiation:** Learn to create unique value propositions 6. **Strategic Thinking:** Make decisions based on market conditions --- ## THREE TIME OPTIONS ### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity) **Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling **Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes **Activity:** Menu Design Challenge **Materials Needed:** - Paper for menus - Crayons, markers - Sample menu template (optional) **Steps:** 1. **Scenario Setup (2 min):** "Four lemonade stands all sell regular lemonade for $1. Which would you choose?" Discuss: they're all the same! 2. **Differentiation Introduction (2 min):** "How could each stand be different?" Brainstorm ideas: flavors, sizes, decorations, extras 3. **Menu Creation (5 min):** Each student designs a lemonade stand menu with something unique: pink lemonade, extra large, cookies included, fun cup 4. **Share & Compare (1 min):** Hold up menus. "Now which stand would you visit? Why?" **Discussion Questions:** - "If all stands sell the same thing, how do you choose?" - "What makes one stand more interesting than another?" - "Would you pay more for strawberry lemonade than regular?" --- ### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity) **Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling **Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes **Activity:** Lemonade Stand Simulation with Play Money **Materials Needed:** - Paper or cardboard for stand signs - Play money (printable or from classroom economy) - Menu cards (4 different stand concepts) - Optional: cups, pretend lemonade (juice or colored water) - Tally sheets for tracking sales **Steps:** 1. **Form Four Teams (2 min):** Divide class into 4 groups. Each is a lemonade stand. 2. **Round 1: Identical Stands (6 min):** - All stands sell "Regular Lemonade - $1" - Create simple signs - Half class shops with play money, half runs stands - Rotate: shoppers become sellers - Tally sales per stand 3. **Mid-Activity Discussion (2 min):** "Were sales even? How did customers decide? What problem do you see?" 4. **Round 2: Differentiation (8 min):** - Each stand adds something unique: Stand 1 = Strawberry, Stand 2 = Extra Large, Stand 3 = With Cookie, Stand 4 = Special Cup - Adjust prices if desired - Repeat shopping/selling rounds - Tally sales again 5. **Compare Results (2 min):** Graph Round 1 vs Round 2 sales. Discuss: "What changed? Why?" **Discussion Questions:** - "In Round 1, how did you choose which stand to visit?" - "In Round 2, did you have a favorite? Why?" - "Which round was better for the stand owners? Why?" - "What happens when everyone sells the same thing?" - "How does variety help everyone?" **Extension:** Create actual lemonade (or juice) for taste test of "different flavors" --- ### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity) **Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration **Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes **Activity:** Complete Lemonade Economics Simulation **Materials Needed:** - Stand materials (cardboard, markers, signage supplies) - Play money with various denominations - Product cards for each stand - Sales tracking sheets (detailed) - Price tags - Optional: actual lemonade/juice for taste testing - Optional: small cups, cookies, or extras for differentiation - Timer - Graphing materials **Steps:** 1. **Business Setup (7 min):** - Four teams create identical lemonade stands - Design basic signs - Set opening price at $1 (all the same) - Each team member assigned role: Seller, Money Handler, Customer Service, Inventory 2. **Round 1: Price War Simulation (8 min):** - All stands open with identical product at $1 - Customers visit (rotate half class) - After 2 minutes: teacher announces "Business is slow! Some stands are lowering prices!" - Stands can lower to 75¢, then 50¢, then 25¢ - Watch as prices drop but profits disappear - Track: number sold × price = revenue 3. **Analysis Break (4 min):** - Calculate total revenue per stand - Discuss: "Who made the most? Why not much?" - Introduce concept: price war = race to bottom = everyone loses 4. **Mr. Mason's Advice (2 min):** - Teacher role-plays: "Instead of lowering prices, make your stand DIFFERENT!" - Brainstorm differentiation ideas as class 5. **Round 2: Differentiation Challenge (10 min):** - Each team chooses unique angle: - Team 1: Flavored lemonade (strawberry, raspberry) - Team 2: Size options (small/medium/large) - Team 3: Combo deals (lemonade + snack) - Team 4: Themed experience (fancy cups, decorations) - Can price differently based on added value - Customers shop again (rotate) - Track sales and revenue 6. **Final Analysis (4 min):** - Calculate Round 2 revenue - Compare Round 1 vs Round 2 per stand AND total class revenue - Graph results - Discuss: "What strategy worked best? Why did everyone do better in Round 2?" **Discussion Questions:** - "What was wrong with the price war strategy?" - "Why did customers have trouble choosing in Round 1?" - "How did adding something unique change customer behavior?" - "Can you think of real businesses that differentiate like this?" (Starbucks vs. basic coffee, different pizza places, etc.) - "Is it better to be the cheapest or to be different?" - "What happens when four identical lemonade stands compete? What about four different ones?" **Extension Ideas:** - Calculate cost of ingredients; subtract from revenue to get profit - Research real lemonade stand pricing in neighborhood - Design ultimate differentiated lemonade stand concept - Write persuasive ad for their unique stand - Create customer surveys: "What would make you choose one stand over another?" --- ## THREE BUDGET TIERS

Core Lesson: When supply (lemonade stands) exceeds demand (customers), value drops - Activity Connection: Oversupply of identical stands = struggling sales; differentiation = healthy demand - Age-Appropriate: K-1 see it happen; 3-4 can name the concept 2. **Price Competition vs. Value Competition** - Core Lesson: Competing on price alone leads to race to bottom; competing on value leads to sustainable business - Activity Connection: Price war simulation shows everyone loses; differentiation shows everyone wins - Age-Appropriate: Experiential learning makes abstract concept concrete 3. **Differentiation as Strategy** - Core Lesson: Standing out through unique offerings attracts customers better than being cheaper - Activity Connection: Students create and experience preference for unique stands - Age-Appropriate: Concrete choices (strawberry vs. regular) build to abstract strategy 4. **Market Saturation** - Core Lesson: Too many identical offerings in one place hurts everyone - Activity Connection: Four identical stands struggle; four different stands thrive - Age-Appropriate: Observable through simulation **Non-Moralistic Approach:** - Activity presents market dynamics as neutral forces, not moral judgments - No shame language: "Competing is bad" ❌ Instead: "Different strategies have different results" ✓ - Acknowledges that price competition exists in real world - Focuses on strategic thinking, not character judgments - Celebrates creative differentiation **Connection to Season Arc:** - Builds on CH1 trading to introduce formal market concepts - Introduces pricing strategy explored throughout season - Sets up supply/demand theme that continues in CH3, CH6, CH8 -
