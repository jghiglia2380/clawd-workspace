# CHAPTER 7: THE FAMILY BAKERY - ACTIVITY PLAN **Chapter Theme:** Understanding cost of goods and pricing for profit **Story Connection:** Based on Scenes 1-20 where Mr. Mason shares memories of Italian family bakery where every ingredient costs money and pricing must cover costs plus profit **Target Age:** K-4th Grade (Ages 5-10) **Activity Type:** Single-session (modular scheduling: daily, weekly, or any frequency) --- ## ACTIVITY TITLE **"Baker's Math: Recipe Costs & Cookie Economics"** --- ## STORY CONNECTION **Explicit Scene Links:** - **Scenes 1-5:** Mr. Mason remembers visiting Italian family bakery where grandmother tracked every ingredient cost → Activity begins with ingredient cost tracking - **Scenes 6-10:** Learning that each loaf of bread has specific ingredient costs that must be covered → Activity calculates total recipe costs - **Scenes 11-15:** Understanding bakery must price bread high enough to cover costs AND make profit for family → Activity includes pricing for profit - **Scenes 16-19:** Discovering family businesses need sustainable pricing, not just break-even → Activity emphasizes profit margin importance - **Scenes 19-20:** Reflecting on how food businesses balance costs, quality, and fair pricing → Activity concludes with business sustainability discussion --- ## LEARNING OBJECTIVES 1. **Financial Literacy:** Understand that every ingredient in a recipe has a cost that must be tracked 2. **Cost Calculation:** Learn to add all ingredient costs to find total production cost 3. **Per-Unit Economics:** Calculate cost per serving by dividing total cost by number of servings 4. **Pricing Strategy:** Set selling prices that cover costs AND include profit margin 5. **Business Sustainability:** Recognize businesses need profit to survive and support families 6. **Real-World Math:** Apply addition, division, and money concepts to authentic cooking context --- ## THREE TIME OPTIONS ### 🕐 20-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Quick Activity) **Best for:** Tight schedules, younger students (K-1), daily scheduling **Total time:** 10 min story + 10 min activity = 20 minutes **Activity:** No-Bake Trail Mix with Quick Cost Tracking **Materials Needed:** - 4 simple ingredients (cereal, raisins, pretzels, mini chocolate chips) - Small paper cups (one per student) - Large poster with ingredient prices displayed - Scoop or measuring cup for each ingredient **Steps:** 1. **Introduce Ingredients & Prices (2 min):** Show four ingredients. Point to price poster: "Cereal costs 25¢ per scoop. Raisins cost 50¢. Pretzels cost 25¢. Chocolate chips cost 75¢." 2. **Mix Trail Mix (3 min):** Each student takes 1 scoop of each ingredient, adds to cup, mixes 3. **Calculate Cost Together (3 min):** Write on board: 25¢ + 50¢ + 25¢ + 75¢. Students help count: "What's 25 plus 50? Now add 25 more..." Total: $1.75 4. **Discuss Selling Price (2 min):** "Our trail mix costs $1.75 to make. Should we sell it for $1.75?" (No, no profit!) "How about $2.00? $2.50? $3.00?" **Discussion Questions:** - "How much did our trail mix cost to make?" - "Would you buy it for $2.00?" - "Why do we need to charge MORE than it costs to make?" - "What could a bakery do with the extra money?" (Pay workers, buy more ingredients, keep lights on) --- ### 🕑 30-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Standard Activity) **Best for:** Standard class periods, grades 1-3, weekly scheduling **Total time:** 10 min story + 20 min activity = 30 minutes **Activity:** Recipe Production with Cost Worksheet **Materials Needed:** - Trail mix OR rice krispie treats ingredients - Measuring cups and spoons - Cost tracking worksheet (one per student or group) - Pencils - Calculators (optional, for grades 3-4) - Small bags or cups for packaging - Price comparison chart showing store-bought version **Steps:** 1. **Recipe Introduction (3 min):** Display recipe on board. Show ingredient list with costs. Example: "Cereal $2.00 (we need 4 cups), Marshmallows $1.50 (we need 1 bag), Butter 50¢ (we need 2 tablespoons)" 2. **Production (6 min):** Groups measure and mix ingredients following recipe. One student can be "ingredient tracker" marking off items used. 3. **Cost Calculation (6 min):** - Fill out cost worksheet: list each ingredient and its cost - Add all costs together to find TOTAL COST - Count number of servings made (portions in bags) - Divide total cost by servings: COST PER SERVING 4. **Pricing Decision (3 min):** Groups decide selling price. Must be higher than cost per serving. Compare to store-bought version: "A rice krispie treat at the store costs $1.50. Ours costs 40¢ to make. What should we charge?" 5. **Reflection (2 min):** Share prices across class. Discuss: "Why are prices different? Which would make the most profit?" **Discussion Questions:** - "What was our total cost for all ingredients?" - "How many servings did we make?" - "What's the cost for ONE serving?" - "How much should we charge to make a profit?" - "Is our price fair compared to the store price?" - "What happens if we charge less than it costs to make?" **Extension:** Create price tags and packaging labels for finished product --- ### 🕒 45-MINUTE VERSION (Story + Extended Activity) **Best for:** Block schedules, grades 3-4, deeper exploration **Total time:** 10 min story + 35 min activity = 45 minutes **Activity:** Complete Bakery Business Simulation **Materials Needed:** - Baking ingredients (if oven available) OR no-bake recipe ingredients - Measuring tools (cups, spoons, scale if available) - Detailed cost analysis worksheets - Packaging materials (bags, ribbon, labels) - Calculators - "Bakery" branding materials (markers, cardstock for signs) - Pricing charts and profit calculators - Store-bought comparison items **Steps:** 1. **Business Planning Phase (5 min):** - Teams choose recipe from 2-3 options (different complexity/cost levels) - Receive ingredient cost sheet - Predict: "How much will this cost to make? How much should we charge?" - Assign roles: Head Baker, Ingredient Manager, Cost Tracker, Packaging Designer 2. **Production Phase (10 min):** - Follow recipe carefully measuring each ingredient - Ingredient Manager tracks ACTUAL amounts used (waste costs money!) - Note any mistakes or extra ingredients needed - Package finished products attractively 3. **Full Cost Analysis (10 min):** - Calculate ingredient costs (actual amounts used, not just estimated) - Add packaging costs (bags: 10¢ each, labels: 5¢ each, ribbon: 3¢ each) - Add "labor cost" (optional advanced concept): "If each team member earned $5/hour and you worked 20 minutes, that's $1.67 in labor" - Calculate cost PER ITEM (total cost ÷ number of items made) - Document all calculations on worksheet 4. **Pricing Strategy Development (6 min):** - Research: Display store prices for similar items - Set price that covers ALL costs + desired profit - Create pricing justification: "We charge $2.50 because it costs $1.20 to make and we want $1.30 profit per item" - Design price tags showing price and why it's worth it - Create bakery brand name and logo for labels 5. **Business Analysis & Reflection (4 min):** - Calculate: If you sold all items at your price, what's total revenue? - Subtract total cost = TOTAL PROFIT - Discuss: "Is this a sustainable business?" - Compare prices across teams: "Why are prices different?" - "What could you do with the profit?" (Reinvest, pay yourself, expand business) **Discussion Questions:** - "What surprised you about the costs?" - "Was any ingredient more expensive than you expected?" - "How much profit should a business make—a little or a lot? Why?" - "What happens if you price too low? Too high?" - "Why do real bakeries charge different prices for different items?" - "Besides ingredients, what OTHER costs does a real bakery have?" (Rent, electricity, wages, equipment) - "How does knowing exact costs help you run a business?" - "Could your family run a bakery business with what you learned today?" **Extension Ideas:** - Actually "sell" to another class using play money, track real profit - Compare costs of making vs. buying (make a batch vs. buy from store) - Research local bakery pricing and cost structures - Design complete business plan including monthly costs and revenue projections - Calculate break-even point (how many items must you sell to cover costs?) --- ## THREE BUDGET TIERS

**Supplies:** Ensure measuring tools are available or request parent donations - **Backup Plan:** Have backup ingredients in case of spills or mistakes - **Cost Worksheets:** Print or prepare handouts matching recipe complexity to student level **Classroom Management:** - **Hygiene:** Everyone washes hands before food handling; review food safety - **Stations:** Set up ingredient stations to prevent crowding - **Roles:** Assign specific jobs (measurer, mixer, cost tracker, timer) to keep all engaged - **Mixed-Ability Groups:** Pair students with different strengths (good at math + good at measuring) - **Simplified Worksheets:** Provide worksheets with fewer lines, larger numbers, or pictures - **Advanced Challenge:** Give advanced students complete business planning (rent, utilities, wages) -
